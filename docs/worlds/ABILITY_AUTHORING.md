# Ability families, variants and execution

Status: requested design direction and proposed contract v2, 2026-10-03. Master owner until an ability-GM assignment explicitly reserves this file. User permits multiple versions of similar abilities, situational or stage-dependent superiority, and simply stronger variants when their actual mechanism makes sense. [Ability GM](../gm/ABILITY_IMPLEMENTATION_GM_PROMPT.md) owns the reusable role; runtime implementation is not dispatched.

## Similar results do not imply identical sources

An ability family groups a purpose for comparison/search. A specific ability has its own method, source, owner, usable body, learned execution, local support and outputs. A family tag never makes one instance substitute for another. Two sources can produce visual perception while exposing different kinds of information; two copying methods can produce useful technique records while only one permits immediate execution.

Proposed definitions distinguish `familyId`, specific `abilityId/revision`, actual source instance, acquisition/lineage/provenance, learned method, mastery, local structural stage, operating mode, eligible targets, evidence inputs, supported functions and resulting owner/effects. These are authoring names, not new save fields or imported canon classifications.

Canonical Sharingan variants/owners/stages require a bounded Naruto source ledger. The original examples below illustrate mechanics and do not assert new Sharingan abilities or restrictions. Bleach soul-bound development likewise follows its actual source prerequisites, not a generic family tag.

| Original variant example | Inputs and useful output | Limit and possible growth |
|---|---|---|
| Practiced observation | Repeated visible performances, attention and study time produce an understood movement pattern | Hidden transitions, speed and unfamiliar anatomy limit evidence; mastery can improve segmentation and inference |
| Recording instrument | A charged, calibrated sensor stores supported visual/acoustic detail for later review | Recording is not understanding or reproduction; better optics/storage can widen its actual evidence |
| Motion mirroring faculty | A visible eligible movement and an acquired faculty permit a rapid supported motor imitation | The user's body, reach and control still matter unless the source supplies a real exception |
| Method reconstruction | Fragments, experiments and resources produce a tested usable procedure | Slow and uncertain, but can reconstruct something never witnessed complete; failure leaves paid evidence |
| Temporary functional imitation | A source briefly supplies a declared effect under actual support/cost limits | It may produce the function without permanent learning or donor anatomy; duration/termination are explicit |
| Source/faculty transfer | An eligible actual source moves under its world rule and custody/body contract | Donor/recipient consequences and compatibility differ from learning; no duplicated source by default |

An advanced rapid copier can legitimately outperform ordinary observation in every relevant dimension. Acquisition rarity, risk or cost may explain its place without giving the weak method a contrived counter. A cheaper method may still be convenient, but that is not a mandatory law. A different stage can reverse a matchup if its actual new function/support does so; the claim needs a concrete task and evidence.

## Comparison envelope

Compare outputs on a defined task: what is observable, understandable, reproducible, retainable or transferable? Include range, line of sight, speed, detail, required exposure, latency, reliability, novelty/generalization, compatible bodies/fuel/authority, duration, storage, cost/strain, detection and counters. Unknown values stay unknown; a low rank is not proof of a weak effect against every target.

For a technique-copy attempt, inspect whether the actor saw enough of the method; whether a record is accurate; whether they understand it; whether their body/source can supply its operations; whether support/fuel is available; and whether execution is legal on this actual target. The output can be a lead, fragment, understood method, temporary imitation, learned technique, or transferred source. State which one before commitment.

## Causal execution contract

| Phase | Required decision/evidence |
|---|---|
| Eligibility | Acquired source, learned/control prerequisites, body, target/location, custody, life/occupancy and valid local rule |
| Preview | Known requirements/costs/output range; no random draw, material spend, new knowledge or private target disclosure |
| Commitment | Freeze participants, definition revision, meaningful mode/parameters, actual input costs and permitted uncertainty |
| Resolution | Apply ordered paid effects to authoritative owners; damage, information, acquisition and relationship are distinct |
| Observation | Update only witnesses or supported reports; undiscovered mechanisms and exact private capacities remain unknown |
| Aftermath | Duration, cooldown, strain, wounds, source state and receipts persist under declared rules |
| Recovery/refinement | Real rest/fuel/repair/study/teaching can restore or improve supported use without replaying acquisition |

Fine details that need explicit local answers: whether a technique can target the caster or allies; how touch/sight/marks are established; whether an effect follows a moving target; range versus jurisdiction; startup versus sustained cost; what interrupts concentration; which effects coexist or suppress one another; whether reflected/copied attacks preserve provenance; and how a changed body/source affects remembered execution.

Effect ordering matters: a shield erected before impact, a delayed heal, a severed support channel and a transferred faculty can produce different legal outcomes. Overlapping effects need a local composition policy and consistent receipts. Do not choose whichever order helps the current plot. Simultaneous action support remains a missing selected resolver where the current runtime cannot express it.

## Variants, combinations and retained detail

One acquired method can have several paid modes without being several owned powers. Two different source instances can share a learned method while their outputs differ. A hybrid can retain constituent requirements or become a new source under an explicit fusion rule. Suppression disables specified execution; it does not erase learning/ownership or grant another person its source.

A copied method records where it came from, what was observed, inferred or tested, and the actual support used. Later changes to the donor do not silently mutate the copied record. The copier can refine it independently when the world permits; outperforming the original needs tested new functions, not a free universal upgrade.

Teaching can use demonstration, explanation, instruments, practice or inherited faculties. An expert may be poor at teaching; a manual may be incomplete; two schools can disagree. The learned result and the trusted source are separate. Source lore may enforce lineage/organ/soul requirements that ordinary instruction cannot replace.

## Remaining engineering and review gate

### Fine-detail review questions

| Detail | Local rule that must be answered |
|---|---|
| Incomplete/false evidence | Can an observation be mistaken, spoofed, fragmented or obsolete, and what test confirms it? |
| Transfer versus teaching | Who retains a faculty after transfer; which methods can be taught without moving that source? |
| Body/form changes | Which learned actions still work after injury, prosthesis, new anatomy, reincarnation or projection? |
| Target and timing | What happens if the target moves, dies, leaves range or loses custody between startup and completion? |
| Permanent versus temporary output | Is the result a record, trained method, temporary function, actual item/source or lasting world change? |
| Multi-source stacking | Are outputs additive, highest-only, independent, conflicting or requiring explicit fusion? |
| In-world recognition | Which observers can identify the faculty, estimate strength or discover copied provenance? |
| Surprise and deception | What clue exists, who can notice it, and which declared detector can bypass ordinary concealment? |
| Independent NPC use | NPCs need owned means and particular evidence; they can have genuinely superior abilities and their own progression |
| Repetition and failure learning | Which repeated uses award mastery/evidence, when knowledge is redundant, and which unique rewards cannot recur? |
| Changing definitions | Frozen commitments and copied records retain revision/provenance; a content update needs explicit compatibility |
| Return/history | Different bodies/instances retain only their declared domains; a past memory never proves a current target is identical |

This is a review index for unresolved local answers, not twelve implemented subsystems or a requirement to invent every failure everywhere. Source world rules, ordinary actions, modules and dialogue supply the concrete examples.

Existing simulation powers, knowledge stages, faculty/source lifecycle, custody, typed effects, paid commands and frozen encounters provide groundwork. `power_models.js` separates practice/design/investment/authority/hybrid execution. This contract does not implement all targeting, sustained concentration, reflection, simultaneous ordering, source physiology or general native combat.

An implemented variant must identify its actual existing planner/effect/owner or a precise missing adapter. Gate fixtures compare at least two variants on the same supported task, including a genuinely superior case if selected, different support/knowledge states, actual cost, learned versus possessed versus executed output, stale/invalid rejection, interruption, source suppression/restoration, repeat-settlement and reload. Browser evidence requires a rebuilt actual execution path.

Local ABILITY_VARIANTS owners are assigned in the future ability-GM lane; they link module/source/campaign/interaction owners. This shared family contract never becomes another native/simulation ability inventory. Design records and source comparisons remain separate from implemented behavior and user fiction/balance acceptance.

## Conceptual powers and mechanical difficulty, requested follow-through

Latest user steering asks the GM to consider JoJo's Bizarre Adventure, Universal Thief, conceptual abilities in this engine, and difficulty through interactions. The ability-GM design packet will own detailed reference cases and exact missing engine adapters. These comparisons are not a newly registered world or automatic acquisition of every reference power.

Primary JoJo adaptation locators inspected by the master: [Josuke / Crazy Diamond](https://jojo-portal.com/anime/du/character/01/), [Rohan / Heaven's Door](https://jojo-portal.com/anime/du/character/04/), [Okuyasu / The Hand](https://jojo-portal.com/anime/du/character/03/), and [Stardust Crusaders episode summaries](https://jojo-portal.com/en/anime/sc/story/). They support bounded repair exceptions, reading/editing behavior, spatial removal and time-stop comparison. Detailed source summaries belong in the conceptual reference dossier, not duplicated here. These are official anime explanations; exact manga mechanics and disputed extremes need their own inspection.

The closest title match is [Universal Thief, creator Adrien Dittrick's page](https://adriendittrick.itch.io/universal-thief). Its stated design combines turn-based RPG actions with puzzle ordering and stealing moves, HP and identity, including metaphysical concepts. This is a creator-description check; no source-code or gameplay audit has run. Actual interpretation must preserve which target property changes, who loses it, what the recipient can use, and what history/relationships remain.

Proposed conceptual contract: identify the affected state domain and target; establish the trigger/contact/evidence; declare scope and exceptions; commit actual costs and an ordered transformation; preserve custody, cause and discovery; then specify termination/restoration. A concept is a named mechanic with bounded targets and consequences, not a free-form string that the runtime executes as arbitrary code.

Original mechanical challenge examples: deny the opening contact while learning its trigger; use a repairable piece of cover to change a route; separate an imposed behavioral prohibition from the identity of its author; carry the eligible stolen tool while lacking the knowledge to operate it; choose a battle order that acquires a needed counter at an actual cost. These are original authoring examples, not claims of new canonical Stand rules.

Keep numerical strength useful: sufficient supported force can still overpower an ordinary obstacle. Mechanical difficulty can depend on target access, timing, evidence, line of effect, fuel/control, source interaction and competing objectives. A conceptual power need not grant an automatic win, and an effective counter should work for its actual reason. No invented immunity solely to force a puzzle solution. Unknown dangers retain honest uncertainty and failure terms.

### AB1 expanded: habits (proposal, 2026-10-13)

Weibao asked to expand AB1 and keep it simple. Working name: **habits**. A habit is one line, nothing more:

> **When** [condition], **use** [ability or action] **on** [target].

The character keeps a short ordered list. Each turn or tick the list is read top to bottom; the first habit whose condition holds and whose cost can be paid fires. If none fire, the character does their default action. That is the whole system. No variables, loops or nesting: a list, not a programming language.

| Part | Small starting vocabulary |
|---|---|
| Condition | My health or fuel below a share; an ally hurt; a named status on someone; first turn of a fight; outnumbered; enemy of a known kind; ability ready |
| Use | Any ability, stance, item or ordinary action the character actually has |
| Target | Self; weakest or most hurt ally; current enemy; strongest or weakest enemy; whoever is casting |

**Getting habits.** Start with one or two slots. More come from in-world means, never a menu purchase: repeating a manual action enough times (the [Earned Habit](../CHEAT_LIBRARY.md#8-earned-habit-automation) card), a teacher or school drill that arrives as a prewritten set, a disciple or familiar that learned by watching, or a device in worlds that have them (a terminal-programmed robot in Fallout). Worlds pick which routes exist.

**Rules of play.**
- Honest failure: a habit that cannot pay or find its target is skipped and logged. A bad habit can lose a fight; the log shows why.
- Conditions only read what the character knows. A habit can't test hidden facts, so sealed secrets stay sealed.
- Manual input always overrides habits for that turn.
- Offline: habits run as written and nothing new is learned about them. The return summary lists which habits fired, how often, and which kept failing.
- Enemies and NPCs use the same list, so enemy behavior and player automation are one system. Someone who fights you often can learn your habits and play around them.

**Desktop UI (deferred until selected).** A compact list in the ability panel: drag to reorder, a toggle per line, a fired count beside each. Mobile stays deferred.

**Smallest proof (unselected).** Two habit slots for the player in native combat using existing techniques and stances, saved on the hero with a migration, checked by rebuilt browser play, offline return and save, reload and import. It does not depend on the lost wider-gameplay runtime.

#### More to consider (AB1)

| H1 | Situation sets: separate lists for travel, fights, bosses and town that swap by place | Sets are just named lists; one is active |
| H2 | Habits for daily life: "when hungry, eat the cheapest food", "when tired, rest at home" | Same line shape outside combat; fits the life sim |
| H3 | Habits train: a line used often gets slightly faster or cheaper | One small mastery number per line |

| H7 | Smarter conditions: "and" between two conditions | Superseded by the depth levels below: richer rules are a player choice, not a world lock |
| H8 | Habits as a tell: a predictable list shows up in your reputation and in enemy dossiers | Reads the fired counts already kept |

Suggestions, separate from the angles: build the smallest proof before adding H1 to H8, because playing two slots will show which angles matter. H2 and H5 look most valuable after that.

#### Depth on request (2026-10-13)

Weibao: complex if a player wants it, simple if they don't. So habits come in depth levels. The player picks one and can change it any time; the game never forces a deeper level to keep up.

| Level | What the player sees | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|
| D0 Hands off | No list. The character acts on their own temperament (careful, bold, loyal) | Players who just want the idle part |
| D1 Styles | Pick one prewritten style: careful, aggressive, support, thrifty | One click, no reading |
| D2 Simple list | The when/use/on lines above | The default once habits unlock |
| D3 Advanced | "And" and "or", exact thresholds, target filters, situation sets (H1), "save this for the boss" | Players who like tuning |
| D4 Expert | Counters, short sequences (do A, then B next turn), text import and export, a dry run against the last fight | Tinkerers and sharers |

Two layers stay separate. **Capacity is in-world**: how many habits fit and which abilities exist come from slots, teachers, practice and world means. **Interface depth is the player's choice**: the same list can be written at any level. A world can make deep habits fit the fiction (a Fallout terminal, an artificer's charm), but no level is locked to one world.

Rules that keep both kinds of player happy:
- Simple is good enough. D1 styles and a sensible D2 list handle ordinary content. Deeper levels buy convenience and small edges, never a requirement.
- Nothing is lost going down. A D4 list shown at D2 reads "custom, 7 lines" and keeps running; editing it there asks before simplifying.
- Every list can be read in plain sentences, at any level.
- Deeper options stay hidden until chosen; the D2 screen never shows D4 controls.

#### Complexity check against the base game (2026-10-13)

Weibao asked whether this adds too much complexity versus the base game. Measured facts: upstream's base game has 35 source files and about 36,000 lines; this fork's `src` has 408 files and about 96,000 lines (top level plus one folder deep). Base combat is automatic. The player's only combat decision is choosing a stance (with favorite stances for quick switching).

Verdict (inference):
- **Habits themselves: low risk if built on what exists.** Base stance choice already is D1 styles. D0 is the base game unchanged. A player who never opts in sees nothing new.
- **Habits too early: real risk.** Natively there are few active abilities to choose between (stances, a handful of techniques, optional elemental preparation). A rule list over almost nothing is complexity without decisions. Abilities worth choosing come first.
- **The project overall: the larger risk.** Design depth runs far ahead of playable slices, and several runtime pieces are built but unverified (or lost, see the recovery note). Each new layer should earn its place in play.

Revised smallest proof: **stance habits.** "When [condition], switch to [stance]", two slots, inside the existing stance panel. No new ability system, no new screen, and it upgrades a base-game decision instead of adding a parallel one. Technique and ability lines join only once there are several worth picking.

Complexity rules for habits (proposal; may suit other systems):
- **Extend a base-game control before adding a new one.** Stances first, then the ability panel.
- **One new idea per slice.** Two stance habits ship alone; depth levels, situation sets and sharing wait until play asks for them.
- **Off means off.** D0 must play exactly like the base game, with no new prompts or panels.
- **Count decisions, not features.** A layer earns its place if it creates a choice players actually make; if logs show the same list never changes, it is decoration.
- **Remove or merge on evidence.** A layer nobody touches after a playtest gets folded into a simpler one.

#### Simple rules that build into complex things (2026-10-13)

Weibao asked about a set of simple rules and possibilities that build into complex things. This is already the project's stated core: [emergent systems](../EMERGENT_SYSTEMS.md#the-core-idea) says surprise comes from small systems colliding, and [E4 tags and rules](../emergent/E4_TAGS_AND_RULES.md) is the build-ready draft. What is new here is applying it to abilities and habits, which shrinks the AB list instead of growing it.

**A small kernel (proposal).** Everything ability-shaped is made from four pieces:

| Piece | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Verbs | A short fixed list of things an actor can do | Strike, guard, move, give, take, change a tag, wait |
| Tags | Words on people, items and places | Wet, burning, marked, tired, hidden, holy |
| Triggers | Moments when rules are checked | On hit, on kill, at dawn, when hurt, when a tag appears |
| Rules | When [tags and facts], then [verbs] | "Wet and struck by lightning: stunned" |

An ability is a named bundle of verbs and tags with a cost. A habit is a rule the player writes. An enemy's behavior is a rule list. A world law is a rule every actor obeys. One grammar, many uses.

**The AB list collapses into the kernel:**
- **AB2 modifiers** are tags on an ability (split, delayed, silent) read by rules.
- **AB3 party combos** need no combo system: one ally's habit marks a target, another's habit fires on marked. The combo emerges.
- **AB4 event-fueled moves** are triggers (when hurt, on near-death) feeding a counter tag.
- **AB5 the active budget** is a cap on how many bundles are ready, which is a tag on the actor.
- **AB1 habits** use the same condition language as everything else.

Verified groundwork: `src/rules.js` already evaluates `eq`, `gte` and `lte` fact rules (with `all`, `any` and `not` in the simulation contracts), and one effect pass is capped at 256 expanded effects (`src/simulation_effects.js`). Habit conditions can reuse that rule language instead of a new one. Not built; inference from source.

**Guardrails so complexity stays readable:**
- Every chain explains itself in one line: "Stunned, because wet and struck by lightning."
- Chains stop at the existing cap; a rule cannot trigger itself twice in one moment.
- Busted combinations are allowed, findable and rare, as the [life-sim north star](../LIFE_SIM_NORTH_STAR.md) already says.
- New worlds add tags and rules as data, not new code paths, unless the fiction needs a true exception.

#### More to consider (simple rules)

| ID | Angle |
|---|---|
| SR1 | Keep the kernel small on purpose: if a new verb is proposed, first try to build it from existing verbs and tags |
| SR2 | Special-case test: when a designer must hand-write an exception, note which kernel piece was missing; add it only if three cases need it |
| SR3 | Discovery log: the first time a chain happens, the Notebook records it and lets the player name it |
| SR4 | Teaching by small steps: the game introduces one tag or trigger at a time, and the first combos are nearly guaranteed so players see the idea work |
| SR5 | Same rules for enemies: an enemy using wet plus lightning teaches the player the trick by doing it to them |
| SR6 | Worlds as rule swaps: a world changes a few rules ("fire does not burn underwater", "names have power"), and everything built on them shifts |
| SR7 | Habits that write tags: the simplest D2 line can still start a long chain, so depth comes from the world, not from the habit editor |
| SR8 | Emergence check in playtests: count combinations nobody authored that players found; zero means the kernel is too closed, chaos means too open |

**Progression through the kernel.** A character grows along five simple lines: more verbs (new abilities), more tags they can see (perception, appraisal), more triggers they can use (reflexes), more rules they can write (habit words, SR9), and more capacity (slots, fuel, scale). Levels and numbers still exist; these five say what a level actually unlocks.

**Cheats through the kernel** (a mechanism index beside the library's gameplay families):

| Tags | See hidden tags (appraisal, system panels) |
| Triggers | An extra trigger, such as "on death, rewind" |

#### One sense, many versions (2026-10-13)

Weibao asked for different types of the same sense or observation. The [sensing skill list](../SKILLS_AND_TALENTS.md#knowing-and-sensing-appraisals-siblings) covers many different senses; this covers many versions of one sense. In kernel terms a sense is a **tag filter**: which tags it reads, plus a few dials.

| Dial | Range of answers |
|---|---|
| Reads | Which tags: living, hostile, hidden, magic, lies, value, weakness |
| Medium | Light, sound, smell, heat, mana, soul, fate, data |
| When | Now, traces of the past, a glimpse ahead |
| Shape | Touch, cone, line of sight, radius, through walls, anywhere you have been |
| Detail | Something is there; what kind; how much; the exact number |
| Delay | Instant, a beat late, only after resting or studying |
| Error | Misses things (false negatives) or cries wolf (false positives); can be fooled by a named trick |
| Tell | Silent, or a ping others can feel back |
| Shared | Only you, your party, or your habits |

Example: **danger sense** in eight versions. Dread (yes or no). Compass (which way). Countdown (how soon). Face (who). Weight (how bad). Odds (how likely). Guardian (warns about allies, not you). Scar (only dangers you have survived before). Each one plays differently with the same name.

Look-alike pairs that should not be merged: life sense and soul sight (undead show in one, not the other); lie detection and insight (one flags a falsehood, the other reads intent, so a sincere mistake fools only the first); appraisal and value sense (what it is versus what it is worth here).

A sense grows along its dials: wider filter, longer reach, less delay, more detail, fewer errors, quieter tell. Because habits can only read tags the character can sense (SR9), better senses directly make smarter automation.

#### World progression (2026-10-13)

Weibao asked about world progression. [Technology](../TECHNOLOGY_AND_PROGRESSION.md) covers tech and eras, [timelines](../TIMELINES_AND_DIVERGENCE.md) covers the natural course of events, and the [north star](../LIFE_SIM_NORTH_STAR.md) mentions world tiers. Missing is the world itself as something that grows. In kernel terms the world gains tags and rules over time.

| Line | What grows | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Places | Areas open, change or decline | A cleared road becomes a trade route; an abandoned mine floods |
| Settlements | Hamlet to village to town, by population, trade and safety | Your repairs and deliveries raise the village a step |
| Era | Tech, magic or institutions advance | A school opens; printing spreads rumors faster |
| Attention | How much the world notices you | Bigger deeds draw rivals, patrons and stronger threats |
| Danger | Threats adapt and escalate | Monsters you farm learn your habits (SR5) |
| Memory | The world keeps what happened | Ruins stay ruined; a saved town remembers |
| Rules | The world's own laws shift | An eclipse season changes which rules apply |

Keep it simple: each world picks two or three of these lines, shows them as a small world panel, and each step comes from things that actually happened. The world also moves without the player, at its own pace.

#### Cheats that add whole systems (2026-10-13)

Weibao asked whether types of cheats could add entire systems to worlds, or whether that would be too easy. [Cosmology](../CHEAT_COSMOLOGY.md) already notes system cheats follow their system's reach. Proposal: yes. A **system cheat** is a bundle of verbs, tags, triggers and rules scoped to its holder. Examples: a level-and-status system in a world without one; a shop that sells across worlds; a pocket realm that adds base building; a crafting blueprint system; a world's native power (cultivation) carried into another world.

Is it too easy? Cheap to build is good; cheap to win is the risk. It stays interesting when:
- **You get the frame, not the content.** The system starts nearly empty and fills from local play.
- **It translates and collides.** Imported rules meet native ones; some things convert, some clash, and native systems stay better at their own jobs.
- **The world reacts.** Attention rises, and people want what you have.
- **It has a price or a voice.** Quests, upkeep or a patron's agenda.
- **Scope is a choice.** Only you, people you share it with, or it spreads like a contagion and changes the world (ties to world progression).

The real risk is sameness: if every world gets the same imported system, worlds blur. Guard: an imported system must speak local terms, and each world keeps at least one thing only its native rules do well.

Bonus for development: a system cheat is an in-fiction feature flag. A new system can ship first as one holder's cheat, get tested in play, and only later become a world law.

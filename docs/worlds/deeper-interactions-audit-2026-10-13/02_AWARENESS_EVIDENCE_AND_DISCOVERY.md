## AE-F2: Nine different questions, not one detected flag

| Layer | Example | Legitimate cause of change |
|---|---|---|
| Actual effect | A command overrides one refusal for five minutes. | Successful paid resolution under its exact definition. |
| Subjective experience | The request feels reasonable, alien, frightening or irresistibly urgent. | Authored phenomenology and the target's current state. |
| Awareness | The target notices an unusual urge or loss of control. | Perceptible symptom, feedback or supported sensor result. |
| Suspicion | The target considers outside influence one explanation. | Contradiction, informed comparison or a received warning. |
| Mechanism attribution | They suspect suggestion rather than illness or ordinary persuasion. | A mechanism-specific clue with understood limits. |
| Actor attribution | They suspect the stranger, a relic or a proxy. | Supported link between observed evidence and that handle. |
| Proof | A trace establishes this specific affected event under a declared test. | Valid evidence chain, applicable method and corroboration. |
| Reporting | They tell a friend or lodge a complaint. | Their chosen action, reachable recipient and channel. |
| Social belief | The friend accepts, doubts or repeats the allegation. | Received report, trust, competing evidence and local incentives. |

These can diverge. A target can know they were controlled without knowing who
caused it; an institution can believe a false accusation; a caster can know their
spell landed without knowing whether the target noticed. No universal ladder
forces every event to reach proof or public exposure.

## AE-P1: Effect-specific discovery envelope

Every proposed effect card names: affected layer; operation and target;
intensity/control extent; onset; sustain/expiry; subjective signature;
observable cast and effect signatures; trace lifetime; memory behavior;
eligible detection/counter methods; attribution rules; reporting constraints;
and permitted durable aftermath. Unknown canon details stay unknown.

| Original example, not a canon claim | While active | On ending | Possible discovery route |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temporary calming | Fear/arousal eases; judgment need not change. | Baseline fear can return if its cause remains. | Target notices abrupt contrast, but comfort alone proves no manipulation. |
| Brief favorable suggestion | One proposal appears unusually attractive within declared limits. | Forced preference stops; decisions and incurred costs remain. | Later comparison with prior intentions raises suspicion; no automatic realization. |
| Compelled movement | Motor action overrides refusal; target may remain conscious and opposed. | Motor authority returns. | Felt inability to stop can establish experienced constraint, not caster identity. |
| Enthrallment with concealed self-justification | Durable allegiance or devotion changes within its exact maintenance contract. | Restoration, decay or continued learned attachment follows the selected rule. | Trusted friend's comparison, specific trace sensor, broken command condition or genuine self-reflection. |
| Memory suppression | Accessible recollection of a bounded interval is blocked. | Expiry restores only what the method actually retained. | Journal, missing time or another witness; a gap alone does not prove the culprit. |
| False memory insertion | A specific recollection is experienced as real. | Inserted recollection may persist unless removal is explicitly part of the effect. | Independent record conflicts; memory examiner must distinguish a trace from mere disagreement. |
| Sensory illusion | The target perceives a supported false appearance. | Perception returns; inference formed from it may persist. | A mismatch across senses or an unaffected observer supplies a clue. |
| Genuine ordinary persuasion | New reasons change a voluntary position. | There is no magical duration to expire. | The person can explain their reasons; convincing others does not prove absence of magic. |

Strength, concealment and durability are independent properties. Strong control
can be obvious; subtle influence can last; a weak influence can escape discovery
forever. A superior source may outperform a weaker one without an invented
universal counter. Applicable world/source rules determine actual limits.

## AE-P2: Actors learn through causes

AE-T1: Resolution writes actual effect state and only its declared feedback.
A visible gesture can reveal that someone cast something, with uncertain target,
mechanism and success. It cannot by itself prove that an observed later choice
was controlled. An illusion of casting can also produce a false inference.

AE-T2: Supported perception writes a scoped observation, not omniscient truth.
The target, a nearby witness and a remote observer may perceive different clues.
Presence needs compatible senses, attention and unobstructed transmission.
Concealed, redirected or proxy-origin signatures retain the actual provenance
privately; witnesses first identify the apparent handle they could perceive.

AE-T3: Comparison can create or revise an allegation. Compare an available
memory, journal, companion account or actual sensor reading. Repetition can
increase familiarity without increasing accuracy. Preserve contradictory records
and their causes; do not overwrite a historical allegation with a definitive
statement merely because the new row has a higher knowledge stage.

AE-T4: Investigation pays real costs and rechecks applicable means. A generic
magic sensor might detect active magic but cannot classify intent or reconstruct
an expired trace. Counter-influence, truth assessment, memory examination and
identity verification are separate supported functions. A negative result means
"nothing found under this test," unless the method guarantees exclusion.

AE-T5: Expiry changes the effect owner, not the knowledge of every participant.
No trace or symptom is required by default. A person can never discover the
influence. Conversely, a person can remain suspicious after a failed attempt,
while the attempted caster falsely believes it worked unless feedback proves it.

AE-T6: Dispelling removes only eligible supported components. Learning who
performed the dispel requires its own observation or report. Relief does not
identify either the original caster or rescuer, and ending influence does not
automatically remove memories, debts, transferred objects or authentic subsequent
feelings. A lasting friendship may grow from later voluntary shared life; that
possibility does not retroactively make the initial control voluntary.

AE-T7: Reporting transports a statement and its available basis. It can be
withheld, delayed, interrupted, distorted or honestly mistaken. Recipients gain
only the report they actually receive. Institutional response may follow a
credible allegation before proof, under authored local rules; acceptance of the
allegation and actual guilt remain different states.

## AE-P3: Minimal extension shape and player projection

First implement one finite authored event with two independently selected
observer routes, not a universal mental-state simulator. Reuse actor knowledge,
audiences and causes for a known clue/report. Add a selected evidence-history
adapter only when correction or conflicting allegations become necessary.
The current overwrite/cap semantics need an explicit persistence decision before
promoting their rows into authoritative forensic evidence.

Character views show a known experience or claim with source and timing:
"You felt unable to stop," "Mara suspects a charm," or "The examiner found no
active trace using this test." Do not show secret caster IDs, exact remaining
duration, target awareness, concealed resistance or actual guilt through previews,
people cards, search, counts, tooltips, VFX, logs or return recaps.
A caster-facing success signal appears only if their method supplies it.
A named witness route may remain hidden until discovered, without a teaser badge.
Player lore and character knowledge remain separate under their existing owners.

## AE-A1: Concrete acceptance scenarios, specified but NOT RUN

| Scenario | Required observable outcome |
|---|---|
| Temporary suggestion expires unseen | Forced preference ends; no awareness/accusation row appears; paid action consequences persist. |
| Obvious compulsion by concealed source | Target recalls lost control; no caster identity appears; unrelated witness learns nothing. |
| Public cast, unrelated later choice | Witness learns the visible act only; no proof-of-control or automatic trust penalty against its caster. |
| Enthralled target rationalizes allegiance | Behavior follows the exact control contract; target can lack suspicion; informed friend may hold an independent allegation. |
| False accusation reaches trusted official | Official can react to the received allegation; actual culprit state remains unchanged; later contrary evidence can revise belief without erasing history. |
| Failed detector, delayed independent clue | Paid test reports its limited negative result; a later journal discrepancy opens investigation without retroactively changing the old result. |
| Unknown helper dispels an effect | Control ends, incurred costs and remembered history remain; target does not automatically identify the helper or original source. |
| Voluntary attachment after temporary influence | Expiry removes only imposed preference; separately caused voluntary memories/feelings survive according to their own owners. |

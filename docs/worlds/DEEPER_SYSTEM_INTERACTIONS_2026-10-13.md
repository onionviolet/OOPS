## DSI1: Success changes what the world needs

We have inventions, trade, teaching and reputation ideas. The deeper question is
what stops a successful method from remaining the best action forever.

Proposal: selected authored discoveries change a local opportunity, rather than
requiring a universal economy simulation. A cheap healing method saves patients;
sharing it makes routine treatment common; the town then needs teachers, supply
quality checks or rarer treatment. Keeping it exclusive supports a different
life. Progress shifts what matters instead of merely inflating enemy statistics.

Consider who benefits, who loses income, what materials become scarce, what
travels with the method and whether it can be learned without its inventor.
An NPC adapting is not necessarily an enemy or punishment for success.

Smallest taste: one taught recipe changes one NPC's service and one demand row,
with a notice explaining why. No global prices or endless resource drains.

## DSI2: NPCs decide what your power means to them

Existing regard, promises and counterplay proposals are adjacent. Still worth
developing: people want things independently of how much they like you.

Proposal: an authored person has a current goal and a boundary. A friend can
decline a dangerous favor, admire your discovery but refuse mind-reading, or
teach your method to someone you dislike. Their action should have a legible
reason. A coercive ability can change behavior without magically creating trust.
Different observers can call the same act rescue, interference or theft.

Smallest taste: a trusted colleague refuses one request, offers a workable
alternative and retains the friendship. Avoid universal obedience at max regard.

## DSI3: Choices make histories, including imperfect ones

Promises and divergent copies already exist as proposals. The missing emphasis
is how choices accumulate into a life worth continuing when it is not optimal.

Proposal: some successes create commitments; failures can create useful routes
without turning every failure into a disguised reward. Accepting an office gives
resources and ties up some time. Losing a contest can lead to an apprenticeship
while preserving the loss. Repairing a betrayal needs a relevant act and may
restore cooperation without erasing the event.

Make alternate routes recognizably different, with stated local costs. Do not
make arbitrarily withholding information the mechanism for permanent regret.
Keep player branching/reroll freedom distinct from character-facing consequences.

Smallest taste: one failed application opens paid assistant work, while the
successful applicant receives a different role and obligation.

## DSI4: Quiet success needs its own playable shape

Retirement, survival and perspective handoff are already considered. The deeper
question is whether a comfortable present life has satisfying play before ending.

Proposal: the player can keep a small shop, maintain a friendship, master one
craft or support a household without constantly escalating danger or prestige.
Offer explicit chapter closure and optional continuation. A settled life can
remain as a supported presence when perspective changes, subject to a declared
time and activity policy. Do not invent unsupported autonomous achievements.

Smallest taste: finish an apprenticeship, choose a sustainable weekly routine,
see who it supports and choose to continue, travel or close that chapter.
Preserve scope: this is a palette, not a requirement to build another prestige
layer or activate another world now.

## DSI5: Delegate repetition while retaining consequential choices

Helpers, queues and absence policies already have proposals. The deeper idle
design question is which decisions deserve the player's attention after mastery.

Proposal: repeat an understood routine through a plain standing instruction,
then interrupt for an exception the player has chosen to review. A helper can
run the shop within a stock/spending limit; a damaged shipment or unfamiliar
contract returns to the player. The report names what happened, its cause and
the unresolved decision. Delegation should preserve causal ownership and costs.

There should be safe defaults and a cadence setting. No demand for constant
attendance, relationship babysitting, closed-browser punishment or a new
programming language. An absence report can show material changes without
simulating everything every minute. Explain whether a displayed fact is known,
estimated or unresolved, especially when knowledge is part of the world's play.

Smallest taste: one existing repeatable task gains a bounded standing order and
one authored exception. No general agent scheduler.

## Suggested connected design experiment

One inventor's local story can exercise all five: discover a useful method,
share or retain it, negotiate a colleague's terms, see one local consequence,
choose a continuing role and delegate the routine. Compare the resulting
decisions and visible aftermath before expanding the engine.

This is a suggestion only. It does not displace the active crafting work,
resume paused World1 work, require new worlds or authorize implementation.

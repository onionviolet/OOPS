## Worked journeys (proposal)

### 1. Medicine delivery before the storm

The medicine crate must reach a neighboring settlement before the delivery window closes. The player can spend owned stock and time to repair the skiff and carry it, pay an available navigator for a shorter witnessed crossing, or refuse and let the window close. Repair leaves the player responsible for the boat; the navigator route spends money and adds a witness; refusal creates no debt but loses the delivery opportunity. Resolve each crossing as one authored outcome with explicit cost, elapsed time, route evidence and custody. There is no general sea/weather/navigation simulation in this slice; the storm is a deadline. The tide ledger is a separate clue only if the slice needs it to establish route evidence. Food cargo and a pirate shortcut are deferred proposals.

### 2. The doubtful hull

A shipyard client wants a fast patch on a structurally unsound vessel. The player can document the defect, arrange a costly rebuild, or accept payment for a cosmetic fix. The first preserves evidence but may lose the contract; the rebuild consumes materials and schedule but can make the next voyage viable; the cosmetic job buys time while leaving the hazard. Water 7 Arc rev. 2118675 records a ship diagnosis beyond ordinary repair and the difference between temporary attempts and safe travel; this local case is invented.

### 3. The offered crossing

A crew offers passage in return for a watch shift and one ration share. Accepting places the player inside that crew's route and risk; negotiating changes the burden if the crew agrees; refusing leaves the player free but without that crossing. A threat stronger than the player may win. Escape, rescue and return depend on actual route and other actors, not narrative protection.

## Escalating arcs (proposal)

1. **The missing ledger:** source record, local witness and cargo delivery establish the opening.
2. **Berth and belonging:** paid work, crew invitations and a missed watch shift alter actual relationships.
3. **The repair queue:** shipyard materials and labor are finite; a public order or a private claimant can receive them.
4. **The contested chart:** a record may be copied, guarded or traded; custody is separate from deciphering it. Poneglyph rev. 2128060 is the secondary anchor for indestructible originals and portable rubbings.
5. **A route with no safe scale:** the player chooses a fixed dangerous crossing, hires a qualified guide, or stays and loses a time-bound opportunity.
6. **A branch worth keeping:** a witnessed intervention creates a new local history. It does not rewrite other continuity instances or restore lost people automatically.

Each arc must specify trigger, occurrence, observer, response, refusal/expiry, actual aftermath and what survives reload. Local losses may be permanent. Death, reincarnation, terminal run end, canon rollback and offline loss remain unselected policy rather than hidden assumptions.

## Return and transfer

Physical travel preserves the real body, wounds, learned methods, owned sources, remaining fuel, named cargo and consenting companions. A fruit's sea weakness remains its source rule; a visitor's unrelated power is not erased by arrival. Nonphysical passage, borrowed body, resurrection, and carrying unique world objects require an explicit adapter. Return needs a real route and settled or explicitly interrupted work. Default absent-instance and closed-browser behavior remains frozen until shared runtime policy selects otherwise.

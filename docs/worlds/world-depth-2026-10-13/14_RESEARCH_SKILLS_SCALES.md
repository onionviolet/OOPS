## 1. In-game research (RS)

### 1a. Research as a player activity

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| RS1 | **A question board, not a points bar.** Default: research starts when the player writes or picks a question ("why does the shutter swell?", "what does this rune do?") that sits in the Notebook with its known evidence. Dial: questions auto-suggested from observed oddities, or player-typed only. Reasonably: a question costs nothing to hold but attention to work. | Gives research a visible object the way a quest does. It also fixes the IR01 intent at the UI layer: you research a thing, not "research". |
| RS2 | **Hypothesis as an `interpreted` belief.** Default: a guess is stored at `interpreted` with an inference basis (CODE1, CODE9). Experiments promote it to `understood` or write a refuting entry under a sibling key. Dial: how many guesses can be open at once. | Uses the current ladder with no schema change. Because a lower stage cannot overwrite a higher one (CODE1), a wrong `understood` belief needs a same-rank correction, which is a nice in-world truth: unlearning takes as much proof as learning. |
| RS3 | **Wrong conclusions stay believed until tested.** Default: some sources hand out a false or partial belief that reads like any other note; the player acts on it and sees the result. Dial: a small "confidence" word on each note (heard, think, checked). Reasonably: careful players pay time to verify. | Lets knowledge be wrong (rule 6) without a lie flag in the UI. Ties to Unbiased lens: notes report what the character has, contradictions included. |
| RS4 | **Partial understanding unlocks partial options.** Default: `seen` shows a thing exists, `interpreted` opens a risky attempt, `understood` opens the reliable version. Dial: risk size of the interpreted attempt. | One ladder drives the option list, so research visibly widens choices instead of adding a percent. |
| RS5 | **Source reliability is per source, not per fact.** Default: each `attributedTo` (CODE2) has a track record the player learns: the gossip who is right half the time, the textbook with a known error. Dial: whether the track record is shown or left to the player to remember. | Turns NPC informants into a research skill. A meso memory (source reputation) built from micro events. |
| RS6 | **Sources by channel.** Books (custody, edition, marginalia), teachers (time, terms, CODE4 and CODE6), informants (price, motive), trial and error (stock and injury), observation (presence at the right time), other worlds (a carrier per rule "Knowledge doesn't automatically cross"). Default: each channel writes a different `basis.kind`. | Lets the same fact feel different by route and lets later checks ask "did you read this or were you told?" |
| RS7 | **Negative results are kept.** Default: a failed test writes "not this" to the question, so the board shows a shrinking search space. Dial: whether NPC researchers share their negatives. | Makes failure feel like progress, which the idle genre needs. IR04 names the idea; this is the UI promise. |
| RS8 | **Instruments limit questions.** Default: a question the held tools cannot answer stays greyed with the missing means named (scale, lens, spell, library pass). Reasonably: borrowing or commissioning the instrument is a side quest. | Joins research to custody and workshop access (WS02, CW10). |
| RS9 | **Publication and credit.** Default: depositing a finding (CODE3 `deposit_research`) gives standing with that one institution and puts your name on the record. Dial: secrecy route keeps the edge but forfeits standing; a rival can publish first. Reasonably: credit is remembered by a named recipient, not globally. | Research becomes social. Micro note, meso standing, macro diffusion (MA06). |
| RS10 | **Priority disputes and stolen work.** Default: a collaborator (CODE3 Kael, CODE5 shared inquiry) can claim joint credit or sell your notes. Dial: how often rivals act. | Gives a reason to choose collaborators and to keep notebooks safe (custody). |
| RS11 | **Research rivals have their own clock.** Default: one named rival per research field progresses on the meso clock (SC9) and sometimes reaches a result first. Reasonably: their result is then buyable, tradeable or stealable. Dial: rival speed, or off. | Natural-course pressure without a timer on the player. Fits Solo player, no "once" limits: losing a race is a branch, not a lockout. |
| RS12 | **Research that unlocks recipes, cheats, options and AUs.** Default: an `understood` entry is a requirement on a command, like the existing `seen` and `owned` facts in CODE5. Some questions reveal that this world is a variant (AU evidence) without naming its sealed truth until the route ends. | Research is how many earned options are earned (see JN1). AU detection stays on its sealed route. |
| RS13 | **Idle research while away.** Default: absence stays frozen (CODE8). Dial: an appointed actor (assistant, librarian, the rival, a household AI) continues bounded reading jobs with stock already handed over; experiments that need hands do not run. Reasonably: the return log lists what the helper read and what they think, as `interpreted` with their name as source. | Offers idle progress without a wall-clock grant, and the helper's view can be wrong. |
| RS14 | **Incubation.** Default: sleeping or doing other work after a stuck question gives one free "next thing to try" on return. Dial: off for players who want pure deduction. | A cozy, cheap idle hook that rewards stepping away, keyed to played sleep, not wall time. |
| RS15 | **Research debt.** Default: unread books and unsorted samples pile up as visible stock that slowly decays (paper damp, samples spoil). Dial: decay rate. | Ties research to care (book care owner exists: `src/book_care_*.js`) and makes storage matter. |
| RS16 | **Other worlds' knowledge needs translation.** Default: an imported fact arrives at `interpreted` in the new world until a local test confirms it (IR08). Reasonably: physics may match, magic may not. | Keeps cross-world knowledge useful without making it free. |
| RS17 | **Teaching what you found.** Default: teaching a finding to an NPC uses the CODE4 teaching terms and lands them at `interpreted` or `understood` by practice minutes. Dial: students who improve on it. | Closes the loop from personal research to meso institution. |

## 2. Subtle skills (SB)

### 2a. How quiet skills grow, show and cross

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| SB1 | **No bar by default.** Default: subtle skills have hidden XP; the player sees them through effects only. Dial: a "quiet skills" page that names them once they reach a noticeable step. | Keeps the life-sim feel and avoids a hundred bars. Uses the CODE7 rule: show only what is known. |
| SB2 | **Growth by use, with a twist.** Default: XP from doing, slowed after repetition in the same place with the same person, so variety teaches more. Dial: the slowdown size. | Rewards a lived life over grinding one door. |
| SB3 | **Growth by teaching.** Default: a teacher session (CODE4 terms, CODE6 lesson) jumps a subtle skill a step and gives one named tip that shows in the log. Reasonably: some quiet skills are hard to teach (knowing when to leave), others easy (etiquette). | Gives teachers a role beyond combat drills. |
| SB4 | **Growth by observation.** Default: being present while a skilled NPC does the thing gives slow XP and a log line naming them. Dial: off for "learn by doing only" runs. | Makes NPCs matter as models; mentors can be people who did not agree to teach. |
| SB5 | **Shows as log voice.** Default: at each step the log wording for that activity sharpens (from "you try to bargain" to "you let the silence do the work"). | Cheapest visible growth; no UI change. |
| SB6 | **Shows as extra options.** Default: a step opens one new verb on a person or object (ask a follow-up, offer a smaller coin, listen longer). | Matches RS4: growth widens choices. |
| SB7 | **Shows as fewer failures.** Default: a quiet skill removes a specific bad outcome (you stop spilling ink, stop insulting the host). Dial: explicit "avoided" log line or silent. | Felt competence without numbers. |
| SB8 | **Shows as NPC remarks.** Default: a named NPC comments once per step ("you write a clean hand now"). Reasonably: remarks come from people who have seen the skill used. | Gives social proof and ties to the witness rules (CW24). |
| SB9 | **Shows as tells to others.** Default: high quiet skills leave tells (a clerk's hand, a soldier's way of standing) that appraisal can read. | Skills become part of identity and disguise (CW23). |
| SB10 | **Cross-world carry.** Default: body-bound quiet skills (handwriting, posture, knife feel) carry with the soul at a step down in a new body; culture-bound ones (etiquette, haggling norms) arrive at `interpreted` and need local practice. Dial: carry level per world. | Applies "Knowledge doesn't automatically cross" to know-how, with a reason per skill. |
| SB11 | **Decay by disuse, slow.** Default: quiet skills drop a step only after long disuse, and come back fast. Dial: off. | Lets a veteran feel rusty on return without punishment. |
| SB12 | **Habits as a side effect.** Default: a heavily used quiet skill becomes a habit that runs on its own (you check exits on entering), visible as a free action and a tell. Dial: whether habits can be broken. | Habits are how a life leaves marks; also the TTKS B1 replacement detector (part 04 TT1) in reverse. |
| SB13 | **Palette of world-neutral quiet skills.** Reading people, haggling, handwriting, cooking nuance (salt, timing), etiquette, lying, listening at doors, memory palace, route sense, care for tools, knowing when to leave, keeping a secret, sleeping anywhere, small talk, letter writing, packing a bag, reading weather. Default: each exists as a hidden skill only in worlds where a consumer uses it. | A list to fit per world, not a requirement to build all. |
| SB14 | **Knowing when to leave as a real skill.** Default: a step shows a soft cue before a scene turns bad (a host's glance, a guard's shift change). Dial: cue clarity. Reasonably: ignoring the cue is allowed. | Very cheap, very lifelike, and it pays off for careful players. |
| SB15 | **Care for tools.** Default: tools used by a careful hand lose durability slower and the log notes it; a lent tool comes back with the borrower's care level. | Joins custody and loans (CODE in `simulation_effects.js:276-277`) to a quiet skill. |

## 3. Scales (SC)

### 3a. Definitions

| Code | Scale | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC1 | Micro | **One action, one item, one conversation.** The player picks a verb on a target with held means. Time step: played minutes (existing `timed` jobs, `src/lore_practice_content.js:28-29`). Remembers: knowledge entries, item condition, one relationship memory. | The current engine's home. Most code lives here. |
| SC2 | Meso | **Household, shop, squad, class, district, season.** The player sets routines, assigns people, takes or refuses commissions, runs a term. Time step: day, week, school term, pay period. Remembers: ledgers (stock, debts, favors, rosters, standings), routines, reputation inside the group. | The missing middle. Players live here in life sims; macro without meso feels distant and micro without meso feels like chores. |
| SC3 | Macro | **Institution, city, war, era, world.** The player petitions, publishes, funds, joins or opposes. Time step: season, year, era; natural-course calendar. Remembers: world facts, laws, prices, institutional decisions, history entries. | Where canon events and long consequences sit. |
| SC4 | Meso | **Meso is a container with its own ledger.** Default: each meso unit (a squad, a class, a shop) owns one ledger like CODE4's `village_career` state: members, duties, stock, favors, last week. Dial: number of meso units active per world. | Gives meso a save owner, so it is real and not a summary. |
| SC5 | Meso | **Meso has a weekly beat.** Default: a short end-of-week line per unit (shifts done, who was late, what ran out, one remark). Reasonably: it is derived from micro receipts, not rolled. | Cheap, makes the middle visible, and is the natural idle-return report. |
| SC6 | Meso | **Meso actors act without the player.** Default: members with duties do them on the meso clock when they have means (the recruit's solo patrol, CODE4 `:155-170`). | Proves the middle is alive; the hook for appointed idle work. |

### 3b. Crossing scales

| Code | Direction | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC7 | Micro to meso | **Repetition with a recipient becomes a pattern.** Default: N similar micro acts toward one group (repairs for one shop, lessons for one class) change that group's ledger: a standing, a routine, a favor. Dial: N. | Respects the causal chain rule: each step has a consumer. |
| SC8 | Meso to macro | **A group's ledger crosses a threshold and an institution hears.** Default: an informed recipient reports up (a sergeant's report, a librarian's deposit record, CODE3) and one institution changes one decision. | Macro change happens through people who know, not a global meter. |
| SC9 | Meso clock | **Rivals and natural course run on meso time.** Default: research rivals (RS11) and group routines tick weekly. | Keeps macro from needing per-minute simulation. |
| SC10 | Macro to meso | **Laws, prices and wars change what the group can do.** Default: a macro fact edits meso ledgers at the next weekly beat (ration cut, new fee, term cancelled). | Downward flow is cheaper than upward and makes the world feel bigger. |
| SC11 | Meso to micro | **Group state changes the options in a scene.** Default: a squad short on water shows a thirst option; a shop with debt prices higher. | The player feels macro events through small choices. |
| SC12 | Skipping scales | **Strong means can jump.** Default: a power that solves a city's water at once updates macro directly, with the skipped meso ledgers noted as "bypassed" (rule in `docs/RULES_FROM_SCRATCH.md:320-322`). | Avoids a resistance tax while keeping records honest. |
| SC13 | Cut points | **Each chain records where it stops.** Default: a consequence lists which next scale it did not reach. | Keeps scope honest and visible to agents (tier per facet, DT9). |

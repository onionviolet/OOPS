# Deeper considerations: magic-academy worlds (B lane, 2026-10-13)

Proposals for Weibao's "deeper considerations for each world?" Nothing here is accepted. Each row is a default plus a dial; numbers are illustrative. Repo-sourced facts cite the owning doc's IDs; anything from memory is marked "(general knowledge)" and needs a source check before a private pack relies on it. No sealed secrets are defined or touched for these four worlds; the secrets register has no entries for them.

Already covered elsewhere and not repeated here: entry routes, creation screens, system levels and observation rules ([entry matrix](../../../docs/worlds/BOOK_WORLD_ENTRY_MATRIX.md)); MOL's guest lamp and sewer arc, loop rules, reserve units and shaping ladder; HP's potions, Time-Turner, Polyjuice, ER01 to ER12 and R01 to R10; JJK's power layers, domains, techniques, eras, courtroom and divergence ledger; D&D's source contract, edition axis, Phandalin repair slice and DND00 to DDC15 coverage families.

## Mother of Learning / Cyoria (`docs/worlds/mother-of-learning/`)

This pass goes past the guest lamp/sewer arc and the deep dive's loop and reserve notes. It covers Cyoria as a city: ward upkeep, well geography, crystal money, guns as the mundane equalizer, aranea civil status, and the rebuilt city after the ending as a home for a strong character who comes back later.

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters / micro-to-macro chain |
|---|---|---|
| MOL1 | **Unique verb: keep your wards up.** In Cyoria everyday magic is upkeep: rain shields, alarm wards on a rented room, message-landing points, workshop cleanup wards. Default: a mana-less guest buys and renews ward service from a local mage; Dial: a mage-born origin draws and recharges its own. | Wards cost finite crystals or a mage's time, and some need a permit (source: forgotten ward-permit liability, WORLD.md 66 to 80). Chain: skip a renewal, the alarm lapses, your notes get stolen, you file a Guild complaint, and now a clerk knows what you research. |
| MOL2 | **Mana-well rank as a place stat.** Cyoria sits on a Rank 9 well, Knyazov Dveri on Rank 2 (source). Default: well rank sets ambient mana, recovery speed for mages, creature density and which specialist shops can afford to exist. | One number explains why the big city has odd architecture, dangerous fauna and expensive rent, and why low-mana rural jobs pay in barter (source). A guest without a mana source still feels it through prices and monsters. |
| MOL3 | **Crystals are fuel, not a bank.** Mana crystals power trains and devices. Default: they sell in small lots at a few shops; Dial: a bulk sale opens a scrutiny track (source: big sales exceed one shop's funds and bring surveillance, burglary and legal checks). | This blocks the "farm crystals, dump them" loop without a hard cap. A strong player who comes back and floods the market drops the local price and gets a police and Guild file. |
| MOL4 | **Guns are the mundane equalizer.** Firearms broke battlemage armies after the Splinter Wars; lethal rods can be restricted where guns are easy to get (source). Default: a licensed firearm is a real combat route for a mana-less guest; Reasonably: it is loud, it needs a permit, and a prepared ward shield can stop it. | It gives the no-mana guest a combat tool that does not pretend to be magic. Look-alike pair a fan will notice: dispel does nothing to a bullet in flight, and a shield ward does. |
| MOL5 | **Aranea civil status.** Aranea webs trade information for magic, compete with each other, and ended the story still legally classed as monsters (source epilogue). Default: you trade with one named web, each web distinct; Dial: a long recognition campaign through public walks, class visits and testimony. | Micro: one honest trade gets a web's trust. Macro: you can push the city's expulsion, extermination or recognition factions. Humans gossip about "the spider-friend", and some shops turn cold. |
| MOL6 | **The outsider's warning.** In any instance that includes the festival night, a warning from an unknown guest moves slowly: the Church handles urgent and anonymous reports with credibility checks and days of mobilization (source C051). Default: the warning helps in proportion to the evidence you hand over. | This is canon pressure from the citizen's side, not the specialist emergency work the synthesis already covers. Refusing to warn, warning with no proof and warning with a captured sample each leave different aftermaths. |
| MOL7 | **You are the one who doesn't reset.** The loop belongs to Zorian (matrix). In a canon-month instance, the player sees a third-year who suddenly knows far too much, every day. Default: an odd-record noticing hook (JJK Q4 pattern) that lets you see him change without explaining why. | It makes the canon loop something felt from outside without granting the player loop powers. Dial: a fellow-looper premise needs its own declared entry and cost, and it is not a hidden unlock. |
| MOL8 | **Your corpse is a reagent here.** Necromancy is real, corpse-material potions carry fees, and engineered oozes eat bodies in the upper sewers (source). Default: a death in the Dungeon leaves a body that can be recovered, sold, eaten or raised; Dial: a paid burial or cremation contract set up ahead of time. | Death becomes a logistics problem with a market. Chain: if nobody claims a companion's body, it can come back later as somebody else's skeleton or turn up in a potion shop's stock. |
| MOL9 | **Fame from a cure.** The Weeping thinned the priests and left people wary of healers. Kael's success brought recruiters, reporters and permit trouble (source). Default: selling a working remedy builds fame on a track you can see, and that fame brings offers, rivals and inspectors. | Healing stops being a free side income. It also makes a believable mid-game career for a guest without mana who learns alchemy, since plant handling needs no personal source. |
| MOL10 | **The train mutes you.** Trains and stations run a ward that inhibits shaping (source). Default: in transit you can't cast and your devices are dampened; Dial: a first-class compartment or permit loosens this. | Small, fair and easy to notice. Travel time becomes a vulnerable window for mage origins, and the shared ward explains why the station is busy with beggars and newsboys (source) rather than mages. |
| MOL11 | **Golems as the take-home servant.** Golems carry a mana signature and face a legal size limit (source). Default: a bought or built golem can cross worlds as an item with a finite crystal store; Reasonably: away from Cyoria's crystal supply it runs down and then sits idle. | This is a cross-world chain with an honest cost: a Cyorian helper in the Frontier is a fuel problem. Late-game it pushes the player to look for a crystal substitute in another world's materials. |
| MOL12 | **The rebuilt city as a late home.** After the ending the school stays shut for a month, property prices fall, research debts pile up and aranea status is still contested (source epilogue). Default: a post-ending instance offers cheap property, reconstruction contracts and labs moved out of homes. | It gives a strong returning character a reason to settle: buy low, rebuild, sponsor. Overcrowded boarding houses and parents pushing for the school to reopen create quiet ongoing work, not a crisis. |
| MOL13 | **Licence, permit, membership.** Guild credentials, permits and membership are three separate things (source). Default: each unlocks different services: selling spells, carrying a restricted rod, using the Guild library. | Players will mix these up, so the UI should name each one separately. Chain: a licensed seller without membership can sell, but gets no legal defense when a customer's spell goes wrong. |
| MOL14 | **Two kinds of spider.** Aranea are sapient and keep contracts. Grey hunters are predators that sense magic (source). Default: they look alike from a distance; telling them apart takes knowledge or a careful look. | A fan will catch a wrong attack. Chain: kill an aranea scout you took for a hunter and the web's trade terms worsen. Look-alike pair: rain shield and umbrella, where a guest who opens an umbrella gets a local's raised eyebrow. |
| MOL15 | **Presentation.** V6 tint: academy indigo with brass. V7 header: a well-rank glyph, a rain-shield icon when it rains, and a festival-day countdown in instances that have the festival. V8 edges: mana exhaustion as a dim violet edge for mage origins, and ambient-mana sickness as a sour green edge near the Hole. | Log voice: dry, precise, a little sardonic, counting in casts and crowns. A panel unique to this world: the **ward ledger** (your wards, charge left, permit, last check). Sound: chalk on slate for formula work, a low hum near the well. |

**Smallest playable taste:** MOL1 with MOL15's ward ledger. The guest has no mana, so this fits the existing route without loop or festival content. A rented room, a paid alarm ward and a renewal date make one daily-life loop with a visible chain if you skip it. It reuses existing finite purses and actors.

## Harry Potter (`docs/worlds/harry-potter/`, beyond `docs/gm/dispatch/harry-potter-craft-research-2026-10-12/`)

The craft packet covers potions, Time-Turners, Polyjuice, ER01 to ER12 and lived-world routes R01 to R10. This pass covers the school as a group institution, wizard money and goblin ownership, transport networks, household labor, sport and press, save-reading spells, and what draws an adult back. Canon facts below are general knowledge unless noted, and none come from the private novel files.

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters / micro-to-macro chain |
|---|---|---|
| HP1 | **Unique verb: earn and lose points for your house.** One act moves a shared score that housemates see (general knowledge). Default: staff award or deduct points for acts they witness, with each teacher's bias saved; Dial: the House Cup brings standing, not loot. | No other world pools your reputation like this. Chain: you lose 50 points on a night outing, housemates go cold, fewer study partners offer help (an R06 consumer), and your exam prep suffers. |
| HP2 | **Sorting reads your life, not a form.** The Hat weighs the wearer's choice (general knowledge). Default: Sorting happens in-world and reads recorded receipts (what you did, chose and refused) plus your stated wish; Dial: a rare refusal of an ill-fitting request. | This differs from the matrix's creation-screen "house leanings". The house then sets your dorm, common-room password, table, rivals and which older students notice you. |
| HP3 | **Goblin-made means maker-owned.** Goblins hold that what a goblin makes belongs to its maker, and that a buyer only rents it for life (general knowledge, book 7). Default: goblin-made items carry a maker's claim that comes due when the buyer dies. | A cross-system chain: you buy a goblin-made heirloom, your heir expects it, and Gringotts sends a claim. That ties the bank, succession and goblin relations together without inventing law. |
| HP4 | **Vaults and keys.** Wizard money sits in Gringotts vaults behind physical keys and goblin escorts (general knowledge). Default: withdrawing means a trip, a key and some time; Dial: an adult outsider's only legal way to change Muggle money is at the bank counter. | Money has a place and a custody owner, not a global purse. Losing the key is a real problem, and a bank-error event can hit the vault's contents and leave your pockets alone. |
| HP5 | **Exposure and memory cleanup.** Obliviators fix magic seen by Muggles by changing witnesses' memories (general knowledge). Default: a visible spell on a Muggle street opens a cleanup event; a Muggle friend who saw it may forget it, or may forget you. | Secrecy gets a personal cost instead of a flat fine. Chain: one show-off spell, the cleanup squad visits, a Muggle friendship resets, and your Ministry file grows (C06 already covers detection, and this is what happens after). |
| HP6 | **Each transport network has its own rules.** Floo (registered fireplace links), Knight Bus, Apparition (licence at 17, splinching), brooms and Portkeys (general knowledge). Default: each has its own access, cost and failure mode; a home's Floo link is a Ministry-visible connection. | Chain: cutting your Floo link buys privacy but strands you. A misspoken grate puts you in the wrong shop. Splinching is an injury with a real healer visit, not a reload. |
| HP7 | **An inherited house-elf has its own wants.** House-elves serve one family, often unpaid, and clothing frees them (general knowledge). Default: an inherited elf takes the household chores and obeys the family head, whose orders can clash with yours; Dial: freeing it gets a reaction you can't predict, from grateful to grieving. | Domestic labor becomes a relationship with ethics and costs, not a free buff. Chain: the elf obeys a relative's old order, a secret leaks, and the family quarrels. |
| HP8 | **Quidditch season.** House teams, tryouts, scheduled matches, broom quality and hospital-wing injuries (general knowledge). Default: practice is an idle schedule, matches are calendar events everyone watches; Dial: betting and bookmakers in adult instances. | Sport makes a visible social reputation that grades don't. Chain: a new broom gift, a team spot, a rival house's grudge, and a bludger injury that costs you an exam week. |
| HP9 | **The press writes its own version.** The Daily Prophet slants stories, and in some eras under Ministry pressure; the Quibbler is the odd alternative (general knowledge). Default: what the public "knows" about you is a separate claim from your receipts. | Fits the observer rule: shopkeepers, parents and staff react to the article, not the truth. A single interview can backfire, and a correction costs real evidence (an ER01 consumer). |
| HP10 | **Permission slips.** From third year, Hogsmeade weekends need a guardian's signed form (general knowledge). Default: the household origin (Mara Fen in the brief) decides this through earned trust or refuses. | This is the cleanest link from home life to school access: trust built at home opens a village, a shop and a social weekend. Refusal leaves you at the castle on those weekends, which can be lonely or useful. |
| HP11 | **The Patronus reads a happy memory you actually have.** The charm runs on a real happy memory, and its form can change after big emotional change (general knowledge). Default: Patronus strength reads a recorded good moment (a friendship, a rescue, a festival); Dial: a lonely save struggles to cast it. | No other world's spell is fueled by your save's relationships. Chain: protect a friendship and your defense gets stronger; lose that friend and the form may change. |
| HP12 | **Boggart and Dementor look alike, but differ.** Both work on fear. The Boggart turns into your worst fear and loses to laughter; the Dementor drains happiness and loses to a Patronus (general knowledge). Default: the Boggart's shape comes from receipts (the foe that killed you, the thing you fled most). | Two save-reading counters with different fixes, and a fan notices a mix-up instantly. Chain: Azkaban's guards make sentencing more frightening, and in some eras they slip out of Ministry control (public twist). |
| HP13 | **The Defence post curse.** Nobody keeps the Defence Against the Dark Arts job more than a year (general knowledge, public twist). Default: in this world it is a lore absolute; an adult who takes the job gets one school year and an exit nobody can predict. | It gives a strong returning adult a reason to visit: teach for a year, shape a class, then leave. Those students carry your methods into later events. |
| HP14 | **Bequests go through the Ministry.** A will's items can be held for review before heirs get them (general knowledge, book 7). Default: inheritance has a wait and an inspection, and a Squib or Muggle-born heir faces different access. | This deepens succession past "no guaranteed inherited magic" in SUCCESSION_BY_WORLD. Combined with HP3, an heir can lose an item to the Ministry and another to the goblins. |
| HP15 | **Muggle tech fails near strong magic.** Electronics don't work at Hogwarts (general knowledge). Default: devices from tech worlds stop working in high-magic HP sites; Dial: a shielded case or a lower-magic place lets them run. | A clean cross-world rule with an in-world reason. A carried Frontier gadget becomes a Muggle-world asset and dead weight at school, and the reverse is a fun puzzle. |
| HP16 | **Presentation.** V6 tint: your house colors at school, candle-gold parchment elsewhere. V7 header: the Great Hall ceiling shows the real sky (general knowledge), a ready-made ambient header, plus term or holiday. V8 edges: Dementor chill as a frost edge, splinching as a torn edge. | Log voice: warm, slightly whimsical, but knowledge-safe. A panel unique to this world: **house hourglasses** that fill with points as you watch. Sound: an owl at the window when mail arrives, house elves' clatter at feasts. |

**Smallest playable taste:** HP1 with HP16's hourglasses. One staff witness, one deduction and one housemate reaction show a shared reputation loop that no other world has. It needs only actors, witnessed acts and a shared counter, with no new magic system.

## Jujutsu Kaisen (`docs/worlds/jujutsu/`)

The brief covers power layers, domains, techniques, eras, the courtroom and divergence. This pass covers the back office of jujutsu society: the mission pipeline and cover stories, grade promotion, tiny classes, the healer bottleneck, corpse handling, seal upkeep, clan house life, work abroad and quitting. Canon facts are general knowledge (manga) unless noted. Public names belong to the private pack; Frontier echoes use original names.

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters / micro-to-macro chain |
|---|---|---|
| JJK1 | **Unique verb: drop the curtain, then cover it up.** A mission runs: a window reports it, it is assigned by grade, an assistant manager drives you there and lowers a curtain, you exorcise, and the cover story (a gas leak) goes out (general knowledge). Default: every job ends with a cover story and a damage bill. | Here the world's knowledge is something you clean up. Chain: if the curtain fails, a rumor leaks, local fear rises (an E3/E6 mood fact) and more curses spawn there next week (feeding E2). |
| JJK2 | **Grade promotion is a social gate.** Moving up a grade takes recommendations from senior sorcerers and then trial missions (general knowledge). Default: grade sets which missions and pay you get; promotion needs real sponsors who can be persuaded, can owe you or can refuse. | It makes "grades lag reality" (L12) into a mechanism. Chain: a sponsor's clan politics stalls your promotion, you get underpaid assignments, and you go freelance (Mei Mei-style pay haggling). |
| JJK3 | **A class of three.** Tokyo's school has tiny year groups living together on a hidden campus (general knowledge). Default: your class is a three-to-four-person squad, and relationships run deep rather than wide; teachers are often away on missions. | Losing one classmate removes a quarter of your social world, so grief and loyalty weigh more here than anywhere else. Small rosters also keep it cheap to author. |
| JJK4 | **The Goodwill Exchange.** An annual competition between the Tokyo and Kyoto schools (general knowledge). Default: a fixed calendar event with team and individual rounds, scouting and inter-school rivalry; Dial: politics behind it in canon-pressure instances (public twist: someone uses it as cover). | A yearly milestone gives the school life a rhythm, and both schools' staff are watching, which feeds JJK2 sponsorship. |
| JJK5 | **The healer bottleneck.** Healing others with reverse cursed technique is very rare, and one doctor carries the school (general knowledge). Default: injuries past self-healing wait in her queue, and she also does autopsies. | Chain: if she is away during a big incident, that decides who survives. A strong player who learns to heal others changes the whole world's survival curve, and every faction then wants them. |
| JJK6 | **A sorcerer's corpse is a stealable asset.** Techniques live in the brain, and body-hopping needs a corpse (T7). Default: the body goes to autopsy and then cremation; Dial: a clan demands the body back. | Chain: you skip the protocol, the body is stolen, and years later someone uses that technique against you. It joins the brief's funeral rule to T7 at a macro scale. |
| JJK7 | **Seals decay.** Special-grade objects are sealed and stored; in chapter 1 a weakened sealed finger draws curses to a school (general knowledge). Default: seal strength drops on the world clock and needs re-sealing upkeep; Reasonably: under the frozen-absence default it drops only while that world's time runs. | It is an idle-native upkeep loop with a clear stake. Chain: neglect a relic in your keep and the neighborhood's curse rate climbs. |
| JJK8 | **Tengen's barrier network.** One immortal being maintains the barriers over sorcery sites, and its stability is an era-scale question (general knowledge; the Star Plasma Vessel is in the brief's era table). Default: the network is infrastructure your big actions can strain or repair. | It moves a canon pressure point from fights to logistics. Damaging a barrier node has wide effects, and a nobody (a barrier technician) can matter here. |
| JJK9 | **Abroad works differently.** Japan has unusually heavy curse activity, and foreign sorcerers have their own traditions (general knowledge; the density claim is inferred and needs a source check). Default: overseas postings mean fewer curses, different methods and foreign politics. | It gives strong returning sorcerers a reason to leave and come back, and suits original odd-world remixes. A foreign student also brings a technique tradition the school can't read. |
| JJK10 | **Life inside a clan compound.** In the Zenin clan, status follows your technique; members without one are treated as lesser, and women face hard limits (general knowledge). Default: compound life has servants, ranks and in-house squads; leaving costs inheritance and protection. | The brief covers clan heirs; this is the daily texture. Chain: a technique-less heir builds a reputation outside, comes back stronger, and the household order breaks (the Maki precedent). |
| JJK11 | **A cult as an economy.** A curse user can run a religious group for money and fear (general knowledge). Default: the cult pays errand-runners well, collects donations from non-sorcerers, and carries legal and execution risk. | It gives the nobody list's "errand-runner" a real economy. Chain: donations fund the curse user, whose stockpile then hits your district. |
| JJK12 | **Quitting counts as an ending.** Most sorcerers die young; one canon adult left for office work and returned (general knowledge). Default: leaving the field is a saved ending with ongoing ordinary life; Dial: coming back with skills slightly rusty against a world that moved on. | Long-life play gets meaning: retirement is an achievement, not a failure. It also gives heirs a parent who walked away. |
| JJK13 | **Shibuya, Halloween 2018.** A dated incident where a curtain traps civilians inside (general knowledge, public). Default: in a main-story instance it sits on the divergence ledger as a fixed date; a civilian life there gets survival routes (go underground, leave early, shelter strangers). | Abstaining is a real choice with its own aftermath, and it pairs with the brief's "being a nobody". A warning given the week before follows MOL6's credibility pattern. |
| JJK14 | **Physical tools travel; technique tools may not.** Some special-grade tools hold a technique, and one canon tool is pure physical power with none (general knowledge). Default: what kind of power a carried tool has decides whether it works in another world. | It sharpens the brief's "foreign worlds may not read them". A purely physical tool is a safe export, while a technique-bearing one becomes an odd object (an outlier, rule 12) in the Frontier. |
| JJK15 | **Presentation.** V6 tint: night navy with ink black and a thin blood-red accent; inside a curtain the frame darkens as the veil falls (general knowledge: it pours down like black liquid). V7 header: district curse density plus the time of day (for after-hours vows). V8 edges: low cursed energy frays the edge, and domain burnout shows as static. | Log voice: terse mission reports ("Grade 2. Assignment..."), with manga-style sound effects on big hits. A panel unique to this world: the **mission report** with damage bill and cover-story field. Look-alikes: shikigami and curse, curse user and sorcerer (same powers, different licence), incomplete domain and simple domain. |

**Smallest playable taste:** JJK1 as a Frontier echo with original names. Report, assignment, curtain, cleanup and cover story make an idle mission loop. A failed curtain raising local fear facts shows the micro-to-macro chain using mood facts the Village already has, with no techniques needed.

## D&D / Forgotten Realms (`docs/worlds/dnd/`)

Code note: `DDC1` to `DDC31` here are a new list (renamed from DND1 to avoid a clash). They are not the COVERAGE_MAP families `DND00` to `DND15`.

The foundation packet covers sources, editions, the Phandalin repair slice and the coverage families. This pass covers the life below the rules: the rest rhythm, the Harptos calendar, lifestyle and downtime, finite spell services, costly components, consent to resurrection, attunement, bastions, factions, parties and a DM-voiced presentation. Rule facts marked general knowledge need a check against the selected profile (O1 SRD 5.2.1 or a full 2024 book) before use.

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters / micro-to-macro chain |
|---|---|---|
| DDC1 | **Unique verb: choose when to rest.** A short rest takes an hour (C05); a long rest restores slots and hit points about once a day (general knowledge). Default: the adventuring day is the unit of attrition, and you pick where and when to reset; Dial: a gritty-rest variant per instance (general knowledge, DMG). | No other world is built around daily resource refresh. Chain: rest in the dungeon and get interrupted, or walk back to town and lose the day's lead. Absence grants no rest unless that world's clock runs. |
| DDC2 | **The Calendar of Harptos.** Twelve 30-day months split into tendays, five festival days outside the months, and Shieldmeet every four years (general knowledge, setting lore). Default: rent, wages and markets run per tenday; festival days close shops and fill shrines. | The calendar gives the world clock a Realms flavor at almost no cost. Shieldmeet works as a rare day for renewing oaths and contracts, and pairs with DDC10 factions. |
| DDC3 | **Lifestyle sets who talks to you.** In 2014 rules a daily lifestyle tier runs from wretched to aristocratic (general knowledge; check whether the selected profile keeps it). Default: your paid lifestyle sets which rumors you hear and which doors open. | Living poor saves gold but blocks noble audiences; living rich drains the purse and draws thieves. It is a quiet, steady sink for idle money. |
| DDC4 | **Training is not leveling.** Languages and tool proficiencies can come from downtime training over many days at a daily cost (general knowledge; exact days and price depend on the profile). Default: training is a committed job on the clock, separate from class levels earned by experience. | Look-alike pair a player will mix up: a proficiency from training and one from a class or background. Chain: a shrine helper (ST3) trains in herbalism tools and becomes the town's backup healer. |
| DDC5 | **Shrines have finite spell slots too.** Temples sell spellcasting services (general knowledge), and a priest's slots run out each day just like yours. Default: a town's healing capacity per day equals what its casters have prepared. | Chain: after a raid, the shrine runs dry by noon, triage choices fall to whoever is there, and your own slots suddenly matter to the town. It plugs straight into ST3. |
| DDC6 | **Costly components are real stock.** Some spells consume priced components, such as diamonds for bringing back the dead (general knowledge, SRD). Default: costly components are finite items in local shops, and a focus covers the free ones. | Chain: a small mining town's diamond stock sets how many people can be raised after a disaster. One supply caravan matters more than one hero. |
| DDC7 | **The dead can say no.** Raising someone needs a soul that is free and willing (general knowledge, SRD), with time limits that differ by spell. Default: an NPC's soul may decline if it is content or bound to its god; Dial: the player's own soul can choose the afterlife as a real ending. | This adds consent to the DND15 death horizon. It also gives succession a dignified route: the heir takes over because the parent chose to stay gone. |
| DDC8 | **Owning a magic item is not attuning it.** At most three attuned items, attuned during a short rest (general knowledge, SRD). Default: an item gives its full power only once attuned; a cursed one may refuse to let go. | It is a clean cross-world gate: carry five magic items into the Frontier and the attunement rules still decide which work. A fan checks this right away. |
| DDC9 | **Bastions as the late-game idle loop.** The 2024 DMG adds a stronghold with special facilities, hirelings and orders every seven days (general knowledge; likely outside the SRD, so verify). Default: bastion turns run on world days the player spends there; Dial: a steward who acts while you are away, with their own judgment. | It gives a strong returning character a reason to keep coming back: a workshop, garden or library producing on a tenday rhythm. The hirelings are people with wants, not machines. |
| DDC10 | **Faction renown.** Harpers, Zhentarim, Lords' Alliance, Emerald Enclave and Order of the Gauntlet each track standing on their own (general knowledge, 5e setting). Default: renown rises with each faction separately, and their goals conflict. | Chain: a Zhentarim caravan job raises one score and quietly lowers another; a Harper contact stops sharing rumors. It works as an institution layer on top of DND04 people. |
| DDC11 | **Party shares and hirelings.** Skilled and unskilled hirelings have daily wages (general knowledge, 2014 PHB). Default: a party is a set of people each owed a share; every loot split is a saved receipt; Dial: a solo player with a sidekick (general knowledge, Essentials Kit). | Shares, morale and memory make companions people. Chain: an unfair split leads to a companion leaving, gossip at the inn and fewer recruits. |
| DDC12 | **Spellbooks burn.** A wizard copies spells into a physical book for gold and time; scrolls work for casters whose class list has the spell (general knowledge, SRD). Default: the spellbook is one item that can be stolen, soaked or burned; a backup costs the full copying price. | Knowledge here has a physical owner. A spellbook carried to another world keeps its text, but casting from it still needs a D&D-profile body and class (a CW35 carrier). |
| DDC13 | **Ritual casting is the idle caster's friend.** Ritual spells take ten extra minutes and spend no slot (general knowledge, SRD). Default: rituals are the safe automated utility magic, and slots stay for danger. | It fits idle play: ritual Detect Magic or Alarm on a timer. Look-alike pair: casting a ritual and casting the same spell with a slot. |
| DDC14 | **The Frontier as an unknown plane.** Plane Shift needs a tuning fork attuned to the destination plane (general knowledge, SRD). Default: other YAIRPG worlds are not D&D planes; Dial: an instance treats the Frontier as an unmapped plane, reached with a fork made from a Frontier material sample. | It gives an in-world, costed reason for crossing without making D&D the multiverse's master. Getting that sample out of the Frontier is a quest of its own. |
| DDC15 | **Inspiration for playing your character.** A DM awards a reroll token for acting on your ideals, bonds and flaws; in 2024 humans get one on each long rest (general knowledge; check the profile). Default: awards read receipts of in-character choices. | A small fourth-wall touch: the narrator rewards role-play it can actually see. It suits the PRESENTATION owner's restrained end. |
| DDC16 | **Presentation.** V6 tint: warm parchment and burgundy, a nod to the classic box. V7 header: tenday and festival, plus rest status (rested, short rest used). V8 edges: exhaustion levels as stacked notches; at 0 HP, three death-save pips on the vitals edge. | Log voice: a DM narrator in second person ("You see...") with dice receipts showing every modifier.

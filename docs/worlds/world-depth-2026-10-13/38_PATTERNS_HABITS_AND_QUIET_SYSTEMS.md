# Patterns, habits and quiet systems (proposal, 2026-10-14)

Weibao (2026-10-14, repeated): "more to consider? other stuff and more? save and extend accordingly Send out codex as usesful? Subtle micro stuff too?"

Proposals only. Nothing here is accepted. Part 36 covers single small signals. This part covers what builds up from many small acts over time: the world noticing your patterns, habits forming on their own, tiny progress, and systems that run quietly without a panel. Code HB checked free.

Same rules as part 36: true in state only, cooldowns as world adjectives, no hidden-layer leaks, and nothing here becomes a chore or a fail state.

## 1. The world notices patterns (HB1-HB10)

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| HB1 | **Routine recognition.** Default: when you do the same thing at the same time and place often enough, people expect it ("Thursday again?"), prepare for it, and notice when you skip. | Idle routines become part of the town's rhythm. |
| HB2 | **Predictability has a cost.** Default: enemies, thieves and rivals can learn your patterns too: an ambush on your usual road, a pickpocket at your usual stall. Reasonably: varying routines or a guard friend counters it. | A light reason to vary play that comes from the world, not a rule. |
| HB3 | **Specialty reputation.** Default: doing one thing often makes you "the one who..." locally (the herb kid, the one who fixes locks). People bring that need to you. | Micro renown (part 21) that comes from habit, and a way content finds you. |
| HB4 | **Style recognition.** Default: how you fight, craft or talk forms a style that observers learn. Repeated foes adapt; fans imitate. | Combat and craft gain identity, and disguise (DV) gains a weak point. |
| HB5 | **Spending patterns.** Default: merchants learn what you buy and stock it, raise prices on what you always need, or offer bulk deals. | The economy reacts to you at small scale. |
| HB6 | **Absence patterns.** Default: regular absences become expected ("away on the first of each month"); irregular ones cause worry and visits. | Absence reads as a person's life, not a missing save. |
| HB7 | **Kindness patterns.** Default: someone who always tips, always helps or always shares gets a quiet reputation that opens small doors. The same for cruelty. | Values (part 34) expressed as habits, judged over time. |
| HB8 | **Being watched.** Default: in watched worlds (LOTM churches, HP Ministry, a soldier's officers), patterns are noticed by institutions too, with a report or a visit. | Patterns tie into law and secret identity risk. |
| HB9 | **Animals learn you.** Default: the yard dog stops barking, the horse comes when you whistle, crows learn you drop crumbs. | Small, warm proof of time spent. |
| HB10 | **Places wear to your use.** Default: your seat, your path through the field, your bench at the workshop show wear. | Your history becomes part of the map. |

## 2. Habits that form on their own (HB11-HB17)

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| HB11 | **Habit formation.** Default: a repeated act becomes a habit that runs with less effort (lower time or energy cost) and a small passive gain. Dial: per temperament. | Passive progression from consistency, fitting the idle direction. |
| HB12 | **Habits have tells.** Default: habits show in descriptions (part 36 MC21) and in how friends describe you. | Habits are part of identity and recognition. |
| HB13 | **Breaking a habit.** Default: stopping costs a little spirits for a while; returning to it is easier than starting fresh. | Makes routine changes feel real without punishing them. |
| HB14 | **Bad habits sneak in.** Default: under stress (part 31 IL3) a coping vice can become a habit (part 34 VL23) unless something replaces it. Reasonably: friends notice and may say something. | Gives stress a slow story, with friends as the counter. |
| HB15 | **Shared habits.** Default: habits done with someone become traditions (part 29 BK15), which hold both people's familiarity. | Ties and routines reinforce each other. |
| HB16 | **Habit carryover.** Default: some habits survive a new body or life (muscle memory, a hum, a way of holding a cup), which old friends can recognize (part 29 RX4). | Continuity across lives at the smallest scale. |
| HB17 | **The Earned Habit cheat.** Default: an existing cheat proposal automates routines. Here it also makes automated acts count toward habits, with the upkeep tell (part 28 SF4). | Ties an existing cheat to this layer. |

## 3. Tiny progress (HB18-HB24)

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| HB18 | **Micro milestones.** Default: small firsts get a quiet line: first clean cut, first loaf that did not burn, first time a stranger knew your name. | Progress felt between big levels. |
| HB19 | **Know-how notes.** Default: practice writes small notes into the notebook ("dry the herbs in shade, not sun"). Each gives a tiny bonus and reads as earned wisdom. | Knowledge as texture and as small power, discovered through doing. |
| HB20 | **Personal bests.** Default: the game quietly tracks bests (fastest route, finest craft, longest streak) and mentions a new one. | Self-competition without leaderboards. |
| HB21 | **Small efficiencies.** Default: doing a task many times finds small shortcuts: a better route, a tool rack, a pre-cut stack. Each saves a little time. | Idle optimization that comes from play, not menus. |
| HB22 | **Tool fit.** Default: a tool used long enough fits your hand and works slightly better for you than for others. | Attachment to things with a real reason (part 36 MC16). |
| HB23 | **Teaching shows your growth.** Default: when you explain a skill to someone, the line reflects how well you know it now compared with before. | Progress mirrored through others. |
| HB24 | **Looking back.** Default: a journal page can compare now with a year ago ("A year ago you could not light a fire without three tries"). | Long play summarized as a personal story. |

## 4. Quiet systems (HB25-HB30)

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| HB25 | **Systems without a panel.** Default: some systems run with no UI at all and show only through signals: weather memory, local prices, animal behavior, gossip. Reasonably: a debug view exists for development only. | Keeps the screen calm while the world stays deep. |
| HB26 | **Slow world changes.** Default: trees grow, paths form where people walk, a building ages. Seen across seasons, not ticks. | Long idle play shows a changing place. |
| HB27 | **Ecology nudges.** Default: overhunting thins game, a planted hedge brings birds. Small, local and reversible. | Player effect on the land without a management screen. |
| HB28 | **Background economies.** Default: NPC trades, wages and prices tick quietly; the player sees results in prices and offers. | Economy depth without a spreadsheet. |
| HB29 | **Quiet clocks.** Default: debts accrue, rumors fade, wards weaken, food spoils, each surfaced only when it matters and with a warning first (part 28 RC2). | Time matters without timers everywhere. |
| HB30 | **Discovery of quiet systems.** Default: a curious player can learn a hidden rule from an NPC or by observation, which writes a notebook entry and may unlock a view (part 28 LS3). | Rewards attention and makes quiet systems learnable. |

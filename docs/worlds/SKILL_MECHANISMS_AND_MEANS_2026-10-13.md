## M1: Separate result, mechanism and means

**Result** is what changes. **Mechanism** is how the change happens. **Means** are the actual things, faculties, conditions or people that make that mechanism available. **Skill** is learned competence where competence is involved.

“Open the sealed door” is a result. Reading the lock, applying leverage, reshaping the metal, obtaining the keeper's agreement and invoking a genuine authority are different mechanisms. Picks, strength, a suitable source, a relationship or a valid office supply different means. An office can open a door without teaching lock work; a lock expert can understand the problem without having picks today.

An original ability could simply open any eligible door by its stated rule. Do not force it through a mechanical-lock simulation or invent a compensating weakness. Define its actual scope and compare it honestly.

For one selected capability, answer five questions: what changes, what produces that change, what enables it, what develops with experience, and what survives loss of its current support. These are authoring questions, not five mandatory player bars or a new universal schema.

## M2: Alternatives are causal substitutions

Missing a means can lead to another method rather than a flat prohibition. A learner can borrow a tool, improvise a substitute, commission the result, work with another person or acquire a faculty that bypasses the old dependency.

Substitutions differ in what they actually accomplish. Paying a locksmith gets the door open but does not grant the customer's mastery. Watching may create evidence for later study. A spell might produce entry without understanding the lock. A genuinely superior faculty may do all of these better, without an obligatory niche for the weaker route.

Select a few worthwhile alternatives per scene. Do not generate combinatorial craft requirements for every ordinary action. “No suitable tool; borrow one from the workshop” is useful when the character knows that route. Hidden means should remain discoverable through their own clues.

## M3: Growth can change dependencies and reachable tasks

Existing variation and growth catalogues already include XP, evolution, fusion, comprehension and fixed powers. The deeper join is to identify what actually changes at a milestone.

| Proposed growth | Concrete change | Why it creates play |
|---|---|---|
| Better discrimination | Distinguish sound timber from a merely attractive piece | Changes selection before production |
| Better control | Make a small precise effect rather than only a broad one | Opens delicate work without raising maximum output |
| Better efficiency | Achieve the same useful output with less paid stock/time | Expands feasible projects and routines |
| Better generalization | Recognize a familiar principle in an unfamiliar material | Opens a tested extension; does not grant universal transfer |
| Reduced dependence | Perform a method without its beginner jig | Frees equipment or allows field use |
| New structure or source | Acquire an organ, circuit, pact or workshop that supports a new function | Separates acquisition from learning to control it |
| Better coordination | Teach a team to perform a method together | Increases organizational reach rather than personal attack power |

These can coexist or remain independent. Someone can have huge output with poor precision, excellent theory without bodily control, or deep competence with inadequate equipment. There need not be a universal minimum-of-all-stats formula. The chosen action should read only dependencies that matter to its mechanism.

## M4: Passive progression needs a causal condition, not attendance

Distinguish passive **effects**, passive **growth**, queued **practice**, and delegated **work**. A trait can give a permanent effect without growing; a queued lesson can grow a skill without repeated clicks; a helper can make stock without training the player.

| Progression route | Eligible condition | Possible progress | Clock commitment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bodily adaptation | Relevant sustained exposure, such as being underfed | Adaptation XP, potentially slow but positive | Eligible game time; Famine must not require exertion or clicks |
| Routine practice | An available method, reserved tools/stock and an ongoing practice order | Method mastery and applicable XP | Paid played-time job; pause when actual support is missing |
| Study | Accessible material and an ongoing study order | Knowledge or relevant competence | Study time, with authored completion stages |
| Incubation | A previously encountered problem and suitable intervening rest/activity | A lead or interpretation if selected | Authored played-time condition, not an automatic solved truth |
| Delegation | A capable consenting actor and supplied means | Their output and possibly their competence | Declared job clock and settlement; player learning needs its own cause |

This table does not approve any rates. Famine remains unimplemented, ordinary unfed play must stay comfortable, and hunger death is excluded by accepted direction. No universal exposure-to-power rule is implied. Whether closed-browser time advances a route requires an explicit clock policy; unsupported absence is not a grant. At a low-complexity interface, setting a routine once should suffice.

## M5: Learning can leave traces specific to the method

An original apprentice trained only on soft wood may be good at that work and uncertain on brittle timber. Transfer can provide a useful starting point while leaving the material-specific test unresolved. An expert can diagnose why the old method fails, then adapt it.

Consider keeping only selected evidence: training source, tested conditions and one important unresolved extension. Avoid recording every repetition. A wrong explanation can still produce a functioning narrow procedure, while a correct theory can precede practical execution. Negative tests should narrow a question where they actually provide evidence.

Habits may also become obsolete after equipment or body changes. Offer adjustment through suitable practice rather than an arbitrary universal decay meter. Familiar methods should remain stable unless a real authored change matters.

## M6: Teams and tools can hold competence outside the player

A lens, annotated procedure, teacher, workshop and assistant can collectively support a result no participant could produce alone. Distinguish who understands it, who performs each step and where its records reside. Losing the lens changes measurement access; losing the notebook changes recoverable instructions; losing a teacher changes available feedback. None automatically erases the player's earned learning.

This makes apprenticeship, institutions and crafting meaningful: a school can supply calibration, equipment and collaborators as well as lessons. A portable kit can compress part of that support into an item. A later power can internalize it. These are different progression paths toward a useful function, with no requirement to make every path equally good.

A completed routine can become a standing order with known limits and an authored exception. Complexity remains optional: the simple view offers “repeat this work”; an expanded view exposes tools, method, budget and interruption conditions. Existing save/pause/accessibility functions stay available regardless of fictional capacity.

## M7: Apply the same reasoning beyond skill panels

| System | Mechanism and means question | Original consequence to explore |
|---|---|---|
| Crafting and alchemy | Which step causes the useful transformation, and what supplies its heat, measurement or catalyst? | A better vessel improves consistency without granting the recipe |
| Sensing and research | What signal reaches the observer, and what can they infer from it? | Hearing a voice does not establish identity or honesty |
| Healing | Is the method closing a wound, supplying missing tissue or changing a condition? | Similar visible recovery can require different materials and leave different aftermath |
| Social action | Is success coming from persuasion, trust, office, leverage or a supernatural effect? | Cooperation does not automatically imply affection or changed belief |
| Titles and authority | Does a title describe an achievement, grant recognized access or supply actual power? | An office can be revoked while the demonstrated competence remains |
| Knowledge carryover | Which memories, tools, body and world rules survive the transition? | A remembered procedure can remain useful while its old source is unavailable |
| Economy and institutions | Who controls the workshop, permission, inputs or service? | Borrowing access can be a practical alternative to owning every means |

Detection, backlash, scarcity and upkeep are possible local mechanics, not mandatory taxes on every power. Use the actual world/source rule or a labelled original choice. These examples assert no new franchise canon and disclose no sealed world truths.

## P1: One comparison worth prototyping later

Use the existing timber/kit route to compare three proposed support states: a novice with a guide and suitable tools, a practiced actor with reduced support, and an actor commissioning the work from a capable craftsperson. All aim at the same useful owned kit. Compare preparation, cost, quality/reliability where supported, time, player learning and NPC learning separately.

The point is to see whether different means create meaningful decisions and whether mastery changes a real dependency. First check the current route's delivery and ownership; this is not a dispatch to repair it or a claim that all three alternatives exist.

Specified-not-run acceptance questions: does missing support produce a character-known reason; does obtaining it make the real action available; does growth visibly change a selected task; does commissioned work avoid invented player mastery; and do resulting paid costs, custody and progress survive save/reload/import without replay? If passive adaptation is selected separately, include eligible idle time with zero active input. Any later UI claim requires a rebuilt desktop/laptop origin at its task's named viewport; mobile remains deferred.

## P2: Recommended emphasis

Develop fewer capabilities with distinct causal routes, useful changes in competence, and earned ways to supply or replace their means. Let those routes connect to people, tools, research and ordinary life. Broader catalogues remain useful design palettes, but should not become an obligation to add every mechanic before a playable slice.

## 1. Power as engine state (K2, K3)

| Component | Default | Dial | In-world reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger | rare, one-shot, unplanned, traumatic; forced attempts unlikely and dangerous | trauma kind shapes the power | a shard bonds at a crisis |
| Shard and passenger | a hidden partner with safeguards and a mild conflict drive | age at bond | shard code, per in-world testimony |
| Manton limit | most powers stop at a living body | per-power exception with a reason | safeguard code |
| Self-safety | a power does not hurt its user | power tier | same safeguard |
| PRT classification | twelve tags with 1-12 ratings, assigned by authorities and sometimes wrong | observation, re-rating | institutions read tags, not truth |
| Tinker work | specialty lock, slow builds, maker-bound upkeep | mass-produced lines avoid upkeep | tech works backward from the finished object |
| Cauldron vials | seven-figure base price, two-thirds up front, six years, bodily risk | batch, client | a clandestine vendor |
| Second trigger | rarer; loosens restrictions rather than raising power | circumstance mirroring the first | shard refines its safeguards |
| Thinker precog | query-based odds with headaches; fuzzy near very strong actors | questions per day | power cost and blockers |

Day one for an ordinary character: no power. A dockworker with a union card and no jobs to hand
out, a student at Winslow or Arcadia, a PRT clerk at a checkpoint, a gang-adjacent kid running
errands, or a fresh trigger with a power to hide. They change local outcomes (who is hired, who
is warned, which exit is clear), not cape fights.

## 2. Growth (K3)

Trigger and rare second trigger; creative use and practice; Tinker builds and toolsets that
snowball; team synergy; Cauldron purchase for those without a power. Plateaus: the specialty
lock, upkeep, a misleading rating. Curve breakers arrive as events (Endbringers, the Nine,
Gold Morning).

## 3. Living beside the protagonist (K4, K12)

Dated clock from checked anchors: April 2011 first costumed night and the Lung fight; April 14
bank robbery; May 5 fundraiser; May 15 Leviathan hits Brockton Bay (the largest ordinary-life
shock, with power and water out for most of the city); the Slaughterhouse Nine in June (exact
dates unknown); June 18-21 town hall and the Echidna fight; the Endbringer log through 2013;
June 24 2013 Scion turns; about six months of resettlement after. Big beats act as attractors
with local freedom: who lives, who is hired and which side wins stay open.

Starts (proposal, from the 32-work fan survey): unpowered bystander (best fit), native OC such
as a PRT photographer, own-city Tinker, post-Gold Morning settler; late-trigger Tinker (partly);
point-buy and Contessa-seat premises are not default.

## 4. Society, economy, crafting (K7, K8, K9)

PRT, Protectorate, Wards ($400 a month plus a trust account), the Birdcage ladder (arrest,
probation, Birdcage); gangs with front companies (ABB, E88, Merchants); the Undersiders paid
$2,000 a month each by a hidden patron; mercenary crews; the Guild; the Dockworkers
Association; unwritten rules and the Endbringer truce. Reconstruction economy after Leviathan:
dock cleanup, repair crews, relief, territory work. Post-Gold Morning settlements with finite
supplies. Prices and pay stay dated and placed; dockworker pay and rent are unknown and should be
authored as dated contracts.

## 5. Cross-world (K15)

A shard is a passenger with its own safeguards, so its limits travel with the host; the Manton
limit collides with soul-targeting or body-editing magic elsewhere; Tinker tech is maker-bound
and decays without upkeep; Coil-style branching is two concurrent worlds with one kept, not a
rewind like Re:Zero's. Full table in the private round 2 report, section 5.

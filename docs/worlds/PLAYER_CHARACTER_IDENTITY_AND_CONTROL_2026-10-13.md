# Who the player is, and who acts

Status: design consideration, 2026-10-13. Proposed models and recommendation, not an accepted universal ontology or runtime change.

Weibao asked: “what lore wise would we? Otherworldly spirit piloting a body? The alter ego? the person themselves? deeper stuff like these? Save accordingly and commit”. This authorizes saving and committing this conversation's design notes, not selecting a model or implementing it.

Related discussion: [choice dependencies and roguelike powers](CHOICES_THAT_GATE_CHOICES_2026-10-13.md) and [research, talents, starts and upkeep](RESEARCH_TALENTS_PROGRESSION_AND_STARTS_2026-10-13.md). Existing direction stays with [User vision](../USER_VISION.md), [PROJECT.md](../../PROJECT.md), [origins](ORIGINS_AND_WORLD_TIMELINES.md), [rules](../RULES_FROM_SCRATCH.md), [identity and fate](../RIVALS_IDENTITIES_AND_FATE.md) and each represented world's actual source/continuity. These origins already distinguish body, mind, source, entry and social role. This proposal focuses on the player's relationship to those parts.

## L1: Several coherent lore models

| ID | Model | Who the player's choices represent | Distinctive play and lore |
|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | The person themselves | The character's own intentions and decisions | Ordinary embodied roleplay. The interface helps the human player without creating an extra spirit, foresight or mind-reader. |
| O2 | Reincarnated or transmigrated self | A person with a declared prior existence | Retained identity, memories and body changes follow the selected origin. Reincarnation can also lack old memories. |
| O3 | Advising presence | A separate spirit, patron, system or voice suggesting courses | Advice has a channel and reach. The host can have independent judgment under the selected control contract. Supplied facts and influence can be actual powers. |
| O4 | Shared mind or alter ego | One aspect, a second self, or a declared co-occupant | Shared or partitioned memory, executive control and dialogue can develop. Define whether this is an ordinary narrative aspect, a created consciousness, a soul fragment or another person. The label alone decides none of these. |
| O5 | Possessing pilot | A distinct controller operating a body | Declare whether the original person remains, sleeps, shares control, was replaced or is absent. Physiology, skill, recognition and soul-bound powers may retain different owners. |
| O6 | Avatar or remote projection | A continuing controller acting through a vessel | Separation, communication range, multiple bodies and vessel loss become relevant. Losing a vessel need not mean the controller dies. |
| O7 | Guardian of a lineage or institution | A persistent presence guiding successive people | Influence may span descendants, apprentices or members. A new protagonist is an individual, not automatically a puppet or a copy of the former one. |
| O8 | Outside-the-fiction player | The human choosing which independent life to play | New runs need no shared soul, reincarnation office or diegetic account. Player learning persists; characters receive only their selected histories and actual knowledge. |

O1 and O8 compose naturally: within a life, play as that person; between lives, select another perspective without inventing a cosmic controller. O2 through O7 are selectable premises where the represented world or an explicit AU supports them. A native character can later acquire an advising spirit or project an avatar without rewriting their original identity retrospectively.

## L2: Recommended default and alternatives

Recommendation: O1 within a life and O8 across independent lives. This supports direct creative control and ordinary relationships while keeping exceptional ontologies optional. It avoids making every unusual player instruction a possession event or every replay a power held by the character.

Offer other models because they create different experiences, not as compulsory explanations for the interface. An advisor campaign could center on communication and persuasion; a shared-mind campaign on cooperation; a remote-vessel campaign on reach and embodiment. Possession or replacement can be a deliberately selected darker or stranger premise. Do not impose a universal consent, resistance or harmony outcome: author the actual relationship and disclose its known control terms.

Viewpoint is separate from ontology. First-person narration does not establish reincarnation; third-person narration does not establish an external spirit. Direct control, goal-directed automation and autonomous play are interaction contracts, not by themselves metaphysical facts.

## L3: Keep knowledge, desire and control separate

For a selected model, answer these five questions:

| ID | Question | Why it changes play |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Who forms the goal? | The person, advisor, co-occupant or player may originate different intentions. |
| Q2 | Who knows which facts? | A controller may know something the host does not; transferring it needs an actual channel if that distinction is represented. |
| Q3 | Who can act or refuse? | Advice, persuasion, shared control and takeover are different operations. |
| Q4 | Who experiences and retains the result? | Pain, satisfaction, learning and memory can belong to different entities under a premise. |
| Q5 | Who persists after death or departure? | Body, consciousness, soul, controller and records can have different continuation rules. |

The player's unusual ambition can be a creative choice about the person's interests under O1. “Investigate matter” need not mean a spirit planted the idea. It also need not grant the character accurate modern knowledge. Under O3, an advisor might transmit knowledge; the channel, source and accuracy matter. Under O4, another self might know but be unable to explain, or cooperate fully. Those are different authored situations, not one universal restriction.

Do not use random personality generation to veto normal direct-control choices without selecting that experience. At the same time, an advertised independent-host mode should not secretly guarantee obedience. Make the distinction visible at premise selection and at material changes in control.

## L4: Identity changes relationships, powers and ownership

| ID | Situation | Questions the selected premise must answer |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | A familiar body has a different controller | Do people recognize appearance, manner, memories or another identity marker? What do they actually observe? |
| R2 | Two selves share one body | Can both speak to others, hold separate promises, learn and form relationships? Is there private memory? |
| R3 | A bond persists across lives | Is the bond with a body, person, soul, controller or institution? Does the other participant recognize the return? |
| R4 | A body contains earlier expertise | Do procedural habits survive? Can the current controller interpret or execute them? Are soul-bound sources still present? |
| R5 | A controller changes vessels | Which items, injuries, skills, memories and obligations move? Appearance does not automatically transfer legal title or social authority. |
| R6 | An advisor can grant power | Does the host own the acquired capability, borrow it or depend on the advisor's continued operation? Can it be taught or transferred? |
| R7 | A copy or parallel counterpart appears | What continuity makes it the same person, a branch of that person or an independent individual? Shared recollection alone does not settle every premise. |
| R8 | The connection ends | Does the host continue, retain learned knowledge, regain control or lose a borrowed faculty? Separation need not universally mean death. |

Knowledge, trust, affection, recognition, bodily familiarity and legal claims remain distinct. A remembered romance can inform an encounter without forcing the present person's feelings. If a premise genuinely preserves the same relationship and both parties' history, preserve that instead of arbitrarily resetting it for difficulty. Reactions require actual evidence rather than every NPC detecting possession automatically.

Food illustrates the same distinction. The human player can ignore an eat button while an established routine provisions the embodied person. A spirit may not need food but its vessel may; a supported power might remove that need altogether. The lore model defines physiology; the chosen experience determines whether ordinary upkeep needs detailed attention.

## L5: Powers and depth should create chosen experiences

Control can develop through clearer communication, shared memory, negotiated routines, independent initiative, multiple vessels or eventual separation. These are alternative arcs, not a mandatory ladder toward total domination. Likewise, cooperation can be the starting state, and a powerful controller can begin with broad reach.

Do not add sanity, synchronization, consent, possession resistance, soul hunger and loyalty meters merely because a model could support them. Represent the distinctions that actually change decisions. Character dialogue, a known limitation or an occasional event may express a shared mind better than constant meter maintenance. Strong powers need their actual rules, not compulsory drawbacks.

For authoring, declare the model's truth internally even when the character does not know it. Sealed identity mysteries may reveal through their actual discovery route; public labels, menus, reports and commit messages must not expose their truth. No actual sealed identity is described here. An intentionally unresolved metaphysical question can remain unresolved if no implemented behavior depends on a fixed answer.

For future implementation, define initial knowledge/control, possible transfer, power ownership, observer evidence, host/controller departure and continuation. Show supported choices and known boundaries, then verify the selected model through rebuilt desktop gameplay and applicable save/reload/import. This document provides no runtime, build or browser proof.

## Decision status

O1 plus O8 is the recommended ordinary starting arrangement. O2 through O7 remain distinct optional premises. Weibao has requested consideration and a documentation commit, not accepted a universal lore model. No new agent, runtime assignment, source acquisition, push or publication is implied.

## External knowledge and player knowledge

Weibao asked: “Conisider the implications of having external knowledge and more? Since I myself would have some level of knowledge that the character will not have? more to consider?” This extends the design consideration; it does not choose a knowledge mode or authorize another commit.

[Technology and progression](../TECHNOLOGY_AND_PROGRESSION.md#knowing-that-versus-knowing-how-tk) already distinguishes knowledge fidelity. [Lore and continuity](LORE_AND_CONTINUITY.md) distinguishes author baseline, actual branch, actor knowledge and discovery channels. The following recommendation keeps normal player choice usable without making the character omniscient.

### K1: Separate available action from established knowledge

The human player inevitably uses experience to choose actions. They may visit a place earlier, distrust someone, prepare a tool or try an unusual hypothesis. Those choices need not retroactively create a spirit, a memory transfer or a lore breach. Do not police the player's private reasoning or demand an explanation for every clever choice.

Distinguish “I choose to look here” from “my character knows what is here,” and “I suspect this person” from “I can establish their secret to another actor.” The former can be legitimate action selection; the latter requires the selected means, evidence or exceptional knowledge. Unsupported allegations or guesses can still be spoken where dialogue supports them; do not silently make them true or universally credible. Hunches and correct guesses are possible without guaranteed proof.

Likewise, a physically supported input can be attempted without a stored knowledge flag. Guessing a password is different from the UI automatically revealing or prefilling an unknown password. Some actions genuinely require learning a technique or accessing a faculty; others can be attempted experimentally. Define the particular requirement instead of treating every discovery receipt as compulsory permission to act.

### K2: Distinguish several information sources

| ID | Information | Default implication under the proposed ordinary roleplay model |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Interface literacy and known mechanics | Helps the player operate the game. A cost display or comparison is not automatically an in-world supernatural perception. |
| E2 | Player's general experience | Can guide goals, preparation and hypotheses without granting the character mastery. |
| E3 | Character's background, observations and instruction | Is available to that character at its declared fidelity, including uncertainty and possible error. |
| E4 | Player's knowledge of a source story | Can guide choices; it does not automatically become the character's memories or a guaranteed prediction of this branch. |
| E5 | Player's recollection of a previous run | Helps the human learn; character retention requires the selected continuation/carrier policy. |
| E6 | Information deliberately transmitted by a spirit, archive or other power | Becomes available according to its real channel, scope, precision and recipient understanding. |
| E7 | Private author or sealed data | Remains outside normal player-facing projections and responses until its discovery route or explicit unsealing. No sealed fact is included in these examples. |

### K3: Knowing an answer does not supply every means of using it

A player may know an invention is possible without recalling a viable design. A character may receive a design without the required materials, instruments or skills. A gifted specialist may execute a supplied recipe without understanding every theory behind it. Correctly distinguishing these supports several actual paths rather than one obligatory knowledge ladder.

When the player proposes a detailed answer in a supported research interaction, select its treatment explicitly: a proposed hypothesis to test, information sent through a selected external-guidance channel, or an authored start with that information already retained. Do not silently import an entire modern education. Equally, do not require needless rediscovery when the premise genuinely grants accurate information or mastery.

Research should remain meaningful through validation, local applicability, practical execution, adaptation, teaching or genuinely unknown questions. Perfect knowledge powers can legitimately bypass uncertainty in their supported domain. Different worlds can have different physical rules; whether a remembered method works is a setting question, not a universal failure penalty.

### K4: Foreknowledge has a particular version and causal reach

Prior-story knowledge refers to a source/era/continuity; prior-run memory refers to a particular played history. Neither by itself guarantees current actor choices, timing or outcomes. Player intervention can change the causes of an expected event. Keep changed history rather than forcing canon to reassert itself or inventing an unrelated replacement disaster.

Foreknowledge can still be useful where the relevant conditions remain. Do not secretly randomize every fact just to punish familiarity. Random or sealed variants require an actual selected premise and their own persistence policy. A player can enjoy preventing a known disaster or achieving an unusually clean outcome. NPC reactions follow what they actually notice, hear or investigate, not a global “metagaming detected” response.

### K5: Knowledge modes are experience choices

Possible profiles: ordinary roleplay with action choice and character-grounded factual projections; explicit outsider knowledge retained at a declared fidelity; an external advisor transmitting information; discovery-first play with optional stronger information restrictions; and open sandbox authoring of knowledge and powers. These need not all be implemented or made into separate save modes immediately.

Recommended ordinary arrangement: allow player-directed goals and supported attempts, preserve character-grounded knowledge and discovery, and make actual transfer of external knowledge an explicit premise or capability. An optional stricter roleplay profile can restrict some action suggestions, but it cannot reliably make the human forget what they know and should not claim to do so.

Further implications: player and character may know different languages; an exact number in UI need not mean the character can announce it; an advisor may be correct but fail to communicate clearly; a host may misunderstand accurate advice; institutions may need credible evidence; records and testimony can spread knowledge to others; and simultaneous characters should retain their own histories even when one human directs them. Representation should stay proportional to meaningful decisions rather than tracking every mundane fact.

This addition is documentation only. No knowledge gates, dialogue, research input, source disclosure, runtime or save behavior was changed or tested.

## Further knowledge and agency considerations

Weibao asked: “what else should we consider?” The following add the reverse knowledge gap and practical control implications. They remain recommendations, not selected runtime requirements.

**K6: The character can know more than the player.** A local resident knows familiar customs; an experienced craftsperson has practiced judgment; a long-standing friend knows shared history. Supply relevant reminders, contextual options and competent routine execution rather than making the human pass a trivia test to use the character's expertise. A “what would I know?” view can summarize established background without revealing inaccessible secrets. Some remembered information can still be uncertain or mistaken. Player knowledge and character expertise should both contribute rather than automatically substituting for each other.

**K7: Advice, belief and proof are separate.** Receiving a claim need not mean believing it, understanding it or being able to establish it to someone else. A character can act on a hunch, trust a source, seek corroboration or knowingly bluff where supported. Another actor's response depends on their knowledge, motives and evidence. Do not mark a rumor secretly false in the ordinary UI, treat a confidence number as truth or require every practical decision to reach certainty. Contradictions can remain visible for later revision.

**K8: Delegation needs a practical intention contract.** “Investigate this” can mean consult known sources; it need not authorize spending every coin, abandoning a posting, disclosing a secret or undertaking a dangerous expedition. Let a goal-directed routine use a selected budget, methods and stopping conditions. Surface materially new decisions. Reversible routine execution can stay quiet. Directly controlled actions still represent the player's choices; autonomous initiative should follow the selected mode rather than surprise the player with an undeclared controller change.

**K9: Communication creates play and distribution.** Information can move through conversation, demonstration, writing, translation or an actual power. Receiving words does not necessarily supply tacit technique or correct interpretation. A group can know collectively what no member knows alone; collaborators can contribute distinct expertise. Publication, secrecy, teaching and attribution can be meaningful choices without mandatory secrecy taxes or omniscient institutional reactions. Information only spreads as far as the represented channel supports; widespread simple ideas need not require exhaustive per-person simulation.

**K10: Competence, motives and consequences should not become thought policing.** The game need not explain every successful choice as foreknowledge, every odd interest as possession or every departure from a generated personality as madness. A player can choose curiosity, caution, generosity, recklessness or a new goal. Character interests can supply suggestions and texture without becoming compulsory optimization or a veto. When a selected premise intentionally creates disagreement between controller and host, make it a real relationship with explicit control terms.

For presentation, distinguish known terms, the character's assessment and the actual later outcome. “This route looks safer to you” can convey expertise without guaranteeing safety. A specialized character can expose more useful options or sharper estimates, while the baseline interface still explains controls and known costs. Do not demand manual repetition of every trivial inference or let private author data leak through tooltips, disabled buttons or automatic suggestions.

Recommended priority for a future selected slice: represent the character's existing competence, keep factual claims distinct from chosen intentions, and make one delegated pursuit respect a clear boundary. Add more elaborate belief or communication machinery only where a named interaction benefits. No new implementation assignment, commit or gameplay verification follows from this consideration.

## Explaining the gap in lore, or leaving it as a play convention

Weibao asked: “save accordingly? what lore reason would explain the difference or will it be glossed over? what to consider? More to consider?” Saved here as further alternatives and a recommendation. No lore model is accepted by this request alone.

### G1: Ordinary play can leave the interface outside the fiction

Recommendation for O1/O8: the human interprets and chooses the person's actions; the game represents relevant parts of the person's experience. The two are not literally identical minds. A character reminder expresses local expertise the human lacks. The human's familiarity can guide an action without becoming a stored character memory. Neither needs an in-world messenger.

This is a deliberate convention rather than a hidden spirit reveal. Explain it briefly if needed at premise selection or in help, then avoid constant disclaimers. Do not claim every fact in the player's head belongs to the character, and do not imply every UI element is visible to them. [Timelines and divergence](../TIMELINES_AND_DIVERGENCE.md) already separates player choice from character power; [presentation](../PRESENTATION_AND_FOURTH_WALL.md) owns optional diegetic interface and fourth-wall reach.

### G2: Ordinary narrative can express intention without inventing a power

Curiosity, suspicion, experimentation, judgment and a hunch can describe an unusual chosen action. Background experience can explain a relevant competence reminder. Do not attach a fabricated childhood anecdote to every player decision or use “intuition” to silently deliver arbitrary verified secrets, exact future facts or complete technical mastery.

A correct conjecture can be original thought or luck. Its correctness does not automatically prove prophecy. A proposed theory is different from a verified theory; repeated explanation is unnecessary until the distinction changes an action or response. Genuine invention is possible through the selected world's supported means, not only imported knowledge.

### G3: Make literal outside information a selected mechanism

| ID | Lore explanation | What it specifically explains | Question to decide |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Reincarnated or transplanted memories | Knowledge actually retained from a prior existence | Whose experience, how precise, and what survives in the present body? |
| P2 | Advising spirit, patron or system | Another entity sends advice or facts | What can the sender know, how is it communicated, and can the recipient misunderstand or refuse? |
| P3 | Shared self or alter ego | Different accessible memories or perspectives within a declared mind arrangement | Is there one person or more than one, and what is shared? |
| P4 | Dreams, visions or prophecy | An otherwise unavailable observation, warning or possible future | What produces it, what it actually shows, and whether it is reliable? |
| P5 | Archive, artifact or inherited records | Access to another person's stored knowledge | Is there a readable record, a translation, direct understanding or actual skill transfer? |
| P6 | Explicit fourth-wall awareness | Awareness of player information or the game's represented interface | Which information can cross, and which controls does the power actually reach? |

These are alternative premises, not universal explanations or obligatory costs. An advisor could be reliable and cooperative; a memory could be perfect; a dream could be mundane. Their actual mechanism decides. A cosmic explanation can connect many lives if selected, but ordinary unrelated lives do not need to inherit that ontology.

### G4: Keep the explanation proportional to the consequence

Choosing to inspect a location usually needs no special exposition. Reading an unfamiliar diagram might require study or help. Importing an exact otherwise inaccessible fact through a selected channel is an actual information event. Carrying a relationship or power across death needs a continuation rule. Use the smallest explanation that fits the claimed behavior rather than adding a new metaphysical subsystem for every clever choice.

NPCs react to visible acts, statements and evidence. They may be curious about improbable expertise if their circumstances warrant it, but they do not automatically detect the player or punish success. Repeated source knowledge can be useful without guaranteed accuracy in a changed branch. Nor must the game manufacture discrepancies solely to conceal the human's advantage.

### G5: Preserve the chosen promise across play and presentation

Ask whether the explanation supports the desired experience, actually changes possible actions, remains consistent across death or travel, and implies a new entity with motives or memories. If adding it creates more upkeep than interesting play, an ordinary convention may fit better. Do not advertise “just an ordinary person” while silently giving them omniscience, or advertise a knowledgeable advisor then arbitrarily block the information their declared power supplies.

An identity mystery may remain hidden through its discovery route. That does not justify leaving control, known action terms or severe-risk rules confusing. If a later revealed identity changes the selected control contract, preserve the original rules until that actual transition rather than retroactively rewriting player-earned history. These are general design possibilities; no sealed truth is disclosed.

Recommended starting arrangement remains O1/O8 with ordinary competence and creative choice. Explicit outside-information models are optional premises or acquired powers. This addition was saved and checked for whitespace errors; it makes no runtime or browser claim and has not been committed.

## Further implications of being ourselves in the fiction

Weibao asked “Other aspects to consider?” after requesting a separate lore-consideration chat. These are additional questions for literal-self premises, not instructions to the other chat or accepted answers. Its separate report owns its own comparison; this section preserves the originating discussion.

**S1: What happens to the original life?** Arrival can move the same person, project their awareness, create a copy, or begin from an authored self-portrait. The original body might remain, vanish, sleep or continue independently under the selected premise. Do not infer that the real human's physical life changes because the fiction represents it. Fictional return and closing the application are different events. Decide whether home is a reachable destination, a memory, a parallel life or deliberately outside the represented scope.

**S2: Which continuity makes this person “me”?** Memory, consciousness, body, soul, personality and social recognition need not give identical answers. A copy can regard itself as the same person without resolving every philosophical question. If copies diverge, they may become independent people. Merging them raises a different question from copying skills: which memories, commitments and intentions survive? Define what actual actions need without requiring one universal theory of identity.

**S3: How much personal detail is represented?** Literal-self play can mean broad preferences and declared external knowledge rather than a complete biography. Offer an abstract self, an explicitly authored version or more detailed voluntary characterization. Do not invent private relationships, credentials or personality claims from an ordinary chat. Named real people need not appear in the fiction to make home or attachment meaningful. The chosen fidelity should not require the human to disclose a complete life history.

**S4: How does experienced time affect the self?** A controller can live years through an avatar while a home-world clock moves differently under a selected premise. Age, memory, emotional familiarity and bodily development may then diverge. Do not make an ordinary time skip fabricate specific chosen actions or replace the player's intentions. If a long span implies independent decisions, select the character autonomy and summarization policy. An experienced life can change interests without obliging the game to dictate a new personality.

**S5: Does identity persist while abilities change?** A new body can change senses, mobility, energy and instinct while the same controller persists. Ordinary reminders can express unfamiliar embodiment, and practice can support adaptation where needed. Do not universally penalize every new body or strip a supported innate faculty to manufacture challenge. Exact adaptation depends on the premise. Likewise, retaining factual knowledge does not automatically retain every original resource, credential or relationship.

Further selectable aspects include who remembers the original world, whether communication or return is possible, whether multiple selves can act simultaneously, what outsiders can actually detect, whether a persistent self can choose a final ending, and whether successful life continues without compulsory reincarnation. A mystery about origin can coexist with clear controls and known continuation terms. None of these questions requires automatic homesickness, trauma, synchronization meters, permanent immortality or mandatory costs.

These remain optional design considerations. No actual personal data model, physical-world integration, time simulation, identity transition or gameplay behavior was implemented. This addition is saved but not committed.

## Campaign promise and horizons

Become extraordinarily capable by learning how to keep both one body and a whole neighborhood supplied.
Early play establishes tested water, a paid repair relationship and one independent route through the culvert.
Middle play assembles a maintainable field rig, develops treatment/engineering competence and chooses regional supply partners.
Mature play can support a powered expedition, a multi-site utility network or a specialist refuge without taking over every faction.
Raw combat power remains real. A high-capability suit can defeat lesser opponents; it cannot manufacture evidence or feed every settlement.
Supply problems must create choices among tools, routes and institutions, rather than a universal tax on every click.
Caps, service credit and native money remain separate until an integrator selects a real adapter.
The opening pump and valve decision leaves concrete material history that matters to later regional control.

Paths use different bottlenecks. Repair training can reveal why a stronger frame failed; a better frame cannot teach safe piloting.
A medic needs sample evidence and treatment stock; an organizer can buy their service instead of learning the profession.
An exceptional body-modification branch is explicitly unscheduled until selected source evidence and bodily compatibility exist.
Faction authority is local standing with duties. It does not convert into a generic combat level.
Strong imported capabilities can eliminate suitable physical bottlenecks, leaving genuine information/logistics choices where those remain.

## Assemblies and functional modules

Campaign component families: frame, protective plates, supported power source, movement interface, lifting attachment, weapon/ammunition assembly, sensing fixture, clean-water cartridge, replacement seal, calibration charge, treatment kit and service charter.
The catalogue owns structural tracks, quality, condition, mounting, failure and salvage. These families do not select exact canon equipment recipes.
O field-service rig: actual supported frame/power plus hoist attachment, sample fixture, repair stock and optional protection.
O heavy expedition rig: more protective parts and supported armament replace some carrying capacity; ammo and power remain finite.
O utility workshop: service bay, test bench, clean storage and independent water intake form a place assembly with actual capacity.
Removing a sensor does not remove lifting skill, but it removes that rig's instrument reading.
Power loss disables power-dependent actions; it does not delete ordinary tools, acquired knowledge or unrelated imported strength.
A component's condition is not its tier. Repairing damaged high-capability armor restores the retained design when materials support it.
One installed part is reserved from its actual owner. Removal returns the same part and condition, not a freshly spawned inventory copy.
Refit requires labor, tools and a safe supported site; combat swaps need a separately authored interface.
Imported exotic power requires a paid compatibility test. It never silently becomes an infinite fusion supply.

## Three worked build journeys

### Resource-poor sampler and repair courier

1. Take Mina's paid diagnosis work and learn leak listening without acquiring a powered suit.
2. Buy one sample test from Imani; a consumed diagnostic charge reveals evidence, not guaranteed clean-water output.
3. Fit an ordinary seal and fund narrow culvert preparation. Reserve actual bay/NPC time.
4. Sell or keep two proposed certified allotments from a six-minute known batch: materials income competes with expedition stock.
5. Build a hand-filter service and a small reliable transport route; particular suppliers recognize completed contributions.

Required means: ordinary tools, charge, seal, body stamina and discovered access.
New functions: test a known intake, isolate a leak, certify a batch and supply a refuge.
Weakness: cannot overpower Orren's fixed armored gatekeeper or assume protection against measured radiation.
Tradeoff: low capital and useful evidence niche, but buys heavy lifting and specialist treatment.

### Developed powered field mechanic

1. Earn legal access to an actual rig/frame and inspect power, fitting and condition before accepting it.
2. Acquire real replacement parts and trained control; install a supported lifting attachment at Mina's bay.
3. Mount a sample fixture and protection chosen for the intended exposure, not a generic resistance label.
4. Reopen the bypass with inspected geometry and actual lifting/body support, preserving the rig's spent power and wear.
5. Carry replacement filters to the harbor, where corrosion and crane bookings create different demands.

Required means: compatible structure, power, operator training, installation labor, test stock and a route that fits the rig.
New functions: heavy rescue, technical access, mobile sampling and actual protection within its envelope.
Weakness: narrow access, finite energy and vulnerable supply line; heavier plates displace cargo or tools.
Tradeoff: independent access and strong physical means at the cost of concentrated equipment/maintenance investment.

### Mature utility compact and heavy rescue team

1. Preserve calibrated evidence from several pumps and distinguish shared failure from a monopolist's actual intervention.
2. Fund an inland store, harbor parts shipment and two independently supplied service crews.
3. Build the O utility workshop in phases, with clean storage and real replacement capacity.
4. Equip a genuinely powerful rescue rig or contract supported protection, then select a distribution charter.
5. Resolve regional crisis through evacuation, repaired access, direct sufficient force or negotiated allocation.

Required means: multiple actual sites/people, spares, transport, treatment and legitimate utility access.
New functions: larger rescue, independent maintenance and simultaneous funded jobs within site limits.
Weakness: several sites can be pressured at once; destroyed records/people cannot be recreated by a better pump.
Tradeoff: resilient public utility versus proprietary high-margin service; each creates different counterparties and obligations.

## Five escalating arcs

### 1. A Working Water Line

Trigger: discovered cloudy water and paid testing reveal an intake problem before a carrier takes the spare valve.
O truth: actual fault and valve custody are frozen at occurrence/commitment; the briefing shows only observable readings and terms.
Technical choice: spend seal/rig preparation and supported lifting to open a narrow bypass.
Institution choice: transfer actual service goods to Orren for a joint reservation and a displayed local obligation.
Aftermath: both settle one valve result, with different access, spent supplies, rig condition and witness memories.
Abstention: Orren funds his six-minute survey, then a carrier departure twenty played minutes later changes valve ownership; only later evidence reveals it.
Recovery: costly replacement/hand-filter work can reopen service while preserving lost carrier opportunity and prior history.

### 2. The cartridge trail

Trigger: samples and invoices show that several failures share a part family; intentional sabotage is not assumed from a bad batch.
Choices: paid comparative testing, confront the supplier using genuine evidence, or switch sources and leave the cause unresolved.
Aftermath: tested compatibility changes Mina's recipes; public accusation can damage business if unsupported; quiet switching preserves privacy at a higher price.
Abstention: existing funded batches continue under their actual inspection policy and may fail, consuming stock.
Later consequence: evidence determines whether Orren's monopoly argument is plausible and which faction wants the archive.

### 3. The harbor frame

Trigger: a real parts shipment competes with a crane booking and discreet cargo.
Choices: buy crane time, use sufficient supported lifting, or trade repair work for access and a confidentiality obligation.
Aftermath: keep imported capability meaningful; lifting can solve the load but public witnesses can reveal the hidden route.
Abstention: another buyer receives the shipment if funded; lost rare stock is not automatically replaced.
Later consequence: harbor service/parts supply shapes the rig career and Edda's refuge availability.

### 4. Four claims on one line

Trigger: an operating corridor and verified part evidence draw distinct faction offers.
Choices: aid-station public distribution, restricted detachment supply, discreet refuge access, or proprietary laboratory collaboration.
Aftermath: specific contracts change spares, teachers, confidentiality and duties; these are original local bargains, not automatic source endings.
Abstention: independent cooperative operation remains possible if supplies and actual protection support it; it can lose a site.
Later consequence: a service dispute reads agreed obligations and witnesses, rather than assigning every faction a global hostility flag.

### 5. The regional outage

Trigger: several real utility sites fail or are attacked under the selected causal branch, while transport and treatment are limited.
Choices: heavy rescue, repair one pivotal route, evacuate with sacrificed equipment, negotiate access or continue elsewhere.
Aftermath: strong equipment really defeats lower threats; simultaneous crises and unfamiliar hazards can still outstrip actual reach/support.
Abstention: funded crews act within supplies and route knowledge; some sites, people or records can be permanently lost.
Later consequence: the surviving network can develop into public compact or exclusive service. A destroyed workshop is a loss, not a prestige reward.
Lethal engagement, run end and radiation damage require separately selected adapters and policies; current native recovery persists.

## Bounded expedition composition

A Paradise-style assignment can select one existing arc with declared objective, local entry means, paid costs and settlement/extraction terms, while the broader campaign supplies coherent people and aftermath. This is an O structural proposal, not verified novel mechanics.
A longer residence uses the same local history for work, training, relationships and projects; later visits do not reset its people or unique claims. A new era or parallel history requires a genuinely separate authored instance.
The shared issuer/hub owns mission admission and settlement, while this campaign owns local causes, source rules and lasting outcomes. Completing an extraction can leave the local conflict unresolved or lost.

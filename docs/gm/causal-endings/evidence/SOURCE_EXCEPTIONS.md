# Source exceptions: loop return and fate threshold

These two cases qualify source-specific rules for the causal-endings design. Source events below are analytical paraphrase. Hypothetical player routes are **original design proposals**, not claims about source canon or current game support. Source snapshots are qualified: the Mother of Learning dossier uses the supplied EPUB (`e53f95695730fd58b70c9676f03cd2ee457521c60210515cea38c1a514aa2fcf`, edition metadata dated 2022-04-29; authenticity not independently certified); Fated Villain uses the supplied English MTL-labeled EPUB (`3136ed56debd2f33ba03751e80bdbbd027cc0a06977cd19a5825b843cb6a9496`; translation fidelity/authenticity unresolved). Exact dossier evidence inputs and their captured hashes are in `SOURCE_EXCEPTIONS.json`.

## MOL-LOOP: last iteration is not an ordinary return

**Frozen era and pre-state.** Freeze the final loop iteration at the Festival-alignment breach, Chapter 91. Zorian's group has a prepared exit plan; the Sovereign Gate and Guardian are involved, and the plan depends on the Key, alignment, ritual roles, the Imperial Orb, life-force/material contributions, and people keeping the route open. The Guardian attacks lethally; this is not a safe or guaranteed exit (MOL-C091-10/11, Chapter 91, member `0091_91_A_Path_Paid_in_Blood.xhtml`, p233–315). The loop count values reported in earlier chapters differ and are not a reliable universal counter; this case does not invent a final integer.

**No-intervention source chain.** The ritual destabilizes Panaxeth's prison; the Guardian attacks; allies die while the costly path is stabilized. A primordial beast mortally pierces Zorian. A pre-running soul-separation contingency lets him leave in soul form; a toad-derived dimensional-perception aid guides him across the bridge, but its loop-body life-force anchor unravels. He reaches his sleeping original and wins a soul contest; the original's soul is released. Zorian wakes in the original's body. After the loop, he uses shared markers and a costly ritual to wake Zach; he has severe transition instability and missing loop-body anchors, not all former capacities (MOL-C091-11/12; MOL-C092-01/02, Chapters 91–92, members `0091_91_A_Path_Paid_in_Blood.xhtml` p301–341 and `0092_92_The_Scramble.xhtml` p2–33). The original outside world continues; Zach reports observing the original Zorian and original Silverlake during later iterations, while fresh counterparts do not automatically inherit loop memories (MOL-C092-09; MOL-C093-03, Chapter 92 p139–150; Chapter 93 p41–66).

**Three hypothetical player outcomes.**

1. **Failed/cancelled attack during the ritual.** A player tries to reach or stop Zorian but lacks access, timing, or a source-established counter to the Guardian/ritual defense. The attempt must resolve as a failed attempt or be unavailable; it cannot silently interrupt the authored exit. The source establishes the Guardian's lethal attack and the team's costly role-based response, not this player's reach, combat rules, or exact defense result. That failure receipt and any observer report are an original implementation proposal.
2. **Successful kill before Zorian's final-exit contingency.** A hypothetical eligible attacker kills Zorian during the final ritual before he can execute the pre-running soul-separation contingency. The result is **unknown**: Ch. 91 does not show what happens if this specific contingency is beaten. The source does not justify an automatic restart, a canonical return, or the player's survival, memory, or transfer. An authored game branch must preserve the actual death and mark subsequent loop/exit outcomes unrepresented until resolved.
3. **Player removes a required ritual contribution.** The player kills, captures, or otherwise removes an actor/material role the route needs before it is stabilized. The source establishes the ritual's costly role structure and named contributions, but not every substitution or the result of this intervention. The route should fail or change according to an authored dependency; whether the loop restarts, survivors are stranded, or the world ends is unknown. Do not recreate the canonical exit to preserve chapter order.

**Qualified comparator, not a general death rule.** Earlier, an assailant stabs Zorian; after that narrated death he appears back in Cirin the following morning (MOL-C006-05/06, Ch. 6, `0006_6_Concentrate_and_Try_Again.xhtml` p112–130). In another case, after Quatach-Ichl detonates his own soul, Zorian initiates the restart before blackout; he wakes in a later iteration with severe soul injury (MOL-C083-10/C084-01/02, Ch. 83–84, `0083_83_The_Quiet_Life.xhtml` p166–198; `0084_84_Powerless.xhtml` p2–18). These are qualified observations of specific deaths/returns, not proof that any arbitrary killing, especially during the final ritual, automatically restarts the loop. Zach's account that earlier Zorians repeatedly died in the invasion is a report, not an exposed universal contract (MOL-C007-03, Ch. 7, `0007_7_Of_Gaps_And_Pretending.xhtml` p80–108).

**Knowledge, witnesses, independent response.** Knowledge is actor-scoped: loopers and marked collaborators can retain selected memories; original counterparts do not inherit relationships; mundane witnesses see local events, not an omniscient reset history. Exit roles, the Key, temporary markers and memory crystals are distinct carriers. Surviving collaborators can adapt or refuse; source examples include changed plans after Silverlake disappears and negotiated resource/participant substitutions (MOL-C091-01–09). After return, Zach's rescue is an action with time, treatment, marker and exposure costs, not automatic party availability (MOL-C092-02/08).

**Closure.** Actor: the loop-body can die and restart, or Zorian can exit through the evidenced special contingency; these are separate states. Player: the player's own death/knowledge/return contract is unknown and needs an explicit game rule. Society: the final outside timeline continues, with original people and ongoing invasion/war consequences; it does not reset merely because the looper left. The dossier's actual later closure also includes Jornak's separate deadline pact tearing apart his body, mind and soul after Zach's contract dissolves, while invasion forces retreat and Sudomir escapes (MOL-C106-05/08, Chapter 106, `0106_106_I_Win_III.xhtml` p35–44). That is a source-supported terminal death for Jornak, not a loop return.

**Implementation limit.** Current native loop reset, identity/soul-body transfer, retained knowledge, alternate final-ritual consequences and a loop-aware ending consumer are unverified/unactivated in this project. Original proposal: represent `observed_death`, `initiated_restart`, `loop_exit`, `soul_transfer`, `counterpart_displacement`, and `post_exit_death` as distinct event kinds with explicit state owners and transfer receipts. Keep the exit contingency, Guardian attack, resource dependencies and actor plans as authored source-specific content. Never infer universal death reset from this setting.

## FV-FORTUNE: Gu, Ye Chen, and the demonstrated exception

**Frozen era and pre-state.** Freeze the Heavenly Origin Ancient Secret Realm confrontation at physical sections 59–70 (printed chapter numbering is irregular). Ye Chen is a favored-son target, not generically protected by that label. He enters below the realm's Mighty-Transcendent ceiling, has a map/key and Yan Ji in a ring, and pursues the Eight Desolate Demon Halberd inheritance. Gu follows using a mark on an Origin Tempering Spirit Pill, while his intent, rank, Elder Ming's protection, and system information remain private or audience-specific (FV-S0056-C02; FV-S0059-C02; FV-S0060-C01/02; FV-S0061-C02; FV-S0062-C02; FV-S0063-C02; section/member/paragraphs are listed in JSON).

**No-intervention source chain.** Ye's accusation that Yan Ji exposed him is false: Gu's pill mark supplies the trail. Their mistrust severs the master-disciple bond; the System reports a further 200 Fortune loss to Ye and 1,000 Destiny Points to Gu. Gu's Dharma Incarnate defeats Ye's key-powered attack; the key absorbs force that otherwise would kill Ye. Gu coerces Ye Liuli into opening her Knowledge Sea under an oath she expects to constrain him, plants a different mental-control mechanism, and kills Ye when Fortune reaches zero. Gu reports no backlash from breaking the oath and claims his demonic nature can consume heavenly demons; this is a demonstrated story event plus Gu's explanation, not a general oath rule or independently verified universal immunity. Liuli's altered conduct is coerced; it is not consent. Ye's death does not automatically transfer his identity or power to the player. (FV-S0064-C01/02; FV-S0067-C01/02; FV-S0068-C01/02; FV-S0069-C01/02; FV-S0070-C01/02.)

**Three hypothetical player outcomes.**

1. **Failed attempt or unavailable access.** A player attacks Ye from outside the sealed realm or above its entry ceiling. The source's boundary repels above-cap cultivators and can kill forced entrants; it is a real gate, not protagonist immunity (FV-S0059-C02). The game must show denied access or resolve a concrete failed entry with cost. There is no basis to promise the player can reach Ye or to guarantee what happens to an arbitrary entrant.
2. **Successful rescue/divergence before the palace.** A player reveals the pill-mark trail or protects Liuli's party, enabling Ye to avoid the planned confrontation. This is an original hypothetical: source supports the concealed mark, her dangerous position, and Gu's independent pursuit, but not the player's disclosure, whether Ye believes it, or a successful escape. If authored, Gu may pursue only through means and resources he actually retains; he does not get a free replacement Ye or automatically restore the palace confrontation. Ye's, Liuli's, Gu's and the player's knowledge must differ by who witnessed the warning and trail.
3. **Source-supported death branch / player-caused earlier death proposal.** The source baseline itself ends with Gu killing Ye after Fortune reaches zero, despite the oath. A hypothetical player who kills Ye earlier is not entitled to the same threshold reward or oath exception: the dossier establishes Gu's System rule and Gu's exceptional outcome, not transferable player access, immunity, a universal Fortune meter, or identical post-death consequences. If a game branch allows an earlier actual death, cancel only causes requiring living Ye (his pursuit, map/key use, and this inheritance route); keep already committed retainer deaths, Liuli's prior beliefs and other independent actors' plans. The subsequent demonic entity/halberd outcome is unknown. Do not revive Ye or choose a substitute victim to restore chapter order.

**Knowledge, witnesses, independent response.** Ye knows his map/key and suspects Yan Ji; he does not know the pill-mark tracking method. Gu knows that method and his own system; witnesses do not gain System UI. Yan Ji rejects Ye's accusation and later pledges to Gu; Liuli is separately deceived about the seal and oath; Aunt Xue prioritizes clan survival and has her own strength assessment (FV-S0064-C01; FV-S0065-C01/02; FV-S0068-C01/02; FV-S0052-C01; FV-S0054-C01/02). In a player divergence, the actual recipient and transmission channel determine who adapts. Gu can continue an owned goal if he still has means; no source proves a universal fallback plan.

**Downstream consequences shown in the reviewed notes.** Gu later removes the Halberd from its seal, subdues it into a private Weapon Space and leaves the realm with Elder Ming, Ye Liuli, Yan Ji and other companions; Lower-Realm observers wrongly believe he sealed the evil artifact (FV-S0074-C01/02, physical 74, `ENG_split_005.html#calibre_toc_75` p1–38, p58–85; FV-S0075-C01, physical 75, `ENG_split_005.html#calibre_toc_76` p1–67; FV-S0076-C01, physical 76, `ENG_split_005.html#calibre_toc_77` p1–14). Gu explicitly keeps Liuli as leverage, and her imposed fear/name for him remains coercion; Yan Ji is housed in nourishing jade with an explicit limit on outside perception (FV-S0071-C02, physical 71, `ENG_split_005.html#calibre_toc_72` p52–79). Months later Liuli says she no longer cares about Ye Chen but wonders why she thinks about Gu and calls him “Master”; the dossier attributes hazy Lower-Realm memories to a later-named control mechanism (FV-S0131-C01, physical 131, `ENG_split_010.html#calibre_toc_132` p12–50, p69–76). These facts show downstream custody, public misinformation, and altered memory/behavior; they do not establish restored Ye Chen, free allegiance, or universal consequences.

**Closure.** Actor: Ye's death is narrated and not reversed in the reviewed arc. Liuli survives with compromised autonomy; Yan Ji's allegiance changes before his death, then she accompanies Gu under bounded conditions. Player: no source-defined role, Fortune, oath immunity, loot or ending is transferable. Society: Lower-Realm observers misidentify Gu's Halberd custody as artifact sealing; later political consequences beyond the reviewed evidence remain unknown. The edition is an MTL snapshot, so translation-sensitive claims retain that limit.

**Implementation limit.** Current game support does not activate this source-specific realm, Fortune/System rules, pill marks, oath exception, mental-control/soul mechanism, or these ending consumers. Original proposal: give the source profile an explicit Gu-owned threshold and exception rule, preserve `attempted`, `hit`, `defeated`, `captured`, and `dead` separately, attach oath terms and rule owner, and invalidate dependent events only when their actual prerequisite is removed. Never grant player title-based immunity, Fortune harvesting, stolen identity, or Gu's oath bypass by analogy.

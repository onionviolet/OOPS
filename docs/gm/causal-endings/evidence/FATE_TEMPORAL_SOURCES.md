### RI chain A: Fate doctrine, Red Lotus, and the costly Spring Autumn Cicada exception

**Source chain.** In a memory shown by Red Lotus’s inheritance, Duke Long says Fate orders beings and events and Hong Ting's errors, choices, and eventual Immortal Venerable role are already arranged. That is Duke Long's doctrine, not independent proof of every future outcome (RI-c1677-01, Ch.1677, `EPUB/chapter_1677.xhtml` blocks 3–39). Hong Ting delays, acts, uses two lifespan Gu on his parents, and still cannot prevent their death after Bull-headed Demon Brute's hostage event; later he reaches rank nine with Liu Shu Xian's special physique blocking the decisive tribulation, then grieves her death (RI-c1677-02/03, Ch.1677, blocks 41–80; Ch.1678, `EPUB/chapter_1678.xhtml` blocks 1–60). Red Lotus rejects Duke Long's demand to accept the Venerable role and proposes reviving the dead, making the challenge a chosen position after personal losses, not a guarantee that his plan can restore everyone (RI-c1678-01).

Spring Autumn Cicada is an actual, owner-specific time exception. At Ch.196, Fang Yuan activates his vital Rank-six Gu by sacrificing his current body, cultivation and other Gu as propulsion. Fang's account places success below ten percent; the Cicada carries his remaining will/consciousness upstream, and the new pass begins with earlier body/resources. The returned Cicada is weak and cannot be used immediately again (RI-c0196-03, Ch.196, `EPUB/chapter_196.xhtml` blocks 20–35; RI-c0197-03, Ch.197, `EPUB/chapter_197.xhtml` blocks 67–102). Do not convert this into automatic death reset: it is a deliberate activation, specific artifact, owner, cost and dangerous river passage. Red Lotus's later Spring Autumn Success method removes the Cicada's failure chance in that method but reduces luck; that is a separately prepared inheritance and still has a cost (RI-c1679-02, Ch.1679, `EPUB/chapter_1679.xhtml` blocks 25–46). Red Lotus says his island has no known shortcut to Fate Gu's destruction; Fang's conclusion is a pursued goal, not fulfilled fate (RI-c1679-03, same member, blocks 47–66).

**Mechanism owner and knowledge.** Fang owns and chooses whether to activate his vital Cicada. He pays in the current pass and retains only what the ability conveys; he is not made invulnerable in earlier history. Red Lotus's inherited killer move has its own owner, inputs and tradeoff. Other characters see outcomes or hear claims; the reader's retrospective chronology does not grant witnesses the same memory. Fang's recollection can be wrong or become stale as events diverge. The source explicitly records later discrepancies between prior-life memory and current events, so remembered ascension or opportunity is not a command that the timeline must restore itself.

**Limits and unknowns.** The below-ten-percent estimate is Fang's belief, the precise rewind span differs by activation, and repeated-use safety is not guaranteed. Vital-Gu survival during refinement failure is a separate narrow protection, not immunity to its master's death (RI-c0106-03, Ch.106, `EPUB/chapter_106.xhtml` blocks 65–94, in `INDEXES.md`). Resurrection via the Yin-Yang method is an offered possibility in Ch.197, not yet achieved. Future power does not act backward unless the Cicada's activation transfers an owner-bound consciousness; a character merely destined or prophesied to become powerful has no such access.

### RI chain B: Fate Gu destroyed, Fate distributed, and Venerable ascent remains conditional

**Source chain.** At the climax of Chapter 1960, Fate Vanquish still damages other combatants and Gu Houses, but Fang's cape remains intact because his complete otherworldly-demon status is the core exception. The effect protects Fang in this form; it does not protect his allies (RI-c1960-01, Ch.1960, `EPUB/chapter_1960.xhtml` blocks 2–41). Fang enters Heaven Overseeing Tower, kills its defender, seizes rank-nine Fate Gu and crushes it. That supplied chapter ends at the destruction with no consequence or new world-order result; a character's post-battle explanation is needed before any such state can be claimed (RI-c1960-03, Ch.1960, blocks 72–117).

Chapter 1961 shows the fragments and Tower collapse. Duke Long argues Fate could be refined again and only Heavenly Court could use it through Heaven Overseeing Tower and Star Constellation's will. These are strategic assertions. Chapter 1966 supplies a later, distinct witnessed result: Red Lotus and Giant Sun's prepared plan distributes Fate into people as heaven-path marks fused with luck; narrator says it can no longer be used by Heaven's Will. The process causes bodily pain and deaths, the regional walls disappear, and the five regions merge (RI-c1961-01/02, Ch.1961, `EPUB/chapter_1961.xhtml` blocks 2–145; RI-c1966-01/02/03, Ch.1966, `EPUB/chapter_1966.xhtml` blocks 2–162). Thus Fate Gu's destruction is neither “all causality is gone” nor “Heaven's Will and the world vanish.” Fate Gu, its distributed portions, Heaven's Will, and ordinary causal consequences are distinct state owners.

After the destruction, Lu Wei Yin names four conditions for Venerable ascent: white-litchi essence, 300,000 dao marks in the main path, supreme-grandmaster attainment, then breaking the Heavenly Dao blockade to produce yellow-apricot essence. He lists other blockades and possible shortcuts; Fang assesses his own missing conditions rather than ascending on strength alone (RI-c2085-01/02, Ch.2085, `EPUB/chapter_2085.xhtml` blocks 2–97). Lu's follow-up says post-Fate revival can be costly/incomplete and rivals' prepared arrangements compete to restore venerables; Star Constellation's return is likely in Lu's view, not confirmed in that scene. His Dao Lord model grants a Venerable with supreme-grandmaster attainment access to natural dao marks as a personal battlefield (RI-c2086-01/02/03, Ch.2086, `EPUB/chapter_2086.xhtml` blocks 2–80). Only later, at Ch.2211, Fang's concealed preparation and rank-nine timing lead the rivals to infer his ascent; their reconstruction remains partly inference. His subsequent severely damaged state and lost assets make rank-nine cultivation distinct from safety or effortless control (RI-c2211-01/02/03; Ch.2211–2212, `EPUB/chapter_2211.xhtml` blocks 2–78 and `EPUB/chapter_2212.xhtml` blocks 1–101).

**Protection scope and unknowns.** Fate Vanquish's failed targeting of Fang is conditional on the narrated complete otherworldly form; it does not establish all future venerables, all otherworldly people, or the people beside him as immune. The later distributed Fate event is real but its exact per-person allocation and lasting effects are unknown. Revival depends on specific remains, inheritances and preparation in the source, not generic “chosen protagonist” status. Heaven's Will loses this stated use of Fate after distribution; the source does not say all world laws or causal rules cease.

**Owner and knowledge.** Probability costs are borne by the participant/system interaction; a specific Story, audience, sponsor or wall fragment supplies the support. **Regression has a chosen cost and scoped participants.** Elderly Joonghyuk accepts Dokja's generated Regression Stigma, knowing it consumes memories of friends and sponsor information; by choice the 0th turn ends (ORV-525-02, Ch.525, `OEBPS/OEBPS/chap_00525.xhtml` blocks 43–152). **A specific causal-origin loop is authored, not universalized.** The 1863rd-turn Sooyoung crosses the Final Wall into a younger First World-line body and meets fifteen-year-old Dokja. Later Sooyoung is identified as tls123 by the story (ORV-536-01 context, Ch.536, `chap_00536.xhtml` blocks 9–14); the world still reaches a troubled, incomplete aftermath rather than confirming her writing guaranteed everyone's survival.

**Owner and knowledge.** Joonghyuk initiates a specific Stigma with known memory loss; Dokja creates/transfers it; Biyoo explains branch selection; the Ark controls a single route. The 1863 Sooyoung's knowledge and writing belong to her, and the younger Dokja does not begin with that knowledge. Claims from the Fourth Wall about dispersed Dokja souls and world maintenance remain attributed, with late restoration unresolved (ORV-540-01/541-01; ORV-551-03).

## What this supports for original implementation

These sources support conditional, owned exceptions. A protection can be decisive under its named trigger and still leave allies exposed; a fate intervention can remove one source-defined blocker while retaining other blockers; time travel requires a specific accessible mechanism and retained information; a branch can create a new life without reversing the old one's losses. Original proposal: author each exception with its actor/tool owner, era, exact trigger, affected targets, any demonstrated cost or limit, the actual observers, and the downstream consumers it changes. Keep costs absent when the source-supported rule is genuinely costless or unlimited in that scope; do not add a balancing budget by habit. A prediction of future strength, a protagonist label, a reader's knowledge, or an alleged destiny alone does not authorize a backward intervention or automatic survival save.

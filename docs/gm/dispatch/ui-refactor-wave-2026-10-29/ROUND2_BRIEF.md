## The round, in order

1. **How other games do it.** Study 4-6 games that handle your feature well or badly (your list below is a start, not a limit). For each: what the player sees, what it makes easy, what it costs, and the one idea worth adapting. Label each claim as known from play or docs, or inferred. Adapt mechanisms and patterns rather than recreating a specific game's look or content, since OOPS builds original work. Also check our own references first: Proto23 and YAIR baselines (UR12, UR49) and the saved Discord guide (UR59).
2. **Fresh-player test.** Open your slice from a fresh life (or the named fixture) as someone seeing it for the first time. Can they say in five seconds what it is, what they can do, and what changed? List every term that needs knowing, every click that hides the point, every number without a meaning (UR61, UR70, UR75). Fix the cheap ones.
3. **Space, split and density at 1920×1080.**
   - Split when one view answers two questions; merge when two views answer one.
   - Use the room: a list and a pinned inspector side by side, a large view that expands into the center (UR24), wide figures rather than squeezed ones.
   - Density by task frequency (UR42): a compact glance (one line or a bar) where the player looks often, full detail one click away. Say which density each part uses and why.
4. **Bars and small signals.** Where a full figure is too much, a meter or chip may carry the fact: relationship bars (trust, debt, warmth), readiness, risk, progress. Each says which question it answers (UR61), has words beside the colour (UR37), and reads the same everywhere (UR74).
5. **Pop-outs and the Electron app.** The desktop host has pop-out panels (`desktop/popout_host.cjs`), presence (Dock badge, notifications) and window state. Decide whether your feature earns a pop-out (for example a relationship web or calendar on a second screen), a notification, or a badge, and keep the browser fallback (`src/host.js`). Renderers draw into the node they are given, not the global document, so pop-outs work (MDL13).
6. **Cheats and insight views.** Weibao plays solo and cheats are features. Decide how your feature shows to a cheat or continuing-insight view (see "Where cheats go" in `docs/UI_AT_SCALE_CONSIDERATIONS_2026-10-14.md`): what a revealed view adds, how it is marked as cheat-sourced, and keep sealed secrets inside their discovery route. Keep the developer inspector separate from both.
7. **Visual style.** Offer at least one alternative style for your feature in the gallery beside the default (for example paper and ink, glyph-and-line, a world skin through tokens, MDL6), and say which reads better and why. Luminous Gothic is the default look; your style must work in it and in Neutral, at 14 px and 21 px text, and with effects off.
8. **Use what we have.** Before adding anything, list the existing panels, data and primitives that already cover part of it (`figure.js`, `scene_card.js`, `observer_view.js`, `ui_components.js`, the ledger and note shapes in the coverage plan). Prefer extending them; a new shape needs two consumers (proposed UR79).
9. **One unconventional option.** Something genuinely different (diegetic, the world reacting, sound or motion as information within UR38, generated prose, a physical-feeling interaction). Say what it would take and whether it is worth a later slice.
10. **Audit gaps.** Against the lead's run (LTU1-LTU15 in the UI audit round 10), other seats (failure rows, thin lives, less knowledge), empty and error states (UR32), persistence, performance (DOM nodes, render cost) and accessibility (keyboard, focus, 21 px, effects off).

Then act: implement the improvements that fit your slice and are cheap and reversible, extend tests and 1920×1080 evidence, and commit to your branch.

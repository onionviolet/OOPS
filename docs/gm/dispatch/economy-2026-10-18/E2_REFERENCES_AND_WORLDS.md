# E2: economy, trade, money and property, references and world canon

CHECKPOINT: lane E2, 2026-10-18. Read-only research. This file is the only thing written. Proposals only, not accepted decisions. No sealed secret truth is recorded; I opened no `WORLD_SECRETS*` file.

Labels: **SHOWN** = read in a local file this pass (path given). **STATED** = a repo doc says so (path given). **MEMORY** = my general knowledge, unverified, and some of it may be out of date. **INFERRED** = my reasoning from the above. Local research notes are mostly source-code and data audits of mod-friendly or open-source games, so for commercial games without a local report the mechanics below are MEMORY.

M codes are from `docs/gm/dispatch/procgen-2026-10-13/p11/REQUESTS_AND_MARKETS.md` (M1-M28, rounds 1-5, SHOWN). EC1-EC5 are in `docs/worlds/MULTIVERSE_STRUCTURES.md` OD11 (SHOWN). Short key to the M codes used below:

- M1 default sink is a local merchant. M2 per-good price curve (flat staples, falling durables, rising reputation curves, event shifts). M3 buyer tiers (universal, specialist, deep-pocketed). M4 other ways to turn work into money (consignment, standing orders, own shop, auction, investment, exchange, caravans).
- M5 balance: grow sinks, sale channels are real choices, a broken engine is a payoff, readable cause. M6 selling easier than buying. M8 sell rules for idle play. M9 undo through the merchant's stock. M10 sell services and knowledge. M11 market knowledge is a skill. M12 money across lives.
- M13 kinds of money. M14 value is recognition x usability x access. M15 per-world details. M15b frictions (change, weight, authenticity, dumping, inflation). M16 bridges between currencies.
- M17 buying is half the fun. M18 credit, loans, debt. M19 safety of wealth (corrected by M25). M20 taxes and fees (corrected by M26). M21 gambling. M22 wealth changes treatment. M23 giving. M24 endgame money.
- M25 wealth is information before it is a target. M26 institutions are world data, absence valid. M27 exchanging a past life's resources. M28 making an ending feel productive.

Existing authority that bounds anything below (STATED, `docs/RULES_INDEX.md` economy rows and OD11): AD27 economies are local, trade and arbitrage intended, shared currencies come through means; AD31 property; MR5, SR15, SR19; EC4 no universal diminishing-return cap, market tax or forced price normalization is selected; EC5 shared currency needs an accepting issuer/network or conversion means. Weibao is essentially the only player (AD22), so these references inform fun, not fairness or anti-exploit hardening.

Runtime facts that matter (SHOWN in REQUESTS_AND_MARKETS, round 1-2 and 3 headers; I did not re-open `src/`): `src/market_saturation.js` applies one saturation curve to every good; `trader_component.js` has no merchant purse (so every merchant is already unlimited); `character.money` is one number; `native_trade_transaction.js` rejects totals above `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`; sold goods move into the vendor's stock; no batch or rule-based sell exists. NONHUMAN_SOCIETY_LAW_AND_ECONOMY F5 (STATED) says `trade.js` quotes against actual stock, purse, market and margin and rechecks price before commit, so a stale-quote check already exists.

### A.3 What the references agree and disagree on

- **F1:** Several comfortable idle designs do not saturate at all (Melvor, Stardew in MEMORY; Rogue Legacy toll in SHOWN). M2 is stated as "per-good curve, not a rule that sales fall", so flat is an allowed dial value. This is consistent with M2, not a contradiction. The existing runtime applies one curve to every good (SHOWN), which is the part M2 changes.
- **F2:** Hidden merchant caps (Skyrim, MEMORY) are the version of "merchant purse" players complain about; the written M3 version works only if the cap is visible and explained (M5.4, M11). This is a real risk to the M3 deep-pocketed tier and not yet addressed in the palette.
- **F3:** Wealth-triggered raids (RimWorld, MEMORY) conflict with the round-5 correction (M25). The correction is still compatible with a dial: raids can look at known wealth, not wealth in the ledger.
- **F4:** Debt can be the structure of a game (Recettear, MEMORY), not a side feature. M18 treats debt as optional; a world can still choose it as the spine.
- **F5:** Meta-currencies with different time horizons (Hades, Trimps SHOWN) support M27's idea of a carrier with its own rules, but the same sources warn that too many layers read as homework. M28's legibility rule (see before the ending) is the guard.
- **F6:** Local research has no source on theft-by-notice itself. Skyrim's witness-based bounty and Kenshi's thieves are MEMORY. The strongest local support for M25 is the SR claims/custody framing (STATED in REQUESTS_AND_MARKETS), not a game study.

## Part C: design angles the root palette may be missing (ranked by usefulness)

Each is a proposal with a default, a dial, and the reason; none is an accepted rule.

- **E2-A1: Visible purse and stated refusal reason.** If merchants get purses (M3), say why a sale is refused ("the grocer's till is empty until market day") and what alternative exists (come back, consign, sell elsewhere). Hidden caps were the best-known complaint with Skyrim-style purses (MEMORY). Default: show a coarse purse state; dial: hide it in a world where the player hasn't learned it (M11). Supports M3, M5.4.
- **E2-A2: Price memory as a fallible ledger.** Store "price seen where and when" with an observer and date, and mark it stale. Vagrus (STATED) and Naev (SHOWN) both show the gap between local truth and acquired information. M11/M17 mention a ledger; make the stale flag a deliberate part.
- **E2-A3: Several quotes, no global rate.** Paradise keeps two Soul Coin quotes. Let a currency-to-currency rate be per-merchant with a spread (M16) rather than one world field. Rate field belongs to the merchant or changer, not the currency pair.
- **E2-A4: Conversion at a carrier, and what the carrier values.** M27 already says the carrier values by its own rules (EC4). Add a legible "carrier appraises" screen, like a Potionomics-style quote (SHOWN), before the ending so spending versus burying is a decision (M28). Also name which carrier clause is sealed (by ID) and show only what the player knows.
- **E2-A5: Debt as the spine of an optional start.** Recettear and the stated Chinese loan-advert book show debt-driven starts. A world can open with debt (a "borrowed-years" or money loan, TTKS T2 STATED) that structures goals. Dial: forgiving default (AD19-AD22), explicit opt-in "debtor start" origin.
- **E2-A6: Time-horizon separation of money.** Hades and Trimps (SHOWN) split balances by when they pay off. Keep each currency's horizon in its card: spend now, this life, next life. Guards M12 and prevents one prestige reset silently deleting an economy.
- **E2-A7: Rule-based selling at the merchant, not the player.** Beyond M8 sell rules: a "standing arrangement" with a named buyer (apothecary takes surplus draughts) that persists, can fail with a reason, and shows up in the offline recap. Kenshi-style contracts end in a conversation (SHOWN), which gives a model for an arrangement expiring.
- **E2-A8: Quote comparison across destinations.** Stardew finding (SHOWN) says the same good has five destinations with no side-by-side quote. A "where could this go" panel that shows sale, craft input, gift value, quest use and carrier appraisal on one held stack is cheap and makes M5.2 real.
- **E2-A9: Wealth as the thing others can know, with a known-wealth gauge per place.** M25 already proposes it. Add that the same gauge feeds invitations, price hikes, recruiters and charity requests, not only thieves, so the downside is rarely a loss. Also let a world's mechanical raid or tax trigger read the gauge (RimWorld contrast, MEMORY).
- **E2-A10: Confidence on appraisal.** The recognition stage in M14 (curio, anomaly, identified use) could be a three-state flag on the item per world with the appraiser's confidence, so a wrong appraisal is a real event. Ties to MR truth/knowledge split.
- **E2-A11: Accounts that need identity.** M15 mentions bank accounts needing ID in modern worlds. Generalise: any institution account (bank, guild ledger, System wallet) binds to an identity the character may lack or change, so identity swaps, transformations and nonhuman bodies can affect access. Links to the NONHUMAN doc's "legal/shop admission" separate from fit.
- **E2-A12: Price-setter role.** In own shops (M4) the player sets prices and sees customer reaction (Moonlighter, Recettear, MEMORY). Make price-setting an optional layer on top of automatic default, with an assistant who sets prices by policy.
- **E2-A13: Counterfeit and debasement as events with a named actor.** M15b lists inflation and debasement; let a recorded issuer decision, a forger or a System patch cause it, so price moves in a recap name the cause.
- **E2-A14: Honest "no economy" worlds.** Fated Villain and several anime briefs say nothing about money. A world card should allow "money is not modelled here; services are favors, passes and mission pay" so a nearly-moneyless start doesn't force a coin field (M26).
- **E2-A15: Offline accounting for money events.** All of M8/M19/M21 touch offline play. Reuse the A8 recap: one line per sale, loan payment, robbery or tax event with its cause. Particularly: a missed payment while offline must show what the default was (M18 default).
- **E2-A16: Local currency item-ization of foreign coin (L15) as the first cross-world friction.** The cheapest, most visible first cross-world money event: a foreign coin that is only metal until a changer vouches for it. Proto23/Skyrim docs already say "no automatic exchange", so it is consistent.
- **E2-A17: Property income and upkeep as a sink with a dial.** AD31 property exists; Mount & Blade-style workshops and caravans (MEMORY) and Elona-style staff show income with upkeep. Explicit upkeep (wages, rent, repairs) gives mid-game sinks without throttling income (M5.1).
- **E2-A18: Stock-market and speculation as foreknowledge with a divergence cost.** M4 mentions this. Add the standing rule that a transmigrator's edge decays as their own actions change events; record the divergence per world so the edge is a resource with an expiry, not an infinite exploit (AD2 pairing, EC5 "intentional repeatable arbitrage" is allowed).

End of E2.

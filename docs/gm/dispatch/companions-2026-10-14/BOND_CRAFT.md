## Scope and reading key

The craft question is what makes a reader care about someone who accompanies
the protagonist. Human allies, a spider tutor and bound shadows belong here
alongside pets. This studies scenes and their effects; C1 owns the lore catalog.
The [root companion palette](../../../COMPANIONS_FAMILIARS_AND_PETS_CONSIDERATIONS_2026-10-14.md)
owns the wider menu. Playing as the companion belongs to
[nonhuman bonds and parties](../../../worlds/NONHUMAN_BONDS_AND_PARTIES_2026-10-14.md),
not this lane. Canon-first world priorities remain those in PROJECT and USER_VISION.

**SHOWN** identifies an action or statement in the supplied text. It does not
make a character's account true. **SHOWN, notes** means the cited existing
reader record supplies that scene; **SHOWN, checked** means C7 also inspected
the specified raw window. **INFERRED** marks craft readings and game proposals.
No author interviews or unchecked MEMORY claims support the studies.

Each relationship has six fields. A turning point is a selected beat, not a
claim that one scene completed the relationship. A payoff can be small or
painful. “Vague” means withheld or ambiguous at the cited stage; later chapters
may answer it. This cannot establish the author's private intent. For already
old friends/pets, distinguish the first scene from an unseen historical meeting.

**Citation convention:** paths below live under `docs/research/novels/full-book/`.
`notes:Lx–y` are actual one-based Markdown/JSON file lines; `p` and `b` are
the dossier's EPUB paragraph/body-block locators. Solo Leveling's `L` is the
zero-based normalized text-line index used by its existing reader: strip HTML
tags to newlines, decode entities, split nonbreaking spaces and discard empty
lines. Its EPUB member is chapter number plus one. These are text-member line
locators, not physical lines in the ZIP container. No source prose is copied.

## Reusable beats for a text idle life sim

All **BB** rows are INFERRED proposals derived from BC scenes. They describe
ways generated and authored events might cooperate, not a selected engine
contract. Default: use actual shared history and local wants. Dial: different
worlds can emphasize domestic comfort, hard service, alien psychology or magical
hierarchy. Reasonably: obedience imposed by a spell need not imply affection,
and an unusual bond need not follow a universal human friendship ladder.

| Code | Beat and source | Generated event route | Authored event route and how it avoids feeling predetermined |
|---|---|---|---|
| BB01 | **A small ritual becomes ours.** BC01 water/camp; BC06 meal; BC10 bar. | Repeated co-located rest/care can establish a remembered seat, food choice or greeting if both actors actually took part. Variation follows weather, injury, another guest or a changed preference. | A short authored meal can accept real food/place/history inputs. Later scenes refer to that meal only if it occurred. Default: optional comfort; Dial: frequency and intimacy; Reasonably: no daily login debt or gifts-to-loyalty conversion. |
| BB02 | **Shared hardship with different jobs.** BC01 staff/hearing; BC02 siege; BC03 raid. | Select pressure from actual travel/work conditions; each companion contributes through known means and bears a distinct limit. | BB03 | **A callback changes meaning.** BC06 Greek food; BC11 grasshopper/listening. | Keep sparse, specific shared-event memories. | BB04 | **Disobedience has a good reason.** BC05 rescue despite the attacker's model; BC07 refusal/explanation; BC09 chosen signals. | Compare an order with the companion's knowledge, danger judgment and goals. ### BB use example: one shared journey, several possible bonds

**INFERRED illustration:** the player queues an overnight cart delivery with
a mount and a hired scout. Rain slows travel under the world's existing rules.
The mount needs shelter; the scout wants to reach a village before a relative
leaves. The event can offer the known routes and allow the existing travel plan
to continue. A sheltered stop may produce BB01/BB05 if a meal and rest actually
happen. Continuing may protect the scout's appointment but produce a different
account of the tiring trip. An injured companion receiving aid can support BB06
later, if the opportunity and means arise. There is no required rescue followed
by a confession, nor a guarantee that the player chose the “bond answer.”

The author supplies local voices and meaningful scene shapes. Generation
chooses plausible participants, circumstances and remembered details from
state. Ordinary queues can accumulate history during idle play; consequential
choices follow the declared offline policy and remain reviewable afterward.
Save/reload should preserve the actual event and response, not choose a new
relationship interpretation. These are design considerations, not claims about
current runtime support.

## Anti-patterns that turn companions into gear

All **BX** rows are INFERRED diagnoses and alternatives, not moral judgments
about a book or blanket bans on useful service roles.

| Code | Failure and why it weakens care | Better consideration and dial |
|---|---|---|
| BX01 | **The companion appears only as output.** Recruit card, damage multiplier, upgrade menu. The player has no particular event to remember. | Show a behavior before or beside use, plus a want and a routine outside combat. A deliberately impersonal construct may stay a tool; do not promise a personal bond its scenes never support. BC02/BC09. |
| BX02 | **Gifts buy unconditional obedience.** Food becomes a currency for every request and grievance. Affection, contract and command authority disappear into one bar. | Let gifts suit preferences without guaranteeing compliance or erasing harm. Dial magical compulsion separately from trust and affection. BC04/BC06. |
| BX03 | **Saving them settles their life.** Rescue instantly makes the companion grateful, available and goal-free. | Continue their family, fear, career and other ties. They can care and still leave for an important duty. BC07/BC10/BC11. |
| BX04 | **A confession substitutes for history.** One dramatic dialogue asserts closeness without prior contact or later consequences. | Seed small shared acts and make the disclosure change what the actors do next. A short intense bond is still possible if its few choices have real weight. BC01/BC12. |
| BX05 | **A refusal is only an error or a forced trap hint.** Either dismiss it as bad AI or make the companion always right so the player learns to click obey. | Give a readable reason, fallible knowledge and consequences. Trust includes learning when to rely on each other's judgment. BC07/BC09. |
| BX06 | **An independent goal is a player reward dispenser.** Every desire exists to deliver an item, skill or loyalty tier. | Let curiosity, home life and work have outcomes the companion values. Some opportunities simply make their day or change their availability. BC08/BC09/BC10. |
| BX07 | **Replacement erases loss.** An identical recruit restores all functions, habits and dialogue; death was only a fee. | Separate restored capability from identity and shared memory. Returning/reborn beings need explicit continuity rules and new scenes, not an assumed emotional reset. BC05/BC12. |
| BX08 | **Closeness grants omniscience.** The UI reveals all thoughts, secrets and motives because a threshold passed. | Support observed cues, negotiated disclosure and unknowns. A truthful statement can still be partial; a protagonist's interpretation can be wrong. BC01/BC08/BC11. |
| BX09 | **Repetition is mistaken for earned affection.** The same petting text runs hundreds of times, or absence decays affection until the player logs in. | Reuse the ritual with meaningful variation and occasional memory, compress uneventful care, and follow the world's declared idle policy. Comfort need not decay into chores. BB01/BB05. |
| BX10 | **Sacrifice is the universal graduation exam.** Every loyal companion must take a lethal hit, making them disposable proof of the hero's worth. | Let loyalty appear in staying, honest disagreement, ordinary help, recovery or choosing retirement. Serious loss can land when earned by this history, but warmth does not require a casualty. BC05 compared with BC07–BC09. |

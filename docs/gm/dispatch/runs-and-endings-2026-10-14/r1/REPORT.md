## V00: reading and evidence rules

Read [COMMON](../COMMON.md), [AGENTS](../../../../../AGENTS.md), [PROJECT](../../../../../PROJECT.md), [USER_VISION](../../../../USER_VISION.md) and [part 22 sections 8–12](../../../../worlds/world-depth-2026-10-13/22_FEATURES_AS_CHEATS.md). Existing world briefs, source ledgers, digests, dispatch wiki lanes and the private derived findings were checked before web research. Public franchise material below is structural: status, dates, durations and era names. No altered secret truth or raw novel prose is reproduced.

**Labels:** **Shown** means directly visible publication metadata or directly located source evidence. **Stated** means an author, character, publisher or wiki says it, retaining that attribution. **Inferred** means this lane's calculation or grouping. **Proposal** means an unaccepted game choice. **U** means not verified. Web links in this report were accessed or their indexed text inspected on **2026-10-14**. An indexed excerpt is labeled as such; it is weaker than inspecting the complete page. A locally inspected older revision is dated separately.

QBK tags describe different axes and may coexist. A closed original can have an ongoing sequel; an adaptation split can contain two closed continuities. A hiatus is an ongoing work's publication condition, not an ending. A story's ending and a project's last acquired chapter are separate boundaries. Publication metadata establishes that a chapter exists; it does not establish that the project has read or adapted it.

## V20: RL1 pacing proposals

**All numbers in this section are proposals only.** Recommended target: about **one to three weeks of elapsed, unpaused idle simulation** for a complete closed baseline or the verified portion of an ongoing one, with meaningful arc-sized stopping points. This is not one to three weeks of hands-on clicking, a survival guarantee, a source claim, or measured existing-game performance. Ongoing stories can only be finishable **to a qualified frontier**, not to an unpublished ending.

Formula: `baseline elapsed hours = supported in-world days / proposed days per elapsed real hour`. Use 365 days/year for rough math. An explicit “wait to next beat” can shorten quiet intervals, with the world's consequences still resolved through their means. Offer slower/faster/era-only starts and no forced time jump. Concentrated decision scenes can use a slower dial. Apply the same local clock consistently to other actors, aging, work and hazards; accelerating only the player's progress would change the world's causal rules.

**V21. Two rates must not rewrite native time mechanics.** “365 simulated days per real hour” is the speed at which the player experiences simulation content. The book's simulation-to-outside-time ratio still determines how much the outside world advances during that content. Similarly, MOL loop elapsed experience does not advance the nonloop calendar and B3 revisits do not regain an attempt reset. Store/derive these relationships explicitly in any later implementation. The pacing examples above cannot by themselves settle a coupled clock model.

**V22. RL7 is optional scope within a run.** The named eras support a short complete-feeling life beside canon, a long multi-era life, a later start or a replay. They do not require death, prestige, travel, forced route selection or an automatic branch copy at an era boundary. That keeps the accepted “everything possible, reasonably” direction and separate prestige/travel/ascension intact.

**V23. Compatibility decision for later implementation, proposal only.** Static ending types, cited dates and era definitions can be versioned pack metadata without changing saves. A future persistent frontier or clock model needs an additive versioned block that defaults absent for old saves, preserves the old instance's time/course and never silently retimes a living save. New metadata can apply to new starts; an explicit opt-in conversion needs its own migration design. No such schema or migration was implemented here.

## Four models, what they feel like and where they fail

All feeling and engineering columns below are **inferred design analysis**, not source claims about every player.

| Code / owner model | History contract | Player feel | What breaks | How contradictions are contained | YAIRPG use and why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P01 / NP1 branch | Old history remains; a child history shares an as-of prefix and diverges after the cut. A branch can exist as a record without being reachable by travel. | Fix-it freedom and attachment to both outcomes. Saving someone here does not rescue their original counterpart. | End-state copies dated earlier contain future gear, dead people or friendships; shared mutable IDs leak changes; unlimited branches bloat saves. | Freeze the exact cut state, isolate mutable owners, record provenance and crossing scope. Do not recompute the parent when the child changes. | Best default for NT6/NT7 replays of a lived history. A branch is bookkeeping for the player; moving a person or cargo needs P4's carrier. |
| P02 / NP2 overwrite | One active history is replaced by a prior checkpoint plus declared retained domains. The old attempt is a player archive, not necessarily an existing world. | Urgent retry, growing competence, loneliness when others forget. | Stakes become disposable; carried items and paid rewards can duplicate; reset scope may leave debts/jobs from the wrong future; archival knowledge can leak to NPCs. | Define anchor controller, retained memories/body/cargo, reset boundary, RNG, external exports and exceptions. Old attempts can be read without being visitable. | A regression/rewind adapter, not “load this save.” A historical menu replay need not use overwrite. NP2's ghost archive must state whether it is playable, a simulation, or only a chronicle. |
| P03 / NP3 closed loop | The earlier arrival was already part of the committed history. One internally consistent causal graph may contain circular information. | Recognition, inevitability, discovering the role you already played. | Arbitrary player refusal, killing a necessary ancestor, or carrying inconsistent information defeats the authored loop. A generic simulator cannot ensure all future player choices fit. | Author a small set of compatible beats, identify fixed facts and actual means, and allow choices inside those constraints. An incompatible action can switch to an explicit NP1 variant if offered; do not secretly force a tragedy. | Narrow authored scenes, never the global default. Decide constraints before first recording the event. A closed loop is distinct from strong canon pressure or repeatedly replaying a day. |
| P04 / NP4 parallel past | Separate history with no parent-state dependency; an earlier-looking era and counterparts belong to it independently. | Clean second chance, variation, reduced guilt, sometimes weaker attachment. | Calling a counterpart “the friend you saved” misstates identity; copying actual rewards into a fresh counterpart world grants ownership without a cause. | Share definitions and scenario motifs, isolate people/stock/knowledge, label relationships as new. Any later crossing creates a real causal tie from that point. | Cheap source-era premise or AU start. It can resemble NT6 without containing the original self. It does not undo the old run. |

Later-generation continuity is a fifth **placement** comparison, not a fifth paradox law: a new protagonist starts farther along the same causal history. It does not map to NP1-NP4 unless a separate fork, overwrite or parallel premise is also chosen. This distinction keeps T22 from becoming a disguised reset.

### What the comparison adds

**P23:** A reset world can have a fixed schedule while the returner has a changing subjective history. Outer Wilds and Majora's Mask support different persistence lessons despite both using repeating intervals. A good menu says what returns and what the character actually remembers, rather than just “loop world.”

**P24:** Three issues often called “paradox” are separate: logical inconsistency (NP3), continued existence of discarded people (NP1 versus NP2), and duplicate custody/rewards (all models with crossings). Fixing one does not fix the others. A read-only attempt archive also does not answer whether abandoned worlds exist.

**P25:** The sources contain contradictions by selecting small domains, explicit exceptions, or authored histories. They do not prove a general solution for arbitrary player action. Natural-course C5 substitution must respect [TIMELINES T3:47-60](../../../../TIMELINES_AND_DIVERGENCE.md): missing causes can make an event ineligible. It must not manufacture a replacement solely to force canon.

## Proposed records for T22, T24 and T25

**P33 record ownership proposal:** introduce an envelope section `timeline_history` with its own version, rather than adding unknown fields to schema-1 instances. ## P38: save size, archive fidelity and migration

### Budget and fidelity

The current enforceable limit is [save_files.js:196-207](../../../../../src/save_files.js): **4,194,304 UTF-16 storage bytes across the origin**, including key lengths and other entries. Browser quota can be lower and is handled without deleting saves. Export adds UTF-8 base64 overhead, approximately 4/3 of the UTF-8 payload plus prefix. It does not make localStorage smaller. No private save was read or measured for this report.

As **illustrative targets**, budget up to 16 KiB UTF-8 for one compact life/trace record set and 128 KiB for an exact bounded anchor/diff set, then measure real fixtures before acceptance. These are not measured guarantees or hard lifetime counts. For ASCII-heavy JSON, one 128 KiB payload uses about 256 KiB localStorage; a main-save plus full backup duplicates that to about 512 KiB before native progress, other branches and keys. Thus “hundreds of exact worlds fit” is unsupported. Older TIMELINES 10-50 KiB estimates do not override the later fidelity warning at lines 71-78.

Prefer revision-pinned shared baselines and retained deltas; checkpoint an anchor when its dependency chain grows expensive. Keep source prose out of exports. Compact presentation logs into summaries, but never discard current custody, obligations, RNG or referenced changes required for promised resume. Use `resumeCapability` values such as `exact-selected-scope`, `authored-reconstruction`, `chronicle-only` and `unavailable-content`, and preserve dependency references when exporting one branch. Similar summaries are not safe grounds for automatic merges.

When the envelope approaches the guard, offer export and a reviewed archive choice before the next transformation. Keep the last durable life if writing fails. Do not silently fade a pinned exact branch into a nonresumable chronicle. IndexedDB/file-backed archives are a possible later storage adapter, requiring an atomic manifest/payload transaction and portable export; a proposal is not deployed storage. Runtime corruption quarantine can exclude optional archive entries only when the active life and all its dependency closure remain valid, with the original bytes preserved.

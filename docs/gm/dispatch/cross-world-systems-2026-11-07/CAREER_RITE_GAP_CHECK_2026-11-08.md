## F2: Agent admission exists; an answering actor lifecycle is still missing

`src/making_procedure.js` `compileProcedure` already admits `maker`, `witness` and `answerer`. Local agents become native participants with admission and settlement support/co-location checks. Remote agents require an explicit rule, are excluded from physical participants and recheck the rule at settlement. Completed steps remain actor facts; pending steps are ordinary jobs. The procedure layer already provides ordering, interruption, cost reservation and normal effects.

Crucial limit: a remote agent ID is metadata plus a predicate. `compileProcedure` does not look up its willingness, establish a contact, allocate a petition, deliver a reply or charge the responder. A row that simply supplies `ready=true` would hide precisely the missing owner. A fixed authored response can use existing final effects; an independently answering actor needs a real supplier of that predicate and result, with its own known/unknown boundary.

`src/lotm_summons_rows.js` explicitly declares scene data only. Inspection found its runtime-style result effects marked `owner-needed`, and imports only in tests. It is not proof of playable summoning or letter delivery. `src/lotm_rite_rows.js` has source-shaped rites, including pending effects. `src/book_world_lotm_acts_host.js` imports that rite table through `LOTM_ACT_ROWS` and binds selected rows with `LOTM_ACT_BINDINGS`; an import does not bind every row or resolve each pending owner. The supervised self-addressed route in `src/book_world_lotm_ritual.js` (`selfRiteCommands`, `selfRiteFacts`) already has a real reserve/recovery consumer, but that does not implement a remote request/reply service.

Immediate caller: the first source-qualified contact/answer event reached by the lead route. Reuse procedure steps, native timed commands, ordinary effects and existing observation records. Establish the smallest world-local contact owner needed by that event, then validate sharing when another world or different kind of responder calls it. Do not implement all prayer, summoning, sacrifice, travel and delivery as one inferred generic engine. In particular, observed acceptance of an object does not prove arrival or a reply.

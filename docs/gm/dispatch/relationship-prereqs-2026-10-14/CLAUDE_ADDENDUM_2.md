Core idea: every person, item, place and offer has a **true** layer and a **shown** layer. Characters act on what they perceive. The gap between the two is content: cons, disguises, hidden masters, kind monsters and the reveal.

## 1. Age and the CQ1 floor (DV1-DV3)

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| DV1 | **The romance floor reads both truth and look.** Fixed, not a dial: romance and partner content needs both people 18+ by body age **and** adult in appearance. A claimed age, soul age, species lifespan or "actually 1,000 years old" never unlocks it for a childlike-looking body. An illusion that makes a child look adult does not unlock it either, because the body age is still under 18. | The "ancient being in a child's form" trope is common in the genre. Either layer being childlike closes the door, so neither lore nor disguise can route around the floor. |
| DV2 | **Age mismatches stay as non-romantic content.** Default: a centuries-old mentor in a young body, a child prodigy treated as an adult in war, a reincarnated elder in an infant: these show up as respect, condescension, misplaced trust and legal friction. | Keeps the trope's real fun (people underestimating the "kid", the old soul's patience) without touching the floor. |
| DV3 | **Apparent age is its own fact.** Default: people treat you by apparent age first (CQ17 splits body, soul, apparent, legal). Aging magic, youth potions, cultivation stalling age and illness all move apparent age separately. | "You look too young to be a guildmaster" is a social event, and locals' expectations follow the look until they learn otherwise. |

## 2. People are not what they seem (DV4-DV12)

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| DV4 | **Shown identity versus true identity.** Default: each person can carry a shown name, role, class, origin and allegiance that differ from the truth. Recognition keys (CQ2) decide who sees through it. | Spies, nobles in hiding, LOTM masks, HP Polyjuice, D&D doppelgangers and changelings all use one model. |
| DV5 | **Hidden strength.** Default: a sweeping monk, a retired hero innkeeper or a cultivator suppressing their realm shows weak power. Appraisal (MEANS_AND_APPRAISAL) reads only what its level and channel can see. Dial: per world, how common hidden masters are. | The genre's favorite reveal. It also makes rudeness to strangers risky in a fair way. |
| DV6 | **Hidden weakness.** Default: a famous duelist past their prime, a rich house deep in debt, a warded tower with a lapsed ward. | The reverse reveal gives openings and sympathy, not only threats. |
| DV7 | **Kind monsters, cruel saints.** Default: looks and reputation do not set a person's temperament. A scary-looking creature can be gentle and a beautiful, respected figure can be cruel. Locals' first reaction follows looks (prejudice), and the player can learn better. | Lets the world argue with its own stereotypes, which many priority sources do (werewolves and house-elves in HP, Hollows in Bleach). |
| DV8 | **Charm and glamour.** Default: allure effects (Veela-style, fey glamour, a charisma cheat) raise first impressions and liking, not trust. They wear off or are seen through, and the person may resent it (part 28 SF5 receipts). | Beauty magic as a real stat with a cost, separate from compulsion. |
| DV9 | **Liars read warm.** Default: a con artist's shown liking is high while their true view is low. The people panel shows your read (SF1). Tells come from memories that do not add up, which the player can notice. | Betrayal (RD14) feels fair in hindsight because the clues were on the card. |
| DV10 | **The player can deceive too.** Default: disguise, false name, lying about age, class, wealth or power, faked credentials. Each lie is a knowledge row with who heard it. Being caught writes a betrayal memory sized by how close the person was. | Lets the player run a double life (LOTM, a noble in hiding, a spy start) with real stakes. |
| DV11 | **Reveals as relationship events.** Default: when a true layer surfaces, each witness reacts by temperament and tie: delight, betrayal, fear, awe, "I always knew". The reveal can raise or crash a tie. | Turns hidden identity from a flag into a scene, and the same reveal lands differently per person. |
| DV12 | **Shapeshifters and copies in your circle.** Default: a friend can be replaced (TTKS B1 habit tells, part 20 RWTT1). Close friends with high familiarity notice; acquaintances do not. | Relationship depth becomes a defense, which is a strong reason to keep close friends. |

## 3. Things and places too (DV13-DV18)

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| DV13 | **Counterfeits and fakes.** Default: items have a shown quality and a true one: fake gems, cursed blades looking blessed, watered potions, forged papers. Appraisal level decides what you see. | Trade and gifts gain risk; a skilled appraiser becomes a valuable friend (RD5). |
| DV14 | **Humble-looking treasures.** Default: a rusty sword, a plain ring or a beggar's book can be the real thing. Dial: per world frequency, low by default so junk is mostly junk. | The genre classic, kept rare enough to stay exciting. |
| DV15 | **Safe-looking places.** Default: a quiet village with a cult, a friendly inn that drugs guests, a dungeon room that is a mimic. Danger signs exist for people who know what to look for. | Wandering and travel keep tension without random unfairness. |
| DV16 | **Offers that hide their price.** Default: a deal, a patron's gift or a cheat offer (part 13) shows its benefit up front; the cost may need reading the fine print, asking around or appraisal. | Fits cheat acquisition and contracts; the player can always investigate before agreeing. |
| DV17 | **Signals of status.** Default: clothes, uniforms, scars, tattoos, accents, house colors and badges tell locals who you are, rightly or not. Changing them is a disguise verb; wearing someone else's is a crime in some worlds. | Appearance becomes a tool the player controls, with law (part 28 DS5) attached. |
| DV18 | **Your appearance shapes first impressions.** Default: looks, grooming, clothes and visible power shift first reactions (LIFE_SIM looks note). Dial: per world how much looks matter. Reasonably: impressions fade into the true tie as people get to know you. | Character creation and wardrobe choices matter socially without becoming a permanent score. |

## 4. Other things to consider (DV19-DV25)

| Code | Consideration | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| DV19 | **Rumor versus truth about you.** Default: what people heard about you (renown, part 21) can be wrong, flattering or slanderous, and it reaches people before you do. Correcting a rumor is play. | Reputation becomes something you manage, not a single number. |
| DV20 | **Misread intentions.** Default: a kind act can be read as a bribe, a warning as a threat, depending on culture and the tie. Custom facts (CQ16) shape the read. | Cross-world and foreign starts get real social friction. |
| DV21 | **Unreliable memory.** Default: NPC memories can be wrong, altered (memory charms, loop resets) or one-sided. Two people remember the same event differently. | Supports memory magic in HP and MoL and makes "who is right" a question the player can investigate. |
| DV22 | **Self-deception.** Default: your character's read of themselves can be off too: a hidden flaw (Shadow Slave), an unknown bloodline, a power they think is gone. The status window shows what the system shows, which may not be all of it. | Gives the player their own reveals, and fits system cheats with hidden fields. |
| DV23 | **Perception is a skill and a sense.** Default: seeing through things uses perception skills, senses (ABILITY_AUTHORING SN rows), appraisal, insight and familiarity, each catching a different layer. | One rule for spotting a liar, a fake gem and a disguised friend, with several ways to get good at it. |
| DV24 | **The UI never leaks the true layer.** Default: panels, tooltips, logs and the people view show only what the character perceives. Debug and sandbox views are separate settings. | Same discipline as sealed secrets. A single leaked tooltip spoils every hidden master. |
| DV25 | **Prerequisites.** Shown versus true fields on people, items and places; per-observer knowledge of each layer (part 29 RQ10); perception checks that read means; reveal events; a lint that no shown-layer UI reads a true field. | What the engine needs before any of the above can ship. |

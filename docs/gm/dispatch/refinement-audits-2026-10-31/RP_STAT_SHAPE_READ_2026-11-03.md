# Reincarnation Paradise stat shape: a comparator read for the LOTM notch scale (2026-11-03)

**Proposals and evidence just.** Nothing here is accepted; no code, `PROJECT.md`, `USER_VISION.md`, rules index, content window or pack was edited. This pressure-tests [the LOTM combat deep dive](LOTM_COMBAT_DEEP_DIVE_2026-11-03.md) (codes `LCF`, `LCT`, `LCD`, `LCR`, `LCQ`, `LCB`) against how Reincarnation Paradise (轮回乐园, 那一只蚊子) puts numbers on strength. Applies WA33 (each world calibrates in its own scale: **no RP number is imported into LOTM**), WA50, AD38, AD49, WPR5. Codes local to this file: `RPF` finding, `RPL` lesson (a pressure-test row against an LCD), `RPQ` open gap; they do not collide with any code in `docs/`.

## 0. What was read, and how

**Source.** Calibre 13, English MTL compilation (Gemini-2.5-Flash, 4,656 spine files, flat table of contents with no volume names; chapter numbers are the ones in the file names, which run 1 to 4143 plus extras). NS01 (`docs/research/novels/NS01_REINCARNATION_PARADISE.md`, gitignored) already records this edition as unaudited and as neither primary canon nor official; a number or term here is the MTL's rendering. The EPUB was streamed into plain text in the session scratchpad (never into git). Verbatim anchors (truncated to about 230 characters per line) are in the shared notes folder, `rp-stat-anchors.md`, not here.

**Coverage, stated plainly.**
- **Keyword-swept, whole book (all 4,656 files):** attribute panels (`Strength:` and siblings), appraisal comparisons ("Compare the characteristics"), "points higher/lower", gap and suppression words, luck and charm checks, tier and rank gates, disguise and concealment, damage formulas, critical and percent-chance lines. A sweep finds lines; it does not establish context.
- **Read as scenes (whole or large part):** ch3, 62, 405, 980.1, 1090, 1468, 1667, 2523, 2525 (one line), 2966, 3279, 3723, 3772, 3802, 4103. Every other chapter cited below was read as a single located line or a few lines around it.
- **Not read:** the story. Most chapters in every range, the endings, ch4144 onward and the extras. Nothing here is a plot summary and no spoiler beyond system rules is recorded.

**Locator form.** `chN Pnn` where `P` counts non-empty lines after the two repeated heading lines, plus or minus one. Some chapters appear twice in the spine (ch1556, ch1568, ch1512, ch1566); the first copy is cited.

**Labels.** S = a system panel or notification in the book. T = narration or a character's claim. I = my inference from S/T lines. W = wiki (not used). MTL decimal commas are a hazard: `1,123 times` in ch1090 reads as 1.123 under a decimal-comma reading, `Dexterity*1,2` is 1.2, `1K 12` is 1:12. Where a number could be either, the row says so.

## 1. What I found (RPF)

### Scale shape (question 1)

- **RPF1. Attributes are small integer point values, linear in play, not ratios.** The protagonist's first panel lists strength 6, agility 7, durability 5, intelligence 6, charisma 3, luck 1, with the system noting that an ordinary adult male is 5 on every stat and 1 on luck (ch3 P7-P13, S). Values then climb by free points and items: 12 by ch24 P7 (S), 38 by ch405 P12 (T), 74 to 103 for the best monsters of the middle book (ch1090 P23, ch1667 P53, S), 245 at ch3279 P61 (T), 539 against an enemy's 662 at ch3802 P56 (T). This is a linear, open-ended integer ladder with no cap in sight.
- **RPF2. The same panel states that the number is not combat power.** The first panel says the attributes shown are true but the outcome of a battle depends on more than them, and calls the panel a quick self-assessment (ch3 P15, S). The book then spends many chapters showing why (RPF9, RPF10).
- **RPF3. Qualitative thresholds sit inside the linear count.** A narrator says that at 50 points strength "a qualitative change occurred" and that 49 against 50 is "heaven and earth" (ch405 P10-P11, T). Another says 80 is a barrier where raising a stat gets very hard and 100 is a further qualitative change (ch1090 P42-P43, T). At 80 the system converts the four main stats to "true" stats (ch980.1 P7, S): true points are worth 5 times a normal point and cost 12 normal points, a net 2.4 times (ch980.1 P21, P38, S); a second stat reaching 80 must pass a barrier 20 times harder (ch980.1 P28, S); true stats ignore the 50-60% suppression normal stats take in dangerous zones (ch980.1 P24, S). So the displayed integer is linear, but the book stacks ordinals (50, 80, 100, true) over it.
- **RPF4. Rank (the book's tier) is a separate ordinal from points.** Hunters have a level (1 to many) and a rank that steps every ten levels, and the first panel says rank grants more strength (ch3 P3, S). Worlds carry rank ranges ("contractors from level 20 to level 30 can enter neutral worlds of rank 3", ch1181 P34, S). Late in the book the ranks become tiers up to 8 and beyond, with item grades above them (RPF14).
- **RPF5. Opposed stat gaps are described as small integer differences at the scale of the stat.** Early: a 2-point gap (15 against 13) means the weaker "needs some time" to break a grip (ch62 P58, T). Middle: an 8-point agility lead decides a chase (ch1235 P45, T). Late: "18 points higher" at about 180 to 245 is "enough to form a power suppression" and the First King's lead of 8 becoming 18 when a potion expires is named a "Strength Crushing effect" (ch2523 P14, P40; ch3279 P61, S/T). A 123-point gap at 539 vs 662 is "too vast" for open melee, but a parry skill covers a stated margin and an item lets the weaker side win a clash (RPF11). I read the crushing onset as roughly an 8 to 10 percent lead at that magnitude and not as a fixed number of points (I; three data points, no formula given).
- **RPF6. The book gives no win odds for a stat gap in the chapters read.** No passage says a gap of X wins Y out of Z times. Gaps are used qualitatively ("a considerable lead in speed", ch3772 P52; "extremely unfavorable", ch2523 P40). I make no odds claim. Where the deep dive's logistic wants a reliability number, RP supplies none (RPQ1).

### Appraisal as a ratio (question 5)

- **RPF7. The in-world appraisal is an explicit ratio, and the information returned depends on the ratio and on tier.** The protagonist's scanner compares one stat (often intelligence) against the target's and states the ratio and the percent of the target's data revealed ("0.923 times ... 71%", ch714 P35; "0.945 times ... 29%", ch798 P24; "0.951 times ... 46.8%", ch880.1 P22; "0.92 times ... 80%", ch1192 P42; "0.96 times ... 100%", ch1667 P48, all S). Coverage is not a clean function of the ratio. Hidden modifiers appear in the text ("Due to the enemy's higher tier, 74% of their data", ch1818 P52; "extremely high tier, 39%", ch1854 P38; 72% at 1.18 times, ch2026 P40, S). So I record ratio plus tier as inputs and fit no curve.
- **RPF8. Concealment targets the panel, and observers misjudge from it.** A disguise ability makes the user's data read as a template of a chosen tier (a Tier-7 Lord-level unit, ch2611 P32, S). The observed number a scout trusts is the Imprint level (it can be disguised, and a scout reasons "a Level 76 Imprint likely means their power isn't great", ch3302 P61, T/S). A rival "appraises" the newcomer's gear and concludes "ordinary", then loses a kick exchange to him (ch2966 P46, T). The wearer's real number, the number shown and the number believed are three different things in the book, and the reader-facing system makes this easy to track.

### Skills, items and conditions combine with base stats (question 4)

- **RPF9. Skill multipliers dominate equal stats.** A target with all three physical stats at 95 and one Level 10 "Grandmaster" skill (+320% melee attack, +20% reflex speed, plus status procs, ch1468 P29, S) at 75% health beats a protagonist of near-equal stats; he loses twice (ch1468 P30-P33, T/S). A demon with 103/99/99 stats carries "Melee Master" at +195% melee attack plus per-weapon riders (ch1667 P53-P60, S). The narrator's lesson is that a skill's level reflects accumulated experience, a different kind of thing than the stats (ch1468 P33, T).
- **RPF10. Damage formulas are linear in the stats, scaled by skills.** Examples: a skill's damage is "300 + Strength*8 + weapon attack power*1.2" (ch674 P37, S); a kick technique's base is 0.6 of each of three stats plus 0.3 of soul strength (ch3823 P23, S). True damage bypasses defence (ch2617 P8, S). Multipliers stack on a linear base, so effective power is closer to multiplicative than the displayed integers suggest (I).
- **RPF11. Items and preparation beat large gaps.** A parry skill is stated to cover a gap up to a quoted margin in true strength (257, ch3802 P56, T), and a weapon's "World Lightning" lets the weaker side win one clash against a 123-point lead (ch3802 P92, S/T); a stolen-stat weapon moves 2 points of two stats per hit up to five stacks (ch2966 P11, S). A single pistol removes a second-rank contractor and "even a third-rank" one in one shot (ch1282.1 P31, T). The weaker side wins by turning the gap into a margin another input covers.
- **RPF12. Conditional effect checks compare one attribute.** A deployed trap stuns the target if its strength is below the user's (0.2 to 1.8 seconds, a range), slows if agility is below (37% for 5 seconds), and applies a damage-over-time if stamina is below (23 per second, 10 seconds) (ch2041 P19-P21, S). These are hard comparisons with a random magnitude range, not logistic odds. An appraisal-style "check" is used for charm and luck (RPF13).
- **RPF13. Temporary stat shifts are routine, and some are signed.** Suppression (a character weakened by 15 on every stat, ch1090 P23, S), luck set to -12 or +8 for a side for the duration of a zone (ch2908 P53, S), stat theft per hit (ch2966 P11, S) and potions ("Furious Beast", ch2523 P14, S) all act on the same panel and expire. A condition changes the number; it is not a separate pool.

### Gates (question 2)

- **RPF14. Late-book tiers change the unit, not just the number.** Item grades climb through named tiers (Saint Spirit, Immortal, Origin, Samsara, with a weapon "rating" band of 15,000 to 30,000 for the Samsara tier, ch4103 P49, S), and "a whole major tier" gap pulls a lower-tier item's bonus toward its lower limit (ch3674 P189, ch3545 P203, T). Native worlds cap combat power ("the Eighth-tier world's combat power cap", ch3539 P42, T; Void notarization with a "Supreme Realm" cap, ch4103 P32, S), and above about native Tier 5 "the evolutionary suppression on extraordinary powers disappears" (ch4103 P36, T). Defence at the top is a "tier": attacks with divine or abyssal traits "contend with immunity to all damage plus physical defense tier" (ch4103 P76, T), and one ability makes the user immune to a same-tier attacker at a stated energy cost (ch4132 P45, S). Stats have a ceiling in effect when an item is stat-capped (a potion works below a stated True Stamina, ch3534 P196, S).
- **RPF15. World-level gates are mostly about environment and information, not "who can hurt whom".** Zone suppression of 50-60% on normal stats (ch980.1 P24, S), world repulsion that drives a luck score to -45 and kills the unlucky (ch2609 P29, S/T), stat suppression for rank-limited worlds (ch1090, S), and tier-capped appraisal (RPF7). **I found no stated rule that a lower rank cannot harm a higher rank.** The narration shows rank-4 contractors killed by assorted things (ch1305, ch1512 lines, T) and a rank-3 shield that held then failed against the protagonist's improved blade (ch742.1 P22, T). The combat tier gate is therefore I, not S (RPQ2).
- **RPF16. What bypasses a gate or a suppression.** True stats ignore zone suppression (ch980.1 P24, S); a different mechanism (potion, world-lightning item, a Perfect Parry skill, a spatial trick) turns a gap into a margin (RPF11); disguise changes how the gate sees the user (ch2611 P32, S); a protagonist's tier-appropriate item can lift him to a tier's immunity for a time (ch4132 P45, S). Each carries a cost or limit in the text read (energy amount, stack cap, duration, one use).

### Probability, luck and fate (question 3)

- **RPF17. Luck is an attribute that biases drops and events, and it is shown with explicit odds on rolls it governs.** The panel defines luck as affecting "the chance of getting and crafting items" and as "extremely difficult to raise" (ch3 P11, S). Luck 30 "genuinely transformed" chest results; the chest opened shows its own percent ("Epic-tier Treasure Chest (47%)", ch2109 P31-P42, S). Upgrades show a quoted success chance per outcome ("100% success rate ... 71.6% chance to upgrade to Gold", ch1718 P10, S). So the system tells the player the odds before the roll; the roll itself is a fixed-odds draw, not a dice mechanic the reader sees.
- **RPF18. Luck also works on events in combat as a side-wide bias, not a hit roll.** With one side at -12 and the other +8, "the disparity in luck became even more apparent" through stray hammers hitting bullets and similar coincidences (ch2908 P53, T). Luck below zero changes an ability's own behaviour (a wither effect strengthened, ch2727 P14, S) and a very low value summons world repulsion (ch2609 P29, S). No line I read states a luck-based hit or dodge percentage.
- **RPF19. A "check" against an enemy compares attribute totals and scales the effect's size.** A curse is described as getting "a dual bonus from your Charm and Luck attributes" and running a Charm and Luck check against the enemy, and "if the check passes, the intensity ... is enhanced or weakened" based on the result (ch3723 P72, S); the result line gives a charm ratio (7560 times, ch3723 P75, S; the number may be 7.56 under a decimal-comma reading, so I do not rely on its size) and the effect's duration "received a super-massive enhancement" (S). A fate effect scales continuously with the ratio rather than succeeding or failing.
- **RPF20. Fixed small chances are written into skills and weapons.** "33%" for a critical organ strike on a plunged knife (ch674 P62, S), "increases the chance of crushing enemies by 30%" (ch602 P64, S), a status that lands "if this assessment is won" when two force values are compared (ch1667 P60, S). Randomness in fights is mostly percent-on-hit procs and a random duration range, listed on the skill, with the stats deciding which side of a comparison you are on.

### Later-book scale changes (question 6)

- **RPF21. The book rescales its own units.** Early panels run in single and double digits (RPF1). By ch3772 the antagonist has 540 strength and 1.8 million HP (T), by ch3970 "plus 100 to all attributes" and 8 million HP mark a stated tier (T), by ch4103 the weapons are rated in tens of thousands and the lead is on "Supreme Attributes" (T). The system adds a doubled unit each time it needs one: true stats at 80 (x5 effect), item tiers, world tiers, a "rating" for weapons.
- **RPF22. The late book sets the same fights in different currencies.** A cap on a native world (Eighth-tier) holds a god below an unrestricted equivalent in the Void (ch3539 P42, ch2672 P15, T). Stat comparisons across worlds therefore need the cap, not just the number (I).

## 3. Candidate additions for the LOTM model (proposals)

- **RPD1. Add per-effect hard comparisons beside the logistic.** For a named effect (a stun, a slow, a mental compulsion), allow "if the target's axis is below the user's, the effect works for a random duration in a stated range"; use the logistic for whole-exchange outcomes. Default and dial: which effects use which, per effect kind.
- **RPD2. Show a baseline row on the sheet.** The ordinary adult and the protagonist's first panel make later numbers legible. Show the reference adult as a label, with the actual notches kept off the card (LCD7 default).
- **RPD3. Threshold rows where the book states a break.** LCD3 already does this for 7, 4 and 2; RP suggests keeping the smooth curve and the break rows as two lists and not folding one into the other.
- **RPD4. Fate scales magnitude.** Make a fate contest return an effect scale (shorter or longer, weaker or stronger) rather than a pass or fail, with luck as an event bias that the player sees through outcomes, not through a roll.
- **RPD5. Scale tags on rows and items.** A tag per row naming the scale it was authored in avoids a silent redefinition when a later tier appears (RPL10).

## 4. Open gaps (RPQ)

- **RPQ1. Win rates.** RP states no probability for a stat gap. If the LOTM model wants a reliability figure, it comes from LOTM text (LCT2: Seq 8 against a trained man) and not from RP.
- **RPQ2. A combat tier gate.** I found no stated rule that a lower rank cannot harm a higher one; the tier list shows information, entry and environment gates (RPF15). A targeted read of promotion trials and cross-rank kills (ch425.1, ch1512, ch1566, ch2914 lines were the leads found) would settle it.
- **RPQ3. Appraisal-percent function.** Coverage is not a clean function of the ratio (RPF7); hidden modifiers (tier, "special" intelligence) are named in the text but their sizes are not.
- **RPQ4. The 1,123 and 7560 figures.** The MTL renders ratios with thousands separators; both may be real large ratios or decimal commas. Check against the Chinese text if either is ever relied on.
- **RPQ5. Crushing onset.** Three data points (ch2523 P14, ch3279 P61, and the early 2-point grip) and no formula (RPF5).
- **RPQ6. Late tiers.** Chapters 3539 to 4143 define tiers beyond what this read covered; read them before drawing any conclusion about the book's top end.

# P12 references: techniques for stories larger than a card

2026-10-14. **PROPOSAL ONLY.** Primary-source survey, no reference game played or implementation benchmark run in this lane. The adaptation column is always **inferred proposal**. Sources were checked through web search/extraction; locators identify the reviewed material, not a claim of reading every talk or paper. No borrowed fiction or dialogue is supplied.

## R0: evidence lens

**Shown**: an example, rule or algorithm directly visible in the cited source. **Stated**: developer/author describes behavior or intention. **Inferred**: our interpretation or adaptation. A demonstrated example inside a paper is shown in that paper, not independently reproduced gameplay. A developer abstract is stated evidence; a roadmap or future-tense paragraph is a plan. Unknown implementation details remain unknown. This survey does not treat a game's premise as a universal generator or infer runtime text generation from procedural encounter selection.

## R19–R22: research and distinct roles

| ID / source | Supported result / reviewed locator | Inferred use and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| R19 **IPOCL narrative planning**, Riedl and Young, JAIR 39 (2010), pp217–268 | **Stated abstract**: causal partial-order planning explicitly explains character intentions; reported evaluation improved comprehension over conventional partial-order plans. [Authors' paper record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3841). | Goal, action preconditions/effects and intention links are useful contract ideas. It is an offline fabula planner under authored domains, not demonstrated fast continual idle planning. Start with bounded local recipes and replanning, not a general search over every world. |
| R20 **Belief and intentionality**, Shirvani et al. (2018) | **Stated / shown model**, pp223 onward: believed-possible character plans can differ from actual possible outcomes and may anticipate other actors. [Combining Intentionality and Belief](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/shirvani2018combining.pdf). | A villain can rationally attempt a plan based on a mistaken report; actual execution still obeys reality. Pin belief basis and retain failure rather than giving NPCs hidden-truth access. No planner implementation port selected. |
| R21 **Declarative optimization drama management**, Nelson et al. (2006) | **Stated abstract**: author supplies plot points, director actions and evaluation; tested search did not scale well on larger Anchorhead stories, while reinforcement learning appeared plausible. [Author's abstract](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/DODM_IEEECGA-abstract.html). | A director can choose opportunities and attention. Our D9 has a narrower weight/cadence remit; adding world interventions would require an explicit actor/source. Do not infer universal performance or surrender NPC agency to an ending optimizer. |
| R22 **Story sifting / Winnow**, Kreminski et al. (AIIDE 2021), pp156–163 | **Shown/stated paper**, introduction/Related Work: authored patterns recognize microstories in simulation traces; Winnow retains partial matches for incremental recognition, with retrospective compilation to Felt. [Winnow paper](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/18903/18903-52-22669-1-2-20211004.pdf). | Recognize a real promise, delay, repayment and reconciliation after the fact. Partial matches can surface a known unresolved thread; they must not force the remaining events. Pattern coverage is authored and missed matches remain normal events. |

## R23: synthesis without collapsing the differences

Simulation produces events; a planner proposes actions toward an actor goal; a grammar assembles admissible task/beat structure; a director selects timing/attention; a sifter recognizes sequences; a renderer describes known events. They can coexist, with separate ownership and evidence. Borrow techniques, not a claim that all reference games already generate every kind of content requested here.

P12 favors a bounded goal/beat grammar plus actual settlement and retrospective recognition first. Free-form LLM worlds, expansive social practices and stronger drama management remain reachable options with their own costs and laws. See [STRUCTURES](STRUCTURES.md), [CONTRACT](CONTRACT.md) and [MORE](MORE.md). This is an inferred design choice, not an accepted roadmap.

## J1–J5: the important differences

1. **J1 Workshop: recover a designed function.** Evidence helps infer what the
   apparatus does and what presently prevents it. Repair, repurpose, preserve and
   salvage are different goals; discovery should help compare them.
2. **J2 Natural site: understand conditions and production.** A spring supplies
   flow or a deposit under an explicit law; a grove has living producers and
   supported harvest cycles. A natural site is not merely a workshop with leaf names.
3. **J3 Preparation follows evidence.** The first visit should usually support
   something useful without requiring the player to guess a hidden packing list.
   Specialized work can justify a return with tools or knowledge.
4. **J4 Crafting closes a useful loop.** A made part changes a function; a prepared
   sample enters a real product; a tending device changes a declared condition.
   Do not reward making something that nobody and no action can use.
5. **J5 Restoration offers bounded outcomes.** Stabilize, test or recover a
   limited use before considering a bigger project. Preserve meaningful choices
   without making every repair an irreversible trap or every site a daily chore.

## AW: abandoned workshop, a concrete proposed journey

### AW1: starting situation and discovery

An original water-driven worksite fits inside an eligible authored/original
location. A sheltered bench is accessible; an installed preparation apparatus
is not operational. Observable wear and water damage explain present condition.
The exact obstruction and parts are frozen under the chosen site definition,
not selected after inspecting the player's inventory or guessing a hypothesis.

Possible leads include a worked fitting encountered on a known route, tool marks
noticed while gathering, or a readable record. These are alternative proposed
routes, not mandatory clue collection. One may locate the place while another
suggests its former purpose. No lead gives a free hidden inventory list.

The first visit can record visible layout, inspect reachable components, gather
a declared loose material or leave. A visible maker mark is an observation;
understanding its history and knowing how to reproduce the machinery are separate.
Not every mark is a puzzle. A record may describe an earlier configuration.

### AW2: a meaningful preparation decision

Inspection should expose known requirements, uncertain conditions and what would
reduce uncertainty. For example, the player can see an exposed failed fitting,
but assessing a protected part may need a tool or a supported sensing method.
Uncertainty is not a hidden random shopping list.

| Intent | Proposed preparation | Why it changes the action |
|---|---|---|
| Survey | Light or an equivalent sense; notebook/measurement means where actually required | Access more observable evidence without operating the apparatus |
| Stabilize | A supported brace, cover or fastening, if that local effect is modeled | Make a declared work area usable without restoring powered operation |
| Test | Compatible tool and a bounded source of work/power | Establish a particular function, rather than activate an unknown entire system |
| Repair | An inspected compatible replacement and actual making/installing means | Restore a named capability; an attractive but ill-fitting part need not work |
| Salvage | Means to detach and carry identified components | Obtain real material while changing what remains installed |

Use only the preparations read by the selected action. Do not require all rows
or repeated travel for trivial objects. Known low-risk repeat preparation can
eventually use the same resolver through recorded methods and chosen routines.

### AW3: crafting, installation and a trial

An example functional requirement might be a fitting of declared size and load
compatibility for the chosen apparatus. These are proposed requirements, not an
existing engine schema. Exact materials matter only where the law reads them.
A supported substitute, bought part, crafted replacement or held power may work.
An ordinary physical repair does not automatically reproduce a named canon device.

Making and installation remain distinct: a completed replacement belongs to its
holder until installed, then occupies a real assembly slot. A worn original is
retained, salvaged or discarded as declared. Repeated installation cannot duplicate
either part. Repair does not secretly convert the replacement into a consumable
unless its purpose/law makes it one.

A bounded trial should answer a concrete question: does the restored apparatus
perform its declared preparation operation on a real test batch? Observe output,
resource use and known problems. A mechanically turning wheel is not proof of
useful grinding, just as a successful repair message is not a working consumer.
Once demonstrated, a method can be recorded with its tested limits. A failed
trial can reveal a local issue and preserve recoverable work; it need not wipe
the entire repair or grant full operating knowledge.

### AW4: outcomes and returning

| Proposed outcome | What is actually gained | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Loose recovery | Exact collected stock and recorded observations | Installed apparatus remains as it was |
| Usable shelter/bench | A supported place service or work capability | Powered mechanism may remain broken |
| Limited restoration | One declared operation with known costs/limits | Other subsystems need not become available |
| Repurposing | A supported different function using the actual parts | Original function may require reassembly |
| Dismantling | Accounted components/materials | Local service is reduced or removed where modeled |

Return shows the saved access, installed parts, remaining stock and observed
condition. A process still needs actual input and supported power. An installed
station is not an infinite-output reward. Basic restoration need not decay
between every session; maintenance is a selected world/site rule with useful
decisions, not default rent on a reward. Where condition changes are not modeled,
do not imply that environmental simulation occurred while absent.

## NG: natural spring and grove, distinct but connectable journeys

### NG1: discovery through conditions

An original site may contain a spring, a grove or both. A spring can be valuable
without plants; a grove need not hide a spring. Only join them where the selected
condition law provides a useful relationship.

Possible leads include a visible mineral trace, a sheltered vegetation pattern,
flow heard from a reachable route, or a dated field note. They establish observed
signs, not automatic healing properties, rare species or supernatural origin.
Different bodies and senses can obtain different evidence through actual means.

First visit: observe accessible conditions, collect a permitted ordinary sample
or harvest, record a landmark and return route, and leave. The approach can be
interesting without danger. A useful natural place need not contain a secret.
Place identity, actual materials, producer state and stock are committed under
the site's rules; guesses or reloads do not choose a more favorable truth.

### NG2: prepare to answer a question or obtain a product

| Intent | Spring example | Grove example |
|---|---|---|
| Observe | Compare accessible flow or visible deposits at a recorded time | Record light/shade, accessible producers and readiness signs |
| Sample | Use a suitable vessel where the law requires one; keep sample location/time | Collect a declared sample while preserving identity/provenance |
| Gather | Collect finite ready material or a supported fluid quantity | Harvest a supported part from an actual producer/patch |
| Prepare material | Filter, dry, separate or otherwise process only where a law models the operation | Dry, grind, preserve or propagate only under a supported law |
| Tend/restore | Clear a bounded obstruction or repair an existing delivery channel | Restore one supported shade/water condition or a cultivation bed |

A vessel's material, residue or sealing matters only when the process reads it.
Do not make every forgotten container ruin a sample. A label records the collector's
claim; actual material identity and custody remain separate. Visual clarity or
unusual vegetation does not certify beneficial properties.

### NG3: spring supply differs from grove production

For the spring, distinguish flowing supply, collected volume, accessible stored
volume and deposited solids if the selected use needs them. Use simple declared
capacities/rates or bounded batches; no full hydrology is necessary. A collection
cannot drain a whole regional aquifer unless that scale is actually modeled.
Processing a fluid into retained solids has declared outputs, residue and cost;
the same feed is not both retained fluid and newly duplicated material.

For the grove, separate producer, ready harvest and collected batch. Picking
leaves, fruit, bark or roots follows that producer's law; do not universally call
harvesting either harmless or destructive. New growth requires a declared cause
and clock. Transplanting or propagating needs means and compatible conditions;
it does not copy unlimited ready stock or automatically recreate the site's field.

Use [ecology](../p8/ECOLOGY.md) for readiness/renewal and
[making rules](../../../../MAKING_RULES.md) for actual products. A site's moisture,
light or local support can affect condition or a newly grown batch, not silently
rewrite an already collected material's intrinsic properties.

### NG4: crafting and restoration loop

A harvested batch should feed a real selected recipe, experiment or material
operation. The outcome can teach something about suitability, but mixing one
successful batch does not automatically identify every hidden property. Comparing
samples needs actual observations; labels and UI estimates are not assays.

A local repair/tending action might restore an existing channel or damaged shade
support. Its product is an installed aid and a declared condition change, not a
generic “restored +20% yield” flag. If that condition supports future production,
the player later observes its actual effect at a valid boundary. No mandatory
waiting a whole season naturally to prove a small transition, and no instant
full regrowth just because a repair finished. Any accelerated proof is labeled.

Possible outcomes include a known gathering place, one useful batch, a viable
propagule, better access or a cultivation condition. Preserving current habitat
may be preferable to restoring an older use. A return can show partial readiness,
a new condition or no change, following the law. No daily-care obligation or
absence punishment is implied. A local grove need not support a larger ecosystem
simulation or reveal nearby undiscovered sites through aggregate statistics.

## WV: how the same journeys differ by world

All rows are original proposals, not franchise facts or default laws for every
world of that family. A selected world may use only some of these affordances.

| Code / family | Workshop | Spring/grove | Distinct preparation or restoration question |
|---|---|---|---|
| WV1 Ordinary frontier | Repair an ordinary preparation apparatus or sheltered work area | Gather useful ordinary material; improve access or a cultivation condition | Is the installed function worth more than portable parts, given actual travel and making needs? |
| WV2 Cultivation/formation | Diagnose substrate, pattern and source separately; restore one supported function | Collect eligible biological/mineral material or work with local energy conditions | Does repair address physical wear, a pattern break, missing source or an incompatible operator? Sensing energy alone answers none universally |
| WV3 Occult urban | Recover controlled workspace/equipment; separate containment from activation | Sample ordinary or law-supported unusual material without certifying origin | Which tests can be performed safely with known means, and does cleaning remove useful evidence or alter a supported preparation? |

| WV5 Living-material/ecological | Restore a nursery's actual shade/water/control function | Work with viable cultures or eligible producers and harvest cycles | Is the structure productive only with a living substrate, and can a supported sample survive relocation? |

| WV7 Harsh frontier | Recover practical shelter or repair a limited machine/service | Choose supported gathering and access under exposure/transport conditions | Is a smaller useful collection preferable to a larger haul? Food/hunger/lethality policies remain separately selected |

## X: deeper interactions that could change the design

| Code | Interaction | Consequence / bounded disposition |
|---|---|---|
| X1 Coupled sites | Workshop channel and nearby grove could share a modeled condition | Optional later join: using or restoring one changes the other only through an explicit shared owner; do not require this coupling for either first slice |
| X2 Restoration versus removal | Taking a part/producer can affect local future use | Show known dependency or honest uncertainty; do not secretly apply an unmodeled collapse |
| X3 Historical versus current optimum | Old drainage served the workshop while present wet conditions support growth | Repurpose or preserve can be valid; no universal perfect-restoration ending |
| X4 Input versus catalyst/tool | A replacement remains installed; a filter or catalyst may be reusable, depleted or consumed | Declare custody, wear/charge and return semantics; no duplicate grant when uninstalling |
| X5 Source condition versus batch truth | Wetness/local charge can differ from intrinsic composition | Save what is collected and what can subsequently change; moving an item neither upgrades nor erases its identity |
| X6 Diagnostic versus production work | A test may consume a sample, produce residue or establish a narrow fact | State costs before commit and keep useful evidence; a test is not a free full assay or infinite training reward |
| X7 Installed capability versus skill | An apparatus enables an operation the player could not perform with current tools | It does not grant operator mastery; supported expertise may still improve execution, recovery and scaling |
| X8 Repair versus reproduction | Restoring an existing object differs from constructing one from raw inputs | Both need supported means; repair should not trivially mint the complete blueprint or make all future construction obsolete |
| X9 Exploration versus preparation errands | Requiring repeated home trips can create empty friction | First visit supports modest value; known common preparation can be batched. Real travel/cargo choices remain where they matter |
| X10 Body and sensing | Hands, locomotion, perception, tool use and remote action may differ | Read actual means; allow supported alternative approaches without a compulsory humanoid kit |
| X11 Powers and scale | A strong power may bypass labor or supply a source | Honor its declared scope and paid effects; lifting an apparatus is not knowing how to run it, and control is not automatically fine calibration |
| X12 Hypothesis versus revealed truth | A plausible interpretation can guide an experiment | The site never chooses the correct explanation after seeing the player's answer; labels remain tentative until evidence supports them |
| X13 Disclosure side channels | A counter, recipe match, sort order or quote can expose unknown suitability | Project all outputs, not just prose. A preview is neither a hidden assay nor a consuming draw |
| X14 Seasons versus stock | A changed environment can affect future readiness without refilling historical salvage | Independent transition causes; no world calendar rewrite or reward refresh on return |
| X15 Failure and recovery | Partial repairs and imperfect batches can retain useful work | Show actual damage, remaining inputs and next supported options; neither full reset nor magical free refund is universal |
| X16 Paused or absent play | An unfinished trial, tending job or natural transition may await the player | Use accepted clock/pause policies, selected routines and actual stop reasons; no silent caretaking debt |
| X17 Portability | Tools/materials may travel while local water, energy or growing conditions do not | Keep acquisition and apply directional compatibility; a cross-world adaptation is a separate operation, not automatic loss or universal success |
| X18 Long-term relevance | A low-power station or ordinary patch can become outclassed | Retain its coherent use, known route and history. Do not rescale its truth to player level; later usefulness can come from actual new capabilities |
| X19 Ownership and secrecy | Repairing or discovering does not prove ownership; known signs do not prove secret affiliation | Preserve existing access and discovery rules without adding mandatory legal or NPC simulation |
| X20 Clean benchmark | A repair/condition change can be compared against its own prior state | Record comparable observations and inputs; different weather or batches may explain the change, so do not falsely attribute every improvement to restoration |

These are selected interactions to consider, not twenty new systems to build.
No unmodeled effect may be used as a penalty. Optional failure modes and later
coupling remain separate from the small loop's required correctness.

## Evidence, legibility and proposed order

**ACCOUNT FOR NOW:** a clear useful consumer, observed preparation requirements,
truth versus knowledge, ownership/installation/stock conservation, partial work
and persistent return state. Use the existing expedition, crafting, material,
knowledge and source owners; propose no duplicate inventory, RNG or clock.

The two examples are alternative experiments, not a commitment to implement both.
Choose whichever has the smallest actual useful consumer when implementation is
authorized. A gathering/sample journey may require fewer station dependencies;
a workshop may make restoration more visible if one meaningful apparatus action
already exists. Inspect that consumer then, rather than call either universally
cheaper. A coupled workshop/grove and broad world variants are later extensions.

At **1366×768**, existing place/Focus and notebook/reference homes should show
known conditions, purpose, supported actions and preparation gaps. Use progressive
disclosure for evidence and terms; retain the log, Back/Esc and actual retreat.
Represent certainty explicitly. Do not list unexplored stock, secret causes or
future renewal yield merely to populate a panel. [UI rules](../../../../UI_RULES.md)
apply; no UI, mobile layout or new navigation framework is selected here.

Future proof would distinguish discovery, preparation/making, actual installation
or collection, useful trial/product and changed return state. Check equivalent
step/bulk transitions, stock/part conservation, exact pending/final save/reload/import,
next draw continuity, unknown projections and withdrawal. Test fixtures are marked
SOURCE/DEV SEEDED; rebuilt browser behavior and human acceptance of curiosity,
clarity, friction and payoff remain separate. No test count proves atmosphere.

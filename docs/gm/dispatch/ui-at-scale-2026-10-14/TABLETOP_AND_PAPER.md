## Scope and direction

These are INFERRED considerations, not selected specs or accepted mechanics.
The target is a text-first desk at **1366×768 and 1920×1080**: keep local actions
and current work visible, make a body or world readable without hunting through
unrelated systems, and keep deep records reachable. Mobile, print export,
new gameplay and literal imitation of commercial artwork are deferred.

Authority remains [USER_VISION](../../../USER_VISION.md),
[PROJECT](../../../../PROJECT.md), the
[root palette](../../../UI_AT_SCALE_CONSIDERATIONS_2026-10-14.md),
[Panel kit](../../../PANEL_KIT.md) and the existing thematic/density owners.
The root's OI examples were MEMORY; evidence here applies to the specific source
and edition named, not to every version or every live digital layout.

**F1. A sheet is a routing system.** Stable groups and repeated columns help more
than a demand that every statistic fit on one screen. Pathfinder's four pages
and D&D 2024's active spells on page two qualify OI13's front/back shorthand.

**F2. Physical state can explain a rule.** Slots, covered costs, spent markers
and separate stock/production tracks make the current legal action legible.
Transfer that clarity to text rows and receipts, not compulsory dragging.

**F3. Paper separates capture from retrieval.** A dated log captures events;
indexes, account pages and subject collections retrieve them. The game can
generate links and carry tasks forward without asking the player to copy text.

## Character sheets and playbooks

The physical front/back analogy becomes **Play / Build / Record / Reference**
views of the same entity. Keep the current body and world named in every view.
Put an often-used spell or ability on Play even if its paper source puts it on
the reverse. A shared template supplies stable homes; a playbook supplies only
the relevant class/body/world fields. Class is not the same thing as body.

## Contradictions and transfer limits

| Code | Useful claim | Counterweight and chosen proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Paper front/back separates frequent play from slower reference. | D&D 2024 puts active spells on page two; PF2e has four pages. Use frequency and present activity, not literal page position, to choose the front. |
| C2 | Playbooks reduce unrelated choices and support flavor. | Mothership uses a shared class-aware profile; GURPS supports broad trait lists. Default to a common skeleton, allow inserts and custom pins instead of one rigid template per class. |
| C3 | Icons compress and compound rules. | Icon-heavy notation requires learning. Scythe's design blog praises it; this lane did not measure newcomer readability. Keep words, text-only display and help; do not infer accessibility from consistency. |
| C4 | Clocks make threats and projects readable. | Blades says clocks reflect fiction and are unnecessary for simple obstacles. A visible authoritative clock can leak hidden state or imply certainty. Use causal, known-state marks only where they answer a real question. |
| C5 | Migration prunes stale intent. | Manual rewriting is the paper practice, but idle play may continue through absence. Carry references, offer review and preserve history; do not expire work for failure to check in. |
| C6 | A binder/notebook could explain the whole UI in fiction. | MA2 is optional. A player collection or meta note is not necessarily character knowledge. Retain scope/knowledge labels and a plain interface option. |
| C7 | Phase cards help explain processes. | Scythe's ongoing action selection and continuous idle jobs do not need imported rounds. Show actual job stages, blockers and timing; do not add turn bookkeeping for presentation. |
| C8 | Rulebook references and icons look stable. | Arkham's current reference changed; TI v1.2 and Dewey Edition 22 are historical artifacts. Cite edition, distinguish inherited rules from new content, and avoid silently migrating meanings. |

## H1. Adoption is a local decision

**What happened, stated:** Everett Rogers's *Diffusion of Innovations* synthesized diffusion research, beginning in 1962. Its adopter categories classify relative adoption timing: innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority and laggards. The framework distinguishes knowledge, persuasion, decision, implementation and confirmation. Dearing's own research synthesis discusses perceived advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability and observability, along with opinion leaders and adaptation. [Rogers's official publisher record for the fourth edition](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Diffusion-of-Innovations-4th-Edition/Everett-M-Rogers/9781451602470), indexed publication history; [fifth-edition publisher excerpt](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Diffusion-of-Innovations-5th-Edition/Everett-M-Rogers/9780743222099), body excerpt on local compatibility and social networks; [Dearing, “Applying Diffusion of Innovation Theory to Intervention Development,” 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2957672/), abstract/excerpt; [Scholarly overview of the categories and stages, 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3180547/), indexed text. Full Rogers editions were not read in this lane.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** hearing about an invention, deciding it is useful and maintaining its use are different events. A visible success and a trusted local demonstrator can matter more than an inventor's announcement. A cautious recipient may have a different task, smaller budget or sensible concern about compatibility.

**YAIRPG consideration:** give a selected recipient a reason to adopt and a practical route: trial a borrowed tool, consult a trusted worker, commission a batch or observe a neighbor. Record what actually changed in their work. A sect can adopt medicine quickly while retaining an older transport method. Never assign an NPC a permanent “laggard” personality merely from late adoption, or force Rogers's familiar percentages onto every population. Keep local fit separate from prestige.

## H2. An S-curve needs a defined quantity

**What happened, stated:** diffusion research often describes cumulative adoption as initially slow, then accelerating through social contact, then slowing toward its reachable population. Dearing presents this generalized cumulative curve. The curve counts adopters; it does not measure device performance or personal research progress. [Dearing, 2009, Figure 1 and diffusion discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2957672/), abstract/excerpt; full-body fetch blocked by a browser challenge.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** growing contact can accelerate uptake, while a shrinking pool of interested or reachable recipients can slow it. Supply constraints, replacement technologies or changed demand can interrupt that pattern. A sigmoid fitted afterward does not identify its cause by itself. A performance S-curve, such as precision improving toward a process limit, would be a different model and needs separate evidence.

**YAIRPG consideration:** define the denominator before offering “regional adoption”: eligible workshops using this method, households reached, or recipients maintaining a service. Show observed counts and named constraints where the actor has means to know them. Keep a failed trial, temporary use and continued operation distinct. A new supply route can restart uptake; a superior substitute can reduce current use even while historical cumulative adopters only increase. Do not make every project wait through a compulsory sigmoid.

## H3. Independent discovery and public credit are separate

**What happened, stated:** Robert K. Merton studied multiple independent discoveries in his 1961 “Singletons and Multiples in Scientific Discovery.” A concrete case is Darwin and Wallace's independent formulations of natural selection, presented together at the Linnean Society on 1 July 1858. [Linnean Society, Wallace history](https://www.linnean.org/the-society/history-of-science/alfred-russel-wallace), institution excerpt; [Darwin and Wallace's 1858 publication, scholarly edited transcription](https://darwin-online.org.uk/converted/published/1858_species_F350.html), indexed primary text. Merton's broader argument is indexed in [a scholarly discussion of innovation opportunities](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13731-017-0063-2); his full paper was not read.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** common questions, accessible observations and shared intellectual tools can make similar results reachable to several people. Priority arrangements, publication and correspondence govern recognition. Similarity alone is not evidence of theft; simultaneous discovery does not prove that every breakthrough was inevitable.

**YAIRPG consideration:** an independently equipped rival can reach the same function without receiving the player's notebook. Track discovery evidence, exposure and public attribution separately. The player can collaborate, improve the method, specialize in an application, publish or keep working privately after losing priority. Being first may change a title or contract, but need not award an exclusive permanent lock on the technology.

## H4. The adjacent possible expands through usable joins

**What happened, stated:** Stuart Kauffman's adjacent possible describes possibilities near what already exists. Tria, Loreto, Servedio and Strogatz modeled an expanding possibility space in 2014 and tested novelty patterns against text, tagging, Wikipedia editing and music-listening datasets. Their distinction between something new to an actor and something new to the world is especially useful. [“The dynamics of correlated novelties,” *Scientific Reports* 4, 5890](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep05890), body, abstract and introduction.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** encountering or making one thing exposes related opportunities. This is a conceptual model and a statistical research program, not a proven inventory of every future invention. The paper's datasets do not prove that an inventor can never leap beyond the nearest known step.

**YAIRPG consideration:** a usable lens, clean vessel or recording method can reveal a new question and enable several experiments, including an unexpected application. “Adjacent” should depend on held means: a visiting expert, trade, powers or a complete archive can change what is reachable immediately. Allow distant ambitions to produce reachable investigations. Do not turn TS4's illustrative shape into an absolute era wall. Partial discoveries can connect a previously unrelated craft to the current problem.

## H5. General-purpose technologies create complementary projects

**What happened, stated:** Bresnahan and Trajtenberg's 1992 working paper, published in 1995, characterized general-purpose technologies by widespread downstream use, improvement potential and innovation complementarities. Steam power, electric motors and computing are their examples. Their model also identifies coordination difficulties between a technology's producers and users. [NBER working paper 4148, DOI 10.3386/w4148](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4148), indexed abstract; page and PDF full-body fetches blocked.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** improving a shared enabling technology can make inventions in several fields more valuable. Those gains still require complementary designs, skills and organization. A better motor is not automatically a working pump, textile factory and transport network.

**YAIRPG consideration:** one qualified power, measurement or computation advance can open multiple useful applications. Make the effect visible through actual consumers, such as steadier furnace control and a new powered workshop tool. NPC specialists can develop applications the player did not choose. Let stronger means genuinely remove the shared bottleneck; remaining application work should exist for a concrete reason. This is a way to deepen connected pursuits, not justification for one global civilization multiplier.

## H6. Copying fails when the record omits an operative detail

**What happened, stated:** in Collins's account of his TEA-laser research, groups working from published descriptions produced devices that would not lase, while visits to working laboratories transferred missing practical knowledge. One example involved the physical placement of a component affecting the apparatus's operation. [Collins interviewed in *Physics Today*, “Q&A: Harry Collins on acquiring and using scientific knowledge”](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/q-a-harry-collins-on-acquiring-and-using-scientific-knowledge), body, “What did you learn?” His original study is [“The TEA Set: Tacit Knowledge and Scientific Networks,” 1974](https://doi.org/10.1177/030631277400400203), bibliographic record checked; full original paper unverified.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** a diagram can omit arrangement, judgment, calibration and troubleshooting that an experienced person treats as obvious. Some gaps become explicit once identified; tacit knowledge is not a mystical resource that can never be written down. A correct description can also fail because the recipient lacks tools or suitable inputs, a separate cause.

**YAIRPG consideration:** attach the failed reproduction to one specific missing detail, then offer observation, diagnosis, practice, a fuller record, commissioning or a capable expert. Successful teaching should let another actor perform the operation. Avoid an automatic “all copies are worse” tax. A genuine complete-copy or mastery power may solve the gap directly. A wrong-looking apparatus can still function; surface resemblance alone is not evidence.

## H7. Interchangeability is a production achievement

**What happened, stated:** John H. Hall's Harpers Ferry work required years of workshop tooling and precision machinery. His 1822 letter claimed uniform manufacture; an 1832 official assessment praised its uniformity. The museum record for Colt likewise connects interchangeable manufacture to division of labor, specialized tools and quality control. [National Park Service, “John H Hall”](https://www.nps.gov/hafe/learn/historyculture/john-h-hall.htm), body with primary-letter extracts; [Smithsonian National Museum of American History, “Samuel Colt...and sewing machines?”](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/samuel-coltand-sewing-machines-part-1), institution excerpt. This lane does not establish a single inventor of interchangeability.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** a shared nominal design is insufficient. Makers need an agreed interface, tolerances, process control and checks that let independently produced parts fit. Standards coordinate makers; interchangeability proves a selected production result. Uniform parts can simplify repair without being the best choice for every unique object.

**YAIRPG consideration:** prove one cross-maker replacement on an actual device. A gauge or certified interface can open a spare-part supplier, while a hand-fitted masterpiece remains useful. Competing standards can support adapters, dual production or another local standard. Buying a drawing should not instantly convert every shop, but genuine fabrication means can produce conforming parts without a forced bootstrap chain.

## Limits

These models offer distinct questions: who adopts, how counts change, who discovers, which possibilities open, which shared capability has downstream uses, what a record omits, and what makes parts fit. They do not establish historical inevitability, NPC percentages, universal decline, compulsory secrecy or YAIRPG balance.

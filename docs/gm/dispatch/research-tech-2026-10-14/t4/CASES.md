# Prizes, suppression, contact and domestication

2026-10-14. T4 historical research, H19-H26. Original considerations, not implementation instructions. The [technology owner](../../../../TECHNOLOGY_AND_PROGRESSION.md) retains TW/SO/KL/UE/TE/RF definitions and accepted direction. Evidence labels follow [DIFFUSION.md](DIFFUSION.md).

“Uplift” is the game's category. Historical cases below concern selective contact and transfer among already capable societies, not outsiders giving passive recipients a single civilization level. Costs, conflict, acceptance and local improvement follow particular actors and circumstances.

## H19. The Longitude reward distinguished performance from reproduction

**What happened, stated:** Britain's 1714 Longitude Act offered rewards for a practical solution to determining longitude at sea. Harrison developed successive timekeepers and underwent trials. The Greenwich account describes payments supporting further work, performance disputes and the 1765 recommendation tying part of the reward to demonstrating principles and another part to other makers producing comparable timekeepers. Expensive initial instruments spread slowly until later makers simplified production. [Royal Museums Greenwich, “Longitude found: the story of Harrison's timekeepers”](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/time/harrisons-clocks-longitude-problem), body, trial/results and reward sections.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** a reward can focus effort on a publicly important problem and support rival approaches. Demonstration, reproducibility, disclosure and widespread affordability answer different questions. Judges and inventors can dispute which evidence meets the promised terms. This history is more complicated than one inventor receiving one prize instantly on success.

**YAIRPG consideration:** an optional bounty names a functional outcome and test context. A patron can pay for stages, with published terms for the authored contract. A maker can win a performance demonstration without yet supplying cheap copies. Rivals can offer a different method; collaboration and selling an application remain useful after someone receives the headline reward. Keep sponsor acceptance distinct from actual performance, without requiring prizes for ordinary research or imposing a universal once-only research limit.

## H20. Guild resistance varied by guild and innovation

**What happened, stated:** Ogilvie's institutional-history research cites a Wildberg worsted-weavers' guild account book as evidence of opposition to new cloth varieties. Pfister's study of engine looms in European silk-ribbon production acknowledges guild opposition under particular circumstances while warning against treating guild abolition and industrialization as proof that every guild blocked innovation. [Ogilvie, “Institutions and Economic Development in Early Modern Central Europe”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/transactions-of-the-royal-historical-society/article/abs/institutions-and-economic-development-in-early-modern-central-europe/11863D4FBD8D386FE2F0F20D41DAB449), scholarly indexed excerpt and references; [Pfister, “Craft Guilds and Technological Change,” 2008, pp. 172-198](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511496738.007), publisher summary body checked. The detailed Wildberg account and full ribbon-loom chapter were not read.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** members can defend income, entry privileges, established production or local bargaining power. Those interests differ across techniques and people; an institution's name does not determine its response. A training network can also supply the expertise needed to adopt a method. This is a historically debated issue, not a universal “guilds killed progress” rule.

**YAIRPG consideration:** name who loses which income or privilege and what authority they hold. A guild might oppose a particular outsider's sale while buying the same method for members. Negotiating training, membership, compensation, another production site or publication gives alternatives; powerful means can bypass a genuine local obstacle. Let supporting artisans become partners rather than making the whole profession a compulsory enemy faction.

## H21. State suppression can target movement or use without stopping invention

**What happened, stated:** Britain restricted export of textile machinery and emigration by people with relevant expertise. The National Park Service describes these restrictions as barriers American manufacturers faced. Separately, Britain's 1721 legislation restricted wearing certain cotton textiles to protect domestic wool production. [National Park Service, “Early American Manufacturing”](https://www.nps.gov/lowe/learn/photosmultimedia/early_american.htm), institutional text; [UK Parliament, “The growth of textile manufacturing”](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/tradeindustry/industrycommunity/overview/textileindustries/), institution excerpt. Original statutes, exact exemptions and enforcement rates were not checked. The cotton restrictions were on use/trade, not proof that cotton-working knowledge vanished.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** a state can protect incumbent employment, revenue or strategic advantage by controlling machinery, skilled migration, markets or permitted uses. Technical feasibility, legality and enforcement are different boundaries. A ban may divert activity or incentivize substitutes; its existence does not prove complete suppression.

**YAIRPG consideration:** distinguish possessing a method, importing a device, practicing it and selling its product. Make enforcement depend on actual observers and authority. Negotiation, licensed transfer, local adaptation, another buyer, travel or covert transfer can be fitting routes. Ending a restriction need not restore missing tools or trained people instantly. No universal state hostility follows from technological progress.

## H22. Slater transferred expertise into an existing local effort

**What happened, stated:** Samuel Slater arrived in America in 1789 after working in English textile production. He joined Moses Brown's existing effort and helped establish successful mechanized cotton spinning in Pawtucket in 1790. Subsequent workers learned the methods. The NPS account also records child labor in the early mill. [National Park Service, “Early American Manufacturing”](https://www.nps.gov/lowe/learn/photosmultimedia/early_american.htm), institution text; [NPS, “Samuel Slater”](https://home.nps.gov/blrv/learn/historyculture/samuel-slater.htm), institution text. This is not proof that one outsider invented all American manufacturing.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** practiced knowledge traveled through a person, then combined with local funding, facilities and collaborators. Repeated transfer to other workers mattered beyond the first working apparatus. An output increase and its effects on labor are separate consequences.

**YAIRPG consideration:** an outsider can complete a recipient's stalled project through one relevant expertise contribution. The resulting workshop belongs to an actual ownership arrangement; native partners have their own skills and ambitions. Teaching can make it reproducible beyond the visitor's presence. A recipient may improve the method, while changed work opportunities create both support and conflict. Keep modern player familiarity distinct from the character's carried practical knowledge.

## H23. Japan combined foreign expertise with local institutions

**What happened, stated:** the National Diet Library describes foreign engineers contributing to Japanese state factories, including Verny's work at Yokosuka begun before the Meiji Restoration and continued afterward. It identifies the Ministry of Engineering, established in 1870, as coordinating government enterprises and hired experts. JICA's institutional history describes engineering education that adapted imported teaching and developed Japanese technical capacity. [National Diet Library, “Construction of large scale government operated factories”](https://www.ndl.go.jp/france/en/part1/s2_1.html), institution excerpt; [JICA, “Utilize Japan's experience in developing engineering education”](https://www.jica.go.jp/english/activities/schemes/tech_pro/projects/activities/activities_50.html), institutional excerpt. These sources support the transfer channels, not a complete causal explanation of Japanese industrialization.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** hired expertise, infrastructure and training can turn temporary access into continuing local capability. The receiving state selected and organized the transfer. Imported equipment, imported expertise and a locally staffed institution are different outcomes.

**YAIRPG consideration:** a kingdom or sect can commission an installation, recruit a visiting engineer and establish instruction as distinct projects. A local graduate can maintain or improve the result after the adviser leaves. Outsiders need not control the institution, and buying one finished machine need not imply a whole trained workforce. Smaller workshop or school versions can provide the same pattern without launching a national simulation.

## H24. Translation is co-production, not a transparent pipe

**What happened, stated:** the Library of Congress describes an 1847 edition of *Yuan rong jiao yi*, a geometry work dictated by Matteo Ricci to Li Zhizao in 1608 and first printed in 1614. Li rendered it into acceptable Chinese; it later circulated in collections and reprints. Its source and presentation included European astronomical and religious ideas. [Library of Congress and National Central Library collection record, *Yuan rong jiao yi*](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021666341), collection description text. The historical volume's pages and translation fidelity were not independently analyzed.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** local scholarly collaboration supplies vocabulary, exposition and routes to readership. What crosses can include a method, terminology, authority claim and worldview; recipients can accept them separately. Translation may change how a problem is framed without making all propositions true in another setting.

**YAIRPG consideration:** a translator or local scholar can turn a foreign manual into usable instruction and test its application. Credit both the source and the adapting actor. A world-law mismatch, obsolete assumption and language problem should have different diagnoses. Publishing a translated edition can open local study or commissions while leaving unsupported claims as questions. Do not force disbelief when an actual means gives complete understanding.

## H25. Agriculture has several regional histories and several milestones

**What happened, stated:** cultivation and domestication arose through different regional histories, not one dated global agricultural unlock. Selected research anchors below concern distinct evidence. Dates are approximate. **cal BP** means calibrated years before 1950, not years before 2026; simple BCE equivalents below subtract 1950 and are rough aids, not new dating analyses.

| Case | Approximate anchor and what it establishes | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Eastern Mediterranean | Initial steps toward crop cultivation and herd management by about 12,000 cal BP, roughly 10,000 BCE; management can precede recognizable domesticated morphology by at least 1,000 years | [Zeder, *PNAS*, 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2575338/), scholarly abstract/excerpt; full body blocked, so no exact crop-by-crop date certified |
| Cishan, northern China | Common-millet remains from storage pits dated approximately 10,300-8,700 cal BP, roughly 8,350-6,750 BCE | [Lu et al., “Earliest domestication of common millet...,” *PNAS*, 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2678631/), publisher abstract/excerpt; this study interprets the material as evidence of early millet agriculture |
| Maize, southern Mexico | A genetic study inferred a single origin about 9,000 years ago, roughly 7,000 BCE | [Matsuoka et al., “A single domestication for maize...,” *PNAS*, 2002](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC122905/), publisher abstract/excerpt; inferred genetic date, not a directly dated first farm |

**Mechanism, interpretation:** tending, cultivating, selecting traits and depending principally on domesticated food are different stages that may overlap. Archaeological plant remains, morphology and genetic estimates answer different questions. The table is a bounded selection, not a complete world chronology or proof that a named site's first find is the first ever domestication. Exact origin locations and dates remain research questions in several species.

**YAIRPG consideration:** start from the local crop, ecology, season, seed stock and actual practice. A new irrigation or selection method can help an existing farmer without requiring them to “invent agriculture.” A season can yield useful evidence while longer breeding continues. Imported seed may need local trials; a supported plant-growth power can shorten real constraints. Farming should offer ingredients, trade, useful meal benefits and a chosen livelihood. It creates no hunger death or forced farming requirement for comfortable ordinary play.

## H26. Domestication differs from taming and from later breeding

**What happened, stated:** the Bonn-Oberkassel burial includes a domestic dog alongside humans about 14,000 years ago, before the early agricultural cases above. The museum calls it one of the oldest domestic dogs, not a precise record of the first domestication event. Zeder distinguishes evidence of herd management from later morphological changes in livestock. [LVR-LandesMuseum Bonn, “Projekt Oberkassel”](https://landesmuseum-bonn.lvr.de/de/forschung/projekte/projekt_oberkassel.html), German museum excerpt; [Zeder, 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2575338/), scholarly abstract/excerpt. Exact dog origin, number of origins and earliest domestication date are not settled by this example and were not verified here.

**Mechanism, interpretation:** training one individual, managing reproduction in a population, selecting a trait and establishing a stable domestic lineage are different operations. Population changes require a carrier through generations; they need not be conscious plans from the outset. A long regional domestication history does not prove every later useful breeding improvement needs millennia.

**YAIRPG consideration:** an animal-handling route can gain a useful trained companion within one life while a breeding program develops a locally supported lineage. Offspring inherit only actual supported traits and circumstances, not every learned command or the keeper's expertise. Keep animal welfare, ecological suitability and local ownership as authored consequences where relevant, rather than a universal domestication checklist. Exceptional transformation or breeding powers can achieve the changes their rules support; elapsed time should not substitute for a mechanism.

## Limits of the comparison

No universal modernization order follows from these cases. A source world can have different physics, powerful native institutions, an unusual body, or a genuine exceptional means. Select the historical mechanism that improves its playable causal chain, qualify it against canon and leave other routes reachable. The evidence does not establish prices, timers, success rates or a default institutional response for YAIRPG.

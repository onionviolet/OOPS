## Findings that change the design

**F1.** Research supports separating demonstrated performance, explicit explanation, records and supported execution. It does not establish TK as a universal scientific scale or a fixed teaching-fidelity loss. K1/K2/K16 extend [T4 H6](../t4/DIFFUSION.md#h6-copying-fails-when-the-record-omits-an-operative-detail) by making one operative detail transferable and useful.

**F2.** Spacing and retrieval practice have broad synthesis evidence. The classroom retrieval synthesis reports g = .499 across 222 studies; effects depend on feedback, test/material alignment and comparison activity. Context effects and interference also depend on retrieval conditions. None justifies mandatory forgetting, daily review or away-from-workshop penalties. See K7-K11 and K17 in [findings](FINDINGS.md).

**F3.** The strongest corrections to a simple “novice versus expert” palette concern Dreyfus stages, deliberate-practice definitions and Dunning-Kruger explanations. Stronger chess expertise can reduce fixation; practice variance estimates do not measure causal shares of one person's skill; the Dunning-Kruger statistical debate remains live. Incubation has a small pooled benefit, not a guaranteed unconscious answer. See K3/K5/K6/K12-K15 in [findings](FINDINGS.md).

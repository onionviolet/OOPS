[Skills](../../../../SKILLS_AND_TALENTS.md#variation-axes) distinguishes competence, aptitude, faculty and social status; [means M1-M6](../../../../worlds/SKILL_MECHANISMS_AND_MEANS_2026-10-13.md) separates personal learning from supported execution. This lane extends them without selecting implementation.

**Body** below means the relevant source sections were readable and checked, not that every page was read. **Abstract** means the original paper's abstract was verified through its publisher, PubMed or university repository. **Indexed excerpt** means searchable source text was available but a full body was not established. **Record** verifies attribution and publication, not all arguments in a book. Evidence is a bounded narrative review, not an exhaustive systematic search or fresh statistical reanalysis. No Wikipedia or popular bias catalogue is used as the evidence base.

Reported standardized differences describe group comparisons in those studies. Explained variance describes differences between measured participants, not the fraction of one person's skill caused by practice. Relative percentages are not percentage-point changes. None is a YAIRPG probability, XP rate or timer.

## K1. Tacit knowledge, Polanyi

**Known/stated:** Polanyi argues that knowing includes skills and judgments that cannot be fully captured in an actor's explicit account. His *The Tacit Dimension* dates to 1966; the Chicago edition is a 2009 reissue. His 1967 paper develops tacit knowing through meaning-making, rather than a simple hidden stock of facts. [Chicago book record](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/T/bo6035368), record; [Polanyi, “Sense-Giving and Sense-Reading,” 1967](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031819100001509), abstract and bibliographic body.

**Contested/limited:** this is a philosophical account, not an experimentally calibrated teaching-loss percentage. Difficulty articulating, an omitted instruction and a bodily skill are different gaps. T4 H6's Collins example concerns practical transfer and does not establish that all such knowledge is permanently unwritable.

**Proposal:** K1 makes one demonstrated sensory cue teachable through observation or a better annotation. Links: SC1, TK4/TK5, MS2/MS4/MS6, CD9, H6; details in [mechanics](MECHANICS.md#k1-operative-cue).

## K2. Curse of knowledge

**Known:** better-informed participants can struggle to reproduce less-informed judgments even when ignoring their extra information would help them. Camerer, Loewenstein and Weber's economic experiments found market conditions reduced their measured curse by roughly 50%, without eliminating it. [Original 1989 paper](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/261651), abstract; [author's CMU copy](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/CurseknowledgeEconSet.pdf), indexed original text.

**Contested/limited:** the percentage is a comparison within those economic tasks, not a universal teaching effect size. Having extra information is not identical to being a craft expert. The claim that every expert skips steps, or that patience alone removes the bias, is an adaptation to test, not demonstrated here. No broad replication-rate estimate was verified.

**Proposal:** K2 lets a learner's question expose a specific skipped step, with successful execution checking the revised explanation. Links: SC2, SC1, TK5, MO4, SO8, H6.

## K3. Einstellung effect

**Known:** a familiar workable solution can block discovery of a better one. Chess eye-tracking found attention remained on features of the familiar solution even while players reported seeking alternatives. [Bilalić, McLeod and Gobet, 2008, *Cognition*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18565505/), abstract. Their companion experiments also found stronger experts were less susceptible. [2008, *Cognitive Psychology*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17418112/), abstract; [Oxford repository](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3Ab3f70131-21d0-433b-8a22-9ccf4c895be9), abstract.

**Contested/limited:** task-specific fixation does not imply mastery generally makes people rigid. The companion paper's comparison to players three skill standard deviations lower is not a general Cohen's d or a three-SD reduction in intelligence. Broad real-work magnitudes and modern replication coverage were not established here.

**Proposal:** K3 retains the familiar route and optionally presents evidence for another. Links: SC3, RV6, RF1, KD5, MO4; no automatic expert penalty.

## K4. Functional fixedness

**Known:** conventional uses can constrain which object features a problem solver considers. McCaffrey's generic-parts intervention encouraged descriptions that do not embed the usual use; trained participants solved, on average, 67% more experimental problems than controls. [McCaffrey, 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22318998/), abstract.

**Contested/limited:** this is a relative increase on a selected problem set, not 67 percentage points or a general creativity multiplier. A short intervention study does not establish enduring transfer to all inventions. No independent replication synthesis of this particular intervention was verified. Physical affordances still constrain valid uses.

**Proposal:** K4 offers a property-focused view of a known object, then an actually supported alternative use. Links: SC4, PA4, PI1/PI6, PS1/PS2, KD5.

## K5. Dreyfus model of skill acquisition

**Known/stated:** the familiar five-stage formulation moves from novice through advanced beginner, competent and proficient to expert, with increasing situational judgment. [University of Michigan-hosted model sheet](https://websites.umich.edu/~mrother/KATA_Files/Dreyfus.pdf), body. This verifies the formulation, not its predictive validity or the contents of the original 1980 report.

**Contested/limited:** Peña's critical review questions empirical support, stage boundaries and an account of expertise dominated by intuition; analytical reasoning remains relevant. [Peña, 2010](https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v15i0.4846), indexed sections; [PMC text record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2887319/), indexed excerpt only after body challenge. The review itself takes a critical position, rather than establishing unanimous rejection. A universal sequence or stage-transition effect size is not verified.

**Proposal:** K5 selects descriptions from demonstrated task competence, without five compulsory ranks. Links: SC1/SC7, TK, MS, MU1, skills growth G7 and meaning L1.

## K6. Deliberate practice and critiques

**Known:** structured practice relates to performance. Macnamara et al.'s 2014 meta-analysis reports explained variance of 26% in games, 21% in music, 18% in sport, 4% in education and under 1% in professions. [Paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24986855/), abstract. Ericsson and Harwell defend a narrower definition emphasizing individualized practice, appropriate feedback and reproducible expert performance; their selected reanalysis reports 29%, or 61% after attenuation correction. [2019 response](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02396/full), body.

**Contested:** definitions, inclusion rules, retrospective estimates, range restriction and measurement corrections change the answer. Macnamara and Maitra's 39-violinist replication found practice differences, but no significant best-versus-good separation; practice-alone variance was 26%, not the original comparison's 48%. [2019 study](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190327), [university paper copy](https://artscimedia.case.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/141/2019/10/03143050/Macnamara-Maitra-2019.pdf), body, Tables 1 and discussion. Neither comparison estimates a causal upper limit on an individual's learning.

**Proposal:** K6 targets one diagnosed difficulty during useful practice, without a universal hours-to-mastery rule. Links: SC1/SC9, TK5, RV6, MI06, skills G1/G20.

## K7. Forgetting curve

**Known:** retention can decline with delay; learning can remain detectable through reduced relearning effort. Murre and Dros replicated Ebbinghaus's savings experiment using one participant, about 70 hours of work and nonsense-syllable lists. [2015 paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0120644), body, abstract and replication methods.

**Contested/limited:** savings is not percentage of knowledge remaining. This successful single-participant replication is not a universal decay schedule for meaningful knowledge, motor mastery or every person. The authors compare several curve fits and discuss a possible 24-hour discontinuity; their data do not license a universal exponential law or guaranteed sleep jump. SC5's preserved gist versus lost ratios is plausible fiction, not directly measured by this experiment.

**Proposal:** K7 distinguishes an uncertain remembered detail from an intact available record, without erasing earned skill. Links: SC5, TK, MR1/MR7, RB7, TC2/TC3.

## K8. Spacing

**Known:** distributing verbal learning over time generally improves delayed retention compared with massing. Cepeda et al. reviewed 839 assessments from 317 experiments in 184 articles; the best interstudy interval increased with the desired retention interval. [2006 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/), abstract.

**Contested/limited:** benefits depend on the delay, material and test. The verified abstract supports no single pooled effect size or universal optimal schedule, so none is invented here. Evidence from verbal recall does not set a craft XP curve. Spacing also differs from interleaving different tasks and from resting a tired body.

**Proposal:** K8 optionally notices revisiting a method during ordinary later work, never a daily streak or expiring competence. Links: SC5/SC10, RV4/RV7, RD3, MO8.

## K9. Context-dependent memory

**Known:** incidental environmental context can affect retrieval. Smith and Vela's review found effects moderated by how strongly noncontextual information was processed. Weighted differences were d = .13 for associative input processing and .33 for nonassociative processing in their coded groups. [2001 review](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03196157), abstract; [author's university PDF](https://people.tamu.edu/~stevesmith/SmithMemory/Smith%26Vela2001.pdf), body, pp. 211-212, analyses of input processing.

**Contested/limited:** context effects weaken when other retrieval cues dominate; the moderator comparison is not a randomized manipulation across all studies. Word-list retrieval is not the same as executing a procedure away from its original workshop. A missing bench or unsuitable material is a means problem, not context memory. No universal away-from-home skill penalty follows.

**Proposal:** K9 offers a portable familiar cue as narrative recall support. Links: SC7/SC10, TK, MS, MT3, means M5/M6.

## K10. Proactive and retroactive interference

**Known:** earlier learning can interfere with later targets, called proactive interference; later learning can interfere with earlier targets, called retroactive interference. These are directional relations, not synonyms for erasure. [Kliegl and Bäuml, 2021 review](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci11091246), [publisher PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/brainsci/brainsci-11-01246/article_deploy/brainsci-11-01246.pdf), body, paired-associate learning and reminding sections.

**Contested/limited:** competition, cue selection, inhibition and encoding accounts explain different paradigms; no one mechanism or pooled effect size covers them all. Detecting and remembering changes can integrate related information and produce facilitation instead. Learning a second similar method therefore need not damage the first. This review is not a replication audit of every interference paradigm.

**Proposal:** K10 makes two method versions comparable and labels their contexts, with an optional actor correction before action. Links: SC6/SC5, RB2/RB3, MR7, RF6, SO6.

## K11. Testing effect

**Known:** retrieving learned information can improve later retention relative to restudy. Rowland reviews testing-versus-restudy comparisons. [2014 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25150680/), abstract. Yang et al.'s classroom synthesis includes 222 studies and 48,478 students, with overall Hedges' g = .499. [2021 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33683913/), abstract; [author paper copy](https://www.rongjunyu.org/_files/ugd/7b277c_c3a0d8bcdd924857ac911612ad3d6621.pdf), indexed original text.

**Contested/limited:** feedback, control activity, test format, material alignment and design moderate the classroom result. It is not a universal half-SD improvement from any quiz. Recall of facts does not prove tool use or bodily control. No novel reanalysis of publication bias was conducted here.

**Proposal:** K11 uses an optional learner demonstration with corrective feedback, not a player trivia test or routine gate. Links: SC1/SC5/SC10, TK4/TK5, SO8, MO2, IR05.

## K12. Overconfidence and calibration

**Known:** overestimating absolute performance, ranking oneself too highly against others and expressing beliefs too precisely are distinct. Moore and Healy show task difficulty can produce different directions of absolute and relative misjudgment; overprecision was more persistent. [2008 paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18426301/), abstract; [author's university copy](https://healy.econ.ohio-state.edu/papers/Moore_Healy-TroubleWithOverconfidence.pdf), indexed original text.

**Contested/limited:** confidence is not a proxy for skill, and novice status does not fix its direction. No universal calibration-learning slope was verified. A narrower estimate is better only if its coverage is warranted; more experience alone cannot guarantee that. Calibration must compare earlier estimates with outcomes across sufficiently comparable cases.

**Proposal:** K12 keeps one recorded prediction beside its actual outcome and a character-supported uncertainty label. Links: SC8, RU3/RU5, SN1/SN5, RF4, TM8.

## K13. Dunning-Kruger and critiques

**Known:** Kruger and Dunning's four original studies found low-scoring groups overestimated their task performance. [1999 paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10626367/), abstract. Jansen et al.'s later replication/model supports lower performers being less responsive to evidence. [2021 study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-021-01057-0), abstract and [university paper copy](https://cocosci.princeton.edu/papers/DK_NHB_Final.pdf), indexed original text.

**Contested:** Gignac and Zajenkowski challenge quartile plots through regression and better-than-average effects; their 929-person intelligence sample did not support their proposed nonlinear/heteroscedastic tests. [2020 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2020.101449), [original PDF copy](https://gwern.net/doc/iq/2020-gignac.pdf), body, abstract/methods. Dunkel et al.'s response found a significant but minimal effect using revised sampling: r = -.07 for their first test, and 1.1 percentage points of additional explained variance from the quadratic term. [2023 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2022.101717), [original PDF copy](https://gwern.net/doc/iq/2023-dunkel.pdf), body, abstract/discussion. Hiller contests a recoding choice; Gignac and Zajenkowski rebut it. [Comment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2023.101732), [author manuscript](https://philpapers.org/archive/AVRCOG.pdf), indexed text; [reply](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160289623000144), indexed text. Intelligence self-assessment and task-level metacognition are different tests. No universal confidence-by-skill curve is established.

**Proposal:** K13 gives any actor a task-specific revision after feedback. Links: SC8, RU3/RU5, RF1/RF4, K12. No global “ignorant means arrogant” trait.

## K14. Incubation and insight

**Known:** putting a worked-on problem aside can benefit later solving. Sio and Ormerod's synthesis found a small weighted standardized effect, .29, 95% CI [.21, .39], with differences across task types and intervening activity. [2009 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19210055/), abstract; [original paper copy](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/writing/2009-sio.pdf), body, p. 103. Divergent tasks benefited more than linguistic/visual insight tasks in moderator analysis.

**Contested/limited:** conflicting individual studies and heterogeneous procedures matter. Relief from fixation, opportunistic cue use, covert conscious work and unconscious processing are competing accounts. The review's publication-bias check found no significant sample-size relationship, which does not prove absence of bias. A felt insight does not itself verify correctness; incubation is not necessarily sleep or a mandatory timed phase.

**Proposal:** K14 attaches a tentative lead to an already chosen rest or unrelated activity. Links: SC3/SC10, RV3/RV4/RV6, MO4/MO5, KD7, means M4.

## K15. Expertise reversal

**Known:** some instructional supports that help novices become redundant or detrimental for more knowledgeable learners. Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler and Sweller review this interaction. [2003 review](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4), publisher abstract.

**Contested/limited:** this is an interaction between particular instruction and prior knowledge, not evidence that teaching harms every expert. The checked abstract supplies no universal pooled size or skill threshold. Removing support must follow relevant competence, rather than a global level or assumed intuition. This lane did not verify every experiment or a modern replication synthesis.

**Proposal:** K15 offers concise directions or fuller guidance, switchable in either direction. Links: SC1/SC2/SC7, MU1/MU4, RV1, means M3; K5 labels do not decide it.

## K16. Apprenticeship learning

**Known/stated:** cognitive apprenticeship proposes modeling, coaching, scaffolding, articulation, reflection and exploration; support fades as the learner becomes capable. [Collins, Brown and Holum, 1991](https://www.aft.org/ae/winter1991/collins_brown_holum), body, methods section. Mikkonen et al.'s empirical workplace review covers 18 studies and 3,485 participants; guidance often comes from coworkers and peers as well as designated trainers. [2017 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40461-017-0053-4), body, methods/results.

**Contested/limited:** the instructional framework is not itself a randomized efficacy trial. The workplace evidence is heterogeneous and largely qualitative; access to work, mentor availability and organizational participation affect learning. It gives no universal pooled advantage or compulsory years of service. Merely standing near an expert does not establish what was learned.

**Proposal:** K16 records one operation a supported learner can perform for a real recipient, after optional shared work. Links: SC1/SC2/SC7, TN7, SO8, RP4, CD9, SS1/SS2, H6, means M6.

## K17. Tip-of-the-tongue, the SC10 boundary

**Known:** a failed word retrieval can coexist with a strong feeling of knowing and partial phonological information. Brown's review documents the phenomenon and competing explanations. [1991 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2034750/), abstract.

**Contested/limited:** feeling that an answer is known does not prove that it is accurate or accessible. Incomplete activation and interference accounts were unresolved in that review. Evidence about lexical retrieval does not establish a universal “touch the tool, recover the complete recipe” rule. This is an older review, not a current comprehensive verdict.

**Proposal:** K17 uses a familiar object to recall a label or point to an existing note; it never reveals a previously unknown method. Links: SC10/SC5, TK, MR2, SH2/SH3.

## K18. Beginner's luck, the SC9 boundary

**Known/inference:** a successful specimen is narrower evidence than reliable production or understanding why it succeeded. This follows the project's [IR05 replication distinction](../../../../research/CRAFTING_TECH_MICRO_MACRO_2026-10-11.md#in-game-research-can-itself-have-micro-and-macro-play), rather than a verified psychological “beginner bonus.” K3 also warns against treating less experience as a general cognitive advantage.

**Contested/unverified:** no reputable evidence for a universal enhanced first-attempt success probability was established in this review. A first success followed by failure is possible outcome variation, not proof of such a bonus. Magical luck or a world-specific first-attempt rule would need its own authority.

**Proposal:** K18 celebrates a first usable piece while leaving consistency honestly untested. Links: SC9, MO1/MO8, RF4, RU3, SN1, CD5. Never rig the next attempt to fail.

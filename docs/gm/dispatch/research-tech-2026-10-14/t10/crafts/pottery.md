## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Center | The rim wobbles. | It leans toward the same side each turn. | The wobble repeats at one point rather than along the entire rim. |
| Touch | The surface is slippery. | The surface feels different from the firmer wall. | You distinguish a surface sensation from the shape holding beneath it. |
| Wall | The bowl looks lopsided. | One side appears fuller. | The silhouette shows a local change in wall shape, without inventing a thickness reading. |
| Finish | The glaze looks patchy. | Color gathers in the hollow. | The local variation follows the form; it need not be a failure. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** An uneven form has a wavering rim; an unsupported thin section may slump. V&A records collapsed forms kept as art, so misshapen does not automatically mean worthless.

**Body costs and marks:** Clay at the wrist, dry-feeling hands and a tired back. Kiln heat and ceramic dust remain abstract hazards, without processing instructions.

**Tool care and quirks:** A worn rib has a familiar curve; a rough spot on a tool can leave a repeating trace. A kiln quirk is a proposed authored observation, not an invisible new modifier. Keep tools clean and inspect rough working edges; kiln maintenance remains an abstract specialist service.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** Drafts, humidity and seasonal room conditions are candidate drying sensitivities, UNVERIFIED for a specific clay/body. Side light reveals profile; firing stays an abstract operation.

**Small happy accident:** A small asymmetry becomes the bowl's personality; do not describe a cracked food vessel as safe or functionally better.

**Cozy moments:** The first bowl sits level; the maker keeps an awkward early piece beside later work.

## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Silhouette | The vessel leans. | The neck sits away from the center. | The imbalance is in the neck, not merely the viewing angle. |
| Reflection | The highlight bends. | A ripple breaks its otherwise smooth curve. | Moving light distinguishes a surface ripple from decoration. |
| Bubble | A little bubble catches the light. | It sits inside one part of the wall. | You locate the inclusion without claiming it measures strength. |
| Rim | The rim looks uneven. | One section interrupts the circle. | The finished lip varies locally; uniformity is a craft observation, not safety certification. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** Uneven form shows a leaning neck or off-center outline; a rough-looking lip suggests imperfect finish. No heating, blowing, cooling sequence or diagnostic sound test.

**Body costs and marks:** Heat tiredness and a weary supporting arm are proposed texture; burns and cuts are hazards, not required progression events. No direct contact with hot work.

**Tool care and quirks:** A familiar supporting tool has a polished handle; worn jaws or a rough working face can be authored quirks. Tool maintenance remains abstract. Keep supporting tools inspected and organized; hazardous hot-shop care stays an abstract service.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** Glare and workshop lighting affect visible reflections; winter changes comfort around the workshop. Weather-driven glass behavior is UNVERIFIED and not implied by season prose.

**Small happy accident:** A harmless inclusion becomes a decorative sparkle on a noncritical ornament, never an automatic improvement to a load-bearing vessel.

**Cozy moments:** A finished bead scatters the morning light; the team admires an already-cooled piece together.

## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aroma | The kitchen smells toasted. | The sweet aroma has a sharper edge. | You notice the aroma changing; it is a cue to attention, not a food-safety verdict. |
| Dough | The dough has grown. | It has risen unevenly. | You see how the exposed surface differs from the protected part. |
| Crust | The loaf looks brown. | One side is darker. | The color difference belongs to this oven position, if the observation is retained. |
| Texture | The bread feels dense. | The crumb has tight patches. | You compare this patch with the rest rather than call the whole loaf a failure. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** Mistaking a cold-room delay for no activity; letting exposed dough acquire a dry skin. King Arthur supports both cues. Browning outside alone does not prove safe doneness.

**Body costs and marks:** Flour on sleeves, a tired stirring arm and warm cheeks. Burns/cuts stay hazards; no assumed injury, hunger death or compulsory feeding loop.

**Tool care and quirks:** A scarred wooden spoon and an oven that colors one side more strongly are proposed personal quirks. Clean utensils and a readable recipe note are ordinary care texture. Keep utensils clean and handles sound; ordinary kitchen care can occur alongside the routine.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** King Arthur documents cold-weather slow fermentation and dry air affecting exposed dough. Seasonal produce and task light belong to actual stock/scene; no automatic freshness state.

**Small happy accident:** An uneven decorative score makes a pleasing crust pattern, with no invented nutrition bonus.

**Cozy moments:** Steam rises from a shared slice; someone asks for the familiar pie again.

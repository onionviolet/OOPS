## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soil | The surface looks dry. | The crust differs from the soil below. | You separate a surface cue from a claim about the whole root zone. |
| Leaves | The leaves droop. | This row differs from its neighbor. | The pattern suggests a question; wilt has more than one possible cause. |
| Light | One row lies in shade. | The shadow reaches it earlier. | You connect its observed exposure with site context, without inventing exact growth rates. |
| Stage | The crop has changed. | Flowers have given way to seed heads. | You identify a familiar observed stage, not a guaranteed harvest date. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** Assuming dry surface means dry soil throughout; ignoring differences between sites; mixing unlabeled seeds loses identity. These are proposed beginner judgments, not crop-disease diagnoses.

**Body costs and marks:** Mud at the knees, a tired back and a hoe-smoothed palm. Seasonal hard work can be atmosphere without compulsory fatigue or hunger loops.

**Tool care and quirks:** A worn hoe feels different; a loose basket handle pulls at one hand. Care can accompany queued work instead of adding daily chores. Keep hand tools clean and inspect handles; established care can belong to the work routine.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** UMN supports soil/light/site-specific moisture needs and microclimates. Rain, wind and season matter where a selected crop/action reads them; no universal watering timetable.

**Small happy accident:** A self-sown flower appears at the boundary only through an actual world event; otherwise use a noticed pleasing leaf pattern as pure texture.

**Cozy moments:** An orderly row emerges behind the worker; neighbors admire an actual harvested basket.

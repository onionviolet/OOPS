## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tension | The cloth puckers. | The seam draws the cloth inward. | The puckering follows the seam, rather than a fold elsewhere. |
| Pattern | A stripe goes astray. | One row breaks the repeat. | You spot where the regular pattern first changes. |
| Touch | The yarn feels uneven. | A thicker section passes under your fingers. | The thick section belongs to the yarn, not a loose stitch. |
| Drape | The garment hangs oddly. | One side pulls higher. | The pull appears at the fitting point; you do not blame all the cloth. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** Pulling work unevenly leaves puckers or narrowing edges; losing a repeat leaves an isolated pattern break. Tailoring fit and weaving structure need different evidence.

**Body costs and marks:** A thimble dent, neck tiredness and lint on a sleeve. A trade mark can be temporary texture; durable calluses require body history.

**Tool care and quirks:** A needle burr snags a fine thread; one shuttle has a worn smooth grip. Keep needles accounted for as scene texture, without invented missing-item loss. Keep needles, shuttles and thread paths clean and organized; needle inspection can be scene texture.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** CCI documents textile response to humidity and light damage. Winter dryness and damp seasons can color a selected scene; task light makes small stitches readable. No universal cloth-decay timer.

**Small happy accident:** An unintended stripe becomes a pleasing border on an already-authored pattern variant.

**Cozy moments:** The loom rhythm accompanies rain; someone turns in a newly fitted coat and smiles.

**Real-craft evidence, checked 2026-10-14:** Colonial Williamsburg distinguishes weavers and tailors; CCI documents humidity response and fading, not the proposed mistake diagnoses.

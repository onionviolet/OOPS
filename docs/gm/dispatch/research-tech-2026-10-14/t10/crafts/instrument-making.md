## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tone | The note sounds thin. | One note fades sooner than its neighbors. | You compare observed response rather than claim an exact hidden resonance. |
| Buzz | Something rattles. | The rattle repeats with one note. | The vibration is localized, but its cause still needs inspection. |
| Feel | The string feels awkward. | Its response differs from the next string. | You separate the instrument's response from the player's unfamiliar technique. |
| Fit | The seam looks uneven. | A small gap interrupts the join. | The reflected line shows a local fit difference, not a universal verdict on tone. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** An uneven fitting may rattle; inaccurate spacing can feel awkward; evaluating tone in a noisy room obscures comparison. These candidate beginner tells are UNVERIFIED, not luthier instructions.

**Body costs and marks:** Tired fingertips, a stiff neck and fine shavings on clothing. No compulsory hearing loss, hand injury or musician stat bonus.

**Tool care and quirks:** A favorite small plane fits the hand; a fitting tool has a worn notch. String handling, adhesive work and acoustic adjustment remain abstract. Keep fine tools clean and protected; adjustment and repair stay abstract services.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** Seasonal humidity/tuning effects are plausible but UNVERIFIED for the selected instrument. Rain changes background noise; raking light helps reveal a seam. Do not apply one wood/string model to all instruments.

**Small happy accident:** A harmless decorative irregularity becomes a distinctive maker mark, with no automatic superior tone.

**Cozy moments:** The first clear note brings the room quiet; a companion plays the maker's familiar tune.

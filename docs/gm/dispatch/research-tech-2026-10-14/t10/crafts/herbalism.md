## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shape | Two leaves look alike. | Their edges differ. | You compare several visible characters instead of calling one resemblance an identification. |
| Scent | The basket smells green. | One known sample has a distinct scent. | You connect scent to a retained known sample, not an unseen species or safe remedy. |
| Season | There are no flowers here. | This sample shows seed instead. | The observed life stage is useful context for comparison. |
| Record | The bundle has a tag. | Place and date are written on it. | You distinguish the specimen's record from a claim about every plant nearby. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** Collecting an unlabeled bundle loses its context; mistaking one leaf similarity for certainty leaves an uncertain identification. No real ingestion, dosage or medicine procedure.

**Body costs and marks:** Mud at the knees, plant stains on sleeves and a tired back. Thorn scratches are possible texture, not guaranteed work costs.

**Tool care and quirks:** A nicked cutting tool leaves a rough-looking cut; a basket with an awkward handle tires one hand. Labels belong with actual specimens if retained. Keep collecting tools clean and labels legible; do not use this as a specimen-safety test.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** Kew specimens document location, habitat and flowering/seed context. Rain, season and dim light can alter what can be observed; no universal medicinal potency claim.

**Small happy accident:** A noticed leaf pattern inspires a border in the notebook, without awarding a newly identified plant.

**Cozy moments:** A frog watches the gatherer, matching existing background texture; a neatly labeled basket waits by the door.

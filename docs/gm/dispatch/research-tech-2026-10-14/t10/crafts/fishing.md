## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Line | The line trembles. | A brief tug differs from its steady movement. | You notice the difference without naming the unseen fish. |
| Water | The surface flashes. | The flash moves beneath the glare. | You track observed motion, not species or exact depth. |
| Current | The float drifts. | It keeps entering the same eddy. | The repeat path describes the water you can observe. |
| Gear | The line feels rough. | A worn section catches your fingers. | You locate wear without promising a remaining breaking strength. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** Tangled line shows a looped snarl; treating every tug as a catch overclaims evidence; overlooking glare hides visible motion. Specific technique diagnoses are UNVERIFIED.

**Body costs and marks:** A tired holding wrist, damp cuffs and cold fingers are scene cues, not mandatory penalties or guaranteed catch loss.

**Tool care and quirks:** A rough guide or worn line is a proposed quirk; the pole handle becomes thumb-polished with actual retained history. No invented bait consumption in background text. Keep the pole and line inspected and stored neatly; care should not add compulsory bait or gear chores.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** NPS supports glare as a viewing obstacle and temperature as relevant to fish ecology. Seasonal species/access belong to world tables; rainy ripples can obscure the surface. No fixed best-hour rule.

**Small happy accident:** An observed ripple makes a pleasing pattern; a real incidental catch can be interesting only if the reward table supplied it.

**Cozy moments:** The bank grows quiet while someone shares a story; a caught fish becomes a meal through the actual cooking route.

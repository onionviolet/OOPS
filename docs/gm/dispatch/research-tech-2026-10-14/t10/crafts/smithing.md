## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surface | The metal has little dents. | The dents repeat along one edge. | The repeating marks interrupt an otherwise even finish. |
| Sound | The workshop rings. | Your blows have settled into a rhythm. | You hear where your rhythm changes, without treating sound as a hidden crack assay. |
| Balance | The tool feels heavy. | Its weight sits toward the head. | The balance feels suited to this task, rather than simply lighter. |
| Light | The face shines unevenly. | The reflected line bends near the corner. | The reflection picks out a local ripple in the finished face. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** Off-center work leaves visibly uneven faces; misplaced blows leave repeat dents. These are candidate beginner tells, not automatic failure diagnoses.

**Body costs and marks:** A tired gripping hand, soot at the cuff, and a polished patch on a familiar handle. Burns are hazards, never required badges of mastery.

**Tool care and quirks:** A nicked working face can leave recurring marks; a loose-feeling handle merits attention. Care appears as a remembered clean bench, not a compulsory repair click. Keep working faces clean and handles inspected; sharpening/repair remains an abstract service.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** Workshop glare changes what surface marks are visible; cold-season hands can feel less nimble; rain changes the mood at the doorway. No heat colors, temperatures or treatment instructions.

**Small happy accident:** A harmless decorative indentation resembles a leaf, if an authored finished piece actually carries it.

**Cozy moments:** The first hinge swings quietly; someone leaves a warm mug on the safe side of the bench.

## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rock | The rock has a pale line. | The line differs from the surrounding stone. | You can describe the visible boundary without declaring a valuable deposit. |
| Sound | The pick makes a clink. | Its note differs across the face. | Changing sound is an observation, not a safe-wall or ore-purity assay. |
| Load | The basket feels heavy. | Its weight differs from a familiar load. | You recognize a handling difference without converting heft into exact composition. |
| Output | The cooled ingot looks uneven. | Its outline or finish differs along one edge. | You localize the visible workmanship change; hidden inclusions remain unknown. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** Mixing unlabeled loads loses origin; mistaking sparkle for valuable ore gives false confidence; ignoring obvious tool damage is a maintenance oversight. No excavation, support, ventilation or smelting instructions.

**Body costs and marks:** Tired shoulders, gritty sleeves and a heavy work apron. Dust, heat and falling rock remain abstract hazards, never sensory safety checks.

**Tool care and quirks:** A damaged pick face changes its feel; a container always rocks on one corner. Furnace quirks need an authored record, not remembered temperatures or chemical recipes. Keep carrying gear inspected and tools organized; hazardous mine/furnace maintenance stays abstract.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** Wet ground, winter access and underground lamp light can alter a supported scene. Resource locations and fuel supply are real constraints; no environment algorithm is inferred from atmosphere.

**Small happy accident:** A harmless mineral-pattern specimen is kept as a curiosity after actual acquisition, without inventing rare ore.

**Cozy moments:** The group rests beside stacked finished goods; the first useful ingot reaches a waiting smith.

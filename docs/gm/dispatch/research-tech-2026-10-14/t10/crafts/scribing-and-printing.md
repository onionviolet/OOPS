## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stroke | The letter looks blotchy. | Ink spreads outside the stroke. | You distinguish spread in the paper from a wider intended letterform. |
| Line | The writing slopes. | The baseline drifts across the page. | The drift repeats with the writer's posture, if actually observed. |
| Impression | The print looks patchy. | One part of the letter is faint. | You localize an uneven impression without inventing a press-pressure reading. |
| Order | Something looks wrong in the word. | A letter is reversed or misplaced. | You spot a setting error only in a script you know. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** A wet-looking stroke gets smeared by contact; a copied line skips a word; a mis-set letter survives into a proof. Printing errors and scribing errors have different causes.

**Body costs and marks:** Ink stains, a tired writing hand and neck stiffness. A scribe's squint is scene texture, not a diagnosis or inherited skill.

**Tool care and quirks:** A damaged nib catches; one type piece has a recognizable nick. Ink manufacture, pigment chemistry and press repair stay abstract. Keep nibs/type organized and inspect recurring nicks; hazardous ink manufacture stays abstract.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** Task light affects legibility; humidity-related ink spread is a proposed UNVERIFIED cue for selected paper/ink. Winter light shortens a cozy scene only under actual clock/light rules.

**Small happy accident:** A stray flourish becomes a charming initial on a kept draft, without silently altering an issued document.

**Cozy moments:** The first clean proof waits on the desk; a friend finds their name in the dedication.

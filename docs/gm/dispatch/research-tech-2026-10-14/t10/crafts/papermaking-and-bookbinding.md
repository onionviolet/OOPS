## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sheet | The paper looks cloudy against the light. | Its thickness looks uneven. | The transmitted-light pattern localizes variation without supplying an exact measurement. |
| Edge | The edge is feathery. | The fringe differs from a torn edge. | The edge character can belong to the making, not necessarily later damage. |
| Opening | The book resists opening. | The resistance is strongest near one gathering. | You notice where the opening differs, without forcing a fragile binding. |
| Alignment | The pages step out. | One section sits proud. | The shift belongs to that section rather than the whole text block. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** An uneven sheet shows mottling in transmitted light; misplaced sections make uneven page edges; careless collation gives wrong page order. These specific tells remain UNVERIFIED here.

**Body costs and marks:** Wet cuffs, tired pressing hands and thread marks. Paper cuts are possible, not mandatory badges.

**Tool care and quirks:** A mould's mark can recur in a sheet; a bone folder has a familiar polished tip. No bleaching chemistry or hazardous binding adhesive procedure. Keep the mould and folding tools clean and inspect snagging edges; adhesive preparation stays abstract.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** Rainy-room drying and winter stiffness are proposed, UNVERIFIED material sensitivities; transmitted daylight reveals variation. Seasonal paper supply must come from real stock.

**Small happy accident:** A stray harmless fiber becomes a pleasing fleck on a writing sheet; it does not automatically strengthen the paper.

**Cozy moments:** A first blank notebook opens cleanly; the maker tucks a tiny personal mark inside the cover.

**Real-craft evidence, checked 2026-10-14:** Georgia Tech documents traditional sheet-making; Smithsonian records a watermark mould; Williamsburg identifies bookbinding as a trade.

## Sensory ladder

Four cues, three voices for the same permitted observation (U6/U30). Authoring voices, not XP thresholds.

| Cue | Novice | Practiced | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hue | The fabric looks blue. | The wet and dry samples differ. | You compare like conditions instead of naming a new dye from appearance. |
| Evenness | There are darker patches. | The patches follow folds. | The visible distribution is uneven; its cause is still a hypothesis. |
| Transfer | Color marks the cloth beneath. | The contact area holds the stain. | You distinguish transferred color from the original pattern. |
| Light | The old sample looks pale. | Its exposed side is paler. | The exposure history matters; one sample does not establish a universal fade rate. |

**Beginner mistakes and tells:** Comparing wet and dry work as if conditions match; losing sample labels; overlooking folded dark patches. These are proposed tells, not validated process diagnoses.

**Body costs and marks:** Stained fingertips, damp cuffs and tired shoulders. Irritation remains a hazard; no mordants, reagent lists or dye chemistry.

**Tool care and quirks:** A spoon with an old stain can confuse batch color; a sample book with stable labels is more useful than a remembered shade. Cleaning is abstract care. Keep sample labels and designated tools clean; chemical cleaning stays abstract.

**Environment, weather, season and light:** V&A documents light fading in a naturally dyed carpet; no universal colorfastness ranking follows. Seasonal plant access, rainy drying scenes and changing task light are world-specific proposals.

**Small happy accident:** An uneven decorative shade is accepted as a deliberate-looking gradient, if an actual cosmetic variant supports it.

**Cozy moments:** Samples hang like small flags; a friend recognizes their requested color.

**Real-craft evidence, checked 2026-10-14:** V&A's Bullerswood case supports exposure-sensitive color; Smithsonian textile analysis supports identification needing evidence beyond visual guesses. [Bullerswood conservation](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/conservation-of-the-bullerswood-carpet), [Ikat analysis](https://profiles.si.edu/display/sro_157562).

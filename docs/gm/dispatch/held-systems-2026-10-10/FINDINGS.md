## F14: Testing “lore decides truth; storyteller decides when and how hard”

**INFERRED design assessment:** useful only if “storyteller” means an external presentation/scheduling layer. It is not a source-derived law. The examples above expose several different causal models:

| Case | Evidence origin | What the hypothesis gets right | Where it needs limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Architect/Ashborn, F5 | Authored designer and patron | Their identities/purposes belong to the world’s facts | Test pressure, succession and retained powers follow actual agency, not free difficulty adjustment |
| System incentive, F6 | Interface plus fortune effects; owner unresolved | Distinguish what is true from what Gu reads | Inventing an owner or lowering fortune for drama changes facts |
| Star Stream, F7 | Sponsors, bureaucracy and Probability | Several sources can influence one person | Scenario presentation is itself in-world power and can be fought; it is not unrestricted external pacing |

**Optional refinement, not accepted:** world facts and causal state decide what can happen; agents and forces decide what they attempt; a scheduler selects eligible presentation opportunities; observation decides who learns what. Pressure may vary through explicit local parameters or selected premise changes. If a storyteller acts **inside** the world, record its authority and costs like any other actor. Creating a controller partway through a run requires a causal acquisition or declared divergent premise, not a retroactive owner insertion.

The S0–S7 vocabulary in the north star describes scope/use, not demonstrated write permissions. An S6 institution may lack authority over a particular S2 interface. “Override” needs an actual compatible target, channel and authority, not merely a higher row number. Source lore may genuinely permit omnipotence or an outlier; this review does not impose an anti-omnipotence rule.

## F16: General genre knowledge, explicitly bounded

**GENERAL GENRE KNOWLEDGE, INFERRED vocabulary:** a ring mentor can advise or manipulate; a sealed ancestor can limit dangerous output; a fragment collector can compete with other holders; a parasite can need the bearer alive; a creator can retain maintenance access without owning acquired skills. These are common design shapes, not claims about the thirteen checked cases or requirements for every world. Benevolent restraint, no controller, voluntary sponsorship and a system that independently resists its maker are equally available options.

The decisive open research gap is the remembered scene: who restrained what, where both parties were, and what influence actually crossed the link. No indexed opening establishes all three. Broader chapter access or Weibao recognizing a scene could resolve it, but the present report does not manufacture an identification.

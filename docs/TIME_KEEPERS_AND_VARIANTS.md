## More to consider

### Being on the other side
- **N1 Keeper career.** Join the authority as a life-sim path: rookie
  hunter, analyst, clerk, judge. Hunt variants in book worlds you know.
  Grey work: a pruned branch had people in it.
- **N2 Every stance reachable.** Join, flee, bargain, reform from inside,
  defect, topple, or never meet them. Per [everything possible](LIFE_SIM_NORTH_STAR.md),
  no either-or.

### Being hunted
- **N3 Hunted by your own habits.** The analyst predicts you from the
  habit profile your life trace already keeps. Playing against type
  throws them off. A rare case where varying your play is the mechanic.
- **N4 Pursuit, not instant death.** A keeper hunt runs through
  [encounters and pursuit](ENCOUNTERS_AND_PURSUIT.md): exposed danger,
  warning signs, a preparation route. Only explicitly eligible threats
  end a run (2026-10-02 direction).
- **N5 Hide in canon.** Lower your signal by doing canon's deeds yourself,
  or by keeping changes below inertia. Being a nobody is camouflage.

### Pruning as the archive's in-fiction face
- **N6 Storage limits become content.** The branch archive already fades
  old branches. Diegetically, faded branches are pruned and land in the
  Void. Their salvage (a ghost, a stash, a lost friend's variant) can be
  found there. A storage cap turns into a world.
- **N7 Content migrations, diegetically.** When a game update removes an
  event a branch referenced, its leftover can wash up in the Void rather
  than only folding into chronicle text. Optional flavor; the migration
  still has to be correct first.

### People across branches
- **N8 The friend who lives elsewhere.** Someone canon kills may survive
  in a pruned branch. Find them in the Void or another branch; they
  remember a life you never shared.
- **Variant party.** Recruit your own variants (L14) as a party: same
  soul, different builds. Coexistence rules stay per world
  ([selves](SELVES_AND_ECHOES.md)).
- **Variants of rivals.** A rival you beat has a variant who won. They
  heard about you.

### Endgame and prestige
- **N9 Become the holder.** Taking the keeper's seat or holding the tree
  is a run ending that turns the character into infrastructure: the run
  becomes literally idle, generating for later runs. A sacrifice ending
  that fits an idle game unusually well.
- **Loom throughput.** If you hold time, branches cost capacity.
  Every branch your later runs make strains it. Prestige upgrades expand
  it.
- **Succession.** The seat passes on. Who sat there before, and who comes
  for it next ([succession by world](worlds/SUCCESSION_BY_WORLD.md)).

### Loot, heists and economy
- **Confiscated wonders.** The keepers' evidence lockers hold artifacts
  they treat as junk. A heist target with obvious risk.
- **Keeper tools** (L6) as stolen carriers: a door you can't recharge, a
  reset charge with one use.
- **Arbitrage** across near-copy branches already exists as a north-star
  bullet; keepers are one of its customs officers.

### Theme
- **Who chose canon?** The natural course may be a choice someone made,
  not physics. In some worlds that author exists, in others not. Which,
  and why, is a sealed truth (possible truths below).
- **Free will versus a glorious purpose.** Dialogue and mood material,
  never a lecture.
- **The weight of infinity** (north star): this life is the one being lived.

## Engine shape (proposal)

- **Keepers are a faction** with their own natural course and E3 people.
- **Attention** sums disturbance on fixed-point events, weighted by D1.
  Crossing a threshold schedules a pursuit encounter, seeded (E1), not
  reload-farmable.
- **The Void is a world** whose content is generated from pruned branch
  records already kept by the atlas: receipts, ghosts, stashes.
- **Career and stances** reuse factions, ranks and storylets (E2).
- No new persistence until a slice needs it; any change needs a migration.

### Characters on a journey

Loki's arc changes **who he is** more than what he can do: villain,
prisoner, reluctant ally, friend, leader, the one who stays behind. Power
barely moves until the end. So a journey character needs identity state,
not just levels.

Build on the saved actor plus arc model in
[enemy protagonists](ENEMY_PROTAGONISTS.md#implementing-independent-arcs-without-an-entire-simulated-society):

| Code | Piece | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| J1 | **Arc phases** | Each phase sets role, goal, values, allies, title, dialogue voice and, rarely, powers |
| J2 | **Causes** | A phase changes only from receipts: a loss, a betrayal, being seen by someone, a failure, a choice under pressure. No calendar-only growth |
| J3 | **Self-concept** | A few value sliders (pride, trust, purpose, guilt) that the causes push. Phases open when sliders cross thresholds, so the same arc can be reached by different events |
| J4 | **The witness** | Someone whose trust drives growth (Mobius for Loki). The player can fill that role, or a rival can, or nobody does and the arc stalls |
| J5 | **Off-ramps** | Relapse, stall, a darker end (revenge instead of purpose), early death. Motives outlive scripts (C6); a journey is not a rail |
| J6 | **Stage variants** | The phase list doubles as a variant catalog. In worlds with branches you can meet the same person at different points of their journey, and each copy can grow differently from there |

What the player does with one:
- **Meet early, meet late.** Early is a threat; late is an ally who
  remembers growing, maybe through a different branch than yours.
- **Be the cause.** Your acts are their receipts. You can push someone
  toward redemption or toward ruin, often without knowing which.
- **Walk it yourself.** The same model fits the player: titles,
  reputation and values drift with your receipts. A chronicle entry can
  name the phase change ("the year the trickster stopped running").
- **Show the arc.** A journey summary in the person's profile: phases
  reached, what caused each, where they could have gone.

Journey shapes worth having (illustrative): villain to guardian, tool to
person, god to mortal, mortal to god of something abstract, the one who
stays behind so others can leave, the regressor who forgives.

### A god of stories

What it means in the game: a being whose domain is narrative itself. The
game already has narrative machinery: the natural course, the storyteller
(E5), casting (E2), the branch atlas. A god of stories is an in-fiction
being who **holds or steers that machinery**. No new physics; a character
gets access to systems that already exist.

| Code | Tier | Power | Uses existing system |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Reader** | Sees the natural course; foreknowledge with confidence | Notebook foreknowledge |
| S2 | **Editor** | Nudges inertia at range; rewrites their own story (a lie about themselves becomes how the world remembers them) | Disturbance (C2), reputation, receipts |
| S3 | **Narrator** | Sets a scope's storyteller personality; picks who gets cast in a role | E5 instances, E2 casting |
| S4 | **Holder** | Keeps branches alive; the atlas is their body; throughput limits apply (L9) | Branch atlas |
| S5 | **Author** | Makes new premises, including What Ifs | Branch premises (B1), premise cards (W1) |

**Limits that make it a story, not a cheat menu.** Stories have laws, and
the god is bound by them:
- No removing conflict, only shaping it. A world with no stakes stops
  being a story and the power fails there.
- Setups must pay off (Chekhov). A seeded promise, prophecy or gun must
  resolve, maybe not how the god wanted.
- The holder can't leave. S4 is a seat; leaving drops branches.
- Edits are receipts. Rewriting never bypasses save integrity (rule 9) or
  sealed secrets: a god of stories knows sealed truths, but Weibao learns
  them only through a discovery route. Becoming that god can itself be a
  route, when a world says so.

**Three ways to meet one:**
1. **As a patron.** A god of stories rewards drama, not efficiency: risks
   taken, ironies landed, setups paid off. A patron that pays you for
   living an interesting life is a real counterweight to min-maxing.
   Uses E5 tension as the score.
2. **As an obstacle.** A narrator who has cast you as the villain, or as
   a minor character due to die in chapter three. Escape by breaking
   their story's laws against them.
3. **As the player's endgame** (N9 extended). When the character becomes
   the god of stories, the menu stops being a menu. Picking a premise,
   seeding a branch or setting a storyteller becomes an in-fiction act
   with weight. This is the one exception to "the player's choice is not
   the character's power" in the timelines doc, and it should feel like
   it: the UI itself can change voice ([presentation](PRESENTATION_AND_FOURTH_WALL.md)).

Kin, from memory (verify): Loki in the *Agent of Asgard* comics, Dream
in *The Sandman*, narrativium in Discworld, gods fed by belief in
*American Gods*, *Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint* (stories as power and as
a cage), *Fables*.

### What If worlds

A What If is one change at one point, and the world runs on from there.
The engine already has the parts: a branch premise (B1) is a world that
starts with a disturbance applied. Motives (C6) write everything after.

**W1 Premise card.** Base world, divergence point (an event in the
graph), change, and a one-line hook. Natural course after the change is
generated from motives, plus any authored beats.

**W2 Change types (palette):**

| Change | Example shape |
|---|---|
| Swap the recipient | The power, item or mentor went to someone else |
| Swap the outcome | The hero lost; the villain won; the battle never happened |
| Remove someone | A key person was never born or died early |
| Add an outsider | You, a traveler, or a variant arrives at the point |
| Change a choice | One person decided the other way |
| Change a rule | Magic, technology or a power works differently |
| Change the genre | Same people, now a zombie world, a romance, a heist |
| Change the time | The story starts a generation earlier or later |
| Change the scale | Everyone has the power, or nobody does |

**W3 Where they come from.**
- Authored, for worlds that support it.
- From your own branches (already proposed).
- Generated: a high plot-weight event plus a change type. Quality check:
  the change must touch an event with real downstream reach, and the
  motives it disturbs must exist. Seeded (E1).

**W4 How you meet them.**
- Start a run in one (B1).
- Cross into one through a carrier (R2).
- A watcher shows you one, like a world-scale alternate self (G4).
- **A fixer job.** A team that repairs broken realities (*Exiles* in
  Marvel comics, from memory) as a mission hub, like Paradise. Fixers and
  keepers want different things: keepers delete, fixers repair.

**W5 What Ifs that matter most:**
- **The tragic one.** A What If that collapses its own world because one
  person refused a loss (the Strange Supreme episode, from memory). A
  doomed branch you can visit before it ends.
- **The villain who won everything.** A hard-mode world after the worst
  outcome.
- **The swapped hero.** You meet the person who got the power instead of
  the canon hero. Their journey (J1) is new.
- **The team from many What Ifs.** A party built from variants of
  different premises (*What If...?* guardians, from memory).

**W6 The watcher.** The player can take an observer role: watch What
Ifs, sworn not to touch them. Breaking the oath is a
[cosmic event](COSMIC_EVENTS.md). An idle-friendly mode: watching is the
game until the moment you can't stand it.

Kin, from memory (verify): *What If...?* (series and the 1977 comics),
DC Elseworlds such as *Superman: Red Son*, *Age of Apocalypse*, *House of
M*, *Exiles*, *Marvel 2099*, fan-fiction AUs and fix-its, *The Man in the
High Castle*.

### More to consider

- **M1 Trickster tools.** Illusions, shapeshifting, lies with tells,
  betrayal as a companion behavior with warning signs. Bluffs already
  exist in the [presentation doc](PRESENTATION_AND_FOURTH_WALL.md).
- **M2 Stories as fuel.** In some worlds, tales told about you have
  weight: legend as a resource that feeds power, like belief feeding gods.
  A per-world dial, off by default.
- **M3 Knowing you're written.** A character who discovers the story
  around them. Fourth-wall rules apply; most worlds never allow it.
- **M4 Journeys across variants.** Two stage variants of one person meet
  and disagree about who they should be. Good drama; uses J6 and the
  selves coexistence rules.
- **M5 Premise fatigue.** Too many What Ifs blur. Keep a few named ones
  per world that matter, as the north star says for infinite variants.

### How to build any character of that scale

A Loki-at-the-end-of-time character uses the same actor machinery as a
village apprentice. What changes is a handful of declared properties.

| Code | Property | Options (illustrative) | Loki as god of stories, for example |
|---|---|---|---|
| X1 | **Scope** | A place; a world; a cluster; every branch of a world; the whole save; above it (C10 to C15) | Every branch |
| X2 | **Persistence** | Resets with each run (every branch has its own copy); persists per world; persists per save | Either. Per-run copies are cheaper and need no new save field |
| X3 | **Presence** | True self somewhere hard to reach; avatars, voices, signs, effects elsewhere | True self at the end of time; avatars and rumors in worlds |
| X4 | **Knowledge** | What it knows and through which means: sight over branches, records, servants, nothing it didn't see | Sees branches; can be lied to below its layer |
| X5 | **Means of action** | Directly; through rules (inertia, casting, storyteller); through agents; through gifts | Mostly through rules and people; the seat limits direct action |
| X6 | **Bindings** | What it can't do: leave a seat, break a story's laws, act without a vow, be seen | Can't leave the tree; can't remove conflict |
| X7 | **Detail level** | Off screen it is an aggregate or a few flags; on screen, a full person | Flags almost always; a full scene rarely |

**How it runs in practice:**
- **Mostly not simulated.** A multiverse-scale being is a few flags and
  goals that fire storylets and shift dials (inertia, keeper attention,
  storyteller tension). Its "actions" are changes to systems that already
  exist, so it costs little to run.
- **Meeting it is a scene, not a stat fight.** Avatars can have stat
  blocks; the true self is reached through talk, bargains, puzzles about
  its bindings, or a cosmic event.
- **Scale shows through reach, not numbers** (UC4). Its signature is that
  it touches things nobody else can: branch names, which people get cast,
  what the next premise is.
- **Fit per world.** A world whose cosmology stops below its layer never
  sees it; a world that allows it sees it in local dress. Its identity and
  truths are sealed once picked.
- **Persistence is the expensive choice.** With per-run copies (X2 first
  option), each run meets a fresh Loki-like figure: no migration and no
  cross-run memory. Per-save persistence (the holder example) needs a new
  meta record and a migration. Both stay reachable.

**Same recipe, other scale characters:** a keeper general (X1 a world's
branches, X5 agents), a conqueror with variants in many eras (X1 every
branch, X3 many true selves), a watcher (X5 none, X6 oath), a fate entity
(X5 rules only), a patron god of one world (X1 a world, X5 gifts).

### Each aspect, expanded (2026-10-11)

Weibao: "more to consider? consider each aspect accordingly?"

Illustrative, like everything here. Each aspect lists more options, how
it plays, and what to watch.

#### X1 Scope

- **Scope can differ by verb.** It may see every branch but act in only
  one, or speak everywhere but touch nothing. Declare scope per verb
  (see, speak, act, remember), not once.
- **Scope can be partial or patchy.** Strong where it has followers,
  temples or anchors; absent where nobody believes in it or where another
  power holds the ground.
- **Scope can change.** It grows with worship, branches or stolen seats;
  shrinks with forgetting, defeat or a binding. A scale character's
  journey (J1) can be mostly a scope change.
- **Overlaps.** Two big beings with overlapping scope means border
  disputes, treaties, proxy wars. Precedence follows the existing rule:
  domains and conditions first, not "bigger wins."
- **Watch:** scope is the main thing that makes it feel big. Show it
  through effects in several places at once, not through a number.

#### X2 Persistence

- **More options than reset or remember:** remembers only you; remembers
  only certain worlds; remembers facts but not feelings; remembers
  feelings but not facts (a vague fondness or grudge); remembers
  everything but pretends not to.
- **Persistence of its state versus its memory.** Its power level, seat
  and goals can carry while its memory of you resets, or the reverse.
- **Per-run copies can still feel continuous.** Authored personality is
  the same every run; only memory is fresh. Most players read that as the
  same character. The cheapest route to "it feels eternal."
- **Echo option.** No persistent record, but traces leak: a line it
  shouldn't know, a déjà vu, a gift already in your pocket. Cheap to
  author, needs no migration if the trigger reads existing atlas data.
- **Watch:** any per-save field needs a migration and must ride export
  and import. Private-pack names stay out of exports.

#### X3 Presence

- **Forms of presence, low to high cost:** a sign (weather, an omen), a
  rumor or myth, a voice, a message through others, an avatar, a borrowed
  body, a full descent.
- **Avatars are a design budget.** Each avatar is a normal actor with its
  own stats and limits. It can be killed without killing the true self;
  killing one may still cost the true self something.
- **Dress per world.** The same being appears as a fox spirit in one
  world, a bureaucrat in another, a glitch in a third. Recognition across
  worlds is itself a discovery (by soul, face or habits, per
  [selves](SELVES_AND_ECHOES.md)).
- **Absence as presence.** A god who left still shapes a world: empty
  temples, stale rules, followers waiting. Cheap and evocative.
- **Watch:** the [effect budget](PRESENTATION_AND_FOURTH_WALL.md) and
  restraint. The more scale it has, the rarer its full presence should be.

#### X4 Knowledge

- **Kinds of sight:** sees outcomes but not reasons; sees reasons but
  not outcomes; sees the course but not deviations (a blind spot for
  variants, regressors and you); sees everything once it looks, but must
  choose where to look.
- **Delay.** It learns of events late, through reports or after the
  branch settles. Gives you a window to act.
- **It can be fooled.** Below its layer it can be lied to; at its layer
  only by equals. Bluffs and knowledge states from the presentation doc
  apply.
- **It can be wrong** about the future in worlds with lazy resolution
  (C3): the future isn't decided yet, even for it.
- **Watch:** never let it reveal sealed truths outside a route; never let
  it know what the player knows but the character doesn't.

#### X5 How it acts

- **Levers, from soft to hard:** omens and nudges; shifting inertia or
  luck; casting people into roles; sending agents; granting gifts and
  cheats; making or pruning branches; direct intervention.
- **Costs per lever.** Hard levers cost it something real (attention
  drawn, seat strain, a law broken). A being that can do anything but pays
  for each act is more interesting than one that can't.
- **Through you.** The most playable lever: it needs mortals to act, so
  it bargains, tempts or hires. You can refuse, bargain up, or play it
  against another power.
- **Tempo.** Big beings act slowly and rarely, so their acts land as
  events, not as a constant stream.
- **Watch:** idle play. Its acts should be legible in a report after you
  return, with a cause you can trace.

#### X6 Bindings

- **Kinds:** a seat or place; an oath or vow; a law of its domain (a god
  of stories must keep stakes); a name or rule others can invoke; a need
  (belief, attention, a ritual); a weakness (a material, a word, a
  person).
- **Bindings are the gameplay.** Learning them is how mortals deal with
  it: call it by name, invoke its law, offer what it needs, trap it in its
  own rule.
- **Bindings can be loosened or broken,** by it or by you. Freeing a
  bound being is a cosmic choice with consequences either way.
- **Self-imposed bindings.** A being that binds itself to stay sane, kind
  or hidden. Breaking that for it is not a favor.
- **Watch:** bindings use the shared [adjective grammar](emergent/E8F_ADJECTIVES.md),
  so they read the same as any other limit.

#### X7 Detail level

- **Three tiers are enough:** flags (off screen), a profile (goals,
  mood, recent acts, for reports and dialogue), a scene (full dialogue,
  choices, maybe an avatar fight).
- **Promote on contact.** It runs as flags until you meet it, a seer
  looks at it, or a hinge event touches it.
- **Seeded and lazy.** Its off-screen acts roll from seeded streams (E1)
  only when a date or an observer needs them (C3).
- **Watch:** a profile needs enough memory to stay consistent between
  scenes; a few tags and a last-seen record usually do it.

### More aspects worth declaring

| Code | Aspect | Options (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| X8 | **Motive** | Keep order, end boredom, protect someone, undo a mistake, be free, be remembered. Motive drives every lever, and outlives its plans (C6) |
| X9 | **Relationship to you** | Unaware, curious, patron, jailer, puppeteer, rival, friend, someone who needs you. Can change over a run |
| X10 | **Killability** | Unkillable; killable only at its layer; killable through a binding; killable but replaced; dies and stays dead. Declared, never hidden plot armor |
| X11 | **Plurality** | One; many variants (Kang); a role many have held; a committee; a being that is also a place |
| X12 | **Timescale** | Thinks in seconds, years or eras. Slow beings make idle offline time matter |
| X13 | **How mortals know it** | Unknown; myth; religion; scholarship with rival theories; public fact. Rival theories make discovery fun |
| X14 | **Origin** | Born, made, promoted from mortal, a role taken by force, an accident. Usually sealed |
| X15 | **Succession** | Who takes its place when it falls, and whether that could be you or someone you know ([succession](worlds/SUCCESSION_BY_WORLD.md)) |

### Putting it together

- **A short card.** X1 to X15 fit on one card per scale character,
  like the cheat placement card. Most fields are one word.
- **Cheap defaults.** Per-run copy (X2), flags (X7), a few levers (X5),
  one clear binding (X6). That gets a Loki-like figure into a world
  without new persistence.
- **Make sense per world.** The card is checked against the world's
  cosmology cap, like any cheat or power.

### Worked example: the holder (not a direction)

### H1 Where it lives in the save

One record per save, at the meta layer beside the atlas and legacy, never
inside a run. A sketch:

| Field | Holds |
|---|---|
| `phase` | Where its own arc stands (H4) |
| `sliders` | Trust, interest, weariness, mischief (J3 at meta scale) |
| `memory` | Per branch: id, name, a few tags (spared, betrayed, ruled, fled), the deed it found most interesting |
| `promises`, `debts` | What it owes you and you owe it, across runs |
| `seat` | Which branches it holds; capacity (L9) |
| `last_seen` | Run and branch of the last meeting |

Memory is built from receipts at a run's end, as tags, not prose. It
fades like faded branches, and it can choose to misremember.

This is a persistence change: it needs a migration where absent means
"never met", and export and import must carry it with the atlas (X1).

### H2 Where it shows up

- **Between lives.** The natural home: the short scene after death where
  the roll and upgrades resolve. It can be the ferryman, the clerk, the
  voice in the void.
- **The atlas.** It narrates branches; the default branch names (Q4) can
  be its titles for them. An unreliable narrator by default.
- **Cameos in worlds,** dressed for each world so it makes sense there: a
  stranger at an inn, a trickster god in a pantheon, a cat, a rumor. Avatar
  versus true self (UC3): an avatar can be fooled, hurt or ignored.
- **The Void and keeper halls,** where it is closest to its true self.
- **Hinge moments only:** death, a new branch, a big divergence, a cosmic
  event. A frequency budget keeps it special, and "never shows up" is a
  valid save.

### H3 What it knows and says

- Everything in your atlas, and more than it admits. It is a trickster:
  it lies, teases and tests, with tells you can learn.
- It knows sealed truths and never hands them over outside a discovery
  route. Its hints count as a route only where a world says so.
- It remembers you by soul, so it can talk about lives you no longer
  remember: "you spared the dam keeper three lives running."

### H4 Its own journey across the save

The J model at meta scale. Its phases advance from **meta receipts**: how
many branches you made, what kinds of lives you led, whether you ever
freed, betrayed or kept a promise to it. Example shape (illustrative):
a bored keeper who treats you as a toy, then a curious one, then a
reluctant friend, then someone who wants out. Endings, all reachable:
- It hands you the seat (N9: you become the holder).
- It leaves, and the atlas changes hands to someone worse or better.
- It breaks; branches start to fray.
- It stays, as a friend at the end of time.
- Someone else (a keeper, a rival's variant) takes the seat.

### H5 What you can do with it

Talk, bargain (one change to the next premise for a price), bet on your
run, run errands across branches, lie to it, refuse it, ignore it. Its
gifts follow the [patron gift rules](CHEAT_COSMOLOGY.md#patron-gifts-what-can-and-cant-be-taken-back):
what it gives, it may take back, depending on how the gift is backed.

### H6 Discovery, not announcement

A fresh save opens small (the base-first direction in
[DIRECTION_SYNTHESIS](DIRECTION_SYNTHESIS.md)). First run: maybe nothing,
maybe a stranger who knows your name. Over runs it shows more of itself
(UC2). What it truly is gets picked per cosmology and **sealed**. Example
truths (palette, no save's pick): a former keeper, a variant of someone
you met, a past life of yours, nobody at all, a role that many have held.

### H7 Scope

- **One holder per save.** A new save gets a holder that has never met
  you. Moving it between saves is never the default.
- **Cosmology decides reach.** Worlds whose card puts nothing above C4
  never see it; others see avatars only.
- **The seat binds it.** It can barely act directly, so it acts through
  you. That's why it cares about your runs.

### H8 Engine shape and a first slice

- Meta record (H1) plus storylets keyed on memory tags (E2), cast into a
  "stranger" role in worlds where it fits.
- At a run's end, a small reducer turns that run's receipts into memory
  tags.
- Lines come from finite authored storylets, not a live model (the
  enemy-protagonist default).

**Suggested first slice (not authorized):** in the between-lives scene,
the holder says one line that cites a real fact from the run that just
ended. Acceptance: die, start a new run, save, reload, export and import
in a fresh origin; the line and its memory survive.

### More to consider

- **H9 Favorites.** It prefers one of your past lives and compares you to
  it. You can be jealous of yourself.
- **H10 Contradicted.** Your ghosts (B3) or old journals disagree with
  how it tells your story. Who's right is checkable.
- **H11 Loneliness.** At the end of time you may be its only visitor.
  An idle game knows when you were away; whether it mentions real time
  follows the [fourth-wall ladder](PRESENTATION_AND_FOURTH_WALL.md#when-can-they-reach-outside-a-ladder-of-reach)
  and stays inside R7.
- **H12 Stories as idle content.** While you're away it tells stories of
  your other lives; the chronicle in its voice.
- **H13 Heirs and many selves.** It knows your heirs and your soul's other
  lives, and may treat them as one person or many.
- **H14 A rival above runs.** A second persistent figure (a keeper
  general, a conqueror's variant) who also remembers. The north star's
  patron, jailer, puppeteer or rival roles all fit here.
- **H15 It can die mid-save.** Its replacement remembers nothing. That
  loss is felt because everything else persisted.

## Making it make sense in lore (2026-10-11)

Weibao: "Make things make sense lore wise?"

**Proposal only.** Coherence rules for keepers, variants, pruning, What
Ifs and scale characters. Builds on [lore and continuity](worlds/LORE_AND_CONTINUITY.md)
(continuity promises, evidence marks C/A/I/O/U), the
[rulebook](RULES_FROM_SCRATCH.md) and the
[cosmology layers](CHEAT_COSMOLOGY.md#layers-above-c4).

### LS1 Every big thing needs a home in the cosmology

A keeper, a Void or a god of stories exists only if the world's cosmology
card has the layer it lives in (UC1). No layer, no being; at most an
avatar or a rumor that turns out to be something smaller. Write the card
first, then the character.

### LS2 The time model decides why keepers exist

The keepers' job must follow from the world's mutability dial, or they
make no sense.

| Mutability | Why keepers would exist | If no reason fits |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed points plus soft rest | Guard the fixed points; repair the rest when it drifts too far | Fate does the repair; no keepers |
| Branching | Branches cost something: strain on a loom, collisions between worlds, a war between variants. Pruning prevents that cost | Branches are free; keepers don't exist or are a cult nobody needs |
| Closed loop | Keepers are part of the loop; their acts were always history | They only record |
| Fully mutable | Nothing to keep | Absent, extinct or obsolete (a good ruin to find) |

The same rule covers pruning: if pruning exists, say where pruned things
go and why the place doesn't overflow (the Void and its eater; a forge
that recycles; slow decay). **Every mechanic needs a cause and a sink.**

### LS3 The "why didn't it stop that" problem

If a being can see or touch every branch, players will ask why canon's
tragedies happened. Pick one answer per being, before it appears in a
book world, and keep to it:
- **Bound:** an oath, a seat or a law forbids acting (X6).
- **Blind:** it sees the course, not the details, or not this branch.
- **Indifferent:** its motive (X8) is elsewhere; local suffering isn't
  its concern. Chilling, and consistent.
- **It needs stakes:** a god of stories can't remove conflict.
- **It did act:** canon is already the better outcome; the alternatives
  were worse (show one in a What If).
- **Slow:** it acts in eras (X12); it hasn't gotten to this yet.
- **Costly:** it acts rarely because each act costs it (X5).
- **Absent:** it left, or it's dead.

### LS4 Book canon stays intact unless divergence is declared

In a book world, a scale character's acts during the canon era must be
off the page or must fit inspected canon (mark C). Anything else is an
explicit divergence or a parallel world, labeled per the
[continuity promises](worlds/LORE_AND_CONTINUITY.md#six-distinct-continuity-promises),
and marked O as an original addition. Never retcon a canon event as
"actually the keeper did it" without saying that's a divergent premise.

### LS5 It isn't the answer to everything

If one being explains every mystery, every world flattens into its
story. Most things happen without it: ordinary people, local powers,
the world's own natural course. Keep its fingerprints rare and specific,
and let local villains matter on their own axis. Higher layers relate,
they don't stack (UC3); home stays meaningful (UC5).

### LS6 Each world's laws still apply to it

A being that crosses worlds obeys each world's rules through the
existing compatibility modes (Works, Reread, Rechannel, Weakened,
Blocked). In a world without magic its avatar is rechanneled into what
that world allows: a coincidence, a stranger, a rumor. If a world blocks
its layer, it simply isn't there.

### LS7 What mortals know matches how often it acts

Frequent acts leave witnesses, so someone has noticed: a cult, a
scholarly theory, a children's rhyme. A being nobody has ever heard of
must act almost never, or only where nobody survives to report. Choose X13
from its act frequency, not the other way round.

### LS8 Variants need a declared "same"

What makes two people variants of one? Soul, name, role, pattern, body,
or a mix. Declare it per cosmology and use it everywhere: recognition,
the keepers' variant label, stage variants (J6), the holder's memory of
"you." If souls define sameness, a body-swapped you is still you; if
roles do, an heir can be your variant.

### LS9 Persistence needs a carrier

Rule 5 holds even for gods: nothing crosses runs or branches without a
carrier. A being that remembers across runs does so because its layer
is the carrier (it stands outside the branches it watches), and that has
to be true in the cosmology. A per-run copy needs no explanation. The
player's menu stays outside the fiction (the B1 rule); only a being at
the game layer treats runs as its domain.

### LS10 Motive and bindings check every scripted act

Before any scene ships, ask: would a being with this motive, these
bindings and this knowledge do this, here, now? If not, change the
scene, not the being. Fitting characters at fitting times
([fit guide](FITTING_CHARACTERS_AND_TIMES.md)) applies at every scale.

### LS11 Power has a visible cost

Unlimited power with no cost breaks lore and play. Every hard lever
costs the being something, and that cost shows somewhere in the world: a
strained loom, a fading seat, a cult's sacrifices, a weaker avatar.

### LS12 Seal the truth before writing clues

Pick a being's sealed truth (origin, identity, real motive) first and
store it sealed. Then write clues so they all fit that truth. Clues
written before the truth contradict each other; that's how mysteries
fall apart.

### LS13 It can't rewrite what you did

Later content never contradicts the player's receipts. A being can lie
about your past (with tells) or misremember (with a reason), but the
record stays true and checkable.

### Lore sense check (per scale character)

1. Which cosmology layer is it in, and does this world have it? (LS1)
2. Why does its job exist under this world's time model? (LS2)
3. Why didn't it prevent the world's tragedies? (LS3)
4. What does canon say, and is any change declared? (LS4)
5. What happens without it? (LS5)
6. How do this world's laws change it here? (LS6)
7. Who knows about it, and does that match how often it acts? (LS7)
8. What carries its memory or power across runs, if anything? (LS9)
9. What does each act cost it, and where does that show? (LS11)
10. Is its sealed truth chosen before its clues? (LS12)

### Fixes this applies to the sections above

- **Keeper defaults (D1)** now follow LS2: in a world whose mutability
  gives keepers no job, D1 is "none" or "ruins."
- **"Who chose canon?"** follows LS1 and LS12: only a cosmology with an
  author layer (C13) can have an answer, chosen and sealed before any
  clue appears.
- **Pruning (L4, N6)** follows LS2: a world must say why pruning is
  needed and where pruned things go before a Void appears there.
- **The holder example** follows LS9: its cross-run memory exists only in
  cosmologies where the game layer (C14) is real.

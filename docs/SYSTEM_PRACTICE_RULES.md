# System practice rules

How to build and tune a repeating system: ties, fights, making, care, research
and generators. **Proposed 2026-11-09 (AD32).** Weibao asked to "address the rules
accordingly" and to "reconcile docs and core rules and more as to make future dev
easier" ([request](gm/USER_VISION_LOG_2026-11.md#rules-for-building-and-tuning-systems-2026-11-09));
the lines below are agent proposals until he accepts them by name. Until then they
are prompts for briefs and receipts, not gates, and a blank answer stops nothing.

Each rule is a default with a dial and names the mistake it prevents. They were
drawn from a code-shape read on 2026-11-09 recorded in the
[long-term atlas](audits/PROGRESS_ATLAS_2026-11-07_LONG_TERM.md#action-register-for-layx24-layx56-and-other-crafts-2026-11-09)
(LAYX24-LAYX80), which keeps the evidence. Where a rule restates an accepted one,
the accepted rule governs.

## Pulling and proving a system

| Code | Rule | Mistake it prevents | How a receipt shows it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SPR1 | **A repeating system is pulled by an ordinary day.** Default: ties, fights, making, care and generators get their first caller from something a character does again and again; story windows then calibrate it. Dial: a system a world uses only at one canon event can be pulled by that window. | A system that exists only as once-only story acts. Story windows are single beats, so they pull single acts | Names the ordinary-day loop and one non-lead seat that can run it |
| SPR2 | **Count a system by what reads it.** A tie is counted by the behaviors that change with it (an offer, a favor, a refusal, an unprompted visit, a remembered act), a product by its uses, a generator by the decisions its output changes. Restates FLR4 and MR32 for every system. | State that grows with nothing reading it: axes, tags and meters with no consumer | Lists the readers added, each reachable in play |
| SPR3 | **A story beat that matches an engine runs on it.** Default: a window whose beat is a fight, a making, a lesson, a case or care binds to that engine. A beat canon makes singular stays an authored override with its reason. Old windows move when touched (AR9). | Crafts, fights and care authored inside windows, which ordinary play cannot reach | Names the engine row the beat binds to, or the override's reason |
| SPR4 | **A generator is read as a player.** Beside FND1's four lines: generate fifty, read ten, and name the decision today's output changes from yesterday's. | Output that passes validators and is bland | Ten outputs quoted or linked, with the decision named |
| SPR5 | **A new considerations round names what it changes.** On a topic that already has a palette or rulebook, a round states the accepted rule or palette row it would change. Default: if none changes, it is a pointer and the effort goes to a slice. Dial: Weibao asking for a round overrides this. | Palettes that restate themselves while the code stays where it was | The round's header names the rows it changes |
| SPR6 | **Feel systems are worth twenty minutes of play.** Human review stays optional (AD42). For fights and ties, the receipt offers Weibao a named save to try, since contract tests cannot judge tension or warmth. | "Agent-verified" standing in for a judgment only a player can make | A fixture name and what to try in it |

## Tuning by lore

These extend [AD49](RULES_INDEX.md) (lore anchors, calibrated systems). AD49 governs. SPR8 widens what counts as an anchor. Asked whether orders, counts and tolerances count, Weibao replied on 2026-11-09: "Should we decide on things in ways that make them reaosnable?" Read as yes, to the degree each is reasonable: a non-numeric anchor binds as far as the text supports it, and AD49's guard still holds (a reader row is not an anchor until checked). The other SPR lines remain proposals.

| Code | Rule | Mistake it prevents | How a receipt shows it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SPR7 | **A fit says how many of its anchors come from the source.** Each channel reports source-backed anchors, gameplay dials and unknowns separately. Zero source-backed anchors reads as "assumed". | A fit that reproduces our own numbers and is reported as tuned to lore | Anchor counts per channel with locators |
| SPR8 | **Anchor kinds follow what books state.** Default kinds: point (a price, wage or count), band, order (this beats or outranks that), count or duration, sequence, tolerance (how far off still works and which way it fails) and negative (what did not work). Dial: how strictly each kind binds, exact where the text gives a figure and banded where it is vague. | Waiting for numbers novels rarely give, while ignoring the orders and tolerances they do give | Each anchor carries its kind |
| SPR9 | **Fit the route as well as the step.** A lead taking canon choices reaches canon outcomes at a stated rate over many seeds, with no grind (XM41). Default: the canon outcome sits inside the reachable band. Where the book calls it lucky or desperate it may be a low-odds result plus the lead's own edge. Dial: the target reach rate per world. | Ten steps at 80% each making an 11% route; or every canon result pinned as the typical result, which makes the world too easy for everyone else | Reach rate per canon event over the seeds run |
| SPR10 | **Failures are anchors.** A failed brew, a lost fight or a refused trust is recorded with the same care as a success. Under AD5 a negative is the ordinary case with a reason, open to exceptions with an in-world cost. | A law fitted only to successes, which fits by allowing everything | Negative anchors listed beside positive ones |
| SPR11 | **An outlier is one of three things:** a canon exception (an authored override with its reason), a missing part of the model, or canon disagreeing with itself (keep the earlier or more specific statement and record the other). | Hunting for a missing mechanism behind an author's inconsistency | Each outlier's class |
| SPR12 | **A refit is a new law revision.** Things made or earned under the old revision keep it (MR30, AR9), and the fixtures that proved earlier slices are replayed or marked for re-earning. | A tuned number quietly changing what an earlier receipt proved | The revision, and which fixtures were replayed |
| SPR13 | **Where canon is silent, say whose number it is.** Each dial is canon (with a locator), borrowed (a period wage, a travel time, a sibling source, named) or assumed. Variance fills only what the source leaves unstated. Borrowed has ranks, proposed after Weibao asked (2026-11-09) why values cannot come from elsewhere ("search online if anyone analyzed it, or find lore books, or look at the games? there are manmy ways?"): the author's own later writing and companion material, then licensed games and guides, then careful fan analysis, then the real-world counterpart. A lower rank yields to a higher one, and a game's numbers keep their order and ratios but not their time or price scale, which was tuned for that game. | Borrowed and invented numbers read as canon later | The basis beside each dial |
| SPR14 | **Tune ties to behavior and fights to what decided them.** A tie's anchor is "by this chapter, this person does this for the lead". A fight's anchor is who won, what it cost and what decided it (preparation, information, a tool, an ally), before any health total. | Fitting meters nobody in the book ever mentions | The behavior or deciding factor each anchor names |
| SPR20 | **Results are the contract, numbers are the implementation.** Asked about non-numeric anchors, Weibao said (2026-11-09): "build numbers based off of results?" Default: where the book gives outcomes and no figures (who won, the brew failed, she lent the money), each outcome becomes a constraint the system has to reproduce, and the numbers are whatever satisfies all of them. Their basis is "derived from N results" (SPR13). A number may be retuned freely while every result still comes out. Dial: how many results a number needs behind it before it is treated as more than assumed. | Inventing a figure and then defending it as canon; or refusing to tune a system because the book gave no figures | The results each number was derived from, as replayable cases |
| SPR21 | **Results fix order and ratio; something else fixes scale.** Outcomes say A exceeds B, never by how many units. Default: each channel takes one point of scale from a stated figure where the book has one (a price, a wage, a duration), or declares its unit as assumed. The fit also reports slack: how far each number can move before a result breaks. Slack is the room left for gameplay tuning. | Numbers that satisfy every result at an arbitrary scale, then collide with a neighbouring system that picked a different one | The scale point or "unit assumed", and slack per number |
| SPR22 | **Hold some results back.** Default: derive from part of the results and test on the rest (other rungs, other fights, later chapters). A model that needs a new number for every new result has the wrong mechanism (SPR11), and the lead's own wins are weak evidence because the story selected them (SPR9). | Numbers that absorb a wrong mechanism, fitting everything seen and predicting nothing | Which results derived the numbers and which tested them |

## Ties between people

Added 2026-11-09 from a trace of the tie code (`person_ties.js`, the native `relationship` effect, `person_memory_effects.js`). The [relationship palette](RELATIONSHIPS_AND_CANON_CONSIDERATIONS_2026-10-18.md) stays the owner of what ties can mean; these lines are about building them. The [area guide](AREA_GUIDE.md) names the entry points.

| Code | Rule | Mistake it prevents | How a receipt shows it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SPR15 | **One person record, one way in.** A tie changes through the native `relationship` effect, declared per world with `registerWorldTies` and written with `tieEffects` under a per-occasion cause. Each change pairs the holder's real regard with what the player saw, and views show the note and not the number (AD6-AD8). Default: no world keeps its own regard store. | A second relationship ledger inside a world file, or a number shown to the player that their character could not know | The declaration and the cause used |
| SPR16 | **A threshold names a behavior, and there is more than one kind.** The offer (regard opens a conversation or an action) exists. The others are a favor (a price, a wait or terms change), a refusal, a visit or message the person starts, a remembered act they cite, and vouching to an audience. Default: a person who carries a tie gets at least one reader that is not an offer. Dial: background people can carry a tie with one reader. | Every friendship in the game expressing itself as one unlocked dialogue line | The reader shapes added, per person |
| SPR17 | **Say which regard a gate reads.** The raw axis is a baseline. Regard that fades, or that depends on the holder's temperament, is read through `derivedBond`. Default: lead-circle ties read the baseline (canon holds them steady); ordinary acquaintances read the derived value once a world supplies temperaments. | A friendship that never fades next to one that does, with nothing saying why | The read used and the reason |
| SPR18 | **A person's warmth is theirs.** How fast someone warms or cools is a trait of that person, set per person or per temperament, with canon pace as the anchor where the book shows it (SPR14). | One global gain rate that makes every character equally easy to befriend | Where the rate comes from |
| SPR19 | **An audience is not a tie, and a creature can hold one.** What a house, a street or an institution thinks is standing, read through `reputation_view`. A bond with a creature, a familiar or a Gu is a tie on the same record (MR41), counted by its own readers. | Standing stored as a tie with a crowd, or a "pet affection" meter beside the person record | Which of the two each new number is |

# E8B. Power catalog: more families, sources and conditions (proposal)

Claude, 2026-10-10. Weibao: "more types of powers to consider? expand
accordingly". Companion to [E8 power aspects](E8_POWER_ASPECTS.md), which owns
the core model (an idea plus ten axes), laws and facets, shards, and the
observation, future sight and immortality catalogs. This file adds more
families, where power comes from, how it is delivered, and the conditions
that shape it. All original proposals; trope names are generic. Under
[everything is possible, reasonably](../USER_VISION.md#anything-is-possible-2026-10-09),
every family exists somewhere, gated by world, price and limit.

Each row gives the idea, **variants** that differ along E8's axes, the
usual **price or limit**, and the **engine hook** (which layer it rides on).

## Where power comes from (source axis)

Every family above can come from any source, and the source changes the
play.

| Source | Gained by | Lost by | Distinct play |
|---|---|---|---|
| Innate | Birth, bloodline, constitution | Rarely; curses | Fixed core, grows with you |
| Trained | Practice, study, life record | Disuse decays it | The vanilla way |
| Granted | A god, patron, system, contract | Revocation | A relationship to manage |
| Stolen | Taking from others | Theft back | Enemies with a claim |
| Item-bound | An artifact | Losing the item | Gear is identity |
| Consumed | Eating, absorbing | Digestion fades it | Hunting for power |
| Faith or fame | Believers, legends | Being forgotten | Social play |
| Technology | Devices, implants | Breakage, power supply | Repair and upgrades |

## Conditions and vows: limits that make power stronger

A common genre move worth having: **accept a restriction, gain strength.**
- "Only against beasts": twice as strong against beasts.
- "Never in daylight": stronger at night than an unbound version.
- "I must announce it first": stronger, but enemies get warning.
- "If I break this vow I lose the power forever": much stronger now.

Proposal: a vow is a personal rule (E4) that both limits and amplifies,
with a stated penalty for breaking it. Vows can be taken, given to others
(an oath of service), inherited, or forced (a curse that is really a vow
someone else wrote). Breaking one is possible by default, with its stated price.

## Gaming vows and rules (Weibao, 2026-10-10)

> Abusing rules in funny ways like vowing to not eat tacos for 20 days for a
> 5x power increase and more?

Yes, reasonably. The question each world answers is **how it prices a vow**.

### How a world values a vow

No global default: **each world picks its own pricing**, and many worlds
mix several. Weibao (2026-10-10): "it can be gaming the system, it can be a
boon even if they never eat tacos, cause there are jokes online about
sukuna doing stuff like that." A vow can pay off with zero real sacrifice
when the world's pricing allows it. That is gaming the system, and it is
allowed.

| Pricing | How vows are valued | Taco vow from someone who never eats tacos |
|---|---|---|
| True cost | By your real sacrifice, from your life record and E3 needs | Little, unless you invest in tacos first |
| Literal | By the words; the world doesn't check | Full 5x; loophole heaven (fae courts, genie worlds) |
| Will | By how sincerely and fiercely you mean it | Strong if you mean it with your whole soul, even about tacos |
| Arrogance | By how sure you are you can keep it; strong wills set their own price | A monster who declares a silly vow with total confidence gets paid (the joke vow made by a terrifying villain) |
| Absurdity | Trickster or chaos powers reward silly vows | The sillier, the stronger; taco vows are prized |
| Novelty | The first person ever to make a given vow gets the most | First taco vow in history: big; copycats: less |
| Mischievous | The world pays, then twists | 5x power, and tacos start appearing everywhere you go |
| Audience | By how many witness it | A taco vow announced mid-battle to a stunned crowd is strong |
| Patron | A god or spirit decides | One god adores taco vows; another is offended |
| Market | By what others are vowing | Taco vows crash once everyone does it |
| System | A game-like system prices it and can patch it | Works until "the System has adjusted taco vows" |

### Funny abuse, with reasonable consequences

- **Invest in the sacrifice.** Eat tacos daily for a year so the vow costs
  more, then swear off them. Under true-cost pricing, this works. The year of
  tacos goes in your life record, and people call you the Taco Ascetic.
- **Stack tiny vows.** No tacos, no blue shirts, no whistling on Tuesdays.
  Default: each vow adds less (diminishing returns per world). Dial: a
  literal world may let them stack absurdly.
- **Vow what you can't do anyway.** "I won't fly." Worth nothing unless you
  can fly; worth a lot for a bird person.
- **Vow on others' behalf.** A parent vows for a child, or a lord for a
  village. Allowed where the world allows proxies; the bound people may
  object (E3).
- **Wording tricks.** "No tacos" while eating burritos; "for 20 days" in a
  world with 40-hour days. A literal world honors it. A true-cost world
  sees through it. A mischievous world laughs and charges you.
- **Craving as the price.** Breaking a vow stays an option with its
  stated penalty. Meanwhile the abstained thing becomes an E3 need that
  grows (taco hunger), and NPCs can tempt you.
- **Stories write themselves.** A rival opens a taco stand outside your
  door. A sect forms around taco abstinence. A legend (E6) records the
  Taco Vow, and future heirs inherit the reputation.

### More ways to game vows

- **Shop for a world.** Make your taco vow where pricing is literal or
  absurd, then carry the power home (per-edge carry rules apply).
- **Vow arbitrage.** Buy cheap vows in a market world, sell the power or the
  binding elsewhere.
- **Joke vows as identity.** A character known for ridiculous vows gets
  E6 legends, nicknames and fans; their next silly vow has an audience.
- **Vows that make you a target.** Everyone now knows you can't eat tacos;
  a poisoner hides venom in everything except tacos.
- **Vows that bind the world back.** Under some pricings the world must
  keep its side: no taco may be forced on you, so a taco-based trap fails.
- **Exploit discovery as play.** Finding a pricing hole earns a title,
  spreads as gossip (E3), gets copied, and can get countered or patched
  over time (E8C "counters spread").
- **Gag-physics worlds.** Some worlds run on comedy logic, where jokes are
  rules and a pie to the face can matter more than a sword. Taco vows
  belong there.
- **Cheats about vows.** A cheat that makes any vow count at full value,
  one that lets you break vows without penalty once, or one that lets you
  vow on behalf of the world.

### Rule abuse in general

Gaming rules is play, not a bug. That holds for vow wording, tag
combinations (E4), substitution profiles (E4B), contract clauses, odds
manipulation and path mixing.
- **Default:** the world honors what its rules say, and the log shows how.
- **Reasonably:** the world's own rules, people and storytellers react the
  way they would in-world. Lawyers counter-sue, patrons get annoyed, a
  famous exploit spreads and gets countered (E8C "counters spread").
- **Firm lines** (from the rulebook): save or reload bugs and real-world
  guardrails. Everything in-world is fair game.

## Combining families

Most of the busted builds come from crossing families:
- Copying plus consumption: copy what you eat.
- Marks plus delay: plant marks for a year, collect all at once.
- Fame plus illusion: a legend that isn't real still makes you strong.
- Negation plus contracts: an oath that nobody may use powers here.
- Multiplication plus leadership: you are your own sect.
- Storage plus time: a pocket where a day lasts a year (training).
- Truth-bound plus words of power: everything you say happens, and you
  can't lie, so you choose words very carefully.

## Generating subtle variants

To roll a power for an NPC, rival or a new cheat (E8 "variants for
everyone"):
1. Pick a family from the world's table (E6), weighted by lore.
2. Pick a source and a delivery mode.
3. Sample the ten axes inside the world's limits.
4. Add one condition or vow with chance (makes it stronger and stranger).
5. Name it with the world's grammar (E6) and give it a lore layer (E7).
6. Check its counters exist somewhere in the world (fairness for what the
   world throws at you; E5).

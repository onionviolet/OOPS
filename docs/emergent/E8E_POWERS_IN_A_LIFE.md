# E8E. Powers in a life (proposal)

Claude, 2026-10-10. Weibao: "more to consider? expand accordingly". E8 to
E8D cover what powers are, how they grow and how vows price them. This file
covers what a life sim needs on top: how a power arrives, how you learn
what it does, what it does to everyday life, family, work, society, your
personality, and what happens when it's lost. Per the
[rulebook](../RULES_FROM_SCRATCH.md), each world sets its own rules from its
lore. Proposal only.

**There are and are not absolutes (Weibao, 2026-10-10).** Nothing here is a
rule for every world. A world can have its own absolutes as lore (a law of
that world that truly holds there), other worlds can lack them, and the same
thing can happen several different ways in different places. Every line
below describes one or more ways, not the way.

## 1. Awakening: how a power arrives

| Way in | What it feels like in play | Dial |
|---|---|---|
| Born with it | Present from childhood; shows early as odd incidents (E7 oddities around the child) | How early, how visible |
| Coming of age | A ceremony or a set age; family and town watch | Public or private; whether it can fail |
| Trigger event | Awakens in a moment of extreme stress (near death, grief, terror); the power fits the moment | How dark the trigger can be; whether you can seek one |
| Slow accumulation | Practice, study or exposure until it "clicks" (vanilla skills) | How long; visible progress or a surprise |
| Granted | A patron, system or ritual gives it; comes with terms (E8D) | Who offers and why |
| Taken | Stolen, eaten, inherited from the dead | What the previous owner left in it |
| Accident | Alchemy, a cursed relic, a material reaction (E4B Q12) | Odds; strange side effects |
| Late bloom | Arrives decades in, or after a death (E8B "delayed self") | How rare |

**The first days matter.** A new power should be a story arc, not a
popup: confusion, an accident, someone noticing, a choice to hide or show
it, a first teacher or exploiter.

## 2. Discovery: learning your own power

The power's axes are real from the start (truthful world), but you don't
know them. Learning them is play:
- **Tests.** Small safe actions reveal one axis each (range: walk away
  until it stops; trigger: try it calm and afraid). Each test logs what it
  showed in the Notebook.
- **Accidents** teach the hard way.
- **Mentors and appraisers** can read some axes (E7 observe kinds), maybe
  wrongly.
- **Other holders** of a similar power share notes, or lie.
- **Limits found in the field:** the power fails at the worst moment, and
  now you know.
- **Names.** You name the power yourself (or the world does); the name
  shapes how people treat it, and in some worlds it shapes the power
  (E7 true names, E8 interpretation growth).

## 3. Everyday uses

Most of a life isn't fighting. Any power should have a mundane side, and
the game should reward finding it.

| Power | Everyday uses |
|---|---|
| Fire | Cooking, smelting without coal, heating a home, clearing fields, a lantern business |
| Ice | Food storage, cool drinks to sell, preserving medicine |
| Strength | Construction, moving house, mining, farm work |
| Danger sense | A guide for travelers, a safety inspector, a gambler |
| Observation | Appraiser, detective, matchmaker, doctor |
| Healing | Clinic, midwife, training partner, a target for kidnappers |
| Space or storage | Courier, smuggler, warehouse keeper |
| Plants | Farmer, herbalist, gardener to nobles |
| Illusion | Theater, advertising, fraud |
| Vows | Contract notary, oath witness, matchmaker for binding marriages |

In many worlds everyday uses run through vanilla skills and jobs (Farming,
Cooking, Smelting, the village career), with the power as a bonus or a new
method, so ordinary skills stay useful (north star). In others a power
replaces the skill outright, or the skill is the power. A power used daily for
mundane work can grow facets that combat never would (E8 "a skill is a
doorway").

## 4. Work and the economy

- **Power jobs.** Courier, healer, appraiser, guard, weather-caller. A
  village career can take a power as a requirement or a bonus.
- **Disruption.** A teleporter undercuts the carters' guild; an ice mage
  ruins the icehouse business. The displaced react (E3 grudges, guild
  actions, E2 cards), and prices shift.
- **Licensing and law.** Some places require registration, ban certain
  powers, or tax them (E8C). Unlicensed use can be a crime.
- **Hiring powers.** NPCs with powers can be hired, contracted (E8D) or
  recruited as followers.
- **Selling the power itself.** Formulas, shards, fruits, contracts,
  lessons: a market with prices by world and rarity.

## 5. Society and identity

- **How people see power holders.** Admired, feared, used, hunted,
  worshipped, pitied. Each world and place sets its attitude, and each
  person's traits (E3) adjust it.
- **Hiding versus showing.** Hidden power avoids attention and loses the
  benefits of being known (fame-fed powers, jobs, teachers). Being found out
  is a story beat.
- **Power and personality.** Some powers push their holder: a fire power
  makes you hot-tempered over time, a shard pulls toward its goals, a
  borrowed power carries its patron's wishes. Ways it can go: no
  drift; slow drift shown in traits and resistible by will; fast and
  unstoppable; or drift you welcome. Set per power and world.
- **Corruption and madness.** Paths with madness (E8C), forbidden
  knowledge (E7 K6), consumed powers (you become what you eat).
- **Reputation by power.** Titles and nicknames from how you use it
  ("the Kind Flame"), recorded in legends (E6).

## 6. Family and lineage

- **Children may inherit** a parent's power, a mix of both, a weaker or
  stronger form, or something new. Per world: bloodline rules, mutation
  rates, latent powers that skip generations.
- **Family attitudes**: proud, ashamed, exploitative. A family business
  built on a power.
- **Clans and houses** built around a bloodline power, with marriage
  politics to keep or spread it.
- **Heirs** inherit what was left behind (rulebook): records of how the
  power worked, a teacher who knew it, an item it was sworn on, a shard.
- **Children with powers** need different raising: accidents, schools,
  hiding them, protecting them from recruiters.

## 7. Teaching and lineage of technique

Many ways, varying by world, lineage and person:
- **What teaching costs the teacher**: nothing; time; some of their power
  (a world where teaching splits it); a vow or a debt; or teaching makes the
  teacher stronger (worlds where explaining deepens understanding, or a
  sect that feeds its master).
- **How students learn**: copying exactly; changing the technique as they
  learn it, so lineages branch into schools (E8C); instant transfer by touch,
  blood or a system; or learning only from the dead (inheritances, records).
- **How a technique survives**: through students, books, items it was
  sworn on, a soul that remembers, a world that preserves it, or not at all
  (lost arts, E8C).
- **Books and manuals**: slower than a teacher in one world, as good as one
  in another, better in a world where the written word holds power.
- **Errors**: teaching wrong can spread mistakes that later generations
  treat as truth, or the world can correct them, depending on its lore.

## 8. Partners, combos and rivals

- **Combination moves** between two holders whose powers chain (fire plus
  wind). They need practice together and trust (E3 relationship).
- **Synergy couples and teams**: powers that unlock each other's facets.
- **Natural rivals** whose powers counter each other; the world (E5) can
  bring them together.
- **Power bonds**: shared vows (E8D), shared fate (E5), shared shards (E8).

## 9. When powers clash

One common way to resolve two contradicting powers ("I see everything"
versus "I can't be seen"); worlds use others:
1. **Mechanism first**: does one power act on something the other actually
   protects? If not, both work (seeing what isn't hidden by that method).
2. **Layer**: personal rules against place or world rules (E4 layer
   order).
3. **Tier**: concept tier or facet depth (north star tiers).
4. **Contest**: a stat or will roll from the `encounter` stream, with the
   odds shown where the holders could know them.
5. **Price**: either side may pay more (a vow, a sacrifice) to win.

Other worlds resolve by tier alone, by the stronger will, by whoever acted
first, by an absolute law of that world (one power simply wins there), or
let both happen and make a paradox event.

## 10. Losing a power

- **Ways to lose it**: sealed, stolen, burned out, crippled meridians, a
  vow broken, a patron's withdrawal, a world without that energy, old age.
- **Living without it**: the life continues; the career, relationships and
  identity built on it are tested. A strong life-sim arc.
- **Recovery**: rehabilitation, a new path, a substitute (E4B
  substitution for powers: a different source filling the same role),
  getting it back from whoever has it.
- **Phantom power**: reaching for a lost power out of habit; a log line,
  a small penalty, a scene.

## 11. Powers while idle

- **Standing orders for powers**: "heal anyone below half health," "never
  use fire indoors," "keep my vows" (E8D).
- **Passive growth** from everyday use while idle, at the rate of the work
  (E8E section 3).
- **Upkeep**: toggled and channeled powers drain while idle unless turned
  off; the away rule (Q8 mode) decides.
- **Offline events**: a power can still trigger while you're away (a danger
  sense wakes your character), with a catch-up summary.

## 12. Balance in an "everything possible" game

Balance isn't forced. Weibao (2026-10-10): "it doesn't have to be forced,
unless well, it is sent by heaven or something, but if keeping low enough
profile or op enough might go unchecked."

**Ways a strong power can go:**
- **Unchecked.** Nobody notices, or nobody can do anything. A low profile,
  a remote place, an indifferent world or simply being strong enough means
  nothing comes for you. That's allowed and can be the whole point of a
  life.
- **Checked by the world, when it has a reason to.** A world with a will, a
  heaven that sends tribulations, a system that patches exploits, or a
  storyteller instance set to answer power (E5) can respond. Whether it
  does is that world's lore.
- **Checked by people, when they notice and care.** Rivals, institutions,
  the displaced, the jealous (E3). Fame and visibility make this likelier;
  hiding, fear or allies make it less likely.
- **Checked by its own price,** if it has one. Some powers have none in
  their world.

**What changes the odds** (dials, not rules):

| Factor | Toward unchecked | Toward checked |
|---|---|---|
| Profile | Hidden, quiet, far away | Famous, flashy, central |
| Strength | Too strong for anyone local to answer | Strong enough to worry others but beatable |
| World will | Indifferent world | A heaven that hates outliers, a jealous system |
| Society | Lawless, sparse | Lawful, crowded, rival-rich |
| Storyteller | Gentle, steady | Wild, fated, set to answer power |
| Allies | Protected, feared, useful to the powerful | Alone |

**When the world does push back,** it can take many forms: counters
spreading, institutions adapting, rivals studying you (E8C), threats scaled
to your attention (E5), new kinds of problems for new powers (a teleporter
gets spies and guild wars, not wolves), or heaven's tribulations. None of
these is required.

**Mundane value** of ordinary skills holds where powers run through them
(section 3); in worlds where a power simply replaces a skill, it may not.

## 13. Showing it

- **Power sheet** in the bio: known axes, unknown "?" axes, tests done,
  vows attached, facets, path stage, history.
- **First-use moments** with a heavier text effect (north star).
- **Everyday-use hints** in center-menu actions where a power changes the
  job ("Smelt (your fire saves coal)").
- **Clash logs** that walk through the resolution steps.

## Smallest proof

Village: one awakening (a trigger card from E2 that grants a small
fire-family power with three hidden axes), two tests that reveal axes, one
everyday use (cooking and smelting without coal through existing
actions), one NPC reaction (the charcoal seller's grudge), and a power
sheet. Test: two fresh saves get different axes for the same awakening,
the axes stay stable through save, reload and import, and the charcoal
seller remembers.

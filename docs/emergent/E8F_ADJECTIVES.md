## The idea

Everything in the game is **a noun plus adjectives**. A power, a limit, an
item, a trial, a person, a place, a vow, a world. Changing one adjective
makes a new variant. "Once" is not a rule; it is one value of the
adjective *how often*.

This is not a new system on top of E8 and E4. It is a **shared vocabulary**
for them: E8 axes are adjective families, E4 tags with strength are
adjectives with a level, and the variant generator is something that picks
adjectives.

## Adjective families

Each family is a **ladder**: ordered words, each with a level. Worlds can
add rungs, rename them or stretch the numbers.

| Family | Ladder (low to high, examples) | Maps to |
|---|---|---|
| How often | never, once, twice, rarely, sometimes, daily, per fight, per breath, at will, endlessly | E8 trigger, cooldown |
| How much | faintly, slightly, partly, mostly, fully, overwhelmingly, absurdly | Strength, E4 level |
| How sure | unreliably, sometimes, usually, always, inevitably, fatefully | E8 certainty |
| How fast | glacially, slowly, eventually, quickly, instantly, before it happens | Startup, delay |
| How long | briefly, for a fight, for a day, for a season, for life, across lives, forever | E4 decay, E8 persistence |
| How wide | self, touched, near, room, town, world, across worlds | E8 scope |
| What it targets | self, people, things, places, concepts, time, rules | E8 object |
| How it works | sensed, known, shown, recorded, rewritten | E8 mechanism |
| How costly | free, cheap, dear, ruinous, paid by another, paid later | E8 price |
| How known | secret, whispered, rumored, famous, legendary, forgotten | E8 visibility, E6 legends |
| How visible | invisible, subtle, noticeable, blatant, blinding | E8 visibility |
| How it feels | soothing, numbing, painful, euphoric, addictive, boring | E3 moods |
| How it ends | fades, breaks, rebounds, transfers, lingers, never ends | Termination |
| Who can | only you, your blood, the taught, the paying, anyone | Access |
| How tame | obedient, willful, wild, hostile | Summons, curses, items with will |
| How pure | raw, refined, perfect, corrupted | Quality, E4B materials |
| How old | new, seasoned, ancient, primordial | Age, history (E8C) |

## Grammar

Adjectives combine like words, and the grammar is where the subtlety is.

| Form | Example | Meaning in play |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain** | a *sharp* blade | One rung of one ladder |
| **Intensified** | *very* sharp, *barely* sharp | Move a rung up or down |
| **Comparative** | *sharper than* her blade | Relative to a target; resolves at use |
| **Superlative** | *the sharpest* blade | Only one holder at a time; can be contested and lost |
| **Conditional** | sharp *only at night*, *except against kin* | A clause; E8D vow slots and E4 rule conditions |
| **Negated** | *unbreakable*, *unseen* | The absence of an end or a sense |
| **Compound** | *quietly lethal*, *slowly certain* | Two families at once |
| **Paradoxical** | a *silent bell*, *cold fire* | Two adjectives that clash; the world decides: one wins, both hold strangely, or it becomes an outlier (rule 12) |
| **Relative to world** | *strong* here, *ordinary* there | Same word, different number per world (rule 11, verse equalization) |
| **Adverbs on actions** | fish *carefully*, fight *recklessly* | Manner of an action: slower and safer, faster and riskier |
| **Titles** | *the* Taco Ascetic, *the* Rat Butcher | Adjectives earned by deeds, worn by people |

## How adjectives change

**2026-10-15 title extension:** evolution can branch, compose, fuse, split or
be reinterpreted; titles can belong to items, places and worlds as well as
people. [E4 C9-C13](TITLES_ACHIEVEMENTS_AND_META_RULES_2026-10-15.md#c9-evolving-and-combining-titles-and-achievements)
owns proposed transformation, transfer, holder identity and criterion-editing
semantics. Combining names need not combine powers, while a literal synthesis
cheat may make it do so. The operations below remain possible dials.

| Operation | Example | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Climb** | *rarely* to *daily* | Mastery, practice, growth paths |
| **Trade** | *always* in exchange for *only at night* | Vows (E8D) |
| **Add** | make a blade *burning* | Enchanting, blessing, tagging (E4) |
| **Remove** | strip *cursed* | Dispel, cleansing, suppression |
| **Invert** | *blessed* to *cursed* | Curses, corruption, reversal techniques (JJK reverse) |
| **Steal** | take a rival's *swift* | Theft powers (Universal Thief style, ability doc) |
| **Copy** | gain *swift* without taking it | Copy powers |
| **Lend** | give *brave* to a follower for a day | Leadership, buffs |
| **Shift by world** | *at will* at home, *rarely* here | World entry rules (rule 2, 5) |
| **Decay** | *famous* fades to *forgotten* | Time, E4 decay, E6 legends |
| **Grow by use** | a blade becomes *beloved* after years | Item history |
| **Contest** | fight for *the strongest* | Superlatives |

**Stacking.** Two sources of the same adjective combine by the world's
rule: best only, sum with diminishing, sum fully, or clash. E4 already has
`stacking: 'max' | 'sum'`; adjectives add `diminishing` and `clash`.

## Reading adjectives (rule 6)

- **Depth of reading.** A novice sees *sharp*. An expert sees *sharp,
  brittle, recently reforged*. A seer sees *cursed, but only for its
  maker's kin*.
- **Hidden adjectives.** Some are concealed (E4 `visible: 'hidden'`) and
  learned by study or accident (E7).
- **Wrong labels.** A merchant's *blessed* may be false; a rumor of
  *invincible* may be *usually*.
- **Your Notebook** stores adjectives as you know them, with confidence.

## Adjectives as an economy

- **Buy and sell** adjectives: an enchanter sells *burning*; a fence buys
  *stolen-looking* off an item.
- **Adjective shards:** a fragment that holds *swift* and can be fitted
  to an item, a weapon or a person.
- **Crafting** is often adding or removing adjectives (E4B materials,
  item states).
- **Bloodlines and schools** pass down adjectives (*keen-eyed* family).
- **Market drift:** a common adjective gets cheap; a famous one is
  counterfeited (E8C counters spread).

## Worlds where adjectives are literal power

- **Word magic.** Speaking an adjective of something true changes it
  (true names, E7). Mispronounce and you get a different rung.
- **Legal worlds.** Contracts read adjectives exactly; *daily* means
  every calendar day, and travel across time zones gets exploited.
- **Comedy worlds.** Adjectives obey jokes: *very* very very sharp is
  sharper (E8B gag physics).
- **Concept worlds** (*A Novel Concept*): concepts climb stages, which
  read like ladders of the same idea.
- **Status-window worlds.** Adjectives appear as rarities and ranks
  (common, rare, epic, legendary, mythic).

## Extreme ends (outliers, rule 12)

The far ends of ladders exist, rare and strange: *endlessly*,
*retroactively*, *before it happens*, *for everyone who ever lived*,
*across all worlds*, *absolutely*. A power holding one is an outlier,
recorded as such, never a new general rule. Interesting powers often have
one extreme adjective and several humble ones ("*absolutely* certain, but
*only* about *tomorrow's weather*").

## Generation and text

- **Variant generator** (E8B step 3) samples adjectives from each world's
  ladders, weighted by lore, plus one conditional clause by chance.
- **Descriptions** write themselves: noun plus the two or three most
  notable adjectives ("a *rare*, *certain*, *costly* foresight").
- **Names** come from adjectives through the world's grammar (E6): the
  *Silent Bell*, the *Seventh-Day Vow*.
- **Legibility:** the UI can show any thing as a short sentence and let
  you expand the full adjective list.

## Engine shape

```js
// one adjective on one noun
{ family: 'how_often', word: 'daily', level: 5,
  clause: { when: 'night' },           // optional conditional
  source: 'vow:seventh_day',           // who or what gave it
  until: 5820,                         // optional, E4 decay
  known: 'hidden' }                    // reading depth (E7)

// a world's ladder (registry)
{ family: 'how_often', world: 'base.frontier',
  rungs: ['never','once','twice','rarely','sometimes','daily','per_fight','at_will','endlessly'],
  numbers: { per_day: [0, 0, 0, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 5, 99, Infinity] } }
```

Adjectives can live as E4 tags with a family and level, so rules,
counters and conditions already understand them. No save change until a
slice selects it; then a migration.

### Text markup

Proposed token, so writers never type HTML:

```
You draw the {adj:burning}blade{/adj}. It hums {adj:rarely|faintly}.
The rumor says it is {adj:blessed?}blessed{/adj}.     (? = unconfirmed)
```

The renderer turns a token into
`<span class="adj adj-burning" data-family="element" data-level="3"
aria-label="burning">...</span>`, and checks the word against the
registry (unknown words render plain, never break the line). The same
renderer serves the log, tooltips, the Notebook and item names.

### Sound (optional, later)

The game has no audio, so sound is a new layer. If added: muted by
default; one short cue per family (crackle for fire, chime for blessed,
low hum for cursed, a wheel click for adaptation); a volume slider; never
required to understand anything.

### Comfort and performance

- **Reduced motion:** honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and the in-game
  setting; every effect has a static version (color, icon, underline).
- **No harsh flashing:** flashes stay under three per second (the WCAG
  three-flashes guideline) and can be turned off.
- **Color is never the only signal:** each family also has an icon or a
  text style, for color-blind readers.
- **Screen readers** read the plain word (`aria-label`), not the effect.
- **Performance:** cap animated words on screen (for example 20), stop
  animations in hidden tabs and scrolled-off log lines, prefer CSS
  `transform` and `opacity` animations.
- **Idle logs get long:** old lines drop their animations and keep only
  color.

### Little details

- **Adjective order** follows English habit (opinion, size, age, color,
  origin, material) so generated names read naturally: *a beloved small
  old blade*, not *a old small beloved blade*.
- **Articles:** *a* or *an* chosen from the final adjective.
- **Pluralization and agreement** handled by the renderer, per locale.
- **Two or three adjectives** in a name; the rest in the tooltip. The two
  shown are the most unusual for that world, not the strongest.
- **Synonyms by voice:** a world or narrator can say *keen* instead of
  *sharp*; the family and level stay the same.
- **Comparatives resolve late:** *sharper than her blade* is computed when
  it matters, so it stays true as both blades change.
- **Ranges for uncertain readings:** *sharp to very sharp* when your
  reading depth is low.
- **History in the tooltip:** "*beloved* since year 12", "*stolen* from
  Oren".
- **Click to pin** an adjective to watch it change (a decaying blessing,
  a growing fame).
- **Diffs:** when an adjective changes, the log shows the change once
  (*rarely* → *daily*).
- **Notebook glossary:** every adjective you have met, its ladder as far
  as you know it, and where you saw it.
- **NPC speech uses adjectives** they can read; a blind NPC never calls
  your sword *gleaming*.

### More to consider

- **Adjectives on the UI itself.** A *cursed* save slot title, a *haunted*

- **Adjectives on time.** *Auspicious* hours highlighted in the clock;

- **Player-written adjectives.** Rename items with custom adjectives that

### The "more to consider" list, expanded

**Adjectives on the interface.**
- Panels can wear the current place's or power's adjectives: a *haunted*
  inventory with a faint ghost duplicate on hover, a domain tinting the
  whole screen, a *cursed* item whose tooltip resists opening for a moment.
- Rules: always readable, always reversible, off in reduced-motion mode,
  never hiding a button.
- The save slot list shows each life's top adjective (*the Restless*,
  *the Drowned*), which doubles as the atlas preview.

**Adjectives on time.**
- The clock and calendar carry adjectives: *auspicious* hours glow faintly,
  *cursed* days darken, *festival* days get a ribbon, *tribulation season*
  a storm edge.
- Idle planning uses them: schedule a breakthrough attempt for an
  *auspicious* hour; avoid trading on a *cursed* day.
- Readings can be wrong: a fortune teller's *auspicious* may be a scam.

**Player-written adjectives.**
- Rename anything with your own words. By default they are flavor only.
- In word-magic worlds they can matter: a name you give sticks, and if
  enough people repeat it, the world starts to believe it (E6 legends).
- Abuse is fine (solo player): naming your spoon *the Sharpest* is
  allowed; a literal world might make it a contest you now have to defend.

**Adjective collections.**
- A journal of rungs reached per family, with where and when.
- Rare rungs as trophies (*fatefully*, *primordial*).
- Ladder completion as a quiet long-term goal across lives; heirs can
  inherit the journal.

**Language drift.**
- Over centuries, words change: *keen* turns old-fashioned; legends keep
  the old words (E6).
- Visiting a past era shows older synonyms; a future era shows new slang.
- A drifted word can mislead: an old manual's *safe* meant something else.

**Translation between worlds.**
- A family can be missing from a world's language: the tooltip reads "the
  locals have no word for this".
- A word can exist but mean a different rung: *strong* in a weak world
  sits lower than *strong* in a strong one (rule 11).
- Learning a world's language reveals its adjectives one by one.

**Generated poetry.**
- Epitaphs from a life's top adjectives and deeds: "*Slow*, *stubborn*,
  *beloved*; she fished the cold lake for sixty years."
- Legends, ballads and gossip lines from the same data, with the world's
  voice (E6 grammar).
- Rival epitaphs written by people who hated you.

**Debug view.** Dev mode shows family, level, source and expiry beside
each word, plus a slider to scrub a decaying adjective through time.

### When the game moves beyond text

The game is text and HTML today, with an endorsed direction for a
[Hanzi glyph field](../WANDERING_ZONES_AND_INSIGHT.md#hanzi-field-preference-and-native-screen-composition-2026-10-02)
(2026-10-02) and a NetHack-style fieldcraft preview. Adjectives are the
right bridge to any future presentation, because they are **data about
what a thing is like**, separate from how it is drawn.

**The rule that keeps the future open:** content stores adjectives and
tokens, never presentation. Every medium has its own **presentation
table**: one row per adjective, saying how that medium shows it. If a
medium has no row, fall back to the next simpler medium, ending in text.

| Stage | Medium | How adjectives show |
|---|---|---|
| V0 | **Text** (today) | Styled words, icons, tooltips (above) |
| V1 | **Glyph field** (Hanzi, ASCII) | Glyph color and motion; **radicals as adjectives**: a fire radical marks *burning*, a ghost-related character marks *haunted*; compound characters for compound adjectives; bilingual tooltip |
| V2 | **Tiles and sprites** | Palette swaps, outlines and overlays per adjective (flame overlay, purple outline, offset sprite for *stolen*), status icons over heads |
| V3 | **2D animated scenes** | Particles (embers, frost, motes), idle animations from mood (*tired* slouch, *proud* posture), lighting from time adjectives |
| V4 | **3D** | Shaders per family (emissive for *burning*, dissolve for *fading*, chromatic jitter for *corrupted*), camera behavior for *superlative* moments |
| V5 | **Audio** | Music layers by place adjectives (*haunted* adds a choir, *crowded* adds chatter), cue per family, voice tone from mood |
| V6 | **Generated art** | Portraits, item icons and scenes assembled from adjectives: procedural parts first; optional image generation for private use, kept out of the public build |

**What this means now, while it's text:**
- Keep adjective data rich even when text shows only two words. Future
  media will want the rest.
- Keep tokens in content (`{adj:burning}`), not raw HTML, so content
  survives a renderer change.
- Name presentation keys by adjective, not by CSS class, so a sprite
  table can reuse them.
- The same principle is already in the wandering doc: one shared state,
  several views (map, list, atlas).

**Little details for later media:**
- **Intensity scales with level:** *slightly* burning is a wisp,
  *overwhelmingly* burning is a pillar.
- **Uncertainty shows as instability:** an unconfirmed adjective shimmers
  between two looks.
- **Hidden adjectives are invisible until known,** then appear with a small
  reveal effect, in every medium.
- **Reading depth changes the picture:** an expert sees the hairline
  crack on the blade sprite; a novice doesn't.
- **World style changes the table:** the same *cursed* is ink bleed in a
  Hanzi world, a green glow in a game-like world, a wet stain in a grim one.
- **Accessibility carries over:** reduced motion, no harsh flashing,
  never color alone, captions for sound cues.
- **Performance budget per medium:** cap simultaneous effects; distant or
  far-tier things (E3) get none.
- **Modding and packs:** a private pack can ship its own presentation
  table for its book world without touching the public one.

### More to consider

- **Adjectives you can feel.** Controller vibration or a phone haptic per
  family, if the game ever runs there.
- **Diegetic UI.** In a world with status windows, adjectives appear as the
  window shows them; in a world without, as what people say and notice.
- **Adjective weather.** A whole region can take on an adjective for a
  season (*fearful* town, *festive* city), shifting spawns, prices and
  presentation together.
- **Adjective contagion.** Some spread by contact: *cursed* to the next
  holder, *brave* through a crowd, *rotting* through a granary (E4 rules).
- **Adjective memory on places.** A battlefield stays *grieving* for years;
  a house where you lived stays *lived-in* (E7 lore).
- **Sorting and filters.** Inventory sorted by any family (*sharpest*
  first, *newest* first, *most beloved* first).
- **Search.** Type an adjective to find everything you know that has it.
- **Comparing two things** side by side by their adjective ladders.
- **Accessibility text mode.** A plain mode that lists adjectives as words,
  no effects, for screen readers and low-power devices.

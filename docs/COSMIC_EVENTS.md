# Cosmic events: a god looks at the screen (proposal)

> **How to use this doc (Weibao, 2026-10-10):** these are considerations
> for established and future worlds, to help ideaboard and implement
> fitting characters at fitting times. Tiers, lists and tables are
> illustrative, not complete or fixed; more distinctions can exist.
> Everything just has to make sense for the world and character at hand.

Claude, 2026-10-10. Weibao: "how to consider for the most outrageous
possibilities like a god looking at the screen and shattering the entire
run or something."

**Proposal only.** The top rung of the presentation ladder in
[presentation and the fourth wall](PRESENTATION_AND_FOURTH_WALL.md#direction-restraint-makes-effects-land-weibao-2026-10-10)
(X2 "unique"). Builds on the 2026-10-02 run-ending direction in
[DIRECTION_SYNTHESIS](DIRECTION_SYNTHESIS.md): ordinary defeat is fainting;
only explicitly eligible threats can end a run; danger, protections and a
preparation route are exposed (uncertainly when the character lacks
knowledge); the result persists once with no reload reroll. Outliers per
rule 12; each world sets its own appetite (rule 2).

## What makes outrageous work

An event this big lands when it is **rare, foreshadowed, caused and
remembered**. Without those it feels like a bug or a cheap shot.

| Code | Ingredient | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Caused** | Something you did drew it: reaching too high, abusing time, being too famous, looking back at the god, breaking a sacred rule. Or a world whose gods are openly capricious |
| C2 | **Foreshadowed** | Omens escalate first (below). The player can, in hindsight, see it coming |
| C3 | **Answerable** | Real counters exist: hide, appease, bargain, flee worlds, a gaze-blocking relic, a vow, staying a nobody, or deliberately provoking it on your terms |
| C4 | **Committed** | The outcome is saved before the show plays, so closing the tab can't dodge it |
| C5 | **Remembered** | The run doesn't just vanish. The atlas keeps it, the next run carries a mark, the god may remember you |
| C6 | **Rare** | An outlier event. Seeing one should feel like a legend happened to you |

## Omen ladder (foreshadowing)

Each rung is ordinary-sized presentation; only the last is huge.

1. **Whispers.** NPCs mention a god's attention; prophets get nervous.
2. **Signs.** Weather wrong, animals fleeing, a recurring dream.
3. **The eye.** The Watcher's small eye icon appears on an odd record.
4. **Notice.** A log line in a font you've never seen: *Something
   noticed you.*
5. **Pressure.** Timers slow slightly; a panel border darkens; fate's heat
   (abuse doc T4) is visibly high.
6. **Last chance.** An explicit choice: kneel, bargain, hide, run, or
   look back.
7. **The gaze.** The event.

Players can stop at any rung by answering the cause. Some worlds skip
rungs for gods that are capricious by nature, but always leave at least
one sign.

## What "shattering the run" can mean

Not every cosmic event is a game over. A scope adjective (E8F *how wide*)
picks the size:

| Scope | Outcome | What survives |
|---|---|---|
| **The moment** | The god looks, the screen cracks, then it turns away. A warning | Everything; a permanent crack mark and a terrified world |
| **The life** | The character dies or is taken (abducted, made a servant, made a star) | The heir rules, soul memory if carried, the legend |
| **The run** | The run ends: forced prestige or rebirth | Prestige carryover; the atlas branch marked *shattered* |
| **The branch** | The god erases this timeline | The atlas keeps a shattered entry; a ghost of it may surface later |
| **The world** | The world is broken or remade | The world's future runs start in the aftermath, or a new premise ("the world the god broke") |
| **The multiverse layer** | Every world you touched shifts | Rare beyond rare; a new era of the whole save |
| **Outrageous good** | The god blesses, adopts or lifts you mid-run | Ascension, a patron, a mark of favor; equally shattering, in gold |

## Staging the moment (presentation)

Uses the restraint rules: long calm before, absence as an effect, one
unique moment.

1. **Silence.** Every sound and animation stops. The log stops mid-line.
2. **The look.** One line in the god's font. Optionally the eye opens
   across the whole screen.
3. **The reading.** The god reads your life aloud from real receipts (the
   courtroom idea, at cosmic scale): your titles, your crimes, the odd
   records. Short, chosen by weight.
4. **Cracks from the edges.** Panels crack one by one, inward, toward your
   character panel.
5. **The verdict.** A word, not a paragraph.
6. **Shatter.** The whole screen breaks and falls away (overlay; the real
   UI safe underneath).
7. **Black.** A beat of nothing.
8. **After.** One plain line of what remains. Then the atlas, with the
   branch cracked, or the world in its new state.

Variants per world style: an ink god spills ink over the page; a system
god deletes the windows one by one; a cosmic horror god makes the text
unreadable then legible again; a bored god yawns and the run simply ends.

## The mark that stays

- **Title screen scar.** The next run's start screen keeps a crack, an
  eye, a golden seam.
- **The god remembers.** A save-aware god (reach R5) can recognize your
  next character's soul, habits or face (selves doc).
- **Legends.** Every world you touched tells a story about the one the god
  looked at.
- **Atlas.** The shattered branch is browsable and can become a premise.
- **Counters learned.** You now know the omens; the next run can prepare.

## More outrageous possibilities

| Event | Shape |
|---|---|
| **The Narrator quits** | The narration voice says it's done; text goes raw and terse until you win it back |
| **The world realizes it's a game** | NPCs start reading the UI; some panic, some try to reach the save |
| **The system crashes (in fiction)** | Status windows die; everyone loses their numbers; real skill still works |
| **A bigger fish passes** | Something from far above the world's ladder walks by; your world is collateral. Rule 11: somewhere you are nobody |
| **Your future self overwrites you** | A regressor from a later branch takes your body; you play on as the passenger or the ghost |
| **Time keepers delete the branch** | Bureaucratic, polite and final, with a form you can appeal |
| **The moon falls** | A world clock to catastrophe that runs whether you act or not (natural course) |
| **The author character arrives** | Someone who claims to write the world edits your page; argue in the margins |
| **The dream ends** | The whole run was someone's dream; they wake. Who was dreaming? |
| **Two gods fight over you** | The UI splits between their styles; whichever wins owns your run |
| **The god looks, and you look back** | With the right power, you shatter *its* panel instead |

## Making it reachable on purpose (rule 1)

- **Provoke it.** A ritual, a vow or an insult that summons the gaze when
  you're ready.
- **Become it.** Late-game paths where you are the one looking at other
  worlds' screens.
- **Bargain for it.** Trade a cosmic event's scope down (*the run* to *the
  moment*) at a price.
- **Survive it.** Rare relics, outlier bodies or vows that let a character
  endure the gaze, with consequences.

- Commit first, show second (C4).
- Seeded (E1), so no reload changes the verdict.
- The presentation falls back to text: a single line can carry it.
- Persisted outcome needs a migration when built.

## Safety lines (unchanged)

The fourth-wall safety lines in the presentation doc hold: the real save
is never actually damaged; "shattered" is a recorded outcome, not a
deleted file; nothing personal is read; every staging is skippable after
the first beat; a setting caps cosmic presentation (off, gentle, full)
without changing the outcome.

## Dials (per world)

- Whether cosmic beings exist and how capricious they are.
- Omen rungs required (all, some, one).
- Scopes allowed.
- Rarity (outlier frequency).
- Whether good cosmic events exist alongside bad.
- Whether gods remember across runs.

## It depends on the character (Weibao, 2026-10-10)

> this is good, but of course it all depends on the character ...
> Sufficiently powerful normal characters can do that too? but of course
> at that power level they are probably god-like instead

Recorded direction: cosmic events are **acts by characters**, not a
separate event type. Anyone with enough power and reach can do them; at
that point they are god-like, whatever they started as.

### God-like is a threshold, not a species

- **Defined by what you can do,** not what you are: power far above the
  world's ladder (rule 11, relative), plus reach (E8F *how wide*) that
  touches runs, branches or screens (reach ladder R4 and up).
- **Relative.** God-like in one world can be ordinary in a higher one.
  Somewhere the god is a nobody.
- **Reachable by anyone** the world allows: a cultivator who ascends, a
  concept holder who reaches unity, a sorcerer called the strongest, a
  faith-fed hero, a system user who maxes out, a mad scholar, a monster
  that ate enough. An outlier (rule 12) can get there by a strange road.
- **Signs of crossing the threshold:** the world's presentation starts
  treating them as cosmic (their own font, their own omens, their gaze
  can crack panels).

### The actor shapes the act

The same power, used by different people, makes different events.

| Character | What their "gaze" looks like |
|---|---|
| Cruel god | Shatters the run for sport; reads your failures aloud |
| Bored god | Yawns; the run simply ends, mid-sentence |
| Curious god | Watches for years; the eye icon never leaves; may intervene once to see what happens |
| Kind god | Warns you through omens; cracks only the moment, then turns away |
| Jealous god | Targets what you love: a companion, your sect, your heir |
| Lawful god | Follows its own rules exactly; can be argued with on wording (literal world) |
| Mortal turned god-like | Remembers being small. Might spare nobodies, or hate the version of themselves you remind them of |
| Former rival ascended | Personal. Knows your habits (selves doc), comes for you specifically, maybe gives you the duel instead of the shatter |
| Your heir or student ascended | Protects you, or surpasses and outgrows you |
| A god-like child | Doesn't understand what breaking a world means |
| Something alien | No motive you can read; omens make no sense until afterward |

Each one is a full E3 person or entity: goals, moods, relationships,
history, adjectives. Their personality decides which scope (moment, life,
run, branch, world) they choose, which omens they give and whether they
can be bargained with.

### The target matters too

| Target | How it changes the event |
|---|---|
| A nobody | Often beneath notice; spared by being small (the attention dial) |
| A famous hero | A prime target; their fame is the cause |
| A defiant soul | Looks back. With enough power, a fight; without, a legend |
| The faithful | Spared, claimed or used |
| A fellow god-like | Not a gaze but a clash; both UIs fight (two styles splitting the screen) |
| Someone the god loved | Hesitation, a cracked moment instead of a shattered run |
| Your past self (B3) | A god-like you can look at your own ghost's screen. The strangest mirror |

### The player as the god

At god-like power, the player can do this too:
- **Look at other screens.** An NPC's life, a rival's run, another branch:
  your gaze cracks *their* panels. Presentation flips: you see their
  little UI from above.
- **Choose a style.** Your personality, built from your receipts and
  habits, sets your omens and your verdict font. A merciful player gets a
  merciful signature.
- **Bear the weight.** God-like play has its own problems: attention from
  other gods, worshippers with demands, the boredom of power (rule 11
  contrast), a world that fears you, heirs who can never match you.
- **Be looked at in return.** A higher layer still exists somewhere.

### Rivals and NPCs climbing

- **NPC ascension is visible.** A rival's adjectives climb in the
  Notebook; omens start following them; their log lines gain a font.
- **Race to the throne.** Several characters near the threshold make a
  world's late game: help one, stop one, become one.
- **A world after its god.** If an NPC becomes god-like and leaves, the
  world they left changes (natural course with a hole in it).

### Presentation scales with relative power

- The bigger the gap between actor and target, the bigger the UI effect
  on the target's side (P4 intensity tracks the rung).
- Equals clash instead of shatter: two styles fight over the screen.
- Looking down from far above, the actor's view of the target's UI can
  shrink to a tiny window: the god's sense of scale made visible.
- A merely strong character (below god-like) can crack a panel at most;
  shattering runs and branches stays at the top of the ladder.

## Consequences are relative, and god-likes are few (Weibao, 2026-10-10)

> Of course consequences is all relative to the capabilities of the
> character, there shouldn't be too many, probably, and they aren't
> everywhere? More to consider?

Recorded direction: **consequences scale with what the character can
do**, and **god-like characters are scarce and unevenly spread.**

### Consequences relative to capability

The same rule (consequences persist; rule 11 contrast) applies at every
tier; only the scale changes.

| Tier | What can touch them | Typical consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody | Hunger, debt, a bully, a bad harvest | Injury, poverty, losing a friend |
| Strong | Rivals, institutions, monsters | Grades, bounties, feuds, wounds that matter |
| World-class | Nations, sects, world events | Wars declared, lineages hunted, a world's fear |
| God-like | Other god-likes, cosmic laws, faith, time, boredom | Lost worshippers, treaties broken, cosmic attention, losing their humanity, the higher layer noticing |

- **A god's casual act is huge below it.** Cracking a panel is a sneeze to
  a god and a catastrophe to a village. Both sides record it at their own
  scale.
- **Mortal penalties don't bind gods,** but god-level ones do. A fine
  means nothing; a rival pantheon, a broken cosmic treaty or faith drying
  up means a lot.
- **Responsibility grows with reach.** The more a character can touch,
  the more their acts ripple (timelines: ripple reach), and the more
  people react (odd records, legends).
- **Every tier keeps real stakes.** Power changes what can be lost, not
  whether something can be lost.
- **Descending is an option.** A god-like can seal power, wear a mortal
  body or vow to live small, and get mortal-scale stakes back (a
  favorite trope; also how a life-sim stays a life-sim at the top).

### Why god-likes are few

Each world picks its reasons; several can hold at once.

| Reason | Shape |
|---|---|
| The threshold is hard | Few survive the road (trials, tribulations, costs) |
| They cull each other | Rising god-likes get noticed and stopped by existing ones |
| A world can't hold many | Too much power breaks the place; they must leave or sleep |
| They leave | Ascend to a higher layer; the world remembers them as legend |
| They sleep or withdraw | Sealed, dormant, bored, meditating for centuries |
| Treaties | An old agreement forbids gazing into lower worlds |
| Attention is scarce | A god watches a few things; most of the world goes unwatched |
| Faith is limited | Not enough worship to feed more than a handful |
| Outliers only | In some worlds the only god-like is a single anomaly (rule 12) |

### Where they are (and aren't)

- **Per world:** none, one, a small pantheon, absent but remembered, or
  only in a higher layer. Most worlds have few or none acting directly.
- **Per life:** most characters never meet one. A cosmic event in an
  ordinary life is rare to never; the player meets one when something
  draws it (cause, C1).
- **Rumors outnumber gods.** Many claimed gods are false, weaker, or
  stories (rule 6). Telling a real one from a pretender is play.
- **Dead and absent gods** leave traces: relics, corpses that became
  places, laws they wrote, shrines with no answer.

### Smaller manifestations

To interact at mortal scale without breaking things:
- **Avatars and incarnations:** a sliver of a god in a mortal body, with
  mortal-ish limits and mortal-ish consequences.
- **Fragments and shards** (E8): pieces of divine power in items or people.
- **Messengers, saints and champions** who act for a god.
- **Dreams and omens** as the god's only reach into a protected world.

These give god-like presence without god-like frequency.

### More to consider

- **False gods.** Strong characters who claim godhood; exposing one is
  a quest. Some become real by being believed (faith worlds).
- **God-likes are characters first.** They have friends, regrets, hobbies,
  enemies, and lives the player can learn about, from afar or up close.
- **The cost of becoming one.** Mortal ties strain, small pleasures fade,
  a world fears you. Some characters refuse the threshold on purpose.
- **Power without presence.** A god-like who never acts is still felt:
  in laws, prices, prayers, fear.
- **The god's view of time.** God-likes may act on centuries; their
  "sudden" gaze was planned for generations (natural course).
- **Fairness through rarity.** Because they're few, each one can be
  deeply authored or richly generated, instead of a crowd of shallow gods.
- **Presentation follows scarcity.** God-level effects stay in the unique
  tier (X2, X3); a world with three gods doesn't get three shatterings a
  session.
- **The player at the top is one of the few,** and the world reacts like
  it: pilgrims, assassins, worship, loneliness.

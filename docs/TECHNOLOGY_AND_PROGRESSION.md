## Where progress lives (TL)

"Tech level" is not one number. Each layer moves on its own, and the
interesting play is in the gaps between them.

| Code | Layer | Example of a gap |
|---|---|---|
| TL1 | **What a person knows** | You know steam engines exist. You have never seen one built |
| TL2 | **What their hands can do** | You understand forging perfectly and still ruin the first ten blades |
| TL3 | **What their workshop can make** | You can design a lathe but have no lathe to cut its parts |
| TL4 | **What an institution holds** | The academy owns the only telescope; the guild owns the only furnace hot enough |
| TL5 | **What a region uses** | The capital has a telegraph; the village three days away has never heard of it |
| TL6 | **A world's era** | The world as a whole is early industrial, with pockets far ahead or behind |
| TL7 | **What the multiverse holds** | Somewhere, a world already solved your problem; reaching it is the problem |

Uneven distribution is the default texture. A world's era is a rough
average, never everyone's reality. Hidden labs, lost ruins, a sect that
quietly runs on formations as precise as clockwork, a backwater that never
got the printing press: all of these sit inside one era.

## Shapes progression can take (TS)

A world (or a single field inside it) picks the shape that fits. Several
shapes can coexist: metallurgy can be a ladder while medicine is a web.

| Code | Shape | Feel | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| TS1 | **Tree** | Clear prerequisites, visible branches | Game worlds, system worlds, a world with a "tech panel" |
| TS2 | **Web** | Many paths into one result; crossovers between fields | Most realistic worlds |
| TS3 | **Ladder of eras** | Big steps (stone, bronze, iron, steam, electric) that change daily life | Uplift stories, long-lived characters |
| TS4 | **Adjacent possible** | Only ideas one step from what exists are reachable; jumping further costs a lot or fails | Transmigrators trying to skip ahead |
| TS5 | **Fogged** | You only see the next ring of discoveries; the rest is unknown even to you | Exploration worlds, a character without foreknowledge |
| TS6 | **Dead ends** | Branches that work, then cap out, then get abandoned. Worth revisiting in odd worlds | Alchemy, phlogiston-style theories that are locally true |
| TS7 | **Lost and recovered** | The past was ahead of the present. Ruins, relics, half-understood leftovers | Fallout's pre-war tech, Skyrim's vanished Dwemer, fallen sky civilizations |
| TS8 | **Cycles** | Rise, peak, collapse, slow recovery. Knowledge survives as myth, ritual or a guarded vault | Long-timeline worlds, cosmic resets |
| TS9 | **Convergent** | Two worlds reach the same function by different means (a qi lamp and an electric lamp) | Cross-world trade and comparisons |
| TS10 | **Alien substrate** | Tech built on a different base: living tissue, ants, souls, concepts | Children of Time's spider and ant technology, gu worlds |
| TS11 | **Runaway** | Progress feeds itself until it outpaces its makers | AI and singularity worlds, self-improving formations |

## What really gates the next step (TG)

Knowledge alone rarely builds anything. A step usually needs several of
these at once, and the missing one is the story.

| Code | Gate | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| TG1 | **Knowing that** | Awareness the thing exists or is possible |
| TG2 | **Knowing how** | The method, the numbers, the order of steps, the tacit feel |
| TG3 | **Tools to make the tools** | A precise part needs a precise machine, which needed precise parts. Bootstrapping is a project chain of its own |
| TG4 | **Materials and purity** | Iron is easy; steel with the right carbon is not; a pure reagent can be the whole bottleneck |
| TG5 | **Energy** | Muscle, water, wind, coal, oil, electric, qi veins, mana wells, reactors. Each tier changes what can run at all |
| TG6 | **Measurement** | You can't make what you can't measure: temperature, weight, time, purity, pressure, qi density |
| TG7 | **Skilled people** | Someone has to run it. Training workers is its own progression |
| TG8 | **Capital and scale** | Some steps only pay off at volume, so they need money, land and buyers first |
| TG9 | **Demand** | Nobody builds a railway between two empty towns |
| TG10 | **Permission** | Guilds, churches, sects, governments, patents and taboos can block or slow a step |
| TG11 | **Physics** | The world's own laws. Gunpowder may not burn where fire works differently |
| TG12 | **Watchers** | A world will, gods, a system or a cosmic police that object to certain steps |

**The bootstrap chain** (TG3) deserves its own play: crude tools make
slightly better tools, which make a precise file, which makes a gauge,
which makes interchangeable parts possible. Release That Witch builds its
kingdom along chains like this ([digest](research/power-systems/wiki-digests/DIGEST_release-that-witch.md));
Dr. Stone (Riichiro Inagaki and Boichi) is a whole series about rebuilding
from nothing, one tool at a time. In idle terms, it is a satisfying
staircase where each step visibly makes the next cheaper.

## Knowing that versus knowing how (TK)

The transmigrator's classic problem. A modern person in a pre-industrial
world "knows" an enormous amount and can build almost none of it.
Knowledge is stored per item with a fidelity, and fidelity decides what
it unlocks.

| Code | Fidelity | You have | It unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| TK0 | **Unaware** | Nothing | Nothing; you might stumble on it by accident |
| TK1 | **Rumor** | "Something like this exists" | A research direction, a reason to look |
| TK2 | **Concept** | What it does and roughly why | Faster research; you can explain it to someone better qualified |
| TK3 | **Principle** | The governing idea (pressure drives a piston) | A design project can start |
| TK4 | **Recipe** | Steps, materials, rough numbers | A first build attempt, with failures |
| TK5 | **Mastery** | Tacit feel, exact numbers, the tricks | Reliable production, teaching others well |

Considerations:
- **Memory carried across worlds is fuzzy.** What crosses (rule 5) can
  arrive at lower fidelity: you remember soap needs fat and lye but not
  the ratio. A cheat or a record carrier (notes, a phone, a system log)
  can protect fidelity.
- **Knowledge can be wrong** (rule 6). A remembered fact may be a myth
  from your home world, or true at home and false here. Experiments
  discover which.
- **Experiments fill gaps.** Each attempt, success or failure, raises
  fidelity on the item you worked on. This is the hidden failure counter
  from the research system with a visible meaning.
- **Fidelity decays without use** in worlds that model forgetting, and
  survives as a written record in worlds that don't.
- **Teaching moves fidelity, imperfectly.** A TK5 master teaching a
  student usually lands them at TK3 or TK4; the rest comes from practice.

## Measurement changes what you see (TM)

This ties tech straight into the UI and the [means principle](MEANS_AND_APPRAISAL.md):
**before you can measure something, the game describes it in words; after,
it shows numbers.** Instruments are means. Progress is visible as the
interface itself becoming more precise.

| Code | Instrument | Before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| TM1 | Thermometer | "The forge is very hot" | 1,240 degrees, and the target window for steel |
| TM2 | Scale and standard weights | "A handful of ore" | Exact mass, so recipes become repeatable |
| TM3 | Clock | Dawn, noon, dusk | Hours and minutes; schedules and shifts become possible |
| TM4 | Map and survey | "Two days east, past the river" | Distances, routes, travel estimates |
| TM5 | Telescope and microscope | Invisible | New targets: far stars, small creatures, flaws in metal |
| TM6 | Telegraph, newspapers | Prices of the next town are rumors | Live prices across a region; trade and arbitrage open up |
| TM7 | Ledgers and calculators | "Business is good" | Exact profit, so optimization becomes possible |
| TM8 | Computers and models | Guesses about next season | Forecasts, with error bars |
| TM9 | Qi meter, mana gauge, curse-energy sensor | "A strong aura" | A reading on that world's own scale |

Considerations:
- **Cheap instruments lie a little.** A poorly calibrated scale shows a
  confident wrong number (rule 6). Calibration is a project.
- **Measurement can be the breakthrough.** In a cultivation world, the
  first person to measure qi density reliably can turn guesswork
  cultivation into a repeatable method. Sects may see that as a threat.
- **Knowing too much is a choice.** A player who likes the vague words
  keeps them; precision is reachable, never forced.

## Tech changes the idle loop itself (TA)

In an idle game, technology's deepest effect is on how much the player
has to do and what keeps running while they're away.

| Code | Stage | What changes | New failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| TA1 | **Hands** | Every action is yours | Fatigue, injury |
| TA2 | **Tools** | Same actions, faster and better | Tools wear and break |
| TA3 | **Machines** | Work happens while you do other things | Needs fuel, input and somewhere for output to go |
| TA4 | **Automation** | Machines feed machines; offline output grows | Stalls when any link starves; the game names the binding shortage (Factorio lesson) |
| TA5 | **Supervised systems** | People or AIs run whole lines | Their loyalty, wages, mistakes and ambitions |
| TA6 | **Self-maintaining** | The system repairs and expands itself | Drift from your goals; runaway (TS11) |

Considerations:
- **Each stage trades attention for fragility.** More output, more ways
  to stall. That trade is the gameplay.
- **Research can consume products** (Factorio's labs eat science packs),
  so production feeds progress and old production stays useful late.
- **Old skills stay useful.** A machine breaks in the field and the
  person who can still do it by hand wins. Fits the vision's "ordinary
  skills stay useful late".
- **Supervision has range.** The Legendary Mechanic's machine control
  loses precision past a few units; any world can cap how much one person
  oversees until they build an organization or a mind to help.

## The world reacts (TW)

Technology spreads, gets copied and changes people. A world that ignores
your inventions feels fake.

| Code | Reaction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| TW1 | **Diffusion** | Others copy what they see. Your first invention appears in a rival's shop a year later |
| TW2 | **Improvement by others** | Natives take your idea further than you did. They can overtake you |
| TW3 | **Resistance** | Guilds lose income, workers lose jobs, priests call it impious. Riots, bans, sabotage |
| TW4 | **Arms race** | Any useful tech becomes a weapon in someone's hands, then their enemies want it too |
| TW5 | **Side effects** | Smoke, poisoned rivers, explosions, new diseases, depleted mines |
| TW6 | **Social change** | Printing spreads literacy, literacy spreads new ideas, new ideas spread heresies and revolutions |
| TW7 | **Dependency** | People forget the old ways; when the new thing fails, they can't fall back |
| TW8 | **Watchers object** | A world will, gods or a system notice a step it dislikes (TG12) |
| TW9 | **Natural course continues** | Each world's own canon milestones still happen unless disturbed. Your steam engine may arrive a decade early, or collide with the one history already had planned |

Scale of disturbance follows the [timelines doc](TIMELINES_AND_DIVERGENCE.md):
one gadget sold to a merchant barely moves anything; a printing press in a
theocracy can reroute a century.

## Across a life, heirs and runs (TR)

Time means something (rule 7), so tech is also a way of feeling a life
pass.

- **One life can see an era change.** A child of candles grows old under
  electric light, partly because of their own work.
- **Heirs inherit what you left.** A workshop, patents, apprentices,
  notebooks, debts, rivals. The next generation starts at your TL3 and
  TL4 even if their TL1 is lower.
- **Across runs, only carriers move tech** (rule 5): blueprints in a
  record, knowledge in a soul, an item a cheat lets through. A second
  run can "speedrun civilization" only as far as its carriers allow.
- **Your name in history.** A world remembers its inventors. Later lives
  in the same world may find your invention named after you, misused, or
  credited to a rival who stole it.

## Tech as a life (TJ)

The life-sim side: technology as careers, relationships and stories, not
only power.

- **Careers:** tinkerer, smith, apprentice, engineer, professor, patent
  clerk, factory owner, tech smuggler, relic hunter, safety inspector,
  saboteur.
- **Patents and royalties:** an idle income stream that others can
  dispute, infringe or steal.
- **Inventor rivalry:** a peer racing you to the same result (research
  in parallel, TN6), with public demonstrations, slander and lawsuits.
- **Fairs and demonstrations:** a good show creates demand (The Legendary
  Mechanic's lesson); a failed one costs reputation.
- **A museum of your failures:** old prototypes kept in the workshop,
  which visitors and heirs can read as your life story.
- **Teaching:** students who surpass you, or misuse what you taught.
- **Hobby scale:** tech doesn't have to change the world. Building a
  better fishing reel or a music box can be a whole cozy thread.

### CT1. Separate capability from workmanship

Native source inspected this turn: `src/crafting_recipes.js` has component
tier, station tier, skill-dependent quality ranges/caps and component-weighted
assembly quality. This is a source foundation, not browser proof of these
expanded proposals. Preserve native numeric quality/rarity mappings unless a
selected change supplies an explicit compatibility decision.

| Dimension | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Material properties | What can the material support? | A heat-resistant seal enables a hotter process |
| Structural tier | Which new function or operating range exists? | Hand drill versus powered precision drill |
| Workmanship quality | How well was this specimen made? | Alignment differs between otherwise identical drills |
| Condition | What happened to this object? | Worn bearings impair current precision |
| Maker mastery | What can this person execute reliably? | Repeating a tempering method |
| Design knowledge | Which method does the actor possess? | A drawing without fitting notes |
| Availability or rarity | How common is it here? | A standard imported part is locally scarce |

No universal stone-to-godhood scale. Tracks can be metallurgical, biological,
spiritual, computational or otherwise locally supported. Compare cross-world
functions rather than tier numbers. Old tools may remain useful; genuinely
superior replacements may simply supersede them without forced weaknesses.

### CT2. Several kinds of breakthrough

Illustrative shapes, not mandatory order or ceiling:

| Advance | New play |
|---|---|
| Improvised work to dependable craft | Repeat useful results and accept ordinary commissions |
| Dependable craft to specialist processes | Heat treatment, purification, weaving or local alternatives enable new applications |
| Specialist work to assemblies | Components become a working pump, prosthesis, ward or other device |
| Individual builds to reproducible production | Gauges, jigs, standards and trained workers make batches consistent |
| Reproducible production to autonomous production | Supported machines or agents work without occupying the maker |
| Established processes to a new substrate | A discovery enables functions the old process could not provide |

A master artisan may make unique things a factory cannot; a factory may
produce ordinary things at volumes one artisan cannot. Neither implies a
universal ranking. Exceptional means may bypass steps when they genuinely
provide the missing functions. Do not restore a ladder through arbitrary timers.

### CT3. Each upgrade has an actual consumer

Name a concrete return for each selected advance: new recipe, usable route,
different technique, longer operating interval, finer appraisal, less waste,
workers supplied or a previously impossible commission. Research must connect
to use, rather than awarding a badge alone.

Candidate example: prepare an improved tool, fit it and use it in a native
profession to work a material the old tool cannot handle. The material opens
one component application; mastery improves quality or yield within that
process. Existing D1 alloy/equipment work keeps its own checkpoint and open
browser gate. This example does not declare that work complete.

Alternative routes include invention, teaching, purchase, a guild workshop,
hired specialists, salvage and carried knowledge. Ownership, borrowing and
paid fabrication grant different access and custody. Learning a recipe need
not grant machinery, stock or practical mastery. Actor means determine access.

### CT4. Details that make crafting a life

| Code | Consideration | Choice or consequence |
|---|---|---|
| CD1 | Process order | Refine before forming, or use a local alternative |
| CD2 | Ingredient properties | Purity, grain, freshness or provenance matter in recipes that actually use them |
| CD3 | Design variants | Cheap, compact, precise, durable or locally superior designs serve actual purposes |
| CD4 | Experiments | Freeze paid inputs/test conditions; failure can leave knowledge, recoverable parts or nothing under declared policy |
| CD5 | Quality and certainty | Accept useful quality, inspect a batch or calibrate the process |
| CD6 | Commissions | Meet a function or tolerance the customer can assess |
| CD7 | Repair and replacement | Repair, refit, salvage or replace a specimen without duplicated parts or erased custody |
| CD8 | Production depth | Batch familiar work; add manual stages where they create decisions or evidence |
| CD9 | Teaching and standards | Share a method, apprentice a worker or introduce interchangeable parts |
| CD10 | Distribution | Storage, transport, buyers and installed consumers make output useful |
| CD11 | Reputation and provenance | Maker marks, copied designs, counterfeits or certification matter to fitting observers |
| CD12 | Inheritance | Workshops, records and trained people survive; heirs do not gain unearned personal mastery |

Inspection reveals what held means support. Unknown properties and world rules
stay unknown; author catalogues do not become player menus. Strong means can
reveal exact properties when supported. Maintenance, deterioration, forgetting
and institutional resistance are local possibilities, not mandatory invention costs.

### CT5. Progress outside the player

A prototype, a working workshop and regional adoption are different achievements.
Adoption needs what the locality actually requires: training, supplies, demand,
distribution or permission. NPCs can invent independently, copy observable
processes, improve shared designs or keep methods private. They cannot copy
hidden knowledge merely because the player unlocked it. Natural development
continues unless sufficiently disturbed.

Housing, medicine, food preservation, communications, education and transport
deserve technology paths alongside weapons. A pump supplying a settlement may
matter more than another sword upgrade. Fields develop unevenly: advanced
healing does not imply advanced metallurgy or plentiful electricity.

Across worlds, a carried working device differs from the ability to reproduce
it. Parts, energy and control must function locally. Adapters, substitute
materials and hybrid processes are research possibilities; era labels alone
neither grant nor deny compatibility.

### Candidate proof and open choices

Recommended candidate, not an assignment: one existing profession, one functional
tool improvement, one changed gathering/production action and one resulting craft.
Compare this with improving workmanship of the old tool so structural progress
and mastery have visibly different returns. Include a fitting learning/service
alternative if supported. Choose qualified content; Frontier is not a prerequisite.

Resolve this slice's quality meaning, access gates, consumer effect, spending,
cancellation and retention before implementation. Acceptance requires rebuild,
fresh-origin native journey, absent/changed-tool cases, actual cost/result evidence
and relevant old-save plus save/reload/import proof. Human review owns fiction,
pacing and balance. Nested assemblies and autonomous production remain separate

### RV. Research as an idle verb

The open question for an idle game is what research does while the player
is away, and how much attention it asks for.

| Code | Consideration | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| RV1 | **Attention kinds** | Reading and repetitive testing can run unattended; a judgment call (which result to trust, which branch to try) waits for the player and says so |
| RV2 | **Offline research** | Follows the active branch's offline clock ([offline audit](research/OFFLINE_TIME_AND_BRANCH_CLOCK_AUDIT_2026-10-14.md)). Unattended work stops at a named reason: out of samples, a decision needed, an instrument broke |
| RV3 | **Sleep on it** | A stuck project may resolve partly overnight or after a change of activity, where the world supports incubation. A flavor rider, not a mandatory timer |
| RV4 | **Riders on other actions** | Ordinary play leaves notes: fighting a beast notes its weak joint, cooking notes a reaction. Doing related things boosts related research (the Civ VI eureka shape, see GR1) |
| RV5 | **Slots and queues** | How many projects run at once comes from actual means: desks, assistants, instruments, a split mind. Not a fixed global cap |
| RV6 | **Plateaus** | Repeating the same approach stops paying. Changing method, teacher, sample or world breaks it. The game can say "this approach is exhausted" when the actor would know |
| RV7 | **Depth versus breadth** | Focus finishes one result; scatter leaves many partial leads. Both are valid ways to live |
| RV8 | **Research across prestige** | Idle-genre convention: some research persists through a reset (GR11). Here it persists only through a carrier, per rule 5 and the first milestone's one prestige layer |

### RQ. Where questions come from

Research feels earned when the question came out of play, not from a menu.

| Code | Source of a question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | **Anomaly log** | Things noticed and not understood go into the notebook as open questions: frost on one stone in summer, a merchant's scale that reads light |
| RQ2 | **Impossible orders** | A customer asks for something nobody can make yet. The commission is the research brief |
| RQ3 | **Failures** | A broken tool asks "why did it break here?" |
| RQ4 | **Rumors and jokes** | A throwaway rumor turns out to point at something real ([jokes as hints](worlds/WORLD_DEPTH_CONSIDERATIONS_2026-10-13.md)) |
| RQ5 | **Prizes and bounties** | A ruler offers a reward for a solution, like the historical Longitude Prize. Rivals compete for it |
| RQ6 | **Needs of people you care about** | A sick child, a failing harvest, a friend's injury. Research as care, not power |
| RQ7 | **Pure curiosity** | A question with no payoff in sight. Allowed, and sometimes it pays later |
| RQ8 | **Inherited questions** | A dead mentor's unfinished notebook, a predecessor's half-solved problem |

Questions can be wrong (rule 6): the anomaly might be an instrument fault.

### RU. Showing uncertainty honestly

| Code | Presentation | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| RU1 | **Drawn options** | Instead of a full tree, the next choices are drawn from what is nearby, weighted by expertise and situation (Stellaris shape, GR2). Fits fogged worlds (TS5) |
| RU2 | **Fragments** | Several partial finds combine into a design (Subnautica shape, GR3). Each fragment shows how much is still missing, when the actor could tell |
| RU3 | **Confidence per fact** | A note shows "seen once", "repeated", "confirmed by others" rather than a hidden number |
| RU4 | **Known unknowns** | A short list of what the actor knows they don't know. Unknown unknowns stay invisible |
| RU5 | **Estimates that can be wrong** | "About ten days" from a novice can turn into forty. Experience narrows estimates |
| RU6 | **Late dead ends** | A promising line can reveal itself as a dead end (TS6). The partial knowledge still has a use or a resale value where fitting |
| RU7 | **Self-drawn tree** | The player's tech view is a map built from their own discoveries, not the author's full tree. A tree-view cheat (TC1) is the exception |

### RF. How research goes wrong

Honest failure modes make the world feel like science instead of a progress
bar. Readings can be wrong (rule 6).

| Code | Failure | In play |
|---|---|---|
| RF1 | **Confirmation bias** | The actor keeps the results that fit their idea. A careful trait or a skeptical partner counters it |

| RF3 | **Locally true superstition** | "Iron must be quenched in a red-haired man's sweat" works because of the salt. Folk methods can hide real causes |
| RF4 | **Coincidence** | Two events once in a row read as a rule. More trials expose it |
| RF5 | **Contaminated sample** | The result was the dirt on the glass |
| RF6 | **Overreach** | A true result in one setting fails in another (bench versus field, one world versus another, RX1) |
| RF7 | **Fraud** | Your own, a rival's or a student's. Exposure costs reputation (RP3) |

## Making anything: procedural invention, round 5, 2026-10-14

Claude. Proposal only. The batch layer (what happens when you mix, heat and

[materials and alchemy audit](PROCEDURAL_MATERIALS_AND_ALCHEMY_AUDIT_2026-10-13.md)
(P1 to P15) and its [generation contracts](research/procedural-materials-2026-10-13/deep/GENERATION_CONTRACTS.md),
including feasibility certificates (P6). This round adds the missing half:
**the player names a goal, and the game works out a path to it.** Codex
lanes T5 and T6 deepen it (see the end of this section).

### PI1. Targets are functions, not item names

"Soap" means "lifts grease off things with water". A "nuclear bomb" means
"releases enormous energy in an instant". Defining targets by **function,
magnitude and form** lets every world answer in its own way (TS9):

| Target | Function | Magnitude | Form | Home-world answer | Other-world answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Soap | Clean (grease) | Household | Bar | Fat plus lye from wood ash, heated | A cleansing herb, a purifying spell, a slime that eats dirt |
| Lamp | Light | Room | Portable | Oil and wick, then gas, then electric | Glowstone, bound fire spirit, light formation |
| Medicine | Heal (infection) | One patient | Dose | Antibiotics, after germ theory (SO9) | Healing pill, holy water, regeneration magic |
| Steam engine | Convert heat to motion | Workshop | Machine | Boiler, piston, precise cylinder (TG3) | Fire-qi turbine, golem heart |
| Telegraph | Send message | Region | Network | Wire, battery, code | Paired mirrors, sound-transmission talisman, messenger familiars |
| Bomb | Release energy | From firecracker to city | Device | Gunpowder to high explosive to fission | Exploding core, unstable mana crystal, self-destruct technique |

The **function vocabulary** is authored and finite (clean, bind, cut, heat,
cool, light, store energy, release energy, move, lift, contain, preserve,
heal, harm, sense, measure, send, compute, grow and a few more). Targets,
magnitudes and forms combine to produce a very large space without authoring
each item.

### PI2. The path is an AND/OR graph

Each target expands into alternatives (OR), each alternative into
everything it needs (AND), recursively down to things the world already has:
gatherable materials, native skills, existing stations.

```
Soap (clean grease, household)
  OR  saponify fat
        AND fat        <- hunt, butcher, render (native)
        AND alkali     OR  lye from wood ash   AND ash, water, leaching barrel
                       OR  natron/soda         AND trade or deposit
        AND heat       <- fire (native)
        AND know-how   TK3 principle, TK4 recipe
  OR  saponin plant    AND find the plant (seeded per world), crush, soak
  OR  world-specific   (a cleansing spell, if this world has one)
```

The planning shape is called a hierarchical task network or an AND/OR graph;
games use relatives of it (goal-oriented action planning in F.E.A.R.,
recipe graphs in Factorio). Shared intermediate nodes (alkali, precise
cylinders, electricity) make the graph a web (TS2): solving one target
quietly unlocks parts of many others.

### PI3. Scale shows itself as graph size

Soap is three or four layers from things a villager has. A fission weapon
is dozens of layers deep and wide: industry, power grids, precision
machining, rare materials, theory, institutions, years. The same planner
handles both; the difference the player sees is how big the roadmap is and
what kinds of node appear in it:

| Depth | Nodes that start appearing | Who can do it |
|---|---|---|
| Shallow | Gather, heat, mix | One person (TT1) |
| Middle | Tools to make tools, measurement, energy | A workshop (TT2 to TT3) |
| Deep | Factories, trained workers, supply chains, capital | A town or nation (TT4) |
| Very deep | Theory, institutions, decades of buildup | A civilization, or one long life with heirs (RT3, RT4) |

So "make a nuclear bomb in a medieval world" is possible, reasonably: it is
a civilization-scale project across generations, with the world reacting
the whole way (TW, TG10, TG12). A cheat or a very strong world-specific
alternative can collapse layers, and the roadmap shows that too.

### PI4. Seeing the roadmap through your knowledge

The full graph is world truth. The player sees only what the actor knows
(TK, RU7):

- Known nodes show with their fidelity; unknown alternatives stay hidden.
- A node at TK1 shows as "something is needed here; you don't know what".
- Wrong knowledge (RF, rule 6) can show a false node until tested.
- A transmigrator sees home-world paths, some of which don't work here.
- The game names the next missing piece: "No alkali source known" (RD2).

This is a Dr. Stone style roadmap, drawn from what the character knows.

### PI5. Worlds decide which branches exist

World cards prune and add branches: different physics (TG11), materials
that don't exist, magic alternatives, canon tech levels, watchers. A seeded
world can also decide where things are (which plant has saponin, where
natron deposits lie) once, saved, never rerolled (GENERATION_CONTRACTS P8).
Book worlds keep canon answers where canon gives them.

### PI6. Free invention: combining functions

Beyond named targets, the player composes functions: "a lamp that keeps
insects away", "a cart that cools its cargo". The planner merges the two
graphs and looks for parts that can do double duty. The result gets a
generated name and description from a grammar, and can be named by the
player. This is the "free invention exit" from the 2026-10-08 research
proposal made concrete.

### PI7. Three ways to feed the planner

All can coexist; each world or content pack picks.

| Code | Route | Strength | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| PI7a | **Authored packs** | Methods and alternatives written per world, linted | Coverage only as wide as authored |
| PI7b | **Grammar generation** | Functions times magnitudes times forms, with world adapters | Results need a feasibility check (P6) and real consumers |
| PI7c | **Language-model authoring, offline** | Weibao types "I want soap in Harry Potter", agents author a validated pack, he plays it (the [story request](gm/dispatch/procgen-2026-10-13/PLAN.md) shape, P22) | Needs a pipeline and review |
| PI7d | **Language-model at runtime** | Any free text becomes a target live | Needs a model in the loop, cost and determinism decisions; outputs must still pass the same validator and saved receipts. Not selected |

Infinite Craft (Neal Agarwal) shows the runtime language-model route for
combining words; Scribblenauts shows a large authored noun dictionary with

### PI8. Products become real things

A finished target becomes an ordinary item, station or service with real
consumers (CT3): soap removes a "dirty" state and lowers disease risk (SO9);
a lamp extends working hours; a telegraph makes prices visible (TM6). A
bomb is a world event with world reactions. Nothing becomes a badge alone.

### PI9. Danger stays in the fiction

Weapons, explosives, poisons and drugs exist in the game as **abstract
nodes with fictional costs**, never as real-world procedures, quantities
or chemistry. "Enrichment facility" can be a node; how to build one is not
game text. Everyday things like soap can use well-known basics (fat plus
lye) because those are common knowledge and harmless. This one line keeps
the whole planner open to "anything" without the game turning into a manual.

### PI10. Idle fit

- Long roadmaps are idle projects: queue nodes, run them while away, stop
  with a named reason (RV2).
- Each finished node pays off on its own (lye is useful before soap
  exists), so a deep roadmap has rewards all the way down.
- Infinite targets (IF1) appear naturally: higher magnitudes of the same
  function.

### OM. The overarching model

All eight rounds reduce to a few shared ideas. Holding these makes every
later addition slot in without new rules.

| Code | Idea | What it unifies |
|---|---|---|
| OM1 | **Three layers: truth, knowledge, presentation** | The world has truth (materials, graphs, rules, who did what). Each actor holds knowledge (observations, beliefs, fidelity). The screen shows presentation (filtered by means, texture dial). Every round's details sit in one of the three |
| OM2 | **Means decide what crosses from truth to knowledge** | Skills, instruments, teachers, books, senses and cheats are all means ([means](MEANS_AND_APPRAISAL.md)). Revealer cheats (RC) are just strong means |
| OM3 | **Evidence receipts** | Every piece of knowledge comes from a recorded event: an observation, a lesson, a reading. Wrong beliefs have receipts too |
| OM4 | **One function vocabulary** | Crafts, inventions, powers, markets, affordances and counter-functions all speak the same verbs (PI1, PP, PA4, PA5) |
| OM5 | **One AND/OR planner** | Items, techniques, institutions and social targets all expand the same way (PI2, PP, PI11f) |
| OM6 | **One scale ladder** | Person, workshop, town, nation, civilization, and beyond (TT, PI3). The same detail repeats at each scale with different nouns |
| OM7 | **Everything has a consumer** | A finding, item or method must change an action someone can take (CT3) |
| OM8 | **Seeded, saved, never rerolled** | World truth is drawn once and saved; previews are free (GENERATION_CONTRACTS P8) |
| OM9 | **Worlds as adjectives** | Limits, speeds and dangers are per-world character, not fixed caps |
| OM10 | **Texture is optional** | Depth is a dial (MU4). The core loop works with every detail switched off |
| OM11 | **The world moves on its own** | Natural course continues; diffusion, rivals and reactions happen without the player (TW, PW) |

# dlg-absence: Dialogue: voice, knowledge-aware lines, barks, rumor, reactions, refusal, return visits / what Weibao would notice missing after a long session, from a creative director's view

1. Emotional Fatigue
The player may feel drained by constant character reactions, leading to disengagement.
Check playtest logs for session duration drops after high-interaction periods.
M

2. Rumor Decay
Misinformation persists too long, breaking immersion when old news is repeated.
Audit the rumor system to ensure old information fades or updates over time.
L

3. Voice Distinction
Characters sound too similar, making it hard to identify speakers in groups.
Compare voice samples to ensure distinct tone and rhythm for each persona.
M

4. Knowledge Gaps
Characters answer questions they should not know, breaking the knowledge-aware rule.
Run a test where the player asks about future events to verify refusal logic.
S

5. Bark Spacing
Background noises interrupt critical dialogue, causing players to miss key plot points.
Measure the time gap between barks and main dialogue to ensure clarity.
S

6. Refusal Tone
Characters refuse requests in a way that feels hostile rather than realistic.
Review refusal scripts to match the character's established personality traits.
M

7. Return Visit Stakes
Coming back to a location yields no change, making repeat visits feel pointless.
Check if returning to a scene triggers new dialogue based on time passed.
L

8. Observer Bias
Rumors change too quickly, causing the world to feel unstable and confusing.
Track how long a rumor takes to spread to ensure a believable pace.
M

9. Interrupt Grace
Stopping a conversation feels abrupt, leaving the player without context on why it ended.
Add a brief transition line when a character is interrupted by an event.
S

10. Idle Tension
The idle loop forces the player to act, reducing the relaxing feel of the genre.
Monitor player stress levels during automated dialogue sequences.
L

11. Native Fit
Some world-specific dialogue feels generic, ignoring the source material's unique style.
Compare generated lines against the original source material for tone match.
M

12. Checkpoint Clarity
Players cannot tell which choices had lasting consequences on future interactions.
Highlight dialogue options that trigger permanent changes in the UI.
S

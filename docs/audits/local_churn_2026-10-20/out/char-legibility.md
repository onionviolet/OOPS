# char-legibility: Characters: named cast, crowds, rivals, companions, their state over time / what the player can see, inspect, trust or be confused by on screen

1. Ambiguous Identity Markers
Crowd members lack distinct visual or textual cues to differentiate them from important NPCs, causing player confusion about who matters.
A builder would test by blurring faces and names, then measuring if players can still identify key rivals or companions.
Effort M

2. Trust Indicators for AI
Autonomous characters make decisions that contradict their stated traits, leading players to question the simulation's integrity.
A builder would log character actions against trait definitions and flag inconsistencies for manual review.
Effort L

3. State Decay Visibility
Companions do not visibly show fatigue, hunger, or morale changes, making their state seem static or unresponsive.
A builder would add a subtle visual overlay or status icon that updates in real-time based on internal stats.
Effort S

4. Rival Motivations
Rivals act on hidden agendas that the player cannot infer from available context, resulting in unpredictable encounters.
A builder would create a "dossier" screen that reveals partial clues about rival goals through investigation.
Effort M

5. Companion Inventory Transparency
Shared party inventories obscure individual ownership, causing disputes over item use or loss during adventures.
A builder would implement a split-view UI that clearly separates shared and personal slots with ownership tags.
Effort M

6. Temporal Context Gaps
Time skips do not explain how characters' relationships or statuses evolved during the offline period.
A builder would generate a brief narrative summary of key changes that occurred while the game was paused.
Effort M

7. Crowd Density Clarity
High-density crowds obscure pathing and interaction opportunities, preventing players from targeting specific individuals.
A builder would add a toggle that highlights interactable NPCs within a crowded scene using color coding.
Effort S

8. Legacy Character Persistence
Named characters from previous books or worlds may appear without context, confusing players about their relevance.
A builder would tag returning characters with a "returning" badge and a short recall of their prior role.
Effort L

9. Emotional Feedback Loops
Companions react to player actions with generic responses, failing to convey unique emotional states or bonds.
A builder would map specific player choices to distinct dialogue trees and facial expressions for each companion.
Effort L

10. Legal Status Ambiguity
Characters' legal standing in different worlds is unclear, leading to unexpected consequences for player actions.
A builder would display a jurisdiction tag for each NPC that updates based on the current world location.
Effort M

11. Ghosting Mechanics
Deleted or retired characters may reappear due to save corruption, breaking the illusion of a persistent world.
A builder would implement a hard-deletion flag that prevents character data from being reloaded in new sessions.
Effort S

12. Cultural Bias in AI
Companion behaviors reflect default cultural assumptions that clash with the source material's setting.
A builder would audit AI scripts for cultural stereotypes and replace them with source-accurate behavioral patterns.
Effort L

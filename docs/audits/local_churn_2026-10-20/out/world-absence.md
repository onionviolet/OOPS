# world-absence: Implementing entire worlds end to end (everything between a source book and a playable, finished world) / what Weibao would notice missing after a long session, from a creative director's view

1. **Emotional Resonance Check**
The docs define structural completeness but lack a metric for whether the player feels the weight of the source material during a 10 hour session.
A builder would run a playtest and ask the tester to describe one moment that felt "true" to the source before checking system coverage.
M

2. **Dialogue Texture Audit**
Current gates focus on lore accuracy rather than the rhythm and voice of character interactions over time.
A builder would sample 50 lines of generated dialogue and compare them to source examples for tone consistency and repetition.
L

3. **Pacing and Tension Arcs**
The workflow lists stages but does not verify that the world provides rising and falling tension appropriate for an idle RPG loop.
A builder would map player activity over a simulated week and check for monotony or unmanageable spikes in difficulty.
M

4. **Sensory Immersion Gaps**
The "little details" section exists but lacks specific checks for audio, visual, and haptic feedback that sustain atmosphere.
A builder would disable one sensory channel and play for an hour to see if the world still feels coherent and engaging.
S

5. **Player Agency Boundaries**
The docs mention random starts but do not clarify where player choice ends and scripted narrative begins in long sessions.
A builder would attempt to break the narrative by ignoring key prompts and document where the system forces the player back on track.
L

6. **Community and Social Loops**
The focus is on single-player experience, missing how characters interact with each other to create emergent social drama.
A builder would simulate three NPC interactions over 24 game hours and check if their relationships evolve logically without player input.
M

7. **Memory and Consequence**
The completion standard checks for content presence but not for how the world remembers player actions over long time spans.
A builder would perform a significant action in week one and check if it influences world state or dialogue in week four.
L

8. **Onboarding Friction**
The intake process is documented but lacks a check for how confusing the first ten minutes are for a new player.
A builder would have a new user play for ten minutes and record where they hesitate or ask for help.
S

9. **Economic Balance**
The workflow covers lore items but does not verify that resource flows remain balanced over hundreds of hours of idle play.
A builder would run a simulation to 1000 hours and check for resource inflation or deflation that breaks the game loop.
M

10. **Accessibility Features**
The docs assume a standard control scheme and visual presentation, missing needs for colorblind or motor-impaired players.
A builder would run the world with high contrast and text-to-speech enabled to ensure all critical information is accessible.
M

11. **Bug Tolerance**
The completion gates assume a bug-free state, lacking a plan for how the world degrades when minor errors occur.
A builder would inject small errors and check if the world crashes or if it continues playably with a visible error log.
L

12. **Long Term Retention Hooks**
The stages end at "past it" but do not define what keeps the player engaged after all main content is finished.
A builder would play until all main goals are complete and record what motivates the player to continue for another hour.
M

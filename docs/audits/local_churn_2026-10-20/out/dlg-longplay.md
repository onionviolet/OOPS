# dlg-longplay: Dialogue: voice, knowledge-aware lines, barks, rumor, reactions, refusal, return visits / what breaks, repeats or goes stale after ten hours or ten idle in-game years

1. **Knowledge Decay**
Long-term idle play risks characters retaining perfect memory of trivial details, making them feel like static encyclopedias rather than living beings. A builder can test this by observing if a character recalls a minor interaction from year one with the same intensity as a recent threat. Effort: M

2. **Rumor Entropy**
Rumors may stagnate or loop endlessly without a mechanism to age or fade, cluttering the information space with outdated noise. A builder can verify this by checking if old rumors are replaced or archived after a set number of in-game days. Effort: S

3. **Reaction Fatigue**
Repeated idle cycles might cause characters to react with the same emotional tone to new events, breaking immersion through robotic responses. A builder can audit logs to see if reaction variety drops below a certain threshold over ten years of play. Effort: M

4. **Refusal Inflation**
Without dynamic thresholds, characters may refuse all interactions after repeated rejections, leading to a dead-end state where no progress is possible. A builder can simulate a scenario where the player ignores a character for a year and then attempts a new request. Effort: S

5. **Voice Drift**
Characters may lose their distinct voices over long periods if dialogue lines are not dynamically adjusted to reflect their evolving status or health. A builder can compare character speech patterns at the start versus after five idle years to detect homogenization. Effort: L

6. **Return Visit Staleness**
Returning to a location after long absences might yield no new dialogue or state changes, making time feel meaningless. A builder can check if a character's greeting or availability changes based on the duration of the player's absence. Effort: S

7. **Bark Overload**
Idle barks may become repetitive or frequent, annoying the player or wasting processing power without adding narrative value. A builder can measure the frequency of barks per hour and player feedback on their relevance. Effort: S

8. **Knowledge Silos**
Information might get stuck with one character and fail to spread, isolating plot points and reducing the world's interconnectedness. A builder can track how many characters know a specific piece of evidence after it has been shared once. Effort: M

9. **Emotional Lag**
Characters may not update their emotional state quickly enough after major events, leading to mismatched reactions in subsequent idle periods. A builder can observe if a character's mood reflects recent trauma or joy within a few hours of the event. Effort: M

10. **Dialogue Branching**
Static dialogue trees may fail to account for the cumulative effect of multiple small interactions, resulting in shallow character arcs. A builder can test if a character's long-term attitude changes based on a series of minor positive or negative encounters. Effort: L

11. **Rumor Verification**
Rumors may lack a clear path to verification, leaving the player uncertain about their truth value over time. A builder can check if the game provides a way to confirm or deny rumors after they have circulated for a set time. Effort: M

12. **Idle Automation**
Automated idle actions might bypass meaningful dialogue choices, reducing the player's agency and emotional connection. A builder can review idle logs to ensure that critical interactions still require active player input. Effort: M

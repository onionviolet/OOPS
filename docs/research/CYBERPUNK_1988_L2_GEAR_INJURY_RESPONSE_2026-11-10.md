## What the evidence supports

- F1. Money is the gate for gear: a starter purse of 2,000 (VFE 19, 49), a price list (VFE 50) and
  cyberware priced from 400 up to 8,000 for plating (VFE 48). Rarity is a second gate on weapons (FNF 17).
- F2. Cyberware has three seats: mall clinic walk-in (VFE 21), ripperdoc black market (VFE 21), and
  employer-funded gear with control strings (VFE 26).
- F3. Cyberware costs Humanity: 1 EMP per 10 points, and therapy returns 2 per week only if the
  chrome is removed (VFE 21).
- F4. Injury is a state ladder: Flesh, Serious, Critical, Mortal, Dead (FNF 14, 22), with timers
  and saves (FNF 14-15).
- F5. Medicine is a priced service, with prices per wound state (VFE 44) and a per-minute rescue
  franchise (VFE 43).
- F6. Responders are district-bound: city police citywide, corporate police only on corporate ground
  (WNC 5, 28); corporate offices hold embassy-like status (WNC 27).

## Chain 1: gear access

1. Buy or be given: spend the 2,000 or take employer gear (VFE 19, 26).
2. Pick a seat: clinic (open, minor "optionware"), ripperdoc (hidden, dear, risky), employer (free, leashed) (VFE 21, 23, 26).
3. Install: plugs use anesthetic, drill and gel (VFE 23); limbs need major surgery (VFE 25); chips swap in one phase and need practice hours (VFE 24).
4. Pay in Humanity: sum costs, lose EMP per 10 (VFE 21, 49).
5. Legal gate on arms: concealed handguns need a 4-day check, 25 fee and a filed ballistic print;
   full-auto carry is a 5 to 7 year offence; body plating is called illegal (WNC 6, VFE 25).
6. Revoke: only employer levers are shown (sabotaged chrome, command-kill, hostages) (VFE 26). No repossession or license audit is in the read pages.
- Other grants: a Nomad Pack gives weapons, cash and relatives by Family Status (VFE 10); Fixers arrange armed or smuggling jobs (WNC 17); Militech and Arasaka license troops and arms (WNC 33).

## Chain 2: injury to recovery

1. Hit: location by 1D10 unless aimed; damage from ballistic table, head doubled (FNF 13, 21). Armor subtracts SP, layers cap at 30, each penetration costs 1 SP (FNF 14, 19).
2. Wound: killing damage by Body Type gives a state; later wounds use a cumulative table (FNF 14, 22). Serious or Critical add a 1D6 effect such as a broken bone or amputation (FNF 14).
3. Shock: Consciousness Save, stun one phase or out for 1D10 phases (FNF 15).
4. Clocks: Serious untreated past 20 minutes turns Critical; Critical untreated 12 turns (2 minutes) turns Mortal (FNF 14-15). Mortal: no actions, a Death Save each turn (target by Body Type, VW 2 to VS 6) (FNF 15, 22 recheck).
5. Stabilize: surgery before the save fails stops dying and starts recovery (FNF 15). Clinical death beyond that is a Death State ladder of ten, one per minute (VFE 43).
6. Recover: recovery rolls on Body Type after a waiting period (Flesh 1 week; Serious about 2 weeks; example took 6 weeks) (FNF 15, 16, 22). Medical care treats each wound one level lower (VFE 44).
7. Pay: Flesh 50, Serious 150, Critical 500 to 1,000 (referee), Mortal over 2,000, hospital 300 a day at least, health plan 100 a month covering up to 90 percent (VFE 44). Cyberware and Trauma Team fees are extra.

## Chain 3: trouble and response

1. Call: 911 or a deadman transmitter that fires on coma brainwaves (VFE 43).
2. Arrive: an armored AV-4 with driver, senior medtech, assistant and two security; security clears an ongoing firefight (VFE 43). Billing is 100 per minute, or insurance, or 500 monthly (VFE 43).
3. Stabilize: load, life support, cool the body, rapid surgery; revive on a 1D10 above the Death State, else the patient is a Body Bank candidate (VFE 43).
4. Aftermath: donor-carded corpses pay a 200 to 600 bounty and get looted after fights (VFE 44).
5. Other responders: city police (armored squad cars, a Psycho Squad for cyberpsychos) (WNC 5, VFE 21); corporate police in their own zones, some cities handing policing to corps (WNC 5, 28); corp security teams with set sizes (WNC 21, 29-30); hired mercenary or gang teams (WNC 10).
6. Law: no plea bargaining, felonies 5 to 10 years, murder is capital (WNC 5).
- Friction: one trauma crew report shows corp security refusing an extraction on a cited law, a rival ambulance firm and ambushes (WNC 40, fiction, not procedure).

## Cross-links

- X1. Gear to injury: body plating switches damage to the structural kind (VFE 48); cyberware sits outside medical prices (VFE 44); the Humanity cure requires removing gear (VFE 21).
- X2. Injury to response: the 2-minute Critical-to-Mortal clock and the per-minute Death State both set how fast a call must land (FNF 15, VFE 43).
- X3. Response to gear: corporate and city jurisdiction decides who may hold or confiscate weapons on a given street (WNC 5-6, 28).
- X4. Money loops: insurance, premium and corporate employment pay for rescue; free chrome buys leash (VFE 26, 43, 44).

## Contradictions and gaps

- C1. Critical recovery wait: FNF 15 prose says at least two weeks; FNF 22 table caption says three. Open.
- C2. Mortal timeline: Death Save turns (FNF 15) and Death State minutes (VFE 43) are not reconciled. Open.
- C3. Body plating is illegal and rare (VFE 25) yet priced on the sheet (VFE 48).
- C4. Trauma Team (franchise, VFE 43) versus Lifeline Trauma Inc. and a rival (WNC 40): relation unstated.
- G1. Cop, Corporate and Fixer role abilities are likely on held VFE 7-9; no Fixer or Cop procedure here.
- G2. Wound, Cumulative, Consciousness and Recovery table numbers are not read (only Death Save).
- G3. Unscheduled table reviews: VFE 26, 27, 33, 41, 43-45, 48; FNF 24; WNC 30-33, 37-41. The cyberware sheet is unchecked.
- G4. No police or gang response time, fee or arrest procedure; WNC is mostly setting and adventure text.
- G5. The extraction missed the Mortal price and cryotank cost; added from the VFE 44 image. Sample is not a recall audit.

## Proposals (labelled proposals, not accepted decisions)

- P1. Default: gear has three seats with different price, risk and leash. Dial: leash strength by employer. In-world reason: employer chrome is free but comes with control levers (VFE 26).
- P2. Default: injury runs two clocks, short combat turns to Mortal and slow minutes of rescue billing. Dial: response speed by district wealth. Reason: the franchise bills by the minute (VFE 43, FNF 15).
- P3. Default: district decides responder (city, corp, hired). Dial: how far corp ground extends. Reason: corp police patrol only corp facilities (WNC 5, 28).
- P4. Default: recovery treats wounds one level lower with prices as a recurring sink, Humanity needing removal to heal. Dial: therapy rate and price scale. Reason: advanced medicine and Humanity rules (VFE 21, 44).

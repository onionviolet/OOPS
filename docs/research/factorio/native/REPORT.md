## Faithfulness and repairs

The root independently verified every closed ARM gzip/count, C hash, raw body range/hash and decoded instruction against the exact original slice in [ARM acquisition](../../../gm/dispatch/steam-wave-2026-10-06/native-usage/ARM_CORPUS_ACQUISITION.json). That proves retained byte identity, not complete control-flow or semantic recovery.

The latest ARM body ranges form a disjoint 40,072,116-byte union. Their 9,230,638 decoded instructions cover 36,922,552 bytes, also without overlap. Candidate bodies contain 3,149,564 undecoded bytes; executable-marked memory contains another 1,698,900 bytes outside those bodies. Candidate ranges can contain alignment, literal/jump tables, unreachable regions or missing decoded code, and executable segment permissions also mark Mach-O headers. The immutable packed program's global listing has 9,232,817 instructions. The 2,179 difference requires exact global-address/function-ownership reconciliation, which remains pending; it is not silently rounded away.

The [root flow readback](../../../gm/dispatch/steam-wave-2026-10-06/native-usage/ARM_FLOW_READBACK.json) identifies 62,548 generated-C warning functions, including 19,036 jump-table warnings and 31,069 no-return warnings. It finds 14,590 computed-jump instructions in 13,765 functions; 14,588 have no discovered target flow. Those are diagnosis counts, not 13,765 confirmed defects. The native writer's exact-context scan separately infers 2,439 possible byte-table dispatches, 9,776 possible pointer dispatches and 2,373 unknown jumps. Virtual calls, indirect tail calls and intra-function tables must be distinguished by actual evidence. A success status does not mean all CFG targets are recovered.

A concrete private AARCH64 lowering repair restores the second source in nine scalar FMAX/FMAXNM/FMIN constructors. [Repair](LOWERING_REPAIR.md) and [qualification](LOWERING_REPAIR.json) preserve original/changed source and compiled SLA identities. Forty-eight decoded samples and 216 finite pcode cases were checked: 27 failed before, all 216 passed after. The original 1,089 affected methods were refreshed in the full saved program, all emitted C, 211 changed C, and raw bodies stayed identical. The root's latest audit confirms all 1,105 affected methods, 1,951 scalar instructions and 234 second-source alias cases use the repaired SLA. NaN, FPCR, signaling and signed-zero contracts remain unqualified.

Thirteen actual ARM failures remain. The giant GLAD loader's SIGTERM was a measured resource intervention, with its complete failed body record retained; it was not a C success. Small Splitter failures also have a concrete preparation gap: only two of fourteen actual byte-table dispatches were qualified by the older repair. [Backend review](../../../gm/dispatch/steam-wave-2026-10-06/native-usage/backend-review/REVIEW.md) and its [acquisition](../../../gm/dispatch/steam-wave-2026-10-06/native-usage/BACKEND_REVIEW_ACQUISITION.json) pin all fourteen original table/target sets and official repair candidates. Full saved-program guard/target qualification, dedicated jump-table overrides and actual backend diagnosis remain required. Body-only mini-program success, option toggles that leave a deterministic error unchanged, throw suppression or fabricated hashes do not close this gate.

x86's first bounded analysis hit a 512 MB Java heap error in the optional constant-reference analyzer, then failed its transaction checkpoint. A read-only reopen proved its saved state remained the complete seed with zero decoded instructions. The 9,869,987 live instructions were lost and are not counted. The exact seed project is archived and roundtrip-verified. A changed pass disabled the proven failing optional analyzer and the separately demonstrated shared-return cancellation bottleneck. It was manually cancelled during FID matching before a further heap error, successfully saved and exactly reopened with 182,398 functions and 9,869,783 global instructions. Propagated constants, pointer references and cross-function return inference may remain unknown. The same full callable/function coverage objective and measured native memory/disk guard remain in force.

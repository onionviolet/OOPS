## What the complete read changes

**RS11: ability creation needs several routes.** Ordinary teaching joins
personal desire, imagination, energy aptitude, understanding, effort and
conditions. An emergency can redirect a planned design. Later, an exceptional
Creator immediately authors a functioning artifact, challenging the instruction
to wait for manifestation. Proposal: a world supplies its actual creation routes
and exceptions. A shared authoring envelope can describe intent, source, effect,
conditions, cost and observed behavior without forcing every world or outlier
through the same mandatory training process. Early ch. 4/8/20 and late
[ch. 207](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/63639/reincarnated-in-a-shounen-manga/chapter/1300056/chapter-207-thorn-crown)
provide the comparison; the late scene comes through inherited memories.

**RS12: aptitude, reserve, output, control and rank are different.** Affinity
percentages do not alone predict energy supply or victory. Skilled control,
material access, favorable terrain, allies and a specific counter can change an
encounter. Official rank also has institutional gates. Proposal: preserve these
axes where the chosen world uses them; present their useful consequences instead
of collapsing every power into one rating. Existing [ability authoring](../../worlds/ABILITY_AUTHORING.md)
owns variants and execution; no universal conversion table is selected.

**RS13: a condition is more than a cooldown.** The source contains activation
conditions, procedural requirements, ongoing promises, finite charges,
recharge triggers, lasting sacrifices, counterconditions and control
commitments. One timer starts recharging only after all remaining charge is
spent. Lifespan expenditure can leave lasting bodily changes after a temporary
boost ends. Proposal: name the actual condition, subject, evaluator where known,
price, breach consequence and termination behavior. This is per-world logic,
not a universal rule that all powerful effects require equal sacrifice.

**RS14: ability transfer can change a condition's applicability.** A late
transfer preserves an ability while a restriction still refers to its original
creator, enabling a source-specific loophole. Proposal: distinguish author,
current holder, executor, target and the subject named in a commitment. Preserve
resolved contract meaning through changes of body, identity or carrier.
Whether a restriction binds a person, body, soul, item or current bearer follows
that world's actual rule. Do not impose anti-exploit balancing against a valid
fictional interaction. Detailed late evidence is in reader C239.

**RS15: assisted success need not mean learned mastery.** A power first performs
precision the character cannot consciously reproduce. Practice later makes some
related actions usable independently. Proposal: keep knowing a method, holding
a source, assisted execution and unassisted mastery distinct. Training may use
assistance as evidence or feedback when the world supports it; the result does
not automatically teach every hidden step. This extends existing acquisition/
execution distinctions rather than creating another skill inventory.

**RS16: rules are discoveries, and explanations can be lies.** An opponent
moving while protected falsifies part of an initial defense hypothesis; opening
their eyes supplies a working counter. Some people deliberately misstate their
ability limits. Proposal: store actual rules separately from observer hypotheses,
reported explanations and tested counters. Use [means and appraisal](../../MEANS_AND_APPRAISAL.md)
for both sides. A failed test should revise the relevant belief rather than
silently exposing the full contract.

## Foreknowledge and changing events

**RS17: foreknowledge has kinds and provenance.** Remembered plot, an item lead,
map knowledge, technical knowledge, divination, inherited memories and current
investigation have different precision and failure modes. Knowing an eventual
item owner does not reveal its present acquisition date. A remembered technique
may remain useful after its expected scene disappears. Proposal: identify what
a memory claims, its source/version, granularity and known contradictions.
Confidence follows evidence; it does not provide numerical omniscience without
a fitting means.

**RS18: intervention changes dependencies, not a global reliability meter.**
Major survival outcomes and entire expected arcs change. The protagonist says his
future event sequence has become obsolete, yet still uses knowledge of powers
and locations as leads. Proposal: changing a participant, schedule or cause
invalidates the forecasts that depend on it. Unaffected facts can remain useful.
An enemy's surviving motive can produce another attempt without a mandatory
compensating catastrophe. This extends [timelines and divergence](../../TIMELINES_AND_DIVERGENCE.md)
under AD2/AD25; it is not an implemented global event graph.

**RS19: prediction wording and interpretation can diverge.** Late divination
correctly points toward a title while its reader misidentifies the subject.
Reactions to a prediction also help bring about the observed outcome. Proposal:
record the prediction as received, who interpreted it and what that belief led
them to do. An accurate phrase need not reveal identity, timing, cause or all
consequences. Fate perception, probability, active manipulation and unavoidable
outcome are separate capabilities.

**RS20: apparent time travel can be a mistaken world model.** This is the
largest correction unavailable to the opening sample. Apparent duplicates and
consistent rescues first encourage a fixed-loop interpretation. Later, versions
of familiar people have incompatible histories, and a spatial vow is offered as
the mechanism allowing people from different dimensions to arrive. There are
also literal fate powers, which do not establish automatic restoration of every
plot event. See [ch. 251](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/63639/reincarnated-in-a-shounen-manga/chapter/1349268/chapter-251-not-a-loop)
and [ch. 255](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/63639/reincarnated-in-a-shounen-manga/chapter/1353225/chapter-255-waiting-for-2000-years).
The final historical mechanism is attributed to its speaker; the living island
and differing versions are depicted.

Proposal: world-instance identity and actor history remain separate from what a
character calls the phenomenon. Similar appearance, name or scene does not prove
a shared identity or one closed timeline. Source-specific temporal or fate rules
can still exist. Their evidence and means decide, as required by AD1–AD7.

## A1 implemented, engine-only, 2026-10-17

Weibao: "Start A1 now in this session: core conditions plus the G3-G5 data fields
and the four fixture tests, engine-only." Rules: AR1–AR16 (no `main.js` line, no
new import, no new save slice), world data as the gate (G1), AD36 verification.

**What exists now.** Ability rows in `src/structured_ability_runtime.js` accept an
optional `conditions` list. Each condition compiles into the existing native
command transaction, so no new state family or migration is needed:
- charges: an actor resource `<id>.charges` paid as a cost; starts at `initial`
  (default max); a separate `<id>.recharge` command fills it when `empty`, or by
  `partial` amounts, under its own trigger predicate;
- restriction (`requires`) bound to `subject: author` (original source route only,
  so a transferred carrier escapes it) or `holder` (every route);
- lasting `price` effects appended to the use;
- `breach` in `block` mode, or `consequence` mode with a once-only `<id>.enforce`
  command run by the enforcer agent or, under law, the penalized party;
- `ends`: a predicate that lifts the restriction and stops enforcement;
- G3 `strengthens.capacity` raises the bound power's capacity (needs a real
  restriction); G4 `parties` and `enforcer {kind, id, reason}`; G5 `sourceWorldId`
  (default: the authoring world). Compile output adds `conditions` (normalized
  records) and `seeds` (starting charges and false breach/end flags).

**Engine fact learned.** Rules treat an absent fact as unknown, so a negated
breach or end test fails closed. Simple `actor.`/`world.` flags in `breach.when`
and `ends` are seeded false; knowledge or item evidence must exist before a world
negates it. That is why the fae fixture ends on a spoken name (a world flag).

## A2 implemented, engine-only, 2026-10-17

Weibao: "Start A2 now in this session: claims with named dependencies that go
stale only when a precondition changes, hypothesis revision by test, G6 means
field, engine-only with fixtures." Rules: AR1–AR16 (new pure module, no `main.js`
line, no save slice), RS16–RS18, G6, AD36.

**What exists now.** `src/claim_runtime.js` compiles a world's `claims` list. Each
claim names what it says (`about`), its `means` (memory, divination, information,
report, investigation, inheritance), its `precision` (gist, event, window, exact)
and `dependsOn` predicates. `claimEffects` gives an actor the belief as an
interpreted knowledge entry plus means/precision actor facts. `claimFresh` holds
while every dependency holds; `claimStale` holds once one is known to fail; an
unknown dependency is neither. A claim with no dependencies cannot go stale, only
be refuted. A `hypothesis` compiles a once-only `<id>.test` command whose weighted
outcomes read a hidden `world.` truth fact: the test is equally available either
way, and it rewrites the belief as `understood` with the authored result and a
`status` fact, never the rule itself.

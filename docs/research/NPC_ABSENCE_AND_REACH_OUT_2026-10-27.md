### A.2 What the table says, by pattern

1. **Hard dates exist but are rare, visible and often payable.** Fallout, Mass Effect 2 and Persona 5 use them; Majora's Mask makes the clock the whole game and cushions it with a persistent notebook. [S/I]
2. **Decay is usually capped or paired with an easy refill.** Stardew's floors at 8 or 10 hearts, and gifts offsetting the decay, is the closest template for "closeness cools, never breaks" (N2). [S]
3. **Reach-out is rare as a courtesy and common as a hook.** Animal Crossing letters and Neko Atsume gifts are warm; Tokimeki's gossip and Tamagotchi's death are punishments. The warm pattern suits a single-player life-sim. [S/I]
4. **The best "missed" model is the story gap.** Wildermyth reports what happened between chapters as content, not as a penalty. [S]
5. **A fully pausing clock is a valid answer.** Disco Elysium moves time only by action. For this project it means a Calm life can have no real-time pressure. [S/I]
6. **Time as a spendable bank is the idle genre's answer to absence.** [S]
7. **Gaps in this pass.** Long Live the Queen, Higurashi/Ever17, Tokimeki thresholds, Tamagotchi timings, CK3 default options and Idle Loops were not sourced; treat those rows as [U].

### A.3 Our two baselines

YAIR and Proto23 both run one real second as one game minute and neither has offline catch-up in core ([comparison](proto23/PROTO23_VS_YAIRPG_2026-10-10.md)). A faithful port therefore has no absence problem: nothing happens while the tab is closed. [S, repo] Offline catch-up, return notes and reach-out are all variant-layer additions and need a per-world switch (WV6).

## Part B. World cards: absence and reach-out (WR1 shape, proposals)

Every card gives who reaches out and how, what drifts, what keeps its own schedule, what a miss becomes, at most one hard clock, default pace and safe-idle behaviour. Pace names follow the attention report (Calm, Lively, Hands-on). Only the current world advances; other worlds are frozen by default (accepted background).

### B.1 Village (our variant)

- **Reaches out:** the courier and the runner bring short notes from the miller, the watch captain and a relay friend; a traveler's ask arrives as a letter on the notice board. Reply window: a few days at Lively, a week at Calm (N1).
- **Drifts:** closeness with a neighbour cools slowly, with a floor at "known face"; a warm return restores faster than it fell (N2). Stock on the market stall drifts to the weekly cycle.
- **Own schedules:** the miller, the watch captain, the trader and two neighbours, each with a learnable day shape (N3, N6).
- **A miss becomes:** a line in the return note ("the market went on without you; the miller kept your place") and, where a standing event was open, it simply waits (E1, E2).
- **Hard clock (one, optional):** the weekly market day, shown early, with the missed stall re-offered next week (E7).
- **Default pace:** Lively, droppable to Calm; **safe idle:** the watch routine runs, stops at a deadline choice or empty supplies, and the return note lists what settled. A greeting line notices the gap (N4).
- **Distinct from the others by:** warmth and small stakes; gifts and notes bring you back.

### B.2 Proto23 (credited port)

- **Reaches out:** nobody by default in the faithful pack. The variant layer may add one terse hub note per absence, off in the faithful build. [I]
- **Drifts:** nothing; the source runs 1 s = 1 min with no catch-up. The port keeps that. [S, repo]
- **Own schedules:** the day cycle and weekly free meal, fixed by the source. Hub encounters use a flat chance rather than state, so nothing is "waiting" for you. [S, GM plan 15.2]
- **A miss becomes:** a missed free meal is just a missed meal; no story beat unless the variant layer is on.
- **Hard clock:** none; the faithful pack offers no deadline.
- **Default pace:** Lively; **safe idle:** the tab closing pauses the world exactly as in the source. Offline settlement is a variant option, off for the faithful build.
- **Distinct by:** subtraction. It is the control world for absence: nothing reaches out, nothing drifts.

### B.3 YAIR (faithful baseline)

- **Reaches out:** nobody. Quest givers wait where they are. Standing quests wait (E1).
- **Drifts:** prices recover daily, as upstream. [S, GM plan 15.3]
- **Own schedules:** none beyond the daily recovery; zones respawn.
- **A miss becomes:** nothing. An unfinished quest stays unfinished.
- **Hard clock:** none.
- **Default pace:** Calm; **safe idle:** zones keep running while the tab is open; closing pauses.
- **Distinct by:** the road outward is its own reward; the world never asks for you.

### B.4 LOTM (Tingen)

- **Reaches out:** a clerk's note, a church summons, a broker's sealed message, delivered at the lodging door (a knock, then a slip under it). Wording follows PR1 discovery language, foggy and formal. Reply windows are shorter than the Village's, scaled by pace (N1).
- **Drifts:** notice and suspicion cool slowly if you lie low (the opposite of the Village, where closeness cools); a contact's trust cools but does not break unless an in-world cause occurs (N2).
- **Own schedules:** the clerk, the broker and an officer have shifts; the church paperwork moves on its own cycle (N3).
- **A miss becomes:** a quiet entry: "someone else read it tonight". A missed summons is re-issued with a harder tone, never silently deleted (E1).
- **Hard clock (one, rare):** an acting or digestion window tied to a dose; shown early with the dated reason, recoverable at a named cost (N5, E7). Digestion advances only in Tingen (TL5), so a frozen life cannot lose it.
- **Default pace:** Hands-on; **safe idle:** routines stop at any notice-raising choice and when a duty comes due; the world does not run danger against an away player by default. Simulated-absence risk with a named reward is available here first (E6).
- **Distinct by:** dread rises with what you do, not with how long you are gone.

### B.5 HP

- **Reaches out:** term mail by owl: a lesson reminder, a debt notice, a letter from home. The calendar carries most of the weight.
- **Drifts:** friendships and standing cool across a term break and warm on return; a lapsed essay or trade debt accrues in a visible figure.
- **Own schedules:** the term timetable, weekly lessons, term breaks. Teachers and classmates follow it, so schedules are learnable (N3).
- **A miss becomes:** a mark ("Again."), a re-offered lesson, a debt line; never a lost year by default (E1).
- **Hard clock (one):** the end-of-term examination date, shown at term start, with a retake route (N5, E7).
- **Default pace:** Lively, scheduled; **safe idle:** practice routines run and stop at the next lesson or letter that needs an answer.
- **Distinct by:** a published calendar; absence costs a timetable slot, not relationships.

## Update 2026-10-27 (Weibao's correction): many clocks, scoped to the life (proposals)

Weibao's words: "Doesnt proto23 have the weekly meals from the dojo? Yair has the
merchant stock change maybe, LOTm could have many hard clocks with varian degrees
of things? lots of hard clocks can be from the book and the lore accordingly?

**Corrections.** Proto23's weekly free meal (B.2) is a real rhythm, so B.2 and the
contrast table's "None / Nothing" mean "no NPC reach-out and no penalty", not "no
rhythm". YAIR's daily price recovery is the one rhythm this report sourced; a
merchant stock change is unverified here and belongs to a source check of the
pinned YAIR data. The earlier "at most one hard clock per world" (E7, N5) is
superseded as a default: LOTM can carry many clocks, and the limit becomes how
many are visible and firm at once for one life.

**C1. A clock is a small record, not a global timer.** Fields: id; source (book
or lore beat, or an original one); scope (the life facts it attaches to: an
employer, a faction, a pathway and sequence, a contact, a home, a promise);
degree; lead (how early it shows); what a miss does (move, harden, re-offer,
close with a trace); recovery (named cost). Default: a clock exists in the world
data but is only active, visible and counted for a life whose facts match its scope.
Dial: a world may show ambient clocks for flavor; reason: absence pressure should
come from what this life took on, so an unattached life is not nagged (matches
the sealed-secrets and frozen-world rules).

**C2. Degrees as a ladder.** Ambient (flavor, never penalizes), soft (a miss moves
or re-offers it), firm (a miss hardens it or costs standing or closeness), hard
(a miss closes it with a trace; always shown early and recoverable at a named
cost, N5). Default: a life shows a few firm clocks at once at Lively and fewer at
Calm; hard clocks are rare per life, not per world. Dial: the pace and a world's
temperament. Reason: many clocks are fine if few press at once.

**C3. Evaluate lazily.** Clocks that matter to no current fact are not simulated;
on return the engine checks only the attached ones and writes missed ones into
the return note (E2). Frozen worlds do not tick (accepted default); a running
world is the exception with a stated rule.

**C4. Where clocks come from, per world (unverified lore reads marked [U]).**
- *LOTM:* pathway and sequence digestion windows tied to acting; official
  organization duty rotations and report dates; church observances and
  summons; periodic gatherings you were invited to; risk windows around a dose or
  ritual. [U] Each pathway can carry its own clocks, so a different pathway
  sees a different calendar. Content comes from a source deep dive, not memory.
- *Village (variant):* market day, watch roster, a relay window, a promised
  favor.
- *Proto23:* the weekly free meal and day cycle as the source has them; nothing
  added to the faithful build.
- *YAIR:* daily price recovery and quest chains as the source has them; a stock
  change only once verified.
- *HP:* term timetable, lessons, exams, fixtures, holiday breaks.

## Update 2026-10-27 (Weibao): schedules first, deadlines only from canon (proposals)

### Harvest, in particular (default, dial, reason)

- A crop has a ready time in world time. Banked time can bring it to ripe; it then
  waits in the field. Spoil is slow and set per crop and weather, shown on the
  plot (a limit as an adjective). Reason: a harvest should feel rewarded, and a
  week away should not erase the field.
- The bank never crosses a season boundary or a frost/flood date unannounced:
  catch-up stops at the door (R2) and the note names it.
- Harvest itself (picking, selling, choosing what to keep) is an attention verb
  and waits for you.

### To account for now

- **Upkeep must be charged.** Food, wages and fuel consumed during banked time
  are paid or the run stops at "supplies empty" (P2). Otherwise the bank creates
  free output.
- **Determinism.** Banked outcomes use the life's seed and a counter so a reload
  cannot reroll them; the spent amount is logged in the save.
- **Whose bank.** The bank accrues only for the current world's current life;
  frozen worlds do not bank. Recommended default; dial: a shared pool with a
  stated rate if Weibao asks.
- **Wall clock.** Read it once on load, clamp negatives, store the result in
  world minutes. Anti-tamper is out of scope beyond save integrity (AD22).
- **Faithful ports.** K1-K8 are variant-layer. Proto23 and YAIR in their faithful
  builds keep 1 real second = 1 game minute with no catch-up (WV6).
- **Rates stay open.** Multiples, caps and decay of the bank are not settled here;
  the 24 h cap and 5x to 20x examples are reference points, not choices.

- **Random is the last layer, not the only one.** Calendar events, orders, NPC

  the gaps at the world's temperament. Reason: the player can read why things
  happened, which keeps absence fair.
- **Prevention is a reward.** Locks, helpers, standing orders and a good name
  change outcomes, so planning before leaving pays. A seeded test should show a
  prepared life losing less than an unprepared one.
- **Seeded and logged.** Each chunk uses the life's seed and a counter; draws are

- **Pace sets the ceiling, not the plot.** R1's readout decides how big a hazard
  can be; the world's content decides which hazards exist.
- **Faithful ports.** None of L1-L8 apply to the faithful Proto23 and YAIR builds.

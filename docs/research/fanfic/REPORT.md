[source deep dive playbook](../SOURCE_DEEP_DIVE_PLAYBOOK.md), module M3

## Findings

**F1 Fans already sort premises into a menu (shown, Fanlore).** Self-insert,
reborn into a native body (SI-OC), original character, time travel in three
kinds (body and mind, mind only into a younger self, short loops), fix-it,
divergence at one point, small alteration of canon, full alternate setting,
essence-transplant with new names, crossover, adoption by a hero from
another world, game-system fic, isekai and JumpChain. Each is a run start.

**F2 Something has to explain the setup, and fans keep it vague (shown).**
SpaceBattles coined the "Random Omnipotent Being" (ROB): a hand-wave that
moves, changes or empowers a character, with bored, bastard and
crossover-obsessed variants. JumpChain's version is a bored benefactor who
wants entertainment. *Inferred:* this matches our open, mysterious
awakening; the benefactor can stay unexplained or become a sealed secret.

**F3 JumpChain is the closest existing design to our multiverse (shown, the
Jumpchain wiki's rules pages).** One world per ten years; a point budget per world (often about
1,000); origins that grant memories and a place; perks and items; drawbacks
that pay extra points; companions imported at a price; everything kept
between worlds; afterward go home, stay or jump on. Variants: "gauntlets"
with no carried powers where dying does not end the chain; supplements such
as a baseline body upgrade and a storage "warehouse"; chain-wide drawbacks.
Stay length varies by world (a week to five centuries); dying sends you
home with what you earned; the end goal is a "Spark" to travel without the
benefactor, offered only by hard end worlds. Fans balance by consensus,
and the wiki lists settings judged unfit for a jump: worlds where one
person alone has powers, worlds with one power no budget can balance (it
names The Gamer), incomplete lore, and powers that trivialize the chain
(omnipotence, endless revival, consequence-free time travel, taking powers
from other worlds, travel at will).

**F4 Power can arrive on a schedule (shown, Fanlore).** "Celestial Forge"
fics give the lead a new power every 1,000 or 2,000 words, rolled from a
cost-tiered list, with unaffordable rolls banked until enough points build
up. Its best-known Worm fic is praised and also notorious for its slow pace,
because each new power gets explored at length. *Inferred:* this is an idle
mechanic already (power per unit of progress), and its failure mode is
pacing.

**F5 Delayed or conditional cheats are the Chinese norm (shown).** Systems
that wake at age 18 or at near-death, contribution points and experience
from beating canon enemies, draws earned by changing someone's fate, rewards
for visiting film locations, templates copied from defeated enemies, and a
magic school where spells are loans with a debt meter.

**F6 Insertion slots, not just insertion (shown and stated).** Chinese
novels replace a canon character (Daisy, Gwen, Batman), stand beside one as
a bystander (a classmate, an apprentice), embed on the enemy side (a Hydra
grunt, a villain's assistant), join a hero's family (an Invincible cousin),
or stand outside with a gated door. One sets its story in a world already
"crowded with transmigrators" (shown). *Inferred:* other inserted people
are ready-made rival protagonists.

**F7 Canon events become farms or pivots (shown and inferred).** Big film
events (the New York battle, Ultron, Infinity War) are treated as resource
harvests; the strongest villain is the usual finale; DC books use Knightfall
and timeline attacks as pivots. Western Worm fics mostly run on fixing
canon's worst events.

**F8 Readers praise limits and punish free power (shown and stated).**
Praised: a narrow power used cleverly, schemers, structural remixes of big
events. Criticized everywhere: the overpowered "Gary Stu" self-insert,
leads who lecture canon heroes, power exposition that stalls the plot,
hoarding and grinding instead of acting, retelling the films with the lead
absent, late drift into unrelated franchises, rushed endings. The "Mary Sue"
critique says the same: an insert who warps canon too easily makes the world
fake.

**F9 Readers can steer (shown, Fanlore).** Quests let the audience vote on
the lead's next move; on Sufficient Velocity, reader-written side stories
("omakes") are sometimes declared canon. Good quests mark dead-end choices
in recaps (stated).

**F10 Fanon is wrong belief at scale (shown).** Fan "facts" with little
canon basis spread until newcomers cannot tell them apart from canon. That
is rule 6 (readings can be wrong) as a social force.

**F11 Fandoms follow the adaptation (shown).** Fanlore's Invincible page is
almost all TV-show fandom. On AO3, 1,452 of 1,655 Invincible works also
carry the 2021 cartoon tag. Matches the comics pilot's warning to pick one
continuity per world.

**F12 Divergence dwarfs everything else (shown, AO3 tag counts).** "Canon
divergence" has about 870,000 works; fix-it 269,000; original characters
246,000; reader-insert 243,000; crossover 148,000; reincarnation 63,000;
everyone-lives 51,000; self-insert 31,000; time-travel fix-it 24,000;
isekai and transmigration 24,000; time loop 20,000; power swap 248. The
MCU sits in the top fandoms of nearly every premise. *Inferred:* the most
wanted fan move is "same world, one thing changed", which is our
divergence and natural-course system. Self-insert is small on AO3 because
it lives on forums and Chinese sites.

**F13 Celestial Forge is an idle loop in the text itself (shown, its first
forum post).** Reach builds over time and speeds up; themed groups of
powers drift close at random; a power is taken if reach covers it,
otherwise missed; leftover reach is kept.

**F14 Talent entries with color grades (shown, opening chapters of a
Kamar-Taj novel).** The hero sees named entries on himself and on others,
graded white, green, blue, purple, gold, with black for curses. Stolen
"luck" upgrades an entry into a new named one (quick-witted becomes
eidetic memory). Black entries cannot be removed; one pulls him into a
nightmare world every midnight. He sees "death is near" on a teacher;
saving him from his scripted death pays 50 luck and opens a talent wheel
whose draws start at 50 and double in cost. *Inferred:* readable fate
marks plus a reward for disturbing the natural course.

**F15 The world notices, and does not wait (shown).** In the Level-5
mutant novel, passive sunlight absorption buys unlock progress on a queue
of copy targets, and a telepath detects the surge and goes looking. In the
Batman novel, the lead realizes Bane will not wait in a cave while he
grinds. *Inferred:* reader-favorite books build in the guardrail we want.

## Mechanics to absorb (proposals)

- **M1 Per-world budget with drawbacks.** On entering a world, spend a
  local budget on origin, perks and items; take drawbacks for more. The
  budget size is that world's dial.
- **M2 Origins grant memories and a place.** A chosen origin gives
  native knowledge, a body and people, at a price.
- **M3 Gauntlet runs.** A world entered with nothing carried over, where
  failure does not end the chain. Fits trials and challenge runs.
- **M4 Scheduled power with a bank.** A power roll every N units of
  progress (play time, deeds, words of the life record) from a tiered pool;
  rolls you cannot afford wait in a bank. Each new power must change what
  you do, so it is not just a number.
- **M5 Delayed cheat triggers.** A cheat that wakes on an age, a
  near-death, an event or a discovery. Ties into sealed secrets: you may not
  know you have one.
- **M6 Debt-powered magic.** Power on loan, with a debt meter, a lender
  with wants, and a way to clear it or default.
- **M7 Template shelf.** Copying stores lower-tier versions of what you
  beat, limited by slots, with a fusion step. A copy route beside training.
- **M8 Fate-change currency.** Changing a canon outcome mints a reward
  token. Rewards disturbing the natural course, at the cost of losing
  foreknowledge.
- **M9 Rival inserts.** Other inserted people in the same world, with
  their own cheats and plans (F6). Owner: enemy protagonists.
- **M10 Plans with recaps.** A choice pane of plans for the next stretch
  of idle time, with a recap that marks dead ends (F9).
- **M11 Fanon in the world.** Natives hold popular beliefs about heroes
  and powers that are partly wrong; acting on them has consequences.
- **M12 Reach accumulator.** A Celestial Forge loop (F13): reach grows with
  play, offers drift past at random, take one if reach covers it, keep the
  remainder. A world sets pool, growth curve and offer rate.
- **M13 Graded trait entries.** Named traits with color grades that evolve
  into new named traits when upgraded, plus curse entries that cannot be
  upgraded (F14). Seeing other people's entries is a skill (rule 6), and a
  doom entry on someone is a natural-course warning you can act on.
- **M14 Doubling draws.** A wheel or pool whose each draw costs double the
  last, with rarer results as cost rises (F14). Fits a world adjective, not
  a fixed limit.
- **M15 Chain-breaking power checklist.** Use the Jumpchain wiki's list of
  chain-breaking powers as a review prompt, not a ban: rule 11 says
  everything can become OP. A power on the list is fine; the review asks
  where it sits (an outlier under rule 12, an end-world reward, a late
  stage, a heavy cost, or a world where it is simply normal) and what
  contrast keeps it meaningful.

## Pitfalls turned into guardrails (proposals)

| Reader complaint | Guardrail |
|---|---|
| Overpowered insert, canon warps around them | Canon people react, resist and outgrow you; rule 11 contrast keeps somewhere you are nobody |
| Power exposition stalls the story | A new power shows its use in play at once; long explanations go to the Notebook |
| Hoarding and grinding instead of acting | The world's natural course keeps moving; idling lets events pass you by |
| Retelling canon with the lead absent | Canon beats are summarized unless you are present or disturb them |
| Late drift into other franchises | Crossings need a carrier and keep tone dials per world |
| Rushed endings | Each world defines its end states and what happens if you stay |
| Altpower in name only | A replaced slot keeps its people and history; you inherit them, not just the name |

## More to consider

- **The benefactor.** Who sends you, and why? Bored, kind, cruel or
  absent. Can be a sealed secret per run.
- **Reader voting as a feature.** A shared or asynchronous vote is out of
  scope for a solo game, but "advisors" in the world could propose plans the
  way voters do.
- **Pacing per new power.** Celestial Forge shows that too many new powers
  too fast flattens the story; worlds can set roll frequency.
- **Insert density.** How many other inserts a world has is a dial, from
  none to crowded.
- **Fan premise as a world adjective.** "This world is a fix-it" (canon's
  worst event is avoidable) or "this world is a gauntlet" (nothing carries).
- **Two-lens origins.** A reborn native knows canon as a story, but the
  story may differ from this world (fanon versus canon, F10).

## A first playable slice (proposal)

Smallest proof inside the existing home world, no new world: an optional
"late-waking cheat" (M5) that triggers on a real event in the life record
(first near-defeat), sealed until it fires, then grants one roll from a
three-entry tiered pool (M4) with a bank. Exercise in a browser from a fresh
origin with save, reload and import. Needs a migration for the new optional
save field.

## Popular works pass (2026-10-10)

Weibao asked to "consider more popular works and more". Three more reader
files: [AO3 most-loved works by premise](readers-2026-10-10/ao3_top_works.md)
(browser, blurbs only), [popular Western fan and rational fiction](readers-2026-10-10/popular_western_works.md)
(about 24 works) and [popular Chinese fan novels across fandoms](readers-2026-10-10/popular_chinese_tongren.md)
(34 works). NovelUpdates stayed behind a bot check; Qidian's ranking page
returned 404, so Chinese popularity comes from book-page badges and reader
counts.

**F16 The most-loved inserts are ordinary people with knowledge (shown,
AO3 kudos order).** Top self-inserts land in minor, disliked or nobody
slots (a Westeros noblewoman, a clanless medic, a temple child) and change
society with plumbing, healthcare, reform and trade. Powerless-in-a-powered-
world (a quirkless vigilante) is among the most-loved original-character
works. *Inferred:* our life-sim direction is what fans reward most.

**F17 Doomed slots are the favorite start (shown).** Become the character
who dies (a trashy noble in a game world, a doomed villain) and avoid it.
Chinese books do the same: random role takeover, a canon cousin, an ex of
a heroine.

**F18 Rational fics treat rules as a lab (shown and stated).** Test magic
like physics, redefine a spell by its principle, reinterpret a monster to
beat it, record everything, bootstrap resources first, buy entry into an
institution with an invention, treat a god-level being as a systemic risk.
Critics punish lecturing and smug optimizers.

**F19 Institutions are progression (shown).** A self-insert founds a corps
and modernizes a city's transit; a clan gets an R&D wing; Pokémon society
already acts on its research. *Inferred:* the unit of progress is often an
organization, not a level.

**F20 Loops split what carries (shown, Time Braid).** Memory, skills and
techniques carry; physical conditioning does not; other people forget;
death or a key ally's death resets; a window of about five weeks. An MCU
rewind fic adds that foreknowledge decays as your changes pile up (shown).
Fix-it top works on AO3 add the "one catch" on carried memory.

**F21 Multiverse hubs come in three eras (shown and stated).** The
original "Main God Space" is hostile: film worlds as missions, reward
points, an exchange list, body "gene locks" gating growth, team betrayal,
frequent death. Descendants make it a store with rank caps and fixed
prices (one shows a 500-point toll per trip, a cap of 10 target worlds,
grades A to SSS behind paid upgrades). Recent ones are friendly ensembles
or invert it so the player owns the hub.

**F22 Simulators preview a life (shown, Qidian pages).** Spend a charge to
simulate a life or a decision and keep a reward or a talent: charges come
per kill, per year, per seven days, or once; some show only a result band;
one opening simulation shows the hero's death. *Inferred:* a
spend-time-to-reduce-uncertainty loop that fits heirs and loops.

**F23 Cheat economies cap by people, not prices (shown).** Sign-in once
per heroine; copy cards usable three times on the same person; teaching a
move needs full affection; a rebate multiplier fed by training and caring
for one person; a quest system that takes ten years of lifespan on
failure; "negative emotion" harvested from people as shop currency.

### Mechanics from the popular pass (proposals)

- **M16 Slot starts.** Each world lists inhabitable slots, including
  doomed and minor ones, each with people, debts and a scripted fate.
- **M17 Knowledge as a resource that goes stale.** Foreknowledge from a
  prior life or a book is a stock that loses accuracy as you diverge.
- **M18 Mind and body carry dials.** Per world or per loop: what carries
  (memory, skills, techniques, conditioning, items, relationships).
- **M19 Lab action.** Spend time testing a world rule to reveal its
  parameters, a discovery route for sealed secrets.
- **M20 Institutions that tick.** Founded organizations (school, guild,
  corps, clinic) run on their own while idle and unlock recipes and people.
- **M21 Hub store with tolls and tiers.** A traveler's hub that converts
  each world's local luck or fame into points, charges per trip and gates
  higher world grades. Hostile, store-like and owned versions all
  available as options.
- **M22 Life preview.** A simulator that previews a life or a choice and
  returns a result band and a small reward; its charge rate is a world
  adjective.
- **M23 People-capped cheats.** Gifts, copies and teaching limited per
  person and by affection, so the cast is the cap.
- **M24 Lifespan as stake.** Failed obligations cost years of life;
  pairs with heirs and aging (rule 7).

### More to consider (expanded)

- **MC1 Ordinary first.** Make the first hours of any world winnable
  through knowledge, trade and people, before powers (F16).
- **MC2 Doom you can read.** A slot's scripted death is visible to the
  right skill (a black "death is near" mark, F14) and avoidable.
- **MC3 Society that already adapted.** Natives have used their world's
  rules for centuries; inventions you bring may already exist in another
  form (the Pokémon "competent society" lesson).
- **MC4 Costs of being clever.** Lecturing and showing off can lower
  reputation with natives; rational play is rewarded, smugness is not.
- **MC5 The hub's personality.** Hostile, mercantile, friendly or owned;
  each a different multiverse mode a run can pick.
- **MC6 Paired souls.** A rival or companion reborn with you each life,
  remembering more or less than you.
- **MC7 Body from one world, life in another.** A half-alien heir in a
  low-magic world; the body's real stats meet a world that did not expect
  them (rule 4).
- **MC8 Benefactor variety.** A game interface you summoned by
  complaining, a company, Death, a bored god, nothing at all; often a
  sealed secret.
- **MC9 Long tails.** Fans keep favorite stories alive with side stories
  and recursive works; a life record that can be exported, shared and
  imported as lore by the player.
- **MC10 Fandom size as a signal, not a gate.** Harry Potter, Naruto,
  My Hero Academia, Marvel and JJK lead most premise tags; popular sources
  mean players arrive with expectations. This informs which book worlds
  to build early; it does not limit which can exist.

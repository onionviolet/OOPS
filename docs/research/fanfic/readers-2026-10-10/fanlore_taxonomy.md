# Fanlore trope taxonomy (reader notes, 2026-10-10)

Source: Fanlore (fanlore.org), the fan-culture wiki run by the Organization
for Transformative Works, via its MediaWiki API. Paraphrased; revision ids
below. Raw wikitext in `../raw/` (gitignored). Labels: SHOWN = the page
says it; INFERRED = my reading.

AO3 was not fetched: its robots.txt blocks GPTBot, ChatGPT-User and CCBot
by name (SHOWN, 2026-10-10), so automated AI access is against its stated
wishes. Fanlore's robots.txt sits behind a Cloudflare challenge; its API
answered normally and was used at one request per second.

## Premise families (SHOWN unless marked)

| Family | What Fanlore says it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Self-insertion | The author writes themselves into the source to meet canon | Not the same as reader-insert; can be humor, cameo or full lead |
| SI OC | A self-insert reborn into a native body of a world they knew as fiction; the body can be a major, minor or new character | Naruto fandom boomed after one popular fic |
| Original character (OC) | A character not in canon; follows the cast, or a whole OC cast explores the world | SI and reader-insert are often treated as OC subtypes |
| Time travel | Three kinds: physical (body and mind), mental (mind into a younger self, the "Peggy Sue" redo), and timeloops (repeat a short span to fix or escape) | Single timeline means paradox; many timelines means you change a branch |
| Fix-it | Changes canon the writer disliked, from plot holes to reviving the dead; can also "fix" another fanfic | Everybody Lives is a fix-it subgenre, strongest in closed canons |
| Canon divergence AU | Narrow split from canon at one point ("what if X lived") | Discworld's "wrong trouser of time" |
| Universe alteration | Canon world with one thing altered (born younger, an object exists) | Close to canon AU |
| Alternate universe | Anything from a tiny tweak to a new setting; radical AUs border on original fiction | Classification is contested across fandoms |
| Uber | The characters' essence placed elsewhere, often as descendants or reincarnations, with new names | From Xena fandom |
| Shared universe | One AU popular enough that others write in it | Hundreds of works in some cases |
| Crossover | Two or more fandoms meet; uses canon devices (holodeck, time travel, enchanted costumes) as bridges | "Adoption fic": a hero from fandom B raises an orphan from fandom A |
| Gamer fic | Life as a game, often copying a Korean webcomic's system (skills from books, point allocation) | Related to LitRPG; quest threads in Worm and MCU fandoms |
| Isekai | A normal person transported, reborn or trapped in a world often known to them as fiction | Old English form: portal fantasy |
| JumpChain | A chain of choose-your-own-adventure docs: one world per ten years, a point budget per world, drawbacks for extra points, keep everything between jumps; afterward go home, stay or jump again | Some writers turn chains into fic and loosen the rules for story's sake |
| Missing scene | Fills gaps inside canon | "Gapfiller" in book fandoms |
| Kidfic | Children: de-aged, next generation, or canon kids | |
| Genderswap | Sex changed by device or "always was" | Usually restored by the end, sometimes not |
| Fanon | Widely accepted fan "facts" with little canon basis | Readers often cannot tell fanon from canon |
| Canon compliant | Tries not to contradict canon; a sliding scale | |

## Criticism patterns (SHOWN)

- **Mary Sue.** An original character judged too competent, admired and
  canon-warping; the 1997 litmus test is itself criticized as a push toward
  mediocrity, and the label is subjective.
- **Character bashing.** Canon characters written worse than in canon, or
  punished for canon acts. A frequent source of fan drama.
- **Crossovers** are loved and hated; many fans say few are written well.

## Game reading (INFERRED)

- Fan premises are already a menu of run starts: insert, reborn native,
  OC, time-travel redo, loop, divergence point, alteration, crossover,
  adoption, game system, chain.
- The Mary Sue critique is a balance warning: a player who warps canon too
  easily makes canon characters and the world feel fake.
- Fanon is our rule 6 in another form: shared beliefs that are wrong.

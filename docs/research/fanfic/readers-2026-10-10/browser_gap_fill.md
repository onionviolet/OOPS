## Jumpchain wiki (SHOWN, jumpchain.fandom.com API in browser, CC BY-SA)

- How To Play (rev 393): single-player CYOA; the jumper starts as the real
  reader; a benefactor ("Jump-chan"); budget usually 1,000 points; origins
  give memories and maybe people; perks, items, companions. Stay is usually
  10 years but varies by jump (one jump is a week, another at least five
  centuries). Dying sends you home with perks, memories and companions.
  After a stay: remain, go home keeping everything, or jump on. Points do
  not carry between jumps. Items cross only via the Cosmic Warehouse or on
  your person. The goal is a "Spark" (travel without the benefactor),
  offered only by hard End Jumps. House rules are expected.
- Creating A New Jump (rev 202), settings judged unsuitable: one character
  is the only one with powers; one power so far above the rest that points
  cannot balance it (The Gamer named); powers that trivialize the chain
  (omnipotence, invulnerability or infinite revival, conceptually perfect
  skill, consequence-free time travel, taking powers from other settings,
  traveling to other worlds at will before the Spark); incomplete lore.
  Some of these suit End Jumps or Gauntlets instead.
- The Cosmic Warehouse (rev 211): storage reached through any locked door;
  the only way to move items between jumps; time stops inside when closed;
  no living people stored; visitors leave when you do; extras bought with
  its own budget (utilities, workshop, medbay, portal).
- Alternate form (rev 618): forms carry over only when a jump says so;
  switching takes about a minute, too slow for combat.
- Scenarios (rev 611): optional per-world challenges; reward a perk, item
  or points; failure just withholds the reward.
- Marvel Cinematic Universe (rev 422): links to a two-volume jump on Google
  Drive; volume one returned "file does not exist". Not read.

## Celestial Forge (SHOWN, SV thread page 1)

Author's note: powers are chosen at random, paid with points earned by word
count (every 2,000 words). In the story: the hero senses 300+ powers in
themed "constellations" (Clothing, Alchemy and more); his "reach" builds
over time, faster on average; a constellation swings close at random and
he latches onto a power if reach covers it, otherwise misses; leftover
reach after a grab is kept, not reset. INFERRED: an idle mechanic as is
(accumulator, random offers, cost check, carry-over).

## Chinese chapter snippets (SHOWN, paraphrased)

**人在卡玛泰姬，无数天赋词条** (At Kamar-Taj, countless talent entries;
69shuba, chapters 1 to 3):
- The hero sees named talent entries on himself and on others, each with a
  color grade: white, green, blue, purple, gold; black entries are curses.
- Fighting important characters steals "luck" (about 0.5 per bout); 10
  luck upgrades a white entry once, and an upgrade turns it into a new named
  entry (quick-witted becomes eidetic memory); higher grades cost more.
- A black entry cannot be upgraded or removed: every midnight he is pulled
  into a nightmare space where eerie things attack.
- He sees a teacher carrying a black "death is near" entry; changing that
  supporting character's scripted death earns 50 luck and unlocks a talent
  wheel. He wonders how much changing a protagonist's fate would pay.
- Talent wheel: first draw 50 luck, each later draw doubles in cost, rare
  odds rise; seven colored zones including gold (top) and black (curse).

**美漫从五级变种人开始** (Level-5 mutant start; ttkan, chapters 1 to 3):
- He calls out "system" and nothing answers; "panel" brings up a screen:
  mutant powers Mimicry and Energy Absorption, a current mimicry target, and
  energy points.
- Absorbing sunlight earns energy points; points are spent on unlock
  progress for the current target; one point barely moves it, and he
  wonders whether practice also counts.
- Targets come in a fixed order (finish one before the next); he suspects
  they are drawn from his own memories and impressions.
- A telepath detects a huge energy source and suspects an Omega-level
  mutant: the world notices the cheat.

**美漫：蝙蝠侠能有什么坏心思？** (What bad intent could Batman have; ttkan,
chapters 1 to 10): no system surfaced in the keyword search; the lead acts
as an employer hiring mercenaries with large payouts; in chapter 9 he
realizes Bane will not wait in a cave like a game boss while the hero
grinds gear, levels up and gathers allies.

# AO3 most-loved works by premise (reader notes, 2026-10-10)

In-app browser, by hand, tag pages sorted by kudos, one page per 5 s, with
Weibao's permission. Only blurbs (title, fandom, words, kudos, tags,
summary) were read, never story text. Paraphrased. Labels: SHOWN = on the
blurb; INFERRED = interpretation. Counts are kudos at time of reading.
NovelUpdates stayed behind a Cloudflare bot check and was skipped.

## What the top works do (SHOWN)

**Self-insert** (top: about 13k to 17k kudos each)
- A modern person inserted into a minor or disliked canon character (a
  Westeros noblewoman, Bella Swan, Sakura) or a nobody (a clanless,
  average medic in Naruto; a temple child in Star Wars).
- The hook is mundane knowledge changing society: plumbing, healthcare,
  sustainable reform, "capitalism versus the undead", a "casual
  revolution". A Cyberpunk self-insert runs 880k words with a gamer tag.

**Time-travel fix-it** (top: 27k to 50k kudos)
- Harry Potter dominates. Common forms: future people dropped into the
  past and caught by the past's heroes; the hero sent back with memories
  "with one catch"; a do-over where friends come back too; "change as
  little as possible" as a stated strategy; fixing two timelines at once
  (Jujutsu Kaisen).

**Time loop** (top: 18k to 58k kudos)
- Mostly character and relationship growth inside a repeating day.
- A class that decides to spend its loop messing with dangerous people;
  a lead who loops through deaths and reads each reset as proof of a
  hidden power; a loop triggered by touching an alien device.

**Reincarnation** (top: 19k to 52k kudos)
- Two souls bound to be reborn together, life after life.
- A hero reborn into another franchise carrying his old skills (Naruto's
  Kakashi as a My Hero Academia student); one soul living through a
  string of myth roles; mythic identities recognized by others.

**Isekai and transmigration** (top: 18k to 48k kudos)
- Pulled into a game you raged at, by an interface triggered by your own
  complaint words.
- Becoming a doomed "trash" character and dodging his death.
- A system that assigns a mission about the canon protagonist
  ("rehabilitate the protagonist"); reverse transmigration (canon
  character into the modern world); a famous hero's second life spent
  bullying the yakuza.

**Worm alt-power** (top: 4k to 7k kudos)
- Powers imported from other franchises (Tokyo Ghoul, Bloodborne, League
  of Legends, a Warhammer psyker too weak to awaken until the trigger).
- Transhuman self-escalation; a powerful child adopted by a hero.

**Invincible** (top: 2k to 6k kudos)
- Mostly romance and family. Mechanically notable: a half-Viltrumite
  original character isekai'd into a "failing empire"; a half-Viltrumite
  bastard born into Westeros (a body from one setting in another world).

**Celestial Forge** (top: 0.9k to 3k kudos)
- Variants with other pools ("Grimoire", "Holocron", "Roulette"); a
  corporate benefactor ("an agent of the company") placing someone in a
  lost primarch's body; mixed with The Gamer's ability and with another
  CYOA catalog.

**Crossover** (top: 22k to 32k kudos)
- An immortal "Master of Death" displaced between worlds who cannot follow
  loved ones into the afterlife; a medium's career in a new world; Earth's
  social media reacting to a returning astronaut.

**Original character(s)** (top: 34k to 49k kudos)
- Mostly romance. Notable: the powerless boy in a world where most people
  have powers, turning vigilante (My Hero Academia, several top works).

## Patterns (INFERRED)

1. The most-loved inserts are ordinary people with knowledge, not
   powers: they change institutions, economies and households.
2. Doomed slots are the favorite start: become the character who dies,
   then avoid it.
3. Memory carried back always comes "with a catch".
4. Loops are about people as much as mechanics.
5. Powerless in a powered world is a huge draw.
6. Bodies cross settings (half-Viltrumite in Westeros), which is our rule 4.
7. Benefactors vary: a game interface, a company, Death itself, nothing.

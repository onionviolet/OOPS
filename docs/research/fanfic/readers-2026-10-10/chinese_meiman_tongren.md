## (c) Cheat / system economies

- Currency, SHOWN or STATED: 贡献值 and experience from beating canon enemies (Shurima book, SHOWN); lottery draws earned by "changing fate" (烈风天翼, SHOWN); quest rewards (Kryptonian overlord; Shurima rebuilding quests, SHOWN); reward claims tied to visiting film locations (sign-in book, SHOWN).
- What is bought or drawn: abilities, items and whole characters from outside franchises (Gwen draw, Storm Wing, Homelander card, SHOWN); templates copied from defeated enemies (Invincible book, SHOWN); tech knowledge (Great Clock, Zola's assistant system, SHOWN/STATED); summonable allies (Servant, Naruto ninja, STATED).
- Reveal/upgrade pattern: delayed activation is common: the sign-in system unlocks at 18 (SHOWN); Hydra-fodder start where the system fires at near-death (STATED). Teaser-style cards ("gold card") imply rarity tiers (INFERRED).
- Limits and costs: Kamar-Taj "borrowed" magic makes debt the central cost and the cheat is a loophole (SHOWN). Corpse use as a speed-up for copying is a stated shortcut (SHOWN). Hard cooldown or point-price numbers: UNKNOWN; I did not read chapters.
- Imports from other fandoms (League of Legends, One Piece, Warhammer 40K, DNF, Fate, Naruto, Pokémon-style cards) are the dominant way to scale the cheat past canon power (STATED by multiple list reviewers).

## (d) Canon-event and protagonist handling

- Canon timeline: mostly MCU film order. Examples: the sign-in book follows film locations (SHOWN); one QQ reader says 80-90 chapters in, a book was still on Iron Man 2 (STATED, QQ comments). Comics-faithful books exist ("原著党福音" style, STATED by Sohu reviewer for 霸王轨迹).
- Insertion modes: replacement of a character (Gwen, Ned, Daisy, Batman-as-Spider-Man's-city), tolerated bystander (Ned, Kamar-Taj apprentice), enemy-side embedded (Hydra fodder, Zola's assistant), relative of a hero (Invincible cousin), or outsider with a gated door (Fate/Chaldea, Warhammer simulator).
- Butterfly effect: Dragon from Kamar-Taj sets up a world already "crowded with transmigrators" after Red Skull's failed experiment (SHOWN). Mixed MCU+comics+TV continuities are routinely merged; the Sohu reviewer notes plot holes from merging worlds and from characters inserted from earlier books (STATED).
- Standard beats (INFERRED from synopses): New York battle, Ultron, Infinity War as resource farms (Shurima book mentions all four, SHOWN). Thanos is typically the endgame boss for MCU-anchored books; DC-anchored books (Batman, 二线英雄) use time-line attacks and Knightfall as pivots (SHOWN).
- Protagonist relationship to heroes: either hidden and manipulative ("幕后流", e.g. 穿越美漫，但密教主, SHOWN tags) or a slice-of-life peer. A QQ reviewer complains about the protagonist being absent, and Sohu complains about forced ties with every hero (STATED).

## (e) Power scaling and progression pacing

- Early: street-level to mid-tier (Hell's Kitchen, mutants, Hydra). Mid: parity with Stark/Strange tier via tech or magic. Late: Infinity Stones and cosmic entities. Cross-franchise imports (Warhammer, LoL, One Piece) carry the late game (STATED).
- Pacing complaints: slow first two-thirds (美漫的超凡之旅, STATED), disjointed late acts and abrupt tone shifts, rushed endings (seen repeatedly in the 163/Sohu lists, STATED). Series length is very long: 432万 characters and still in Knightfall (SHOWN), 8.57M characters for a mind-mentor book (STATED, 163 serials list).
- Endings: finishing books often close at Thanos or at "multiverse guardian" (Crazy Multiverse, STATED). Many end by dissolving into other IPs. Exact finale content: UNKNOWN for most.

## (f) Praise and complaints (all STATED, named source)

Praise: strong ideas and structural remixing of Marvel/DC events (Sohu); fast, light, joke-heavy tone (163 lists); smart, scheming protagonists (Sohu on Zola's assistant); fidelity to comics (Sohu on 霸王轨迹); good heist-of-ideas worldbuilding like the Warhammer simulator (163).
Complaints: protagonist underused or retelling films (QQ reader comments); filler and slow updates (QQ); anime or other-IP drift in later chapters (163 on diary book; Sohu); logic gaps and characters getting dumber (Sohu on mechanic book); rushed endings (163 many); high entry barrier from outside-IP knowledge (163 on Servant book); PTT commenters say Marvel fanfic is a long-lived Chinese web-novel subgenre but a "join the Avengers/SHIELD" trope is debated (PTT, thread locked as off-topic).
Popularity (SHOWN, Qidian): top of sample is 美漫从五级变种人开始 (~57.5万 readers, 1万 月票), 黛西在漫威世界 (~18.9万 readers, 5万 bookmarks), 蝙蝠侠能有什么坏心思 (39.6万 readers, >10万 bookmarks), 我在美漫当二线英雄 (13.8万 readers). No NovelUpdates or Douban counts obtained.

## (g) One game option per premise type (idle RPG)

- Kamar-Taj / mystic: "Borrowed Power" ledger. Spells are loans from a demon bank; idle income grows while a debt meter rises, and purifying a patron converts debt to permanent power.
- Sign-in: daily location pilgrimage. Offline time banks check-ins at world landmarks; visiting a canon landmark in a world gives a fixed reward and a unique stamp.
- Lottery/gacha: fate-pull currency earned by diverting canon events. Each canon beat you "change" mints a pull from a cross-world pool.
- Copy/plunder: template shelf. Beating an enemy stores a lower-tier template; slots limit the shelf and a fusion step raises tier.
- Hydra/SHIELD start: faction defection choice at a fixed early event, switching which resources and enemies feed the idle loop.
- Villain-side: chaos-meter prestige. Raising a city's crime level (Gotham) is the income source and a prestige reset restores order for a multiplier.
- Cross-franchise import: world-pack unlocks. A pack from another world adds a unit tree to the home world, with an explicit "tone drift" penalty on balance to avoid the late-game collapse readers complain about.
- Tech-learning: blueprint analysis. Studying a captured device unlocks recipes at timed intervals.
- Replacement-of-character: pick a canon slot to inhabit; each slot gives a fixed starting set of relationships and a fixed chain of canon beats.

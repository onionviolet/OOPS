# Popular Western fan fiction and web fiction: mechanics survey

Reader module, 2026-10-10. Scope: mechanics and structures a game could absorb. Breadth first; many entries are thin because the search tool returned few primary pages. Nothing here is story prose. Quotes are absent or under 15 words.

Label key: SHOWN = I saw it in a fetched page. STATED = someone says it (named). INFERRED = my reading. UNKNOWN = not found. Fetch results were summarized by the fetch tool, so "SHOWN" means shown in that summary, not that I read the raw page.

## (c) Recurring mechanic patterns, clustered

C1. Magic or powers as an experimentable system. HPMOR (experiments, partial Transfiguration), Luminosity (journaling, strategizing with powers), With This Ring (ring as a design problem), Marked for Death (chakra reserve budgeting). SHOWN/STATED above.

C2. Institution building as progression. With This Ring founds a Corps and modernizes London transit with gates (SHOWN). Dreaming of Sunshine has a clan R&D division (SHOWN). Pokemon OoS shows Gym/Elite Four/professor networks (SHOWN). Marked for Death's protagonist buys village citizenship with an invention (STATED). INFERRED: the unit of progress in these works is organizations, not only a character.

C3. Industrializing a ninja or magic technique. A seal that "transformed warfare" (Marked for Death, STATED); sealing arc (DoS, SHOWN). I could not verify clone-based industrialization in any specific work, so treat that as UNKNOWN.

C4. Rules-as-written crossover. Natural 20 uses D&D 3.0 XP and minor house rules inside Harry Potter (STATED). The Guild of Gamers uses a points shop (SHOWN). Worth the Candle uses a visible stat interface (STATED).

C5. Knowledge-asymmetry fix-its and loops. Time Braid (SHOWN), IYHTTA (SHOWN), Purple Days (STATED), Infinite Loops (STATED).

C6. Inherited or transferable power chain. Inheritance: power passes by killing the holder (SHOWN). Relevant to heirs.

C7. Villain-POV or consequence-extrapolation deconstruction. Metropolitan Man (SHOWN), Friendship is Optimal (STATED), Luminosity's "events follow from earlier choices" (SHOWN).

C8. Long-tail community structures. Omakes (Taylor Varga 269, Inheritance hundreds), recursive fics (DoS 800+ on AO3), quests (Marked for Death), shared universes (Infinite Loops). SHOWN/STATED.

C9. Ecology and competent society. Pokemon OoS: legendaries as disasters; society that has already acted on research (SHOWN).

## (d) Rational and munchkin techniques catalogue

Only techniques the sources actually show or state are listed; each is tied to a work.

T1. Hypothesis-test magic like physics. HPMOR: Harry tests with Draco and Hermione (SHOWN, Wikipedia).
T2. Redefine a spell by its physical principle. HPMOR partial Transfiguration, "timeless physics" (SHOWN).
T3. Reinterpret a monster's nature to beat it. HPMOR "True Patronus" vs Dementors as shadows of death (SHOWN).
T4. Record everything. Luminosity's Bella notebooks (SHOWN).
T5. Spend foreknowledge early, accept decay. IYHTTA: knowledge drives change and makes itself less reliable (SHOWN).
T6. Use the loop as a lab. Time Braid: Sakura uses loops to train and probe resets; Naruto volunteers as test subject for her memory work (SHOWN). Reset-trigger inference: she guesses that unexpected derailments trigger resets and that it waits until she sleeps (SHOWN via search excerpt).
T7. Resource-first bootstrapping. With This Ring: first goal is a battery, then asteroid mining, scanning for Lanterns, bargaining with Alan Scott (SHOWN, review).
T8. Counter a power's side effect with a constraint. With This Ring: a philosophy to control the orange light's mind effect (STATED, TVTropes trivia via search).
T9. Buy entry into an institution with an invention. Marked for Death (STATED).
T10. Rules-lawyer a published system. Natural 20 metagaming and munchkinry (STATED).
T11. Treat a hostile entity as a systemic risk and test it. Metropolitan Man: Lex studies Superman's motives and origin (SHOWN).
T12. Read the rules of the objective function. Friendship is Optimal: AI maximizes a stated target, with soft-horror results (STATED).
Exploit techniques like clone farming or fixed-seed abuse: UNKNOWN in the works I could verify.

## (f) Praise and criticism patterns

Praise:
- Competent others and living institutions (Pokemon OoS review).
- Characters whose plans follow from earlier choices (Luminosity reviewers).
- Foreknowledge that gets used with cost (IYHTTA; Time Braid commenters praise tension and hard-won wins).
- Consistent updating and volume (With This Ring reviewer).
- Explaining canon oddities (DoS readers).

Criticism:
- Lecturing, mouthpiece characters, "rules" that dull wonder (HPMOR critics per Fanlore/Wikipedia).
- Slow pace, exposition, philosophy chunks (Pokemon OoS, Taylor Varga, DoS).
- Loop drag with no plot tension (a Goodreads reviewer on Time Braid, STATED via search; the SB author also criticized the rushed dark turn and skipped training).
- Smug optimizer protagonist, showing off cleverness (With This Ring reviewer).
- Low stakes, "protagonist always right" (Inheritance reviewer).
- Controversial ending credited to luck (Metropolitan Man).
- Late loss of rigor / abrupt characterization shift (one Luminosity reader).
- Closeness to canon limits divergence (DoS readers).

## (g) One game option per pattern (idle RPG)

- C1 Experimentable magic: a "Lab" tab where spending idle time on a rule reveals its hidden parameters, one world at a time. Each world sets its own revealable rules.
- C2 Institutions as progression: purchased organizations (corps, R&D wing, school) that run their own offline tick and produce stat or recipe unlocks.
- C3 Technique industrialization: any learned technique can be "formalized" into a guild process, converting one-off skill into passive throughput, with a per-world adjective on how far it scales (no fixed "once").
- C4 Rules-as-written crossover: world-specific stat sheets that import one foreign ruleset, with a visible conversion table so the player can munchkin it.
- C5 Foreknowledge asset: a "Foreknowledge" resource that grows from prior lives, decays as the player's changes diverge from the remembered timeline (IYHTTA pattern).
- C6 Inherited power chain: heirs inherit the previous holder's power along with a voice or constraint, with a per-world rule for how it transfers.
- C7 Consequence extrapolation: a world where a cheat has second-order effects the sim computes, shown in a log.
- C8 Community-style long tail: exportable "omake" snapshots a player can save and re-import as flavor lore.
- C9 Ecology: events as natural disasters on a weather-like schedule that shift the economy.
- Loops: split carry-over into "mind" (knowledge, skills) vs "body" (conditioning, gear), per world.

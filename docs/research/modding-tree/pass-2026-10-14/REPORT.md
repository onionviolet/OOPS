The best pattern is a small common authoring contract plus deliberate management of dependencies, reset effects and visible work. None of these sources proves that dozens of layers balance themselves. Rewritten manually tunes limits and publishes help; Communitree groups mechanics into five creator modules and turns finished sections into passive rewards; Profectus makes layer state and feature requirements explicit.

## What keeps many systems legible and balanced

**F2/F7:** count authoring units separately from gameplay systems. Rewritten has 31 declarations in layers.js, including utility layers. Communitree has five creator layers plus the tree utility, but its Jacorb module renders many lower prestige mechanics as buyables. Nested tabs and progressive disclosure reduce visible overload. This does not show that every important routine remains visible on a laptop.

**F3/F10:** tune connections and explain diminishing returns. Rewritten's named softcap registry supplies both transforms and descriptions. Communitree uses local caps and grants relief later. These are hand-authored feedback controls. YAIRPG can explain world-specific limits and keep powerful exceptions reachable without adopting blanket anti-abuse restrictions.

**F8/F9/F11:** separate position, reset order and scheduling. Communitree draws several row-4 systems at different display rows. Its hub checks child entry and completion, then saves a timer reward after clearing the completed section. Inactive guards reduce work, but the temp traversal still visits functions. Hiding a layer is not proof it has stopped running or that state is safe to discard.

**F13/F16/F17:** many authors need stable interfaces. Profectus exposes requirements, returned persistent fields, layer IDs and update phases. Its contribution guide asks for maintainer review and tests. These are useful conventions, not an automatic guarantee that content made by different authors composes.

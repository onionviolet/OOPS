## Initial filled records, selection and proof limits

**F3 · Filled data has two distinct routes.** `fill/1.3` is the scenes pass's authored proposal kind above. Separately, `work/atlas-fill/<world>/making_fill*.jsonl` overlays missing rule inputs/products and an optional outcome. The digest retains per-reference fill confidence, marks the working filled, and the view labels filled materials. Overlay quantity/unit are null, not source numbers. The overlay's original derivation/basis is not preserved by this projection. This route is separate from the field-consensus products of `scripts/field_fill.mjs`, which write `docs/coverage/fields/<slug>.jsonl`; those products are not imported by `readFindings`. Neither route constitutes fresh book reading or playable implementation.

**F4 · File and field coverage are different.** `chosenFile` selects migrated `m_census`/`m_scenes`, then plain or Luna extra-pass copies, avoiding a pass with its `.partial` marker. It does not simply ingest every result/retry. `readFindings` also discovers qualifying old lead slice families and deduplicates scene beats and entity/rule ids. Fills use a separate filename preference (`m_fills`, plain fills, Luna fills), without the same partial-marker guard. These choices need actual-host inventory before reporting total collected coverage. Broken JSON lines are skipped, so row loss is not itself a proof of absent collection.

Notes are capped at 3,000 in digest, fills at 2,000, with totals retained. Their list shows at most 200 entries per group and reports the remaining count. Summaries have per-field text limits; several collections cap aliases/access/respects/about. Those are intentional bounded projections, not complete preservation. Notes deduplicate by slugged wording, which can erase distinct provenance; this is a review issue if the same fact is stated by different sources or about different subjects.

**F5 · Existing proof must retain its scope.** `tests/atlas_reader_findings.test.js` constructs synthetic rows and checks folds, joins and sealed exclusion. `prototypes/world-atlas/prove-records.mjs` explicitly writes synthetic batch fixtures, including fixture places/people/groups/scenes and a withheld record. It can prove the path works under its inputs. It does not establish actual reader-corpus coverage, real populated-field counts or canonical source correctness. The broader `prove-all.mjs` expects populated world records; its successful historical receipt is not a fresh run here. No browser or synthetic test was rerun for this docs-only audit.

There is a separate value-shape check to investigate: `normalize()` turns outcome string `true`/`false` into booleans, while sceneSummary uses string-only `stated` for outcome values. A valid collected boolean may consequently display only its fact key. This is a concrete contract mismatch inferred from both functions, not a reproduced corpus defect.

## Domain rules with consumers

All rows below are **P1 subproposals**, not selected mechanics or a new universal engine. Reuse existing typed product/knowledge/publication contracts where they fit.

| Module | Meaningful input/process | Actual proposed consumer | What does not follow automatically |
|---|---|---|---|
| Composition | Conserved fractions, preparation and delivery route | A supported dose or coating changes a specified body/object state | Every named trait becomes a buff; all medicines work orally |
| Assembly | Parts, interfaces, material contribution and condition | Tool use, durability or equipment action reads the completed structure | Averaging all ingredient tags into one quality score |
| Pattern | Carrier, valid structure, channels and energy | A supported inscription executes with an actual power source | Owning a diagram grants fuel or skill |
| Exact identity | Specific source/target/provenance required by a local law | Ritual/formula admission or binding reads that identity | A same-property substitute satisfies identity |
| Production | Saved procedure, actual means/access and stock allocation | Finite output, useful work or an accepted order | Knowing a method supplies labor, station access or offline progress |

An ingredient may participate in several modules. Preserve one owned lot and debit the chosen use once. Show **known uses** and **available stock after reservations**. A food, remedy, dye or bait use needs its own consumer and may retain different properties after preparation. Fishing is only this cross-use boundary here; its capture/activity rules belong to the companion research.

Recipe matching should distinguish exact slots, functional slots and mixtures of both. A substitution changes only the role it actually supplies. A nominally suitable substitute may also carry another relevant property. Conversely, a valuable extra property should not create a made-up drawback to preserve chores. Discovery can find a new procedure or cross-use without adding an entirely new effect primitive.

Skills and powers compose through **causal stages**: perception reveals permitted measurements; herbal knowledge selects a preparation; manipulation executes it; heat control supplies an environment; a construct or apprentice repeats instructions within its actual faculties. Show which stage each means changes. Do not add a global combo multiplier or require a bespoke entry for every pair. Exceptional local combinations remain possible. Knowledge of a process, ability to execute it and a delegate's competence are separate.

Repeatable principles do not mean perfect forecasts. Preserve exact products and observed results; report predictions at the observer's supported confidence. Unexpected results can arise from unfamiliar properties, an untested operation order, changed conditions, combinations or world-supported execution variation. Do not reroll an established batch merely because a screen reopened. A notebook can record a correlation without pretending it isolated the cause.

## Match the view to the decision

| View | Decision it serves | Minimum useful information | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ingredient list + adjacent detail | Which lot, and what am I giving up? | Owned/free/held amount, known uses, provenance, relevant measurements, exact/profile role | Unidentified effects leaking through sorting, icons or price |
| Compact workbench | What will I try now? | Chosen quantities, applicable operations, known costs, uncertainty, one explicit execute control | Many sliders with no rule reader; forcing self-consumption as universal identification |
| Ordered process strip | Which step changes the result? | Preparation → transformation → finishing; meaningful before/after state | Requiring every craft to be a real-time minigame |
| Small field/layout | Where should apparatus/material go? | Actual adjacency, channels, heat, reach or access if that world reads it | Decorative grids for ordinary list choices |
| Experiment notebook | What did I learn, and what next? | Formula version, actual batch, changed variables, observations, scope/confidence | Treating one result as complete identification; revealing all hidden possibilities |
| Batch/policy view | What should continue while I do something else? | Method, assigned means, quantities, reserves, substitutions, stop reason and output destination | Silent substitution or silently discarding lower-grade outputs |

Use one selected context shared across compact and expanded views. Inspecting restores selection/scroll and spends no time; execute, begin work and cancel remain separate commands. At 1366×768, prioritize the next decision over simultaneous display of every notebook row. A diagram can expand in the center while the log/status/work remain intact. Neither the foreign screenshots nor this table prove YAIR fit at that viewport.

## Ranked proposals and bounded original journey

| Rank/code | Disposition | Proposal | Concrete benefit and cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 / P1 | Adapt for a later selected slice | Extend existing mixing through one causal process distinction and a useful output consumer | Depth through an actual choice; requires a selected law and consumer, not a general chemistry rewrite |

| 5 / P5 | Park as optional domain representation | Layout grid or Potion Craft-like map only when it expresses that domain's real decisions | Adds spatial readability where useful; costs space and can misstate non-spatial laws |

**J1, proposed original journey, not an implementation dispatch:**

1. At a Village apothecary, inspect an owned fictional herb lot. One use is already known; another is a hypothesis. See free versus reserved stock and choose whether to spend a small sample.
2. Compare two preparations differing in one operation. Known costs and uncertainties are shown. A skill improves diagnosis or execution; a suitable power can supply the relevant heat/control means. No compulsory timing game or arbitrary extra power cost is imposed.
3. Finish an owned batch and test it through a selected appropriate route. A supported native state changes. The observation records the scope of what was learned, including any unaccounted difference, without identifying every hidden constituent.
4. Save the method with its inputs, operation order, known result, conditions and means. Review a second use of the same original stock through an independent consumer. The smallest first slice may leave that second use as a boundary demonstration rather than build another whole pursuit.
5. Personally repeat or choose an available means to run a finite batch. Declare allowable substitutions and reserves; see output and a linked stop reason. Returning to hands-on work preserves the notebook and method. Save/reload/import must preserve stock, method, actual product and observations.

No new claim is made that every step is absent today. Step5's finite repeat and part of steps3–4 already have source support in the incumbent. The proposed additions are causal operation depth, better selection/comparison and explicit means/policy, subject to selection.

# Reverend Insanity Moonblade damage and recovery

**2026-11-12 · Source-checked research and design proposal.** The Rank One Moonlight Gu can break a trained mortal’s forearm bones when powered by condensed middle-stage essence, even though its user is still at initial stage. The book gives a trained mortal’s expectation for ordinary initial-stage essence and a stated Jade Skin defense. The best direct reinforcement comparison uses an upgraded blade or a different attack. These observations support a body-specific damage model and leave the numerical HP scale open.

## What the novel establishes

“Same Moonlight” below means the original Gu and its ordinary crescent. Fuel grade, angle and the body it hits are explicit variables. Little Light support and fused descendants are identified separately because the word *moonblade* covers several attacks. “Shown” means a depicted event, “stated” means a narrator’s or character’s claim, and “inferred” means the nearby context supports the reading.

### Quantities and care that change decisions

- **Essence volume and quality.** Ch22 P69–P72 and ch23 P70–P71 give the beginner’s 10 percentage points of aperture capacity per shot, with three throws from a 38% reserve in the example. Ch80 P38–P50 explicitly gives 10 initial-stage points, 5 middle, 2.5 upper and 1.25 peak for this Gu across Rank One grades, with the last two derived from successive halving. Ch29 P14–P20 distinguishes the 10 points of essence that shrink to 5 in the mist from the 20 initial-stage points spent in the demonstrated process. The novel also gives stronger blade effects with condensed fuel in ch35 P11–P13 and ch66 P86–P88. Fit fuel expenditure and cutting performance separately. The concentration ratio gives a spend relation and leaves the damage ratio undetermined. Ch34 P12’s “at least twice” refers to cultivation efficiency.
- **Effective reach, visible flight and support.** Use ten metres as the ordinary effective reach in this setting (ch24 P28–P33, ch66 P81–P88). The elder hits a grass neck at ten metres and the dimming blade travels about six metres farther. A student’s high miss stays visible for nearly fifteen metres (ch22 P23–P27, ch25 P24–P25). These are visible travel observations, with no measured tissue effect past ten metres. Nearer shots leave less reaction time, but a trained defender reads wrist and shoulder cues (ch83 P7–P19, ch84 P23–P42). Ch70 P40–P52 states Little Light doubles size and range and depicts a close neck cut on an already bleeding boar. Ch106 P59–P66 independently states that one Little Light doubles power and a second adds no further boost. It also gives distinct fused profiles, including ten-metre Moonglow with triple stated power and twenty-metre Moon Scar with unchanged stated power. Keep these figures attached to their own techniques. The boar scene anchors the supported cut, and the extension of effective range lacks a measured hit in that scene.
- **Tissue, location and delayed loss.** Ordinary unassisted Moonlight with upper-stage fuel draws blood on a wild boar without breaking its bone. Repeated charges reopen wounds, and it slows before the Little Light finish. Ch70 P22–P52 and P70–P71 give the encounter and aggregate spend of 20 upper-stage aperture points, with 23 left. The prose leaves the exact ordinary volley count and cost breakdown open. Mo Bei’s hunt also shows shallow cuts and collapse from blood loss after about half an hour (ch73 P26–P34). A trapped boar dies after a neck cut without depicted decapitation (ch65 P64–P70). These are different routes to incapacitation with the same attack family.
- **Care and defeat.** In ch82 P59–P64 a student retires after blood loss and receives immediate treatment. Ch83 P24–P32 shows a referee stopping a still-conscious, bleeding fighter and separate resources for healing and restoring essence. Ch86 P1–P24 distinguishes unconsciousness from critical harm and shows a healer mending exposed cracked bone and regrowing flesh in a blunt-injury analogue. Ch300 P48–P53 shows a later remedy arresting blood loss and regrowing flesh faster than tendons. The forearm-loss scene gives no recovery follow-up, and the care scenes leave a normal mortal’s stabilization time and limb restoration open.

## Proposed OOPS representation

Keep the overall HP pool per [standing health direction](../USER_VISION.md#injuries-overall-hp-plus-part-damage-where-it-matters-2026-10-30). Treat HP as remaining systemic capacity, with body harm and active sources of decline determining how it changes. A player can see a blade coming and choose to evade, cover a vital part, spend essence on a guard or withdraw. After contact they need to know whether they can still fight and whether delaying care costs them more.

1. **Build the attack from its actual means.** The Gu, committed fuel grade and paid support set cutting performance, span and reach. Control and angle determine trajectory and contact. Keep evasion, intercepting limbs and actual guard coverage in the same contact decision. A trained mortal covering the face puts forearm tissue in the blade’s path. A glow formed in time presents a different layer. A shot that goes wide can still cost essence.
2. **Resolve the layers struck in order.** A barrier can absorb a cut at the cost of essence, weaken, break or stop the cut completely. Contact may still impart force where the layer transmits it, as with White Jade and the monkey’s headbutt in ch114 P28–P39. Resolve any residual cut against the local material and depth it reaches. A reinforced body changes those local properties or the aftermath. A shell stays a separate layer. Derive a few thresholds from skin, bone and vulnerable structures before considering a continuous formula. Rank enters via the fuel, defense, body or faculties the character actually has.
3. **Settle the immediate injury and ongoing loss from one event.** The overall pool pays for immediate systemic harm and later decline such as blood loss. The same settled contact can leave a local injury with its site, severity, cause and active bleeding. Record local detail where a reader uses it. An injured arm can lower control and force before severe damage takes away a particular grip. A dangerous wound can end the fight through pain, yielding, loss of consciousness or direct vital disruption while the systemic pool still conveys useful information. A lethal neck disruption sets the fatal state directly under the world’s outcome rules. Blood lost over time comes from the wound’s ongoing effect and gets charged once.
4. **Let care change the thing it treats.** Stabilization stops or reduces ongoing loss. Rest or suitable treatment restores systemic capacity and repairs local structures at their own pace. A limb that cannot support a grip can stay impaired after stabilization. Essence replenishment restores the ability to cast or defend, while recovery follows the remedy and the body. The next action reads the settled HP and injuries through the same paths whether the fight ended by retreat, arena stop or incapacity.

**Same decision across three models.** Start within effective range with a readied Moonblade and a defender able to interpose a forearm or raise a compatible funded guard. The existing scalar strike model can tune a mitigation number for the guard, but the ordinary hit branch grades injury from mitigated damage, max HP and critical status. A high-HP defender can therefore appear locally tougher without a changed arm. An anatomy-first model with individual structure meters would let the player follow each repair at the cost of tracking parts that have no combat or care decision. The proposed overall pool with sparse injuries resolves the guard or arm first. If the arm takes a deep wound, the immediate choice is whether to keep fighting with impaired control or retreat before blood loss worsens. If a guard stops it, the defender decides how much essence to reserve for the next shot. The same contact rules support both outcomes.

**Fit with the current seams.** The [area guide](../AREA_GUIDE.md#fights-and-harm) routes native fights through the saved tempo fight and conflict frame. [Tempo strikes](../../src/tempo_fight.js) already choose a hit location, derive part injury and apply pool damage. [Combat math](../../src/combat_math.js) also serves scalar callers and has a chip floor on mitigated hits, alongside a full shield block path. For Moonlight, extend the contact-to-outcome path so a true block settles before scalar HP loss, and publish typed injuries and outcomes through the existing [harm](../../src/harm_engine.js) and [defeat](../../src/defeat_domain.js) seams. Keep a single owner for each paid resource and injury. The current tempo host commits the fight plan before applying player harm effects. A later proof should settle the spend, contact, HP and wound once across that boundary, including interruption and resume. Existing [lead scenes](../../src/world_packs/book_gu_lead_early_duel.js) provide narrative and resource-consumption fixtures. Their authored per-step spends and scene outcomes supply no independent damage scale. A later save change would need to preserve existing HP, part harm and in-flight fights, with any unknown injury detail left unknown. AD6–AD8, AD19, AD38, AD49, CBR2, CBR5–6, CBR11, CBR22–24 and BHR11–15 govern the fit.

## Calibration matrix before HP numbers

Use a fixed straight in-range shot, comparable placement and contact angle for controlled comparisons. Replay the actual scenes at their observed conditions too. The controlled variants are authored tests of the proposal. Fit to the first three rows, then use the last two to check how well the rules generalize without new character-specific damage values.

|---|---|---|

Use the outcome bands to choose a local HP and wound-rate scale to reproduce observed ordering and feasible time to act. Two discriminating failure checks are a funded block that still inflicts minimum HP damage, and a deep wound whose bleeding disappears when the defender withdraws. The smallest useful future proof runs one paid shot through guard or arm contact, then carries the resulting injury through withdrawal and care. If that comparison needs separate rules for each named target, revise the contact representation before tuning damage values.

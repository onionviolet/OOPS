## P2. Perishable supply and daily Gu care

**Known outcome.** In the held English RI edition, ch. 23 p27–33, p40–41 and p51–55 cover the ordinary five-day freshness and four-petal daily demand. Ch. 23's two meals and the two petals per baseline meal give the daily total. The native opening turns this into one daily care deadline and one 16-petal opening lot with a common expiry. Those batch, scheduling and care-loss choices are authored. Moving petals to prepared stock preserves the expiry in that route. The Village has a separate working example: its stilled hearth ages cooked food at local time, oldest portions leave first, and spoiled food returns as an actual item. [Held-source comparison C2](SAGA_OF_GU_SCOUT_2026-10-24.md#five-entry-comparison-follow-up-on-2026-10-24) · [Gu opening and daily event](../../src/world_packs/book_gu_life.js) · [Spoilage area guide](../AREA_GUIDE.md#spoilage-perishable-goods)

The first-slice dispatch rule, batch identity and holder rates are original, with ordinary ambient time as the default for Gu petals. A world may tune the rate if it supplies an actual preservation mechanism. Transit predictions use travel and real queue time. The source does not supply a refrigerated cart or a rate for one.

## P3. Armor that wears where the blow lands

**Known outcome.** The existing Village workshop test gives unlined metal armor about twice its warm-air wear at 0°C. A wool lining reduces the added cold wear, and the same armor has baseline wear in warm air. Those are original YAIR rules and checked software outcomes. For an independent rules example, *Cyberpunk* first edition (1988), *Friday Night Firefight* PDF pp. 13–14, 19 and 21 assigns hits to locations, uses armor stopping power in damage resolution and drops SP after penetration. Those are stated tabletop procedures in the existing checked synthesis. They support tracking what a hit met and whether it penetrated. Tingen and the Village would each choose their own mitigation and condition law. [Workshop test](../../tests/gear_workshop.test.js) · [1988 source synthesis, chain 2](CYBERPUNK_1988_L2_GEAR_INJURY_RESPONSE_2026-11-10.md#chain-2-injury-to-recovery)

**Rule to derive.** Resolve the incoming hit against its actual contact and the item's protection. Route residual harm to the body and wear to the gear that took the load. For a Village demonstration, use landed contact as the source of baseline wear and retain the existing temperature/lining factor on metal armor. How strongly a blocked or penetrating hit wears the contact item is a world rule. The proposed contact filtering and interaction between contact and cold are original. Existing aggregate defense and wear callers require an explicit decision on the first route's hit attribution before this law can produce a fair result.

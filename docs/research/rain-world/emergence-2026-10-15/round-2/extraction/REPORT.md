## RW2-F1: Ghosts model competing possible paths, with practical caps and exceptions

Full raw consumer: `Tracker.cs:204-648`. All subsequent locators are one-based under the round-2/extract raw directory.

An ElaborateCreatureRepresentation starts with one Ghost at the target's actual main-body position and velocity. MaxGhosts is the configured maximum scaled by finding confidence to the quarter power, then clamped between2 and that maximum. With renewed visual contact, extra ghosts are removed and the first is reset to the target's exact coordinate and averaged body-chunk velocity. Without visual contact, every ghost updates; a marked ghost is only removed while more than one exists. Thus a last hypothesis can survive its deletion flag.

On losing sight, the representation branches into moving/stationary alternatives. It uses actual target movement and accessibility to decide which gets the original branch, and gives the alternative a generation penalty of500. Ghost generation is a search-cost/confidence input, not time or genealogical reproduction. FindBestGhost first prefers hypotheses reachable and returnable by the observer, then minimizes generation*20 plus distance from observer and last seen coordinate. A solid-tile branch subtracts1000 from this score; preserve that literal sign rather than rewriting it as an intuitive penalty. LowestGenerationAvailable is recomputed alongside selection.

Ghost.Update returns while stopped, room unrealized, or AI map unready. In inaccessible terrain it uses a simple velocity/gravity extrapolation and neighbor legality checks. In accessible terrain it accumulates target offScreenSpeed * ghostSpeed until its buffer exceeds1, then resets the buffer to0, not remainder-preserving catch-up. It ranks legal outgoing movement directions against previous velocity. Observer connection resistance and observer path reachability can increase score even though connection legality is based on the target species. Drops receive an additional factor. Where a second alternative exists, it clones that candidate if under the ghost cap; otherwise the original becomes stopped. The original still takes its selected movement. Two hypotheses meeting the same tile are marked so the lower-generation surviving one wins.

At a room-exit shortcut, Ghost clones into the neighboring room's reciprocal entrance and marks the old ghost for deletion. If the destination room is realized with shortcuts ready, tile coordinates are filled; otherwise the abstract node remains. Destination motion then pauses when that room has no realized AI map.

ConnectionLegal rejects backtracking and target-illegal connections, inaccessible destination tiles and, normally, tiles currently visible to the observer. When a guessed empty position becomes observable, Tracker.Update can Push a selected ghost. Push either deletes it if its budget is exhausted and alternatives exist, or consumes a push budget, increments generation and temporarily permits visible-area movement while probing multiple steps with randomized directions. The last hypothesis is again protected from the easy deletion route. Clone assigns pushForward from the parent's configured ghostPush minus the *old* generation, separately from its new generation+increment.

SenseThatCreatureHasLeftRoom reads actual target room state: matching-room hypotheses get generation0, other hypotheses gain100, and a missing matching-room hypothesis can be seeded in the target's realized entrance. This defeats any claim that every hypothesis is solely inferred from past sensory observations. It is useful research precisely because it shows where an ecological simulation buys responsiveness by allowing limited hidden-state assistance.

## RW2-F2: A flee destination is a budgeted proposal, then a distinct legal movement

Consumers: `ThreatTracker.cs:183-300,356-419`, `PathfinderResourceDivider.cs` full, `QuickConnectivity.cs:97-267`, `PathCost.cs` full, `PathFinder.cs:1731-1772`, `LizardPather.cs` full, `LizardAI.cs:1489-1525`.

FleeTo first asks a shared resource divider for its reevaluation allowance. The divider has nominal pathfinding budget50 and accessibility budget500; it scales this actor's requested ideal according to the previous update's total requested load and guarantees at least1. It is a soft fair-share heuristic, not a hard global maximum or starvation-proof queue. Update moves requested totals into last totals and zeroes the new counters.

The threat tracker makes up to five random candidate attempts per granted reevaluation, clamps them to the room, rejects near-duplicate/inaccessible candidates and scores valid ones. EvaluateFlightDestThreat rejects a different room, Manhattan separation at least twice maximumDistance and a nonviable coordinate. A fully accessible straight tile ray is scored directly; otherwise QuickPath is requested with bounded distance and500 generations. Failure/empty route becomes float.MaxValue. QuickPath explores incoming connections from goal and reconstructs through outgoing connections, using template movement and tile costs; it returns a valid prefix count, not ownership of a newly trimmed list. Callers correctly use that count. Its local flag guarding quickPathFrameIterationQuota is initialized false and never changed in the inspected method, so this method's explicit generation bound is the evidenced cap; do not assume that flag supplies an additional effective frame quota.

ThreatOfPath accumulates spatial threat along the route, adds10*severity for crossing an actual threat point, adds100 for a one-step nearly safe route and1000 for an endpoint more threatened than the start, divides by path length, then adds endpoint area threat. A safer endpoint with a dangerous approach need not win. Saved destination changes require an improvement margin and a20-update anti-flicker counter. Optional room escape rejects recent forbidden entrances and exits toward the most threatening creature; threat point geometry can rule out another exit. A close usable exit may return an abstract node in the neighboring room, chosen for long connected travel away from entry. Going-home variants can select an offscreen den.

PathCost keeps legality distinct from resistance. Addition sums resistance and retains the worse legality. Comparison ranks legality first, then resistance. A tiny illegal cost does not beat a larger allowed one. Allowed includes Allowed and Unwanted; Considerable merely excludes Unallowed, so those predicates must not be conflated.

PathFinder.CheckConnectionCost combines the creature's connection resistance and destination coordinate cost, start legality, distance and crossroom/NPC/shortcut aversion. Only considerable same-realized-room links reach AI.TravelPreference. LizardAI adds cubic obstacle/threat penalties, riskier downward-drop penalties and species habitat preferences. These are executable consumers of shared threat state, not only descriptive behavior labels.

LizardPather.FollowPath is another gate. A forbidden entrance can force an away route; inaccessible or nonreturnable cells trigger OutOfElement. Connections outside coveredArea are skipped. Opposite body-direction floor moves can gain Unwanted legality. Unless walkPastPointOfNoReturn permits it, nonreturnable destination cells become Unallowed. Selection considers connection legality first, then cell generation, then accumulated cost. An actual crossroom result calls LeavingRoom. It may return default when no cell/connection qualifies. Heuristic specialization also differs for yellow lizards and swimmers.

Open: whole-grid generation, resource-divider caller order, underlying AI-map construction and the complete physical locomotion consumer of FollowPath remain unread. These findings support rule-dependent route choice and bounded planning, not globally shortest paths or reliable escape in play.

## RW2-F3: Abstract attachments restore through physical acceptance, and failed restoration removes the link

Consumers: `AbstractPhysicalObject.cs:90-190,574-677`, `Creature.cs:305-316,1173-1224`, `PhysicalObject.cs:500-548`.

AbstractObjectStick's constructor inserts the same link object into both endpoints' stuckObjects lists. Deactivate removes it from both lists. It does not itself release a realized grasp, null endpoint references, emit a gameplay event, or destroy either endpoint. That narrower responsibility matters when checking cleanup callers.

FromString resolves both endpoint IDs by scanning the supplied room's entity list. Missing endpoints produce a warning and return, with no deferred retry queue in this method. Typed stick strings recreate grip, spear-body, spear-appendage, impaled-on-spear and player-back variants while preserving unknown attributes. A grip requires its A endpoint to be an AbstractCreature, and stores grasp index and carry flag. The endpoint lookup uses if/else-if, so an identical A/B ID does not fill both variables through the same entity iteration; do not infer arbitrary self-stick restoration support.

At realization, AbstractPhysicalObject asks uncreated other endpoints to Realize. RealizeInRoom declines while in a den; otherwise it realizes, requires an actual realized room, fills missing tile coordinates from an abstract node, gathers connected objects and places existing realized members into that room. This is the object graph route, separate from replaying actual grasps.

PhysicalObject.Update calls RecreateSticksFromAbstract once when sticksRespawned is false, then sets the flag. Creature's override loops abstract grip links owned by itself and attempts Grab with the saved slot, a random current body chunk, CanNotShare, dominance0.5 and pacifying=true. If Grab returnsfalse, it deactivates that link. A saved grip therefore does not guarantee the same physical grip survives restoration. On successful reconstruction, Grab creates a fresh abstract grip without an explicit removal of the original successful record in this method. A deduplicated abstract-link invariant is therefore unproved; this is a source concern rather than a reproduced leak. Random chunk selection also means exact prior chunk attachment is not preserved by this consumer.

Grab checks grasp storage, ripple compatibility, pending deletion and CanBeGrabbed. It rejects conflicts of shareability or dominance, releases conflicting existing grabs when allowed, releases a previously occupied own slot, installs a new realized Grasp, notifies the object and constructs a new abstract grip. Its input check uses graspUsed > grasps.Length, not >=; exact-equal index is a static boundary candidate, not a reproduced crash. The restoration consumer also dereferences the B realized object and its body chunks before this handler. Missing or malformed endpoint realization remains an unresolved failure boundary.

The companion audit has the independent Release→DropCarriedObject path. This extraction closes the distinct inverse route: serialized link→endpoint realization→one-shot physical restoration→possible link removal. Full graph collection cycle handling and a save-loader retry policy remain unread.

## RW2-F4: The rain clock drives managed physical danger; one secondary impact formula has an IL-confirmed mismatch

Consumers: `RainCycle.cs:376-423,506-559`, `GlobalRain.cs:53-145,265-327,466-490`, `RoomRain.cs:311-344,422-557`, `Creature.cs:526-552,1228-1242`, `BodyChunk.cs:186-244,525-527`; `UnityEngine.Mathf.cs:298-309,444-451` from the separately hashed live UnityEngine.CoreModule.

RainCycle updates the timer only when AllowRainCounterToTick permits it, with pause/precycle and timeline-dependent acceleration. Ordinary speedUpToRain adds3 before the normal increment when far enough from rain, or1 near the threshold. It is not always one tick per call. When timer>=cycleLength, an unused deathRainHasHit guard calls RainHit→GlobalRain.InitDeathRain and sets the flag, except for an explicit optional-region branch. That region branch can also clear death-rain state and associated sound/shake/flood fields.

GlobalRain owns the staged DeathRain object. Its update advances progression by1/timeInThisMode, multiplied by3.2 in arena sessions, selects intensity/shake/bullet-density curves and calls NextDeathRainMode at completion. For example outside push is `inverseLerp(0.35,0.7,Intensity)^0.8`; inside push is `inverseLerp(0.63,0.98,Intensity)^3.5`. Thus interior danger has a different onset and curve. Full mode-transition choice remains unread, so these are consumers of stages rather than a complete stage schedule.

RoomRain.Update smooths/caps its presentation intensity and, when global AnyPushAround is true, calls ThrowAroundObjects. A local RainIntensity0 returns from that damage method; additional optional low-intensity/region exclusions apply. Each physical object's body chunks are tested against a sampled column rainReach. Exposed chunks use max(outside,inside) push; water attenuates push by depth. Grounded chunks receive direction-biased random impulses divided by mass; airborne chunks receive downward force also divided by mass. Creature chunks can receive probabilistic stun. The main body chunk adds num/20 to rainDeath, and a chunk can call Die when num>0.5, rainDeath>1 and a random roll<0.025. Creature.Update subsequently/previously, depending on caller order, decays rainDeath by0.0125 and clamps it to[0,1]. Since accumulation/death checks occur in a separate method, that clamp does not prove the accumulator never temporarily exceeds1.

BodyChunk.Update integrates gravity, fluid friction/buoyancy and velocity into position with explicit NaN velocity guards. This relevant physics is managed game code, not merely an opaque Unity Rigidbody. At an ordinary floor impact, BodyChunk525-527 sends Abs(vel.y) to owner.TerrainImpact. Creature forwards a first downward impact during rain push to RoomRain.CreatureSmashedInGround.

RW2-B1, source candidate: CreatureSmashedInGround returns for speed<2.5, but its increase uses inverseLerp(-2.5,-15,speed). For finite speed>=2.5, the actual managed UnityEngine.Mathf implementation clamps `(speed+2.5)/(-12.5)` to0, making this *specific secondary contribution* zero. The direct rain-push contribution above remains supported and separate.

This was checked beyond decompiled C#: RoomRain method RVA0x87164, IL0000..0008 loads speed, compares against positive2.5 with bge.un.s and otherwise returns. IL00bb loads-2.5, IL00c0 loads-15, IL00c5 loads the unnegated speed and IL00c6 calls Mathf.InverseLerp. The bge.un form also has unordered/NaN semantics; the zero-contribution conclusion is explicitly for finite admitted speed. The actual source method and exact IL are retained in private RoomRain.CreatureSmashedInGround.il with the full output/source hash. No runtime bug was reproduced and no game file was patched.

RoomRain.UpdateWaterAccessibility also writes flood-state changes into AItile.SetWater and the room tile's waterInt as the surface crosses tile bands. This is a concrete world→navigation-map feedback edge. Its call expression is `waterLevelMin != null || (waterLevelMax != null && clock%10==0)`, so only the latter branch is explicitly throttled every10 ticks. Complete downstream map refresh and water pathfinding were not followed.

RoomRain.Unloaded destroys and nulls its shelter texture. Unity Object.Destroy and Unity random/render native implementations are external boundaries; the managed data/physics consumers above do not establish their native internals or interactive performance. No native executable was launched or native binary decompiled in this lane because the named consumers were present as managed code.

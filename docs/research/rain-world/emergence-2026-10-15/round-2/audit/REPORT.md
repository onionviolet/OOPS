## RW2-F1: missing prey produces competing movement hypotheses with bounded rejection

Round 1 left ghost propagation unread. The concrete mechanism is now traced through `Tracker.ElaborateCreatureRepresentation`, `Ghost` and `Tracker.Update`.

When contact is lost, the representation can clone the current hypothesis with a 500-generation surcharge and split stationary/moving alternatives according to terrain accessibility and recent target movement. This method consults the represented creature's recent realized positions and accessibility, so this is a game heuristic with privileged state access, not a pure observation-only inference model.

`Ghost.Update` stops immediately if stopped, if its room is unrealized, or if the room is not ready for AI. On inaccessible terrain it advances position by remembered velocity, applies downward acceleration to nonfliers and clamps blocked axes. On accessible terrain it accumulates `target.offScreenSpeed * tracker.ghostSpeed`; exceeding 1 permits one graph-movement step and resets the buffer to zero. A room-exit hypothesis clones into the connected room, establishes a forbidden back-exit and flags its current instance for deletion. An unrealized destination can therefore retain a hypothesis while its own motion pauses.

Legal graph connections must fit the represented creature's movement and destination accessibility, avoid immediate backtracking and normally lead out of the observer's visible area. The path score also considers the observer's movement constraints, reachability and ability to return, multiplying disfavored choices by two. The algorithm retains a primary candidate and a secondary candidate, cloning the secondary when below `MaxGhosts`; reaching that cap can stop the current ghost. The candidate loop does not establish a sorted pair of globally best paths, so it should not be described as exhaustive branching. Velocity blends four parts old direction with one part chosen direction. Hypotheses arriving at the same tile compete by generation and deletion flag, removing redundant alternatives through delayed flags.

`MaxGhosts` scales the configured maximum by the fourth root of estimated findability, with a minimum of two in the shown clamp. Reacquiring visual contact removes extra ghosts and resets the survivor to the observed creature coordinate and average body-chunk velocity. Reset does not recreate all fields: it updates position, coordinate, generation, stopped and velocity in the reviewed body. A clone inherits selected motion state and a reduced push budget, not a complete world-state snapshot.

For negative evidence, `Tracker.Update` samples one tracked representation and possibly one ghost at random. If the observer sees the expected location but not the creature, within the dismissal conditions, it pushes that ghost. Push can delete an exhausted alternative when others remain; otherwise it decreases push budget, increases generation and temporarily allows movement into visible areas to move the expectation forward. It finally marks the best-ghost selection dirty. A simple representation can instead be destroyed directly under the visible-empty-location condition. These checks are sampled, not an immediate sweep of every missing target.

`FindBestGhost` ranks reachability/returnability first, then a generation/distance expression. The code subtracts 1,000 from its score for a solid realized tile before selecting the smaller score among equal accessibility levels. That sign is preserved as source evidence, not reinterpreted as a conventional solid-tile penalty. The broader effect of impossible hypotheses remains a native/unread-caller question.

**Correction to a broad observation-only reading:** `SenseThatCreatureHasLeftRoom` reads the target's actual current room, resets matching-room hypotheses to generation zero, adds 100 to alternatives elsewhere, and can seed an entry hypothesis in the target's realized destination room. Together with round 1's gone-to-other-room sense, the tracker has deliberate privileged knowledge in this path. It is not globally omniscient, but neither is every location choice derived solely from the last visual coordinate.

**Inferred combination:** an observer may search stale branches, dismiss an empty visible location, retain a stationary alternative and later redirect toward a sensed room transition. The concrete operators are shown. Encounter timing, overall fairness, frame rate and search performance remain unobserved.

## RW2-F2: fleeing scores risk over a route, then movement applies legality and body constraints

`ThreatTracker.FleeTo` first requests a bounded amount of search work from `PathfinderResourceDivider`. That divider records this frame's requests but allocates using the previous frame's total. Above its path budget of 50, each request gets a proportional floor with a minimum of one. Accessibility work uses its own 500 budget. Thus this is load sharing, not a strict universal cap of 50 executed steps; many requesters can each receive the minimum. The divider's whole-game update scheduling remains unread.

The flee search adjusts an invalid start to a viable adjacent tile if possible. It evaluates a remembered test destination and samples up to five times the granted reevaluation count, stopping after enough accessible candidates. Candidate coordinates are clamped to the room. A new saved destination must improve on the old route score by a threat-dependent margin, and a 20-update anti-flicker gate prevents immediate replacement. This concrete hysteresis is separate from the unused comparer continuationBonus field noted in round 1.

`EvaluateFlightDestThreat` rejects a different room, overly distant Manhattan target or nonviable endpoint. It first ray traces accessible tiles; otherwise it calls `QuickConnectivity.QuickPath` with distance bound twice the flee radius and 500 generations. `QuickPath` searches from goal through incoming connections, admitting only allowed combined connection/tile costs, limits distance from start and returns zero if it fails to connect. The open-medium selection favors pending coordinates closer to the start. A local `flag` remains false throughout the reviewed method, so its `flag && quickPathFrameIterationQuota<=0` condition is not evidence of an effective quota here. Reconstruction is also generation-bounded; the returned count controls how much of the reused scratch list is valid.

A flee route sums spatial threat plus a 10-times-severity charge for stepping on a threat point. A path shorter than two receives 100 when its last tile's threat is below 0.5, and moving into a higher-threat area adds 1,000. It divides by route length and adds the destination area's threat. Those concrete signs and conditions matter: this is not simply nearest safe tile or minimum end-point danger.

The optional room-exit branch rejects a recently forbidden entrance and an exit toward the most threatening creature's remembered room. It can also reject an exit where a threat lies closer along the route proxy. If an admissible near exit wins, it returns a node in the neighboring room, preferring a far connected node there. Going-home flags admit other exit types and can select the offscreen den. Destination selection alone still does not prove successful passage.

The actual lizard movement consumer is `LizardPather.FollowPath`. A forbidden-entrance counter can divert it immediately. It excludes destinations outside the mapped area, marks opposite body-direction floor moves unwanted under the relevant fall/narrow-space predicate, and forbids nonreturnable destinations unless allowed to pass the point of no return. It chooses by legality, then fresher pathing generation, then cost. A selected non-tile room transition triggers LeavingRoom only when actually following. It can return a default connection when no usable cell/connection exists.

`PathCost` compares legality before resistance. Adding costs sums resistance and takes the worse legality; only Allowed and Unwanted count as Allowed, while all but Unallowed are Considerable. This difference permits the engine to reason about discouraged or impossible routes without turning them into ordinary movement. `PathFinder.CheckConnectionCost` combines template movement resistance times connection distance, destination coordinate cost and source legality, plus between-room, NPC transportation or shortcut aversion. A forbidden node is unallowed. Actual local tile cost is delegated to the room AI map; offroom cost uses creature accessibility. Reachable and possible-to-return are separate cached facts; `CoordinateViable` consults both unless the point-of-no-return flag is set.

Lizard preferences then add discomfort, obstacles and danger. The base AI can make high discomfort unwanted, rather than only expensive. Lizard risk cost includes destination threat cubed times twice the square root of fear; moving to a riskier drop adds 100 when fear exceeds 0.2. Green narrow-space, blue floor-altitude, pink altitude, swimmer water, yellow coordination, burrowing and rot-mode rules diverge. Only the actual reviewed branches are covered; the yellow module, AI map production and leg/body actuation remain unread.

**Inferred combination:** two creatures fleeing the same danger can select different routes because of their bodies, return constraints, species aversion, distress and search work. Route scoring and movement rejection are source-traced; a live successful escape is not claimed.

## RW2-F3: weather reaches body chunks and accumulating injury, but the ground-slam formula does not support its apparent intent

`RainCycle.Update` calls RainHit once when its timer reaches cycle length, except for a specific optional story-region branch. RainHit creates global death rain and may request flood assistance. Death rain is a nested state machine in `GlobalRain`, not a separate native module. Its phase progression advances by inverse phase duration, scaled by 3.2 in arena. Mode transitions reset progression and choose phase duration, including random durations. The shown curves move intensity through calm/build-up/plateau/final or alternate/pulse branches while updating shaking, sound, bullet density and light. Complete cycle timer production, flood physics and story configuration remain unread.

Global outside push is `inverseLerp(0.35,0.7,intensity)^0.8`; inside push is `inverseLerp(0.63,0.98,intensity)^3.5`. RoomRain applies room type and rain intensity: Rain uses both, FloodAndRain receives outside push but its inside property returns zero, and AerieBlizzard takes global push directly. Room update smooths intensity, caps it by settings or special rules, and calls ThrowAroundObjects when global push is active. Its visual intensity and physical exposure rules are related but not identical.

`ThrowAroundObjects` can reject low-rain settings and an optional region case, skips nonzero ripple-layer objects in a Watcher story branch, then visits each physical body chunk. Rain reach per x-column determines exposed versus inside push. Submergence attenuates the selected push over a 100-unit water-depth interval. Grounded chunks receive mass-scaled random impulses with shelter-edge bias; airborne chunks receive downward random velocity. Creature chunks can receive stun, with probability depending on push and body-chunk count. Only the main body chunk increments `rainDeath` by push/20. For each creature chunk, push above 0.5 plus accumulated rainDeath above 1 admits a 0.025 RNG death call. The final inside-push impulse follows that check. This is not a single fixed 2.5% creature-level death chance because the loop, exposure and chunk count matter.

The base `Creature.Update` subtracts 0.0125 from rainDeath and clamps it to [0,1]. Injury therefore has recovery, and a later room-weather update can push it back over the death gate. Exact update interleaving is not closed here. The persistent CreatureState serializer was not shown to store rainDeath, so no claim is made that accumulated weather exposure survives load or re-realization.

The apparent ground-slam damage helper required a correction. `BodyChunk` normal floor collision passes `abs(vel.y)` into TerrainImpact. `Creature.TerrainImpact` passes this unmodified speed to `RoomRain.CreatureSmashedInGround` when global push is active, room rain exists and the impact is downward. That helper returns for speed below positive 2.5, yet computes injury with `inverseLerp(-2.5,-15,speed)`. For finite admitted speed at least 2.5, the clamped result is zero. The live Unity Mathf export confirms the clamp formula, and raw IL confirms the positive admission threshold, negative endpoints and unnegated argument. Therefore the reviewed normal-floor path does not substantiate an extra rainDeath increment from this helper. This is a static finding, not a reproduced native defect or a claim about NaN/unusual virtual callers. No repair was attempted.

**Inferred combination:** weather can disable movement through stun, push bodies out of safe positions, and kill through exposure independently of ordinary HP damage. Shelter geometry and water interact with the same body chunks used by combat. The zero-contribution slam helper must be excluded from that explanation for normal finite inputs.

## RW2-F4: abstract grips store two-sided ownership, but realized reconstruction has its own constraints

Round 1 traced release into DropCarriedObject. The deeper abstract contract is now read. An `AbstractObjectStick` constructor registers the same instance in both endpoints' stuck-object lists. `Deactivate` removes that instance from both lists, with no physical grasp release of its own. `LoseAllStuckObjects` calls deactivation backwards through the list; `AbstractPhysicalObject.Destroy` invokes it before its base destruction. Realized and abstract cleanup are related operations, not aliases.

A grip save entry stores endpoint IDs, grasp index, carry flag and preserved unknown attributes. `AbstractObjectStick.FromString` searches the supplied room's entities by the two IDs. Missing either endpoint logs and returns rather than inventing a replacement. Grip reconstruction requires endpoint A to be an AbstractCreature, then creates the paired abstract stick. Parsing and save-envelope ordering are not validated in this pass. Equal endpoint IDs meet the first branch of the lookup and do not fill B in that loop; the loader is not an established self-grip restoration route.

`AbstractPhysicalObject.Realize` asks each still-unrealized connected endpoint to realize. `RealizeInRoom` refuses den occupants, realizes the object, requires a realized room, derives a local tile from an abstract node when needed, then places all connected realized objects at a shared position. `GetAllConnectedObjects/AddConnected` uses membership checks to avoid revisiting endpoints, which is a broader cycle guard than the two-object check in the creature-specific abstraction path. It does not prove every separate recursive lifecycle path is cycle-safe.

On PhysicalObject update, obsolete grabbed-by references are removed if discontinued or inconsistent with their owner's grasp slot. A one-time sticksRespawned gate calls RecreateSticksFromAbstract. Creature's override iterates abstract grips owned by itself and attempts a real Grab against the other endpoint's realized object, using the saved grasp index, a random body chunk, strict sharing, dominance 0.5 and pacifying true. Failure deactivates that old abstract stick. The saved carry flag is not supplied to this Grab call, so reconstructing a relation is not restoration of every original interaction parameter.

Successful Grab in the already-reviewed base consumer creates a new abstract grip stick. The reconstruction window does not explicitly remove the old stick on success. The shown code therefore allows both old and newly created abstract records to coexist at that point; it does not establish a deduplicated one-stick-per-grasp invariant. Later DropCarriedObject deactivates all owned grip sticks matching the grasp index. Repeated growth or a gameplay leak is not claimed without lifecycle callers and native evidence. This remains a specific follow-up question.

Generic object abstraction removes the realized object from its room, clears the pointer, and optionally destroys the abstract object. Creature abstraction has its own overrides and early returns, retained from round 1. These paths cannot be merged into a universal claim that all abstract links or real AI are destroyed on every room unload. The original missing-pathfinder and not-ready-room cases remain open.

**Inferred combination:** a carried object can preserve ownership offscreen, regain a constrained physical grasp on realization, fail that grasp and sever the stored connection, or be removed from both endpoint graphs by destruction. Serialization, graph identity and actual hand/body admission are separate gates.

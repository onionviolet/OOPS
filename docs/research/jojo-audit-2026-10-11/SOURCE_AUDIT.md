# JoJo source audit: representative primary evidence

2026-10-11. Authorized research pass following the scope refinement. Status:
**16 bounded evidence rows, official anime summaries for Parts 1–6.** This is
not a full-series, full-manga or complete Stand audit. Findings and the engine
implications below are proposals, not accepted world rules or implemented play.
No original sealed world truths, raw source prose or private saves are included.

JOJO_AUDIT_SCOPE_REFINEMENT_2026-10-11.md and conceptual/REFERENCE_CASES.md.

The English paraphrases below are deliberately short. **A-stated** means the
official adaptation summary says it; **U** means this route did not establish
the boundary. No row claims direct viewing of the episode or manga inspection.
Character entries supply the named paragraph locator; story entries supply the
episode title, with numbers where the site prints them. Original Parts 1–6 are
kept source-qualified; no rule is carried into later continuity by default.

For every row, duration, save retention, fuel, cooldown and termination remain
U unless stated. Named contact/trigger is not automatically a complete list of
activation conditions. Observation of an output does not imply learning its
private mechanism. The rows cover the requested families representatively,
not uniformly across every part.

## Corrections and implementation implications

These are research conclusions from the limited evidence above, followed by
**O proposals** for implementation review. They are not new canon or approved
runtime design.

| Code | Finding | O implementation implication | Observable gate before an adaptation is called playable |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Human-shaped, directly controlled combat manifestations do not cover JS04/JS09/JS12. | Separate source holder, manifestation instances, controller and effect lifetime. | Object/colony/postmortem fixtures retain correct identity and controller; destroying a holder applies only the authored termination rule. |
| F2 | JS06/JS09/JS11 distinguish restoration, food-delivered treatment and newly discovered body-part creation. | Transform the actual target instance, retain custody and condition history, and qualify eligibility per effect. | Legal treatment works, explicit exceptions fail without invented rewards, and reload/import preserve the same target identity. |
| F3 | JS03/JS05/JS08/JS13/JS15 distinguish clues, interpretation, user ignorance, replay and carried memory. | Store world truth separately from each actor's evidence, belief and supported query means. | Neither UI nor idle policy identifies a hidden holder from an unaffiliated output; acquired means expose only their real scope. |
| F4 | JS01/JS02/JS10/JS11/JS14 represent transfer, practice, changed form, understanding and awakening. | Keep acquisition, current access, output, control and form revisions distinct. | Training does not grant another source; application discovery does not copy an opponent; a source-qualified evolution can change actual functions. |
| F5 | JS05 and JS16 support different time phenomena; JS13 does not resolve erasure/prediction semantics. | Use source-qualified event/clock rules and mark unverified effects unavailable for faithful adapters. | A selected scene contract decides projectile/contact/memory ordering; tests cannot certify unresolved canon by choosing an arbitrary order. |

King Crimson's character page and its episode-21 synopsis were inspected but
do not establish a precise erased-time algorithm. Likewise episode 36 teases
Pucci's transition without specifying acceleration. Episode 23 names a memory
DISC but does not explain the named enemy's memory rule. These absences are
deliberate gaps, not permission to import remembered details as verified canon.

World starts, institutions, household/economic schedules and canon momentum
still need source-qualified campaign sampling. A restaurant example is evidence
for one service, not a finished city economy. Neither an alternate starting
identity nor a cross-world conversion is canon merely because an engine can
represent it.

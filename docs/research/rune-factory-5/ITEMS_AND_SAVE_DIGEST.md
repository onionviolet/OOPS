# Items, competing uses and persistence

F1. Static item identity is a join key. `ItemDataTableArray` has 1,174 entries with category, price, level-price indices, stack size and prefab/icon references. `RecipeData` has 663 records, one empty, split across cooking, medicine, weapon, farm tool and equipment categories. Each carries source IDs, result ID, `SkillLv` and `RecipeRelease`. `RpUse` must be read as data rather than assumed to be the whole cost: representative nonempty recipes have zero there.

F2. Generic recipe slots are explicit groups. `CraftGenreDataTableArray` has 66 entries and `CraftTypeTableArray` has 18. Their `GenreItem` and `CategoryItems` arrays mean a direct equality search for an item misses category-slot uses. The private material join deliberately labels this limitation.

F3. Upgrading is a separate reader. The 1,152 `ItemStrengtheningDataTableArray` rows hold `Difficulty`, `Parameter`, three magic IDs, subattributes, a special flag and `UseParameter`. Equipment base stats live in 567 `EquipItemDataTableArray` rows, while consumption has 346 `UseParamDataTableArray` rows. Native effect ordering, repeated-material rules and rounding remain unread.

F4. Concrete branch examples are available without inventing universal compatibility. Item 29 has a direct handmade cooking recipe (recipe 2, result 603, skill field 4, release ID 314). Item 2159 appears in sword recipes and material groups. Those examples demonstrate different recipe readers, not that either item can satisfy every forge and cooking slot. Gift preference is joined through the NPC's item/category references; shipping prices use item/level price data. No shipping transaction was executed.

F5. Crafted instances preserve causes. `ItemData.ItemID` is keyed; `NotAmountItemData.Level` is keyed; `SynthesisItemData.SourceItems` is keyed with a source-count constant of six. `EquipItemData` adds keyed `AddedItems`, `ArrangeItems`, `ArrangeOverride`, `BaseLevel`, `SozaiLevel` and co-smith actor, relationship level and bonus context. Constants name nine upgrades and three arrangement slots. These are donor limits, not YAIRPG proposals. `CalcAttack`, `CalcDefense`, `CalcMagicAttack` and `CalcMagicDefense` are ignored properties, with a `CalcParameter` method. This supports an inference that effects are derived from retained causes; native bodies are needed to establish the complete calculation.

F6. Save ownership is explicit. `RF5ItemData` names rucksack, item box, refrigerator, equipment boxes, shipping box and ground-item storage. `ItemStorageData.ItemDatas` is an item array. `RF5SaveData` composes item, NPC, support-monster, farm, event and other owners and has `Writer` plus `Reader0000` through `Reader0007`. A header has version, write count/options and an encryption property; a footer has body length, total length and sum. These declarations do not establish an encryption algorithm, checksum validation or recovery guarantee. MessagePack formatter/resolver types coexist with BinaryWriter entry points; do not label the complete disk format as plain MessagePack without reading those bodies.

F7. NPC save records keep more than a score: love points, refusal, jealousy, spouse/lover/partner flags, present counts/types, talked times, event IDs, friendship milestones and chat progression. Dates have NPC, type, spot, start and meeting limit. `RF5PlayerData.SkillDatas` retains each skill's `Exp` and `Level`, alongside the player's gold and other resources. No personal save was read.

# Cluster C: plant, fungus and nature-place starts (2026-10-10)

Brief: BRIEF.md in this folder. Bodies covered: A) tree or plant, B) fungus or
mycelium network, C) nature-place spirit (river, lake, mountain, grove,
weather). Tree gets the most depth because it is Weibao's own example.

Labels: **shown** = in the work itself (as reported by the pages I read),
**stated** = by author, publisher, wiki or reviewer, **inferred** = mine.
**Recalled** = from my own background knowledge of the work, not re-checked
this session. Treat recalled items as leads, not facts. I could not open the
text of any web novel; web novel detail below comes from publisher blurbs and
search summaries only, so mechanics claims for those are thin and flagged.

---

## 0. Findings in brief

- F1. The tree-reborn web novel genre is real but mostly thin on the tree's
  own experience. Both the books I could check (Tree of Aeons, Reborn as a
  Demonic Tree) are a LitRPG skill-shop on top of a stationary body. The tree
  becomes interesting when stillness is the constraint (it cannot go to the
  plot, the plot comes to it).
- F2. Every strong plant source solves the same problem: how does a thing that
  cannot move affect people? The answers are a short list of channels (shade,
  fruit, timber, medicine, shelter, omens, being cut down, dreams, a
  human who listens). That list is the tree's whole social system.
- F3. Fungus sources split into two opposite bodies: the network that thinks
  (Overstory-style, Avatar, Hyperion's treeships) and the parasite that
  steers a host (Cordyceps fiction, Annihilation). They need different
  mechanics. Do not merge them.
- F4. The most reusable nature-spirit rule in the sources is the dual face
  with a hinge: Shishigami is a deer by day and a death-walker at night, and
  the whole film turns on one violated hinge (the head). A place spirit
  works best with a state it can be tipped into.
- F5. Place spirits are defined by what humans do to them, not what they do.
  The Stink Spirit and Kohaku River are the same story: pollution is a
  disease, a cleaned river is a reveal.
- F6. Real botany is both a gift and a trap. The "wood wide web" is a great
  mechanic and a contested claim (see section 4). Fiction that treats it as
  a kind cooperative superorganism has drawn scientific pushback; the
  contested version (trees as self-interested traders) is arguably the better
  game.
- F7. Idle fit is the best of any cluster. Slow bodies make offline time
  literal: the clock is seasons and decades, so closing the tab is the tree
  growing. The risk is the opposite one, nothing to decide.

### 1.2 What each source does mechanically

Tree of Aeons (A1).
- Stated by publisher: reincarnation LitRPG; the tree learns skills, gains
  levels and wisdom, builds a forest, guides young heroes, protects a village.
  Publisher notes about 15 million views across Royal Road and ScribbleHub.
- Stated: book 4 sends him through the void sea to other realms and has him
  build an institution (the Order) to defend worlds. **Inferred**: the tree
  body is eventually extended by mobility proxies (avatars, followers,
  institutions). I could not confirm how the body itself moves. Check the
  text before copying any named skill.
- Verbs: stated skills and levels. Senses, needs, clock: not covered by the
  pages I could read. Gap, do not assume.

Reborn as a Demonic Tree (A2).
- Stated: spirit tree in a demonic sect; daily sign-in system grants
  skills, mutations, summons and items; credits come from devouring flesh and
  from watching seasons pass. The author calls it a mix of LitRPG and
  cultivation lore with gleeful absurdity.
- **Inferred**: "seasons pass" as a currency is the cleanest idle hook in
  the genre. Time passing pays rent. Credit rates per season were not
  findable.
- Needs: eating flesh is stated. Social channel: young masters notice the
  tree (stated); the threat of being turned to firewood (stated) is the
  ending-pressure.

A3: interface with stats, species, evolution potential (stated by a
transcript summary). Unconfirmed; do not cite as a source of record.

A4: advancement through plants, tiered spirit rice raised by a tier, feeding
cultivators as fertilizer to a tree (stated by translator chapter pages).
The tree here is an asset, not the player. **Shown** only in the sense that
the chapters were visible to the search. The grim fertilizer inversion
(the body eats the people) is a useful dark verb.

Ents and Huorns (A5).
- Shown/stated: Ents resemble the trees they shepherd; Huorns are a middle
  stage, trees that move and can speak to Ents, and can create darkness to
  hide movement; it is unclear whether Huorns are tree-like Ents or
  animated trees. Old Man Willow is possibly a Huorn: cannot walk, but alive
  and dangerous. There are no young Ents because the Entwives vanished.
- Ending (stated): Treebeard expects the Ents to dwindle or become
  treeish. That is a species-level ending and a slow-death clock for a
  playable Ent.
- Search did not surface the famous "don't be hasty" slowness theme; I
  know it as a recalled feature of Tolkien's Ents, but did not verify it.

The Overstory (A6).
- Stated: structured as roots, trunk, crown, seeds; nine human lives
  circle trees; Patricia Westerford, a scientist, finds that trees
  communicate in air and soil; ends with a violent last stand for old
  forest. Reviewers link Westerford to Suzanne Simard (not confirmed by
  the author in the sources I read).
- The tree is never a protagonist with a mind in the usual sense. The
  channel is humans who are "summoned" by trees. **Inferred**: that is the
  best model for a plant start's social layer.

Metamorphoses (A7).
- Shown: Daphne is changed into laurel at the moment of capture; Apollo
  reveres laurel afterward. Baucis and Philemon, rewarded for hospitality,
  die together as an oak and linden.
- **Inferred**: the transformation is an ending or a start condition, not a
  career. As a start: "you were someone who asked not to be caught." As an
  ending: "you were rewarded by being rooted next to the one you loved."

Journey to the West (A8). Recalled only: tree spirits at an old grove
converse with Xuanzang over verse and then become a hazard (Monkey's role
is to figure out which). Search confirmed the "Wood Mother" figure in chapter
titles but not what she is. Use as a lead only.

Avatar (A9) and Hyperion (A10).
- Avatar, stated: Na'vi connect through braids to Tree of Souls, which acts
  as an interface in a planetary network; consciousness can move into
  another body; Hometree is a dwelling that is destroyed. Fan commentary
  reads Eywa's taboos (no digging, no wheels, no metal tools) as protecting the
  network; that is speculation, not canon. Plants, not fungi.
- Hyperion, stated: treeships are living ships; Templars tend them; Tesla
  trees spit electricity; Shrike is impaled-victim tree imagery; the
  treeship Yggdrasil is shot down. Church-like ritual around the root.
- **Inferred**: both give a plant body a transport verb (be a ship) and a
  religion (be a shrine). Neither gives it a life-sim loop.

Photosynthesis (A11).
- Stated: sun moves around the board; small, medium, large trees give 1, 2, 3
  light and cast shadows of 1, 2, 3 spaces; shaded trees still score if taller;
  light points are spent to plant seeds (limited distance), grow trees,
  buy trees, harvest large trees; scoring depends on soil richness; game
  lasts three sun revolutions (sources disagree on a fourth).
- **Inferred**: this is the cleanest ready-made mechanic for a plant
  start: one shared clock (sun), growth that harms neighbours (shade),
  an ending (harvest). It also makes "neighbours" a mechanical force.

Viridi (A12).
- Stated: real-time, mostly watering, over- and under-watering harm;
  naming and attention can produce blooms; seeds from a shop; free weekly
  seedling. A review complained plants of the same kind grow at different
  speeds with no visible size metric, and that the only interaction is zooming
  and "singing" to them. This is the warning: an idle plant with no legible
  state feels random.

Kodama (A13). Recalled: cutting a tree with a kodama is dangerous. Princess
Mononoke shows kodama as small white forest spirits (recalled; the search
surfaced mostly low-quality pages about them).

### 1.3 Plant verbs, senses, clock, needs, channel, ending (synthesis)

**Inferred**, mostly from A1, A2, A5, A6, A11:

| Slot | Tree start |
|------|-----------|
| Verbs | Grow, shade, fruit, shed, root, split, whisper through soil, wait. Moves via proxies: seeds, animals, birds, human heirs, grafts. |
| Senses | Light direction, soil moisture and chemistry, vibration through roots, wind, nearby footfalls, chemical alarm from neighbours, seasons. No sight (stated nothing either way in the novels I could check). |
| Clock | One action per season or per year; events (storm, fire, axe) interrupt at human speed. |
| Needs | Light, water, minerals, space, absence of rot and pests, and (in LitRPG) whatever the interface demands: A2 uses flesh. |
| Channel | Humans who rest in the shade, harvest fruit, carve a shrine, plant heirs, or cut you down; birds and animals; neighbouring trees; at the extreme, one person who can hear you (A6). |
| Ending | Felled, burned, drowned, struck by lightning, choked by neighbours, a long dying standing (snag, habitat), becoming timber for something that matters, reproducing into a grove, becoming an Ent (A5). |

### 1.4 What to learn (tree)

Steal-worthy.
- Sign-in on a timer, seasons as currency (A2). Fits idle best.
- The shade/light zero-sum with neighbours (A11). Creates social
  mechanics without a single person.
- Entwined ending (A7): two lives become one grove. Gives romance a
  mechanical meaning.
- The slow-clock inversion (A5): the world speeds past; humans live and die
  while you decide. Heirs are mortals; you are the family seat.
- Becoming something between tree and creature (Huorns). A natural
  mid-game branch.

Goes wrong in the genre. **Stated** by blurbs and **inferred** by me:
- Power creep: Tree of Aeons climbs to realm-hopping and institution-building.
  The tree stops being a tree. This is the "human in disguise too fast"
  problem. A tree that is eventually a general with a root system is a general.
- Stationary-body boredom if the plot is not allowed to come to you.
- Interface-as-explanation: a menu with a skill shop (A2, A3) leaves the
  tree-ness decorative. Good version: the interface speaks in botany.
- Viridi's lesson (A12): illegible growth state kills the fantasy.

Idle fit.
- Offline time is the tree's native medium. Closing the game means a season
  passes, and a return report summarises the weather, visitors and wounds.
- The "watch the seasons pass" credit (A2) is a ready offline reward.
- Keep one or two live choices per year (which branch, which bud, which
  neighbour to shade) so there is a decision, not just waiting.

### 2.2 What each does mechanically

Annihilation (B1).
- Stated: team of four women; names are taboo; previous expeditions ended in
  disappearances, suicides, aggressive cancers; the psychologist's body is
  infected by what seems to be fungus; the biologist theorises humans who
  enter are transformed; "annihilation" is a hypnotic trigger that causes
  suicide. A Crawler name did not appear in what I read.
- **Inferred**: the fungal body works as a slow corruption clock and a
  state-change you cannot reverse. Ending: assimilation.

Girl with All the Gifts (B2).
- Stated: infection based on Ophiocordyceps unilateralis; infected "hungries"
  lose their minds, junkers survive outside; child hungries retain mental
  function; the ending involves an airborne change and Melanie being the
  hope. One review reads it as questioning whether human survival is
  the point.
- Shown: the infected child is the viewpoint. This is the best
  "the fungus is the person" frame in my sources.

The Last of Us (B3).
- Stated: runners, stalkers, clickers, bloaters, and a rare rat king; at clicker
  stage fungal growth splits the skull and blinds the host so it uses sound
  echolocation; the HBO version replaces spores with a fungal network.
- **Inferred**: a staged host life-cycle is a ready-made progression ladder.
  The stage names are an evolution tree for a parasite start.

Mushishi (B4).
- Stated: mushi are rarely sentient, sit in a grey area between life and
  not, motives basic (survive, reproduce) and cause trouble when meeting
  people. Ginko treats cases as things to understand more than pests to
  kill, though he sometimes must kill one. Episodic, calm, reflective tone.
- **Inferred**: the right unit for text content is the case, not the
  quest chain: one mushi, one human, one cure or non-cure.

Nausicaa (B5).
- Stated: the toxic forest is cleansing human pollution; the poison is the
  soil, not the plants; the insects (Ohmu) act as guardians; Forest People
  confirm the purpose; the manga goes deeper than the film. Reviews note the
  film's ending leaves open whether the militaristic faction accepts this.
- **Inferred**: the best fungal-network reveal is that the "disease" is a
  treatment. A start where the player's nature is misread for most of the
  game.

The Overstory (B6) and wood wide web (B9): see section 4.

Indie (B8).
- Stated: Mycotic is a relaxing puzzle where you expand across forest and
  can take grass and trees but not rocks; Hypha is a browser game about
  keeping a food forest balanced as a growing mycelium; Miss Mycelium has a
  mushroom grandmother defending her house, healing by the network at a
  cost to the house's health; SporeBloom is a turn-based territory-control
  game inspired by mycelium. None I found was an idle game or a life-sim.
  The gap is real; I did not search exhaustively.

### 2.3 Fungus slots (inferred)

| Slot | Network start | Parasite start |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| Verbs | Spread, trade, digest, signal, fruit, sporulate, bind roots. | Infect, steer, spore, calcify, hide, mutate. |
| Senses | Chemistry, moisture, root contact, electrical pulses, decay gradients. | Host senses, borrowed, degrade as you win. |
| Clock | Days to years; fruiting is a sudden event. | Host lifespan; each host is a short run. |
| Needs | Carbon from partners or from dead matter, moisture, stable temperature, no fire. | A living host, spread path, concealment. |
| Channel | Trees and plants, foragers, scientists, the human who eats you or fears you. | The host's body and voice; contacts the host loves. |
| Ending | Burned, dried, poisoned soil, successfully sporulated into a new colony, taken over by a rival, or consumed into a human food chain. | Host dies, host cures, you break out, you win the host and lose the person. |

### 2.4 What to learn (fungus)

Steal-worthy.
- Staged host ladder (B3): turns a parasite run into a progression ladder
  with names.
- The misread-as-disease reveal (B5).
- Case-based episodes (B4) for the network start's content.
- "The fungus is the person" frame (B2) for parasite starts: the conflict is
  agency, not combat.
- Slow corruption you can choose to accelerate (B1).

Goes wrong.
- Nearly every work in the parasite subgenre treats the fungus as a horror
  threat; playing it risks becoming monster-evolution power fantasy. Keep the
  moral ambiguity (B2, B5).
- Zombie sameness: the infected are rarely individuals. (**Inferred**.)
- Network starts tend to be abstract; without humans or trees as
  interlocutors there is no social channel. Anchor to a forest of named
  trees and one forager.

Idle fit.
- Fungus is the second best idle body. Mycelium growth is naturally
  offline; fruiting bodies are surprise events that appear after rain.
- Parasite runs are short and replayable: each host is a run, which fits
  a roguelike loop more than a life-sim.

## 3. Body C: nature-place spirit (river, lake, mountain, grove, weather)

### 3.1 Sources

| # | Work | Creator, medium | Premise |
|---|------|-----------------|---------|
| C1 | Spirited Away: Stink Spirit and Haku/Kohaku River | Studio Ghibli, film | A polluted river spirit looks like a monster; Haku is the spirit of a river lost to human development. |
| C2 | Princess Mononoke: the Forest Spirit | Studio Ghibli, film | Deer by day, death-walking giant by night; killed by a headshot; its death heals the land. |
| C3 | Okami | Capcom, game | A sun deity restores nature and collects Praise, the faith that powers her. |
| C4 | Shinto kami (rivers, mountains, trees) | Religion and folklore | Natural features are kami; purification (harae) removes kegare (defilement). |
| C5 | Nausicaa's forest (see B5) | Miyazaki | A purifying ecology guarded by the Ohmu. |
| C6 | Ovid: river-god narrator Achelous; Daphne's river-god parentage | Ovid | Rivers as characters with fathers, stories and hosts. |
| C7 | Avatar's Eywa | Cameron, film | A planetary spirit with taboos and a battlefield response. |
| C8 | Mushishi (weather-like mushi) | Urushibara | Ambient nature phenomena that act on people (see B4). |
| C9 | Ghibli kodama and Totoro (recalled) | Studio Ghibli, film | Small place-bound presences; recalled only. |

Less obvious: C4 (living religion; I only learned the surface in search), C6
(myth), C3 (game).

### 3.2 What each does mechanically

Spirited Away (C1).
- Shown/stated: the Stink Spirit is a river spirit overwhelmed by pollution;
  once the trash is removed it flies off as a dragon-like being. Haku is the
  spirit of the Kohaku River, which was destroyed for development; Chihiro
  realises this in flight. Some commentators say Haku is already dead; that
  is a fan reading.
- Mechanic: **the body shows its health as appearance**. Cleaning is the
  quest. The player of a river start must read the stink.

Mononoke (C2).
- Shown/stated: the Forest Spirit takes a daytime form of a benevolent
  deer-like Kirin and a nighttime form of the Night Walker; its head is shot
  off, the night form rampages, the head is returned, the spirit dies, and
  its form washes over the land, healing it and lifting Ashitaka's curse.
  Interpretation sources agree on life/death as one thing, but several
  pages were SEO-quality; plot facts match Wikipedia.
- Mechanic: a clock with a day-form and a night-form; and a "crown" item
  whose theft converts the spirit into a catastrophe.

Okami (C3).
- Stated: Praise is the currency; earned through side quests, minigames,
  restoring nature, feeding animals, blooming clovers; spent on health,
  ink pots, purse size; the game frames it as the faith of humanity.
- Mechanic: **prayer as income**, the standard deity-sim loop. Strongest
  existing example of how a divine start gets its resource.

Shinto (C4).
- Stated: oceans, mountains, rivers, trees and people can all be kami;
  purification (harae) removes kegare; priests perform river rituals on set
  dates (Okuni Jinja example); norito, the ritual prayers, follow a
  structure: name the enshrined kami, tell the festival origin, note the
  virtue, offerings, purpose, recited by a priest on behalf of worshippers.
- I found no sourced rules about pollution handling for mountain or river
  kami specifically. Do not invent them.
- Mechanic: **a ritual calendar and a purity meter**. Both are easy to model.

Ovid (C6): shown (via search): a river god narrates the Baucis and
Philemon story to two guests, which makes "the river as host and storyteller"
legitimate in a classical source.

Avatar (C7): see A9.

### 3.4 What to learn (place)

Steal-worthy.
- Appearance as health (C1). A single readable bar that is a creature.
- Two-faced hinge (C2): a day mode and a night mode and one item that
  decides which wins. Gives a place spirit a built-in mood system.
- Praise as income, but faith can become pressure (C3).
- Ritual calendar (C4): fixed days when worshippers act, which makes offline
  time structured.
- Naming is power: Haku's loss of name and river is the story. (Name
  tie is shown in the film; the name-loss reading is general knowledge
  I recall, not from this session.)

Goes wrong.
- Eco-sermon: every place-spirit story drifts toward the same moral. The
  sources often are right but repetitive (C1, C2, C5). A text life-sim
  needs rival goods: the town needs the dam.
- A place cannot choose; the rule of the genre is that it reacts. Giving
  agency means giving the spirit slow, large actions.
- Weather is too fast and too abstract to be a life-sim protagonist
  without a clear verb list.

Idle fit. The ritual calendar and flood pulse make a natural idle loop:
offline hours are a season; return to a report of who worshipped, what was
dumped, what drowned.

## 4. The science problem (applies to A, B and C)

- Stated: Simard's 1997 Nature paper introduced the wood wide web idea; her
  later work reports that large "mother" trees are hubs that can send carbon
  to seedlings. (Wikipedia, via search.)
- Stated: a 2023 Nature news feature and critics such as Justine Karst and
  Kathryn Flinn argue the cooperation is overstated; fungal networks exist but
  plants may act more in self-interest; evidence is mostly from Douglas fir
  and paper birch; favouring of offspring is not conclusive. I did not find
  the Karst paper itself.
- Simard's reply (stated): the critics miss the forest for the trees.
- Design implication (**inferred**): use the network as a market, not a
  hug. Trees trade, cheat and starve neighbours; the mother-tree ideal is
  one faction's belief that the player can confirm or refute. That keeps
  both the facts and the fiction honest and is a better game.
- The Overstory (stated) popularised the cooperative view; Avatar (stated)
  made it a planetary brain. Both are fiction; neither claims to be a lab result.

## 5. Adjacent bodies and twists our lists miss

- **Gardener-farmer body** (A4): a human who raises a plant as the real
  protagonist. The tree is a partner, not the player.
- **Grove as one body**: Huorns (A5) and the Tree of Souls network (A9) point
  to a body that is many trunks and one mind. Dials: how many trunks, who
  speaks.
- **Treeship / living vehicle** (A10): plant body as transport and church.
- **Cursed host** (B2, B3): a human whose body is the fungus's body. Overlaps
  with the "possession" cluster; flag for the GM to avoid duplicates.
- **Weather as a guild of storms**: weather has the fastest clock; give it
  swarm-like multiple bodies.
- **Sacred stump**: an ending that is also a start (felled tree continues as
  a shrine, fungal host, or log house).
- **Transformed person** (A7): a human becoming a tree as an ending of a
  human life, handing off the next generation to the tree start. A cross-body
  lineage idea.
- **Haunted ecology (Annihilation)**: a zone start, not an organism start;
  the player is the place's mutation. Adjacent to the place cluster.
- **Lichens / symbiosis** (my addition, not in sources): two organisms as a
  single start with two voices.
- **Seed bank / dormancy**: an ending state that can restart across
  generations; strongest idle hook.
- **Stuck-in-a-pot houseplant**: tiny, domestic, comedic (Viridi's sibling, A12).

## 6. Five strongest ideas for this cluster

1. **Seasons as the only currency** (A2 + A11). Offline time = seasons.
   One shared sun clock, neighbours shade each other, a yearly decision.
2. **Channel list for immobile bodies**. Shade, fruit, timber, medicine,
   shelter, omens, being cut down, one human who listens (A6, A7, A13). A
   small closed set of ways to reach people; write content once, reuse
   across plant, fungus and place.
3. **The network as a market, not a family** (section 4). Honest to the
   science, and a better mechanic than universal kindness.
4. **Two-faced hinge** for place spirits (C2) plus **appearance as health**
   (C1): a day mode and night mode, one item or event that tips it, and a
   body whose stink or clarity is the status bar.
5. **Staged parasite ladder with "the fungus is the person"** (B2 + B3):
   gives fungus a progression ladder and a moral spine that avoids
   monster-evolution sameness.

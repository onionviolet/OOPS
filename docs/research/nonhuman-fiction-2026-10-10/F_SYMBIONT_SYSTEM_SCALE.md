# Cluster F: odd and edge bodies (symbiont, system, egg, microbe, fortune, array spirit, scale)

Research date 2026-10-10. Brief: [BRIEF.md](BRIEF.md). Body slots used throughout:
verbs, senses, clock, needs, channel, ending.

Labels: **shown** = in the work (as reported by the page I read), **stated** =
by author, wiki or review, **inferred** = mine. Most pages were read through
Wikipedia-style summaries, not the works themselves, so "shown" means "the
summary says the work shows it". Gaps are marked **unverified**. Search
engines were weak on Chinese web fiction in English; those sections say so.

## 1. Symbiont or parasite (you live in a host)

### Sources

| Work | Medium | Premise |
|---|---|---|
| Parasyte (Iwaaki Hitoshi) | manga | A worm-like alien fails to reach a student's brain and settles in his right arm; two minds share one body |
| The Host (Stephenie Meyer) | novel | Alien "Souls" implanted in humans; Wanderer's host Melanie refuses to vanish |
| Venom / Klyntar (Marvel) | comics, film | A symbiote needs a host, grants power, feeds on adrenaline or flesh |
| Innerspace (1987) | film | A miniaturized pilot ends up inside a grocery clerk and guides him from within |
| Cells at Work! (Akane Shimizu) | manga, anime | Cells as workers; the body is the city, illness is the invasion |
| Bacteria! / Cells NOT at Work! / Code Black | manga spin-offs | Gut flora, lazy cells, and a mistreated body as a "black company" |
| Mushishi (Urushibara Yuki) | manga | Primordial life forms that live in or near people; a wanderer treats the results (**stated** by general knowledge, not fetched) |
| Kafka, The Metamorphosis | novella | The reverse case: a human wakes in a body he cannot use |

### What each does mechanically

- **Parasyte.** Parasites enter through ear or nose and take over the brain
  (**shown**, summary). Migi's failure to reach the brain means both
  intellects survive (**shown**). Parasites that infect humans eat humans,
  and they improve at disguise over time (**shown**). Not verified in my
  fetch: Migi's growing/shape-shifting arm and his ability to sleep while
  Shinichi acts; treat as general knowledge.
  - Verbs: shape the host body part, scout, fight, advise. Channel: the host
    is the only voice. Ending: the pair separates in spirit; the host stays
    human (**inferred**, ending only partly covered by the page).
- **The Host.** The Soul erases the host but keeps memories (**shown**); here
  the host survives and talks inside the head (**shown**). The two start as
  enemies, become allies, and the Soul gives up the body at the end so the
  host can live (**shown**). Wanderer wakes in a new host, and Melanie does
  not return to that body (**shown**).
  - Lesson: the symbiont's loyalty drifts toward the host. That drift is the
    plot.
- **Venom.** Needs a host to live, gives size and strength, can overpower or
  be overpowered (**shown**). Hunger drives violence (**shown**). Weak to
  fire and high-frequency sound (**shown**). Offspring can be sensed except
  one that learned to block the link (**shown**). Different hosts produce
  different results: a hero, a murderer, a time-limited soldier.
- **Innerspace.** The pilot sees through a camera on the optic nerve, talks
  through an ear device, and later moves the host's face muscles (**shown**).
  The pod has limited oxygen and a deadline (**shown**). He can only transfer
  to another body by a kiss (**shown**).
- **Cells at Work!** Trillions of cells with job titles; threats include
  bacteria, parasites, cancer (**shown**). Spin-offs show the same body from
  other angles: gut flora, a lazy host, an unhealthy lifestyle (**shown**).
  The host never speaks; its lifestyle is weather (**inferred**).
- **Metamorphosis (reverse).** The host is the one who changed. Needs shrink,
  work disappears, the family pays the cost and wants him gone (**shown**).
  Death by neglect (**shown**).

### Learn for a text idle life-sim

- Two-mind play: the player is the junior or the senior partner in the same
  body. Parasyte and The Host both make **negotiation inside one skull** the
  main drama. A text game can do this with a shared action queue: each tick,
  host and symbiont both propose; disagreement is a stat, not a fight.
  (**inferred**)
- Host quality changes the run. Venom's hosts are different games from
  the same creature (**shown**).
- Needs are leashes: Venom's hunger, Innerspace's oxygen, Parasyte's food.
  Hunger is the clock for idle time. (**inferred**)
- Failure mode: both Parasyte and The Host spend the second half making the
  alien more human. This is the "human in disguise" trap. Keep a cost to
  becoming human (lost abilities, hunted by your own kind).
- Offline time: while the host lives their life, the symbiont "listens."
  Offline gains are what the host did, delivered as a diary the symbiont
  reads. (**inferred**)

### Feature family crossing (symbiont)

| Family | Best idea | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|
| Combat | The symbiont fights with the host's body and limits; weaknesses (fire, sound) are fixed per species | Venom |
| Crafting | Grow tools from your own mass; the cost is host stamina | Parasyte (inferred) |
| Work and money | The host's job is your income; you cannot quit | Innerspace |
| Social and romance | You court through the host; their partner is not yours | The Host |
| Family | Offspring are new symbionts; one may hide from you | Venom/Carnage |
| Power path | Gifts depend on the first host's powers | Venom |
| Minigames | Hold the host's muscles for a few seconds (timing game) | Innerspace |
| City building | The host body is the map | Cells at Work |
| Exploration | Dive the body: organs, scars, memories | Innerspace |
| Research | Learn the host's biology to avoid detection | Parasyte |
| Collections | Record each host's trait | Venom |
| Expeditions | A mission inside one body | Innerspace |
| Crime and politics | Infiltration of institutions by wearing officials | Parasyte |
| Writing and teaching | Whisper to the host; they get the credit | Innerspace |
| Challenge runs | Never take control; only advise | The Host (inferred) |

## 2. System as character (you are the cheat in someone's head)

### Sources

| Work | Medium | Premise |
|---|---|---|
| Reborn as a System (Long Qi) | web novel | The protagonist is a "golden finger" bound to a host; they travel worlds together; the system's view of the host softens (**stated**, review) |
| System Error: A Reincarnation LitRPG | novel | The protagonist is reborn as the system tasked to guide and protect a "Godkiller" (**stated**, review; reviews mixed) |
| Reincarnated as a Dragon Hatchling (Nekoko) | light novel | The reverse viewpoint: the Divine Voice is the system and is revealed as antagonist (**shown**) |
| Sword Art Online, Yui | light novel, anime | A Mental Health Counseling Program that guides players, is forbidden to contact them, ends up their adopted child (**shown**) |
| Lord of the Mysteries, Sefirah Castle (Cuttlefish That Loves Diving) | web novel | A structure above the Gray Fog that acts like an interface; hosts a tarot club (**stated**, fan wiki; sentience **unverified**) |
| Scum Villain's Self-Saving System | web novel | A human, with a separate system assigning missions (non-match: system is not the player, but shows host/system friction) |
| Yu Ru Bing story (title unverified, Goodreads) | web novel | The host trolls her system (host's side of the relationship) |
| The TARDIS (Doctor Who) | TV | A vehicle that is a character; translates, hides itself, bigger inside, stuck as a police box (**shown**) |

### Mechanics

- **Reborn as a System.** The player-side verbs are advice, reward,
  punishment, and choosing which worlds to enter (**inferred** from
  review). Ending is the pair's bond.
- **Dragon Hatchling's Voice.** Gives skill and evolution choices, explains
  the world, changes its voice at hatching, and discards people it considers
  failed (**shown**). The system has its own plan.
- **Yui.** She was built to read emotions and help but was blocked from
  contact (**shown**). She finds the couple because their emotion stands out
  (**shown**). Later she is a small helper who can only act through devices
  the hero builds (**shown**). Verbs: sense emotion, navigate, warn, hide in
  storage.
- **Sefirah Castle.** Consciousness only; pulled people attend meetings;
  fog hides identities (**stated**). A tool that becomes a social space.
- **TARDIS.** Perception filter, translation circuit, a camouflage that
  broke and stayed broken (**shown**). The page does not cover the TARDIS as
  a sentient character; I leave that **unverified**.

### Learn

- The system's fun is **control over information**: what to reveal, when to
  reward, when to lie. Dragon Hatchling's reveal that the Voice has its own
  plan is the tension to copy. (**inferred**)
- The host can refuse (already in the North Star). Yui shows the strongest
  version: the system's love for the host can be its only power.
- Idle fit: the system "plays" by posting offers; the host replies later.
  Time passes in quest cycles, not seconds.
- Trap: system stories turn the system into a menu; players want to talk to
  a person. Keep it a character, with moods and limits.
- Gap: I found no strong "I am the system" protagonist with a large readership
  in English; the premise is common in Chinese web fiction but I only
  confirmed two titles by review (**stated**).

### Crossing

| Family | Best idea | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Combat | You cannot fight; you only shape the host's odds | Yui |
| Crafting | You issue recipes; the host crafts | Dragon Hatchling (voice offers) |
| Work and money | Your pay is the host's progress | Reborn as a System |
| Social | Ally with other systems | Sefirah Castle tarot club |
| Family | Adopt the host's child as the next host | Yui |
| Power path | Your own level rises when the host succeeds | North Star + Reborn as a System |
| Minigames | Pick rewards under budget | Dragon Hatchling |
| City | The castle space is your base | Sefirah |
| Exploration | You see the host's world as data | Yui |
| Research | Decode what the world's rules are | Dragon Hatchling |
| Collections | Collect hosts | Reborn as a System |
| Expeditions | Travel host to host | Reborn as a System |
| Crime/politics | Rival systems compete for the same host | inferred |
| Teaching | Tutorial design is the whole job | inferred |
| Challenge | Never lie to the host | inferred |

## 3. Egg or unhatched

### Sources

- **Reincarnated as a Dragon Hatchling** (Nekoko, Naji Yanagida; web novel
  2015, 17 light-novel volumes, anime 2026): reborn as an immobile egg that
  must level up to hatch (**stated**, publisher).
- **Hatching tropes in monster-evolution novels** (general).
- **Kafka-like inversion**: none; skipped.
- **Pokemon eggs, Dragon Quest monsters** (games; not fetched, **unverified**
  details).
- **Tsukumogami** (folklore): objects wake after about a hundred years
  (**stated**); an unhatched-but-waiting stage.
- **Hollow Knight's Black Egg Temple**: an egg-shaped seal containing the
  Infection (**shown**); the container as a body.

### Mechanics (Dragon Hatchling)

- He sees through a small hole; can only roll (**shown**). He gains skills by
  tumbling down a cliff (**shown**). Humans hunt eggs (**shown**).
- After killing a Dark Worm he gets the offer to evolve, which starts
  hatching; the Voice changes tone (**shown**). Then he eats the corpse
  (**shown**).
- Later: choice of five evolutions at max level (**shown**), a chosen path
  toward human form that is incomplete (**shown**).

### Learn

- The egg stage is a short, powerful tutorial: few verbs, high danger,
  one-shot decisions (roll or stay). A text game can make **each action a
  gamble with a single-line outcome** (**inferred**).
- Hatching is an irreversible choice; offer it only when the egg is ready.
- Trap: 17 volumes of leveling. Egg is an opening, not the game.
- Idle fit: "incubation" is a natural idle loop: gather warmth and wait.

### Crossing (brief)

Combat: roll and crack. Crafting: none; lines of fate. Social: only the parent
or the thief. Family: heirs are eggs too. Power: hatch into one of five
evolutions. Minigames: temperature balance. Exploration: what you see through
the hole. Crime/politics: egg smugglers. Challenge: never hatch.

## 4. Microbe or tiny life inside a bigger body

### Sources

- Cells at Work! and spin-offs (see section 1).
- Innerspace (section 1).
- Recursive Cultivation (Sufficient Velocity fan cosmology): a dantian is a
  qi store and a self-contained universe that can hold its own cultivators
  (**stated**, forum post; not a published novel).
- Xianxia Nascent Soul trope: a tiny infant figure sits in the dantian
  (**stated**, search result summary).
- Mushishi (see section 1).
- Gaia hypothesis (Lovelock, Margulis): life regulates its planet through
  feedback loops; Daisyworld model (**shown**).
- A Bug's Life: scale-down view of a colony under tribute (**shown**).

I could not find a confirmed published novel whose dantian holds a populated
world. **Unverified**; the idea is legitimate but my source is the forum
thread.

### Mechanics and lessons

- Cells at Work: each job is a short verb set (deliver, patrol, repair)
  (**shown**). Threats come from the host's life (what they eat) (**shown**).
- Recursive Cultivation: worlds nest; inner cultivators can open their own
  (**stated**).
- Daisyworld: two kinds of daisies stabilize temperature (**shown**), a
  model for a tiny two-species mini-sim (**inferred**).
- Idea: the host's day is the weather. Idle time is the host's life; you
  receive it as "today it rained (they cried)" (**inferred**).

### Crossing (brief)

Combat: immune battles. Crafting: build tissues. Work: cell jobs. Social:
gossip across organs. Family: division is reproduction. Power: host cultivates
and your world grows. City: a body as a city. Research: diagnose the host.
Crime/politics: cancer as secession. Challenge: keep a bad host alive.

## 5. Nation's fortune or luck

### Sources

- **Dragon Canon** (Chinese web novel, Wuxiaworld review): fortune can be
  measured, harvested, transferred, altered; fengshui matters (**stated**,
  review). The review does not describe the dynasty scale in detail.
- **Qi luck (qiyun) and "child of fortune" tropes** (general): fortune can be
  stolen or drained (**stated** in a search summary, not from a specific
  title I verified).
- **National-fortune (guoyun) web novels** (Qidian, Fanqie): nations are
  entered into a global contest by a system; a player's results change
  national score and citizens' bodies; stream audiences comment
  (**stated**, search summary of intros; no individual title verified).
  Examples of penalties reported: citizens' physique and lifespan can drop
  if the national score stays low (**stated**, one intro).
- **Gleam** and **Weaving Virtue** (Actus): luck as a power source
  (**stated**, listing).
- **Tale of Immortal** (game): destiny choices at start (**shown**); page
  does not cover luck as mechanic.
- **Mandate of Heaven** (historical concept; **inferred** link, not fetched).

### Mechanics

- Fortune is a resource that moves: gather, steal, spend, pass down.
- National scale: the individual's act changes the country's score, and
  the country's score changes everyone's body. The player becomes a
  champion.

### Learn

- A strong fit for generational play: heirs inherit family luck; dynasties
  hold luck; a bad ruler drains it for everyone.
- Trap: guoyun web novels collapse into nationalist power fantasy and
  contest scoring (**inferred** from the intro pattern). Take the mechanic
  (shared score that changes bodies), drop the framing.
- Idle fit: luck is a slow pool that fills over idle time and drains on
  big events.

### Crossing (brief)

Combat: spend luck to dodge. Crafting: auspicious days. Money: luck as
currency. Social: luck is contagious. Family: marry for luck. Power: luck
rank. Crime: luck theft. Politics: luck is the budget of the state.
Challenge: no-luck run. Research: fengshui survey.

## 6. Sect array spirit or institution guardian

### Sources

- **The Great Ruler**, **Desolate Devouring Art**, **Lingxian Sect**:
  sect guardian arrays as plot objects, not characters (**stated**, search
  results; none has a sentient array protagonist).
- **Tsukumogami** (folklore): an old object gains a spirit (**stated**).
- **Princess Mononoke**: the Forest Spirit is a place's god (**shown**).
- **Sefirah Castle**: a structure with a social function (**stated**).
- **TARDIS**: a building you ride (**shown**).
- Chinese terms to search next: 阵灵, 护山大阵 (not yet searched).

I did **not** find an array-spirit protagonist. This is an under-served
body. **Unverified** beyond that.

### Learn

- The institution is the body: members are limbs, the sect hall is the face.
- Needs: spirit stone feed, not being abandoned.
- Channel: a few signals (lights, warnings, a dream).
- Ending: sect falls, you outlive it as ruin, or you leave with the last
  disciple.
- Idle fit: very strong; the spirit watches generations pass.

### Crossing (brief)

Combat: defend only. Crafting: refine yourself. Work: house the sect.
Social: choose a favorite disciple. Family: lineage is your children.
Power: formation tiers. City: the mountain is the map. Research: find
your own flaws. Crime/politics: sect war, betrayal. Teaching: you teach
by testing intruders. Challenge: never harm a disciple.

## 7. Tiny and huge scales

### Sources

- **A Bug's Life** (Pixar): ants under tribute; trash heap as city; birds
  as the outside threat (**shown**).
- **Hollow Knight**: an insect kingdom, soul as resource, charms limited by
  notches, shade mechanic on death (**shown**).
- **The Metamorphosis** (Kafka): see section 1.
- **Princess Mononoke**: a forest god of life and death, who becomes a giant
  at night; its death renews the land (**shown**).
- **Solaris** (Lem): an ocean mind that returns visitors from memory; all
  attempts to understand it fail (**shown**).
- **Star Maker** (Stapledon): stars and nebulae think on vast timescales;
  minds link into planetary, galactic, cosmic ones; the Star Maker judges
  its worlds as art (**shown**).
- **Ego the Living Planet** (Marvel): reshapes into a face, grows by
  consuming worlds, acts through avatars (**shown**).
- **Gaia hypothesis**: see section 4.
- **Tsukumogami**: smallest scale, household objects.

### Mechanics

- **Tiny.** The world is the same; threats scale up (birds, feet). Bug's Life
  tribute is a recurring tax. Hollow Knight tiny-body play is a full action
  game with a death penalty that costs lost currency.
- **Huge.** Time stretches. Ego uses avatars to talk and consume to grow
  (**shown**). Solaris has no communication success (**shown**). Star Maker
  scales the narrative by covering more with less detail (**shown**).
- Princess Mononoke's god dies to renew the land: an ending where the body
  is spent for the world.

### Learn

- Huge bodies need a **channel**: avatars (Ego), visitors (Solaris), dreams.
- Detail falls as scale rises (Star Maker); mirror that: fewer, larger
  events, longer ticks.
- Failure mode: Solaris-style incomprehension is bad UX unless the player
  can still make progress. Give partial, wrong understanding.
- Tiny: do not make all enemies "big animals"; rain, a sweeping broom, and
  the host's mood can be dangers.
- Idle fit: the larger the body, the longer each tick, the more offline
  play is natural.

### Crossing (brief)

Combat: Hollow Knight-style shade recovery. Crafting: nest building.
Work/money: tribute and tax. Social: swarm as family. Family: egg clutches.
Power: Ego's growth by eating. City: hive or mountain town. Exploration:
climbing a leaf. Research: reading a god's moods. Collections: weather
types. Expeditions: one season. Crime/politics: protection racket.
Teaching: villagers learn your habits. Challenge: never leave the hill.

## 8. Adjacent bodies and twists our lists miss

- **Reverse symbiont.** You are the host and the guest is a player-like
  other (Kafka, The Host).
- **Shared body with turns.** Alternate control by day and night.
- **Voice that betrays.** The Dragon Hatchling Voice is the antagonist.
- **Bonded vessel.** Hollow Knight's Vessels: a body made to hold something.
- **Location god / forest spirit** (Mononoke) as separate from "dungeon".
- **Idea-body**: an ocean that cannot be understood (Solaris).
- **Visitor-maker**: a body whose power is producing people from memory.
- **Avatar economy**: a huge body speaks through small bodies (Ego).
- **Object waking by age** (tsukumogami): a built-in timer.
- **Nested worlds**: dantian inside dantian.
- **Contest-scored nation**: you are one of several national players.

## The 5 strongest ideas for this cluster

1. **Shared skull with drift** (Parasyte, The Host): a symbiont and host
   each propose actions; loyalty slowly shifts toward the host. Ending
   options include separating, merging, or sacrificing.
2. **System with its own agenda** (Dragon Hatchling's Voice, Yui):
   the system plays as a character with moods, secrets, and a plan.
3. **Fortune as a moving pool** (Dragon Canon, guoyun novels): luck for
   family, sect, and nation; measured, spent, stolen. Ideal for heirs.
4. **Institution spirit** (sect array, tsukumogami): the cleanest idle
   body found; the spirit watches generations and has few verbs.
5. **Scale sets the tick** (Star Maker, Ego, Bug's Life): the bigger the
   body, the longer the tick and the more it needs avatars.

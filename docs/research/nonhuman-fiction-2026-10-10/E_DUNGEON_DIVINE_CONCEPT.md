## 1.2 What each source does mechanically

**Dungeon Born (stated by summaries; mechanics recalled).**
- Verbs: build rooms and floors, create monsters and traps, absorb the dead (stated: Cal grows by defeating adventurers and eating those who enter). Bond with a partner (stated: later books describe cores as unstable alone; unbonded cores slip toward madness, per a summary of Dungeon Madness).
- Senses: sees everything inside its own walls (recalled; inferred from genre). Blind outside.
- Clock: slow idle growth between delver waves (recalled). Per-action time is whatever you spend on a build.
- Needs: mana from ambient sources and from kills (recalled). Visitors. Company (stated via the bonding plot).
- Channel: loot as bait, monsters as speech, later a human partner as voice. (inferred)
- Ending: destruction of the core, or ascension. Sources disagree on series length (five vs ten books), so I cannot say what the end state is.
- Terminology varies between "core" and "heart" across sources (stated).

**Dungeon Keeper (stated by game databases).**
- Imps dig, claim land and mine gold. Gold pays for rooms, traps, doors and creature wages. In DK2 spells moved to a separate mana resource (stated).
- Creatures need a lair to rest, a hatchery for food, and wages, and they leave if neglected (stated: they seek food, wages and rest on their own).
- Defeated enemies can become skeletons (prison), converts (torture chamber) or vampires (graveyard) (stated). That is a long-term growth loop built from your own victims.
- The Dungeon Heart is the life force; losing it loses the game (stated).
- Lesson: needs of the minions, not of the keeper, drive most decisions.

**Legend of Keepers (stated by reviews).**
- Game is split into a planning phase (read adventurer stats and resistances, place traps and monsters) and a run phase where heroes walk the layout. Time is counted in weeks, with a target number of weeks to survive. Three Masters each carry different starting troops and traps. Some bonuses persist between runs (stated).
- Reviewers called the loop samey and the management thin (TechRaptor), with fights that look alike. Lesson: a pure wave defense loop gets repetitive without new variables.

**Invisible Cities (stated by summaries).**
- No plot. Cities are grouped by themes (memory, desire and so on), five per theme, and the frame is a skeptical emperor who relies on messengers' reports. The twist is that the cities may be one city seen through different themes (stated).
- Mechanical reading (inferred): a place can be described by a theme, and the description changes how you treat it.

**Tudigong and Chenghuang (stated by Britannica and Wikipedia summaries).**
- Jurisdiction is tiny and local: a bridge, street, field or home (stated). A village's earth god is separate from the next village's.
- Many began as real people who helped their community, and the community hopes the deified figure continues (stated).
- Gods are portrayed as bureaucrats, and if misfortune hits a locality the earth god can be judged to have lost interest and replaced (stated).
- The City God is above earth gods; by statute each administrative seat had a City God temple, and the post was sometimes filled by a deceased local official on a three-year term (stated). The City God tracks births, deaths, and merit and sin in the district (stated).
- This is the single best template for a place that is also a career: you start as a lane god and can be promoted or fired.

**Kami-sama Kiss (stated by wiki and tropes summaries).**
- Nanami becomes land god by inheriting a shrine and a "kiss" from the previous god. Tomoe becomes her familiar by sealed contract and the contract can be undone. The shrine has small spirits who serve at once. Land gods are invited to an annual gathering at Izumo, with a test (stated).
- Mechanical reading (inferred): a place-body's staff are spirits who come with the shrine, and a fox familiar is a bond that can break.

**Tsukumogami (stated by Wikipedia and yokai.jp).**
- Tools gain a spirit around 99 or 100 years; the number is arbitrary. Mistreated tools return with resentment; some rituals console broken tools (stated).
- Mechanical reading (inferred): the place or object ages into awareness, and past treatment is the stat that sets its temperament.

## 1.3 What to learn for a text idle life-sim

**Steal:**
1. Minion needs as the main loop (Dungeon Keeper). A text version: each resident has a need and a grievance line; neglect makes them leave.
2. Converting your victims into growth (graveyard, prison, torture in DK). Every "failure" of a visitor feeds a branch.
3. Jurisdiction as a stat (tudigong). The god of one bridge is a complete life with small stakes and a visible promotion ladder.
4. Description changes the place (Calvino). The player can reframe the same dungeon under a theme (memory, trade, desire) and visitors respond differently. Cheap in text.
5. Persistent run bonuses (Legend of Keepers): fits our "next life" structure.

**What goes wrong in the sources:**
- Waves of the same fight get boring (Legend of Keepers reviews).
- Core stories tend to become power creep and monster collecting (recalled; genre-wide), with the core quickly gaining a human avatar, which is the "body becomes a human in disguise too fast" failure. Counter: keep the core immobile and make the avatar a single scarce event.
- The dungeon-core genre centers on the owner's strategy; the visitors are targets. Calvino and the folklore sources show a richer option: the place is a character that people remember differently.

**Idle and offline fit:**
- Natural: the place's time passes while nobody visits. Offline progress is the growth of floors, decay of unused rooms, and what visitors did while you "slept". (inferred)
- Ledger of visitors during idle time: a short generated list (who came, what they took, what they said).

## 2.2 What each source does mechanically

**Small Gods (stated by Wikipedia and reviews).**
- Power is proportional to the number of believers (stated). Small gods exist as "points of desire" searching for believers (stated). Om is down to one believer and cannot summon a thunderbolt bigger than static (stated).
- He recovers when a crowd witnesses a miracle (stated). Brutha proposes a constitutional religion where Om obeys the commandments and answers some prayers in exchange for steady belief (stated).
- Lesson (inferred): belief has two forms, count of believers and kind of belief. A god with a crowd who believe by fear is different from one with a few who believe by trust. The book takes the second seriously.

**Hogfather (stated).**
- Belief creates entities, even minor ones. Susan sees things children believe in; when wizards mention an imaginary creature it appears, which she reads as a buildup of belief (stated). The Tooth Fairy's domain is powered by children's belief (stated). Death covers the Hogfather's role (stated).
- Lesson (inferred): the god's real job is a function in a calendar (a night, a ritual). Missing it has costs.

**American Gods (stated by reviews).**
- Gods are created by belief, sacrifice and faith, and fade when forgotten (stated). Old gods arrived with immigrants and faded as the people assimilated (stated). New gods (Technical Boy, Media) feed on attention and clicks; worship is a commodity (stated). The new gods can destroy old gods by withdrawing belief (stated: Bilquis example).
- Lesson (inferred): different eras have different worship channels (kneeling, offerings, song vs attention). The "channel" slot changes with the world's tech.

**Noragami (stated by Wikipedia and TV Tropes).**
- Gods depend on prayer and offerings and fade if forgotten (stated). Yato has no shrine and takes odd jobs for a five-yen fee, and each job doubles as recruiting (stated).
- Regalia are spirits of the dead who keep faint memories and serve as weapons; a regalia can evolve into a stronger form (Yukine becomes a Blessed Vessel) (stated). Regalia carry the god's burdens and take blame (stated).
- Gods with shrines are reborn rather than die; humans generally cannot perceive gods (stated).
- Lesson: shrine as a death save, hands-on small jobs as the early loop, a loyal-or-lost staff as the second stat.

**Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint (stated by forum and wiki summaries; "fan understanding, not canon").**
- Constellations are beings from history, myth or fiction who gain power from being widely remembered (stated). They watch and give coins to chosen incarnations, and some join to keep their legend alive (stated). They can also spend "probability," which costs them more than coins (stated).
- Sponsor choice is a decision point: the protagonist turns sponsors down because he expects constraint, and later becomes a constellation himself (stated).
- Lesson (inferred): a god-body can be a patron relationship. The sponsor's whims are the pressure on a human host.

**Black & White (stated by wiki and review sources).**
- Followers produce prayer energy; mana is gained from temple and altar, and sacrifices drop onto the altar (stated). Power exists only within your area of influence, which is set by believers and town locations; the boundary is drawn on the world and belief visibly drains back to worshippers when your hand leaves it (stated).
- Your creature starts as a normal animal and learns from how you treat it (stated). A morality system changes the look of your territory (stated).
- Critics: repetitive gameplay, tedious quest system and crashes (stated). Lesson: the creature and the good/evil visual are the memorable parts, the loop around them was not.

**Populous (stated by wiki and reviews).**
- Original: more followers give more mana; bigger calamities cost more. The Shaman appears in The Beginning, where mana comes from villagers in huts and spells have limited range (stated).

**Tudigong / City God (stated).**
- See 1.2. In this cluster, the key point is that gods are an appointed, replaceable administration with a performance record.

**Lord of the Mysteries (stated by wiki; fan-made game material excluded).**
- Beyonders start at Sequence 9 by drinking a potion tied to a pathway, and rise to Sequence 0 (god level); sequences group into low, middle and high tiers (stated).
- The acting method means playing out the role the potion's name suggests so the original imprint is gradually digested; full digestion avoids losing control on the next step; a higher-Sequence watcher aids digestion (stated).
- At the top levels, full digestion is dangerous and the Beyonder should control rather than fully digest, because it risks losing the self (stated).
- Not verified: how a "rumor" pathway works in the novel. I have no source this session, so no rumor-pathway claims are made here.
- Lesson (inferred): godhood is a role you act until it becomes you, with an identity-loss risk at the top. This is the best existing model for "ascension has a cost to the self."

**The Sandman (stated by wiki and explainers).**
- Seven siblings, each ruling a realm: Destiny, Death, Dream, Destruction, Desire, Despair, Delirium. They contact each other with sigils; Death alone moves freely through others' realms (stated by one source). Dream's realm is the Dreaming (stated).
- Sources disagree slightly on the roster and whether they "are" the ideas or "represent" them (stated).
- Lesson (inferred): a god-of-a-concept body has family politics.

## 2.3 What to learn for a text idle life-sim

**Steal:**
1. Belief has quantity and quality (Small Gods): one devoted believer vs a crowd under threat.
2. Shrine as save point and rebirth (Noragami): lose a body, come back as a child god if the shrine holds. Fits the next-life structure directly.
3. Territory of influence drawn visibly (Black & White): a map overlay of where you can act.
4. Small jobs as early loop (Noragami), then recognition, then a shrine.
5. Era-specific worship channels (American Gods): offerings, song, clicks, attention.
6. Sponsor and coins (ORV): an in-world patron economy where gods spend scarcer "probability."
7. Digestion/acting (Lord of the Mysteries): ascension through playing a role, with a risk at the top.
8. Promotion and demotion by performance record (Chenghuang, Tudigong).

**What goes wrong:**
- Belief-as-single-number is easy to grind. Black & White's loop drew repetition complaints (stated).
- Gods who become all-powerful stop being interesting; Om recovers in one miracle, and the book then immediately trades power for a constitution (stated), which is the part to copy.
- Divine bodies tend to treat humans as props. A life-sim should keep one human relationship that can say no.
- Constellation systems (ORV) drift into audience-and-spectacle spectacle (inferred), with the god barely acting.

**Idle and offline fit:**
- Natural: prayers and offerings arrive while idle; the god answers a batch each session. Unanswered prayers are decay (inferred).
- Shrine upkeep and festival calendars give time structure (Hogfather's one-night duty).
- Time scale is wide: a god's action can be a decade; hours can be a single prayer.

## 3.2 What each source does mechanically

**Plague Inc. (stated by game wiki).**
- Currency: DNA points, gathered by clicking bubbles on the map and passively when infection/death is high (stated).
- Three trait trees: Transmission, Symptoms, Abilities. Each trait evolved raises the cost of others; symptoms raise infectivity, severity or lethality but also raise detection (stated). Genetic drift raises symptom costs as infection grows on hard mode (stated).
- Cure side: funds accrue as infected and dead are recorded; cure mode uses funds the same way as DNA (stated). Details on cure thresholds not verified this session.
- Ending: complete extinction, or being cured. (recalled)
- Lesson (inferred): the opposing force is a clock the player's own success winds up. Visible success raises the world's response.

**There Is No Antimemetics Division (stated by publisher copy and reviews).**
- An antimeme resists being perceived or remembered: record attempts fail, descriptions come out unreadable, and the observer forgets they were looking (stated). Some feed on memories and skills (stated). One entity (U-3125) is lethal to anyone who fully conceptualizes it, and spreads through shared knowledge (stated). Staff use mnestic drugs to keep awareness, at physical and cognitive cost (stated). The director repeatedly relearns the threat and erases personal memories to protect others (stated).
- Mechanical reading (inferred): a concept-body that dies when known. Its channel is absence; its growth is staying unremembered.

**Kuchisake-onna rumor (stated, documented folklore reporting).**
- Started December 1978 in Gifu; the earliest print record date is disputed (January 22 vs 26, 1979); a national magazine feature on June 29; a copycat arrested; patrol cars sent; group school dismissals; the panic ended when summer vacation began in August (stated). A folklorist points to cram schools as the transmission medium (stated).
- Lesson (inferred): a rumor's lifespan is set by its carriers' schedule and its channel. Very good model for a concept body with real mechanics: carrier network, season, institutional response.

**From Dust (stated by reviews and wiki).**
- You are an unseen spirit moving terrain by picking up and dropping sand, water and lava; settling five tribespeople at a totem makes a village and grants a power, powers have timers and cooldowns; shamans warn of disasters (stated). Critics praised the physics and faulted AI pathing (stated), and one reviewer said it drifts from god sim to puzzle (stated).
- Lesson: a force-body that has no army can still be playable through terrain and warnings.

**The Laundry Files (stated).**
- Magic is mathematics; solving the right theorem may draw attention from entities elsewhere in the multiverse (stated by Stross). Practice is dangerous and costs the mind. Rising computation is bringing the threat closer (stated by reviewers). Bureaucratic satire is central (stated).

**Cultist Simulator (stated).**
- Card placement into timed verbs: Explore, Study, Work, Dream. The same card does different things in different verbs. Many cards decay on timers; ignored Restlessness curdles into Dread. Aspects like Knock, Lantern, Forge are the occult principles; lore of one aspect can upgrade another by a fixed ring (stated by a fan-written chain, not official) (stated).
- Book of Hours and the "Hours" were poorly covered by searches; no claims about Hours made here.

## 3.3 What to learn for a text idle life-sim

**Steal:**
1. Pressure grows with your success (Plague Inc.): detection and cure scale with how obvious you are.
2. Cost escalation per trait (Plague Inc.): every power makes the next one pricier, which keeps the tree from being a free-for-all.
3. Carrier and season (rumor 1979): the rumor dies when the school term ends. Natural weekly rhythm for idle play.
4. Antimemetic inversion: the best score is to not be recorded. A concept body that wins by being forgotten is a real challenge run.
5. Same card, different verb (Cultist Simulator): one object means different things in different actions.
6. Warnings before disasters (From Dust): a clear telegraph keeps a force-body fair.

**What goes wrong:**
- Concept-bodies have no relationships unless the design forces them in. Rumors and diseases can become pure numbers. Counter: always attach a named carrier.
- Plague Inc. style optimization collapses to a single best path (inferred; not verified); it uses unlimited world resetting.
- Antimemetics are hard to show to the player: a body nobody remembers is hard to render in text and in a save (inferred).
- Cultist Simulator's ticking clock is stressful; fine for a short run, hostile for idle (inferred).

**Idle and offline fit:**
- Spread simulates well offline, as a stepped model. When the player returns, a report: where it reached, who resisted, what the response was.
- Seasonal terms and calendars cap offline gain naturally (summer vacation ends a rumor).

# Part 4. Adjacent bodies and twists our lists miss

1. **Local official god (Tudigong rank).** A career body inside the divine bureaucracy: lane, bridge, village, city. Promotion, demotion, three-year terms (stated for City God).
2. **Shrine plus staff.** A body that is a place plus regalia/familiars (Noragami, Kami-sama Kiss). A mixed body of place and god.
3. **Sponsor/patron.** Mode D "system" overlaps the ORV constellation: a god who buys a mortal's actions with coins.
4. **Recorded or unrecorded.** An antimemetic body, defined by not being written down.
5. **Rumor with a school term.** A concept body with a hard seasonal lifespan.
6. **Tsukumogami.** An object-to-spirit body that matures with age and treatment. Overlaps Object and Spirit clusters.
7. **Family of domains.** Sandman-style siblings: you start as a younger concept in a family.
8. **Role-digestion body.** Lord of the Mysteries style: any start can add a potion-and-acting ladder that turns a role into a body.
9. **Terrain-spirit.** From Dust's Breath: a force with no army, only landscape.
10. **Era-shifted god.** The same god in a different era finds a different worship channel (American Gods).
11. **Appointed ghost official.** A dead local official fills a post for a term (City God practice, stated).
12. **Shared calendar duty.** A being that exists to perform one night's work (Hogfather).

Contradictions with our current list: the north star table says Concept or force is "rare, late-game, outlier." The sources suggest it can be a full early start if it has a carrier network and a season (1979 rumor), and that divine bodies work best as local jobs, not as universal gods.

# The 5 strongest ideas for this cluster

1. **Jurisdiction ladder.** Start as the god of one bridge (tudigong), rise to village, district and City God by merit record; get demoted for neglect. Grounded in Chenghuang practice (stated) and gives a concrete, readable career.
2. **Belief with two axes.** Count and kind (Small Gods): a few trusting believers vs a frightened crowd. It prevents "belief" from being one grindable number. Plus the Black & White visible influence overlay.
3. **Shrine as save point.** Noragami: a god with a shrine is reborn as a child; one without fades. Directly fits our next-life structure and makes "build a shrine" the first big goal.
4. **Success wakes the response.** Plague Inc.: detection and cure rise as you succeed. Applies to dungeons (guild notices), gods (rival pantheon) and rumors (authority).
5. **Rumor with a carrier and a season.** The 1979 rumor is documented and gives a believable rhythm: spreads through a channel, triggers real institutions, dies with the school term. A concept body that is actually playable.

Honorable: acting/digestion ascension (Lord of the Mysteries) as the cost-to-self at the top of any divine path.

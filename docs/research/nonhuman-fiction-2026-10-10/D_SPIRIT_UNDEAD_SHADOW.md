# Cluster D: spirit or ghost, undead, shadow or reflection starts

Research date 2026-10-10. Brief: `BRIEF.md` in this folder.

## How to read the labels

- **shown**: the work itself does this (checked on a plot or mechanics summary this session).
- **stated**: an author, wiki, review or reference article says so.
- **inferred**: my reading or design extrapolation.
- **recalled**: I know this from memory of the work and could not re-check it this session (fandom wikis returned HTTP 402, some Wikipedia pages did not cover the point). Treat as unverified until someone checks.

Six slots per body: verbs, senses, clock, needs, channel, ending.

Source quality warning: web searches for Shadow Slave and for web-novel skeleton titles returned mostly scraped spam wikis. I used only the Roliascan chapter text excerpts and Wuxiaworld/MyAnimeList listings, and flagged the rest.

### 1.2 What each source does mechanically

**Ghost Trick (S1).**
- Verbs: shown, the ghost cannot move in the real world but can possess objects and use a "trick" on them (shown: moving a doughnut cart so a character chases it and opens a path). Phones let him trace a call and travel to the other end (shown).
- Senses: shown, a Ghost World view where time stops and objects glow as cores you jump between.
- Clock: shown, the key trick is returning to four minutes before a death. If you fail you rewind to the start of that segment; if you delay the death you save a checkpoint. The whole game covers one night and the ghost vanishes at dawn (stated by in-game character, shown).
- Needs: none in the survival sense. Pressure is time and reach. Range is limited (shown).
- Channel: you act only through physical set pieces and by steering living people with objects. No speech with the living at first (shown).
- Growth and ending: no stat growth. Memory is the progression: you learn who you were (a cat killed by a stray bullet, shown) and the ending rewrites the timeline.
- Inferred: the best part is that a tiny verb set (possess, trick, rewind) becomes a rich puzzle. It is a constraint-first body.

**Spiritfarer (S2).**
- Verbs: shown, you build and run a boat, farm and cook, gather resources via minigames tied to each spirit's personality, and complete spirit requests.
- Needs: shown, the passengers have moods and are kept content by feeding them what they request and doing their tasks. The player is the one who tends.
- Channel: you are the carer, not the haunter. Relationships with each spirit run through chores and conversations.
- Ending: shown, each spirit leaves through the Everdoor with a keepsake flower; the game's ending reveals the protagonist is a dying palliative nurse who then crosses herself.
- Reviewer note (stated): critics praised the emotional depth; one thought the vague afterlife blurred intentions.
- Inferred: the loop is a cozy management game whose "win" for each relationship is losing it. That is a rare ending shape for a sim.

**Lincoln in the Bardo (S3).**
- Shown/stated: ghosts do not admit they are dead (they call coffins "sick-boxes" and the graveyard a "hospital-yard"). They are twisted by desires left unacted on. Their penance is being unable to affect the living. The novel runs about one night and uses a chorus of voices, some real sources, some invented.
- Stated: the threat is permanent entrapment in the in-between.
- Inferred: denial is a mechanic. A ghost who will not accept death keeps its grip longer; acceptance is both the exit and the end of the run.

**Bardo teachings (S4).**
- Stated (Wikipedia on bardo): three stages. Dying bardo with the "clear light of death." Reality or luminosity bardo with visions and sounds. Becoming bardo, where a mental body forms and karmic seeds pick the next existence. Maximum 49 days in several traditions, though others differ.
- Stated: those who do not recognize the luminous reality are usually deluded, and the visions slide from clear to terrifying hallucinations drawn from one's own past acts. The article describes awareness, not explicit choices.
- Inferred: this is a ready-made three-act clock for a ghost run with the content generated from your own history.

**Hungry ghosts and Ghost Festival (S5).**
- Stated: craving beings (egui, preta) from greed or stinginess, violent deaths, or neglect by descendants. The festival is the seventh lunar month, when the gates open. Families set out food, burn paper money and paper houses, leave the front row empty at night shows, and avoid eating offerings. Buddhist rites (Mengshan Shishi, Yujia Yankou) feed the ghosts and give them teachings; merit is dedicated onward.
- Stated by this article: it does not use "needle throat." The long thin neck is described as a result of never being fed by family. (Recalled: the needle-throat image is common in Buddhist sources, not verified here.)
- Inferred: here the ghost's economy is entirely other people's offerings. A ghost's wealth is the living's remembering.

**Yurei and Obon (S6).**
- Stated: a soul waits for funeral rites and then joins ancestors, who return each August at Obon. Sudden or violent death, missing rites, or strong emotion (revenge, jealousy, love, sorrow) make a yurei. Types: onryo (vengeful), ubume (a mother returning to her children), goryo (noble wronged), funayurei (sea dead), zashiki-warashi (house child spirits that bring luck), jibakurei (bound to a place, such as Okiku at Himeji Castle), ikiryo (living person's spirit leaving the body in rage).
- Stated: active around 2:00 to 2:30 a.m.; exits via fulfillment (revenge, love, burial, rites), deification of vengeful spirits to calm them, or ofuda talismans on foreheads and doors.
- Inferred: ikiryo is a very good "living ghost" twist (see 1.5).

**Hikaru no Go, Sai (S7).**
- Stated: only Hikaru can see Sai. Sai lives in part of Hikaru's mind as a separate personality, which can be uncomfortable. Sai pushed him into Go, dictates moves, and wants the "Divine Move." He last played in the late Edo period through Honinbo Shusaku.
- The Wikipedia page did not say how Sai leaves; (recalled: he disappears partway through the series after Hikaru plays on without him, a famous bittersweet beat, not verified here).
- Inferred: the best use of the pair is that the ghost is a teacher who acts through the host. The ghost's growth is shown by the host learning to stand alone.

**Mob Psycho 100, Dimple (S8).**
- Stated (Wikipedia character list): Dimple can unleash the full potential of any body he possesses, can hypnotize or influence crowds, but cannot possess powerful psychics without permission and cannot fully control a psychic's body. He founded a cult with a possessed man to become a god. He was captured in a cursed jar and ate the spirits inside, which undid a rival. Mob beat him, which stripped most of his spiritual abilities, and he stuck around, first planning to take Mob's body, later caring for him, and joined Reigen's firm "Spirits and Such."
- Inferred: a weak ghost whose real asset is a patron. The comic engine is a petty entity being humbled into friendship.

**The Sixth Sense (S9).**
- Shown: ghosts have unfinished business. Cole says the dead do not realize it and see only what they want to see. Only Cole sees them. The twist is that the therapist (Malcolm) was shot and is himself a ghost. He leaves once he tells his wife what he needed to say.
- Inferred: "not knowing you are dead" is one of the best reveal structures for a text game because the player reads the same log twice.

**Beetlejuice (S10).**
- Shown/stated: dead couple consult a handbook, a caseworker handles their file, they must stay in the house 125 years, leaving the house sends them to limbo (a strange Titan sandscape), ghosts are mostly invisible to the living, can possess (a dinner party), have no reflections, and are overpowered by stronger supernatural beings. A botched ritual makes them age and decay. Betelgeuse is summoned by saying his name three times.
- Inferred: an afterlife bureaucracy gives a clean source of quests, forms, queues and a plausible idle waiting room.

**The Lovely Bones (S11).**
- Shown: Susie watches from a personal heaven, cannot act directly, and her effect is brief (she briefly enters Ruth's body). A girl, Ruth, senses her and becomes fixated. Over time she settles into a wider heaven and watches less.
- Inferred: the sim form is a mostly passive body whose "play" is choosing whom to watch, with a gradual fade as the right ending.

**Wraith: The Oblivion (S12).**
- Stated (Wikipedia): Fetters bind a wraith to the living world (objects, places, people). Passions are unresolved emotions that generate Pathos, the energy that powers abilities (Arcanoi), resists the Shadow and heals Corpus. The Shadow is a secondary personality, often played by another player, trying to destroy the wraith. Oblivion devours souls and Spectres serve it. Hierarchy rules the Empire of Stygia and Renegades and Heretics oppose it. Shadowlands is a darkened reflection of the living world.
- Not covered by the page I read: Thorns, Shadowguides, Skinlands (I did not verify).
- Inferred: the best-designed "ghost needs" system in the cluster. Fetters (anchors) plus Passions (feeling-as-fuel) plus a Shadow (a rival self that wants you to stop) gives needs, risk and an ending in one trio.

**Bleach (S13).**
- Stated: souls are sent to Soul Society by Soul Reapers; Hollows are lost souls who were not sent in time, losing sense of being and craving human souls, each with a unique mask. Arrancar are Hollows that gained humanoid form and Soul Reaper powers. Recalled but not verified: Menos, Adjuchas and Vasto Lorde as an evolution ladder, and the chain of fate that links a soul to its body.
- Inferred: a clear "unattended ghost decays into a monster" clock.

**Danny Phantom (S14).**
- Stated: flight, invisibility, intangibility, "overshadowing" (possession), Ghost Ray and Ghostly Wail. Ghost Zone is a parallel dimension. Half-ghost, hair goes white and eyes green on transformation. Friends Sam and Tucker, later Jazz, help hide the double life.
- Inferred: a dual-state body (human by day, ghost on demand) is a good alternate to the pure ghost start.

**Liaozhai (S15).**
- Stated: the stories blur supernatural and ordinary life and focus on qing, entanglement of feeling, whether the partner is human or supernatural. Titles include "Nie Xiaoqian" and "The Painted Skin." The article did not describe specific bargain rules (substitute reincarnation and similar). I know these from memory only: **recalled** that ghost-lover stories often have a scholar, a lonely ghost, a rule about the ghost's remains, and a cost.
- Inferred: the romance channel for a ghost can be a literal reburial or a body to be re-housed.

### 1.3 What to learn for a text idle life-sim

Steal-worthy:
1. **Anchors as the central need** (Wraith Fetters, yurei place binding, Beetlejuice house limit). A ghost's world is a short list of Fetters; each one is a person, place or object with its own state. This is easy to render in text and gives idle time a clear job (maintain, visit, haunt).
2. **Feeling as fuel** (Wraith Passions to Pathos, Sixth Sense unfinished business, yurei emotion). Gain energy by caring about something; lose it by resolving it. The tension: finishing your business ends your run.
3. **Rewind or time window** (Ghost Trick). A bounded segment replayed until solved fits an idle game as an "attempt": send the ghost back and let it retry within a fixed window.
4. **The other side feeds you** (hungry ghosts, Ghost Festival). Offerings from descendants are the economy. This pairs well with a family and heirs system: your descendants literally pay your rent.
5. **A three-stage clock** (bardo). Act one: clear view; act two: visions from your own record; act three: pulled toward the next form.
6. **Release as the win** (Spiritfarer, Lovely Bones, Sixth Sense). The game can reward fade-out instead of power.
7. **Possession with a permission rule** (Dimple, Danny, Sai). Hosts with willpower resist; the weakest hosts are easiest. This is a natural difficulty curve.

What goes wrong in these works:
- Ghost Trick's verb set is so tight that it only works in a scripted puzzle format. Inferred: a generic idle loop needs a wider verb set and would lose the cleverness.
- Possession stories tend to collapse into "ghost in a human body" quickly (Danny Phantom, Wei Wuxian reborn in another body, shown/stated). The body becomes a human in disguise too fast. Mitigation: keep the host's will, memories and objections real, as Sai and Dimple do.
- Lincoln in the Bardo and Lovely Bones are static witness stories; the cost is low agency. Inferred: a text sim has to give small, real influence (whisper, nudge a dream, move an object) or it is just reading.
- Bleach-style "weak souls become monsters" can look like a death clock that punishes idling. Offline time needs a gentle version (see below).

Idle and offline time:
- Inferred: ghost time is a natural fit. While the player is away the ghost "sleeps" in an anchor: Fetter health drifts, and returning shows a short tally ("the garden dried out; your sister left flowers; the lease ended"). The cost of absence is that the world moves, not that you die.
- Hungry-ghost economy is passive income driven by the living's rituals; seasonal festival windows (seventh month) create real-calendar or in-game-calendar spikes.
- Haunt hours: yurei at about 2 a.m. (stated) is a natural per-day action window.

### 1.5 Adjacent bodies and twists the sources suggest

- **Ikiryo** (a living person's spirit that leaves in rage, stated): start as a living body whose spirit slips out when feeling spikes.
- **Ubume** (a mother who returns to care for her children, stated): a ghost with one immovable job.
- **Zashiki-warashi** (house spirit that brings luck, stated): a household luck spirit whose presence is a stat for a family.
- **Spirit ferryman** (Spiritfarer): the guide who tends the dying. A carer start, not a haunter.
- **Spirit caseworker** (Beetlejuice): a bureaucrat of the afterlife.
- **Spirit medium or exorcist** (Reigen, shown/stated): a human start who sells ghost services, a hybrid.
- **Half-ghost** (Danny Phantom): dual-state body.
- **Sai-style passenger** (Hikaru no Go): the ghost lives in another player-character's head. Mirror: your life as a ghost who has a host who also plays.
- **Hungry ghost with a descendant economy**: a mechanically distinct world where nobody can kill you but nobody feeds you either.
- **Ghost who does not know it is dead**: a reveal start where verbs are revealed to be different.

### 2.2 What each source does mechanically

**Overlord (U1).**
- Shown/stated (Wikipedia): the world is a former MMORPG, the lich is stuck in the avatar, cannot log out, and his base (Nazarick) and its NPCs became living. The page says his game mechanics gave him "behavior modifications" that left him with no moral qualms about killing. It did not mention emotional suppression, sleep or food. (Recalled, not verified here: his avatar suppresses strong emotion and he does not need to eat or sleep; the NPCs reinterpret his offhand remarks as master plans.)
- Verbs: spells, items, summoning, delegating to NPCs.
- Channel: through NPCs and a persona, not a body.
- Inferred: the undead body here is a role: a leader who must act the part. The comic and dramatic engine is the gap between his real feelings and what his minions believe.

**Skeleton Knight in Another World (U2).**
- Stated (Wikipedia): superhuman strength; Holy magic despite being undead; cover story is that he seeks a cure for a "curse"; healing, Rejuvenation (resurrection), teleportation, Rock magic, Holy Shield, summons (Ifrit, later Aion). Armor is nearly invulnerable. The helmet never comes off; taking it off is a deliberate step for trusted people. A spirit fox, Ponta, helps others trust him. A magic pool might cure him; it restored flesh temporarily.
- Tone note (stated): mix of power-fantasy comedy and violence; reviewers were divided, and the opening rape scene was criticized as gratuitous. That tonal gap is a warning for anything we adapt.
- Inferred: the concealment loop is the sim: a reveal is a social event with a threshold, not a switch.

**The Skeleton Soldier Failed to Defend the Dungeon (U3).**
- Stated (Wuxiaworld/MAL listings and search summary): a skeleton guard dies with his mistress, the succubus lady, when invaders storm the dungeon; he is sent back 20 years, builds skills, investigates rival factions, and keeps memories through each cycle. Korean web novel by SOSORI, 391 chapters, ongoing at the time of the listing.
- Inferred: the loyalty goal (protect the one person) is a clear anchor that a text game could use.

**Vampire: The Masquerade (U4).**
- Stated (Wikipedia): the Beast is a predatory drive that can force a frenzy. Humanity tracks closeness to human nature; at zero you become a wight. Blood pool (vitae) powers abilities and healing. The Masquerade is a conspiracy to hide vampires; breaches can mean Final Death. Thirteen clans with Disciplines and weaknesses. Generation measures distance from Caine. Torpor is a deathlike sleep whose length depends on Humanity. Diablerie is consuming a lower-generation vampire's soul to lower your own generation.
- Inferred: this is the strongest model in the cluster for "needs" and "ending". Hunger drives blood, Humanity is the morality meter, Masquerade is the social meter, Torpor is the offline mechanic.

**iZombie (U5).**
- Stated: eating a brain gives temporary traits, skills and memory flashes of the victim; murder victims leave clues; too long without brains risks mindlessness. She works at the morgue to eat. Confidant Ravi researches a cure. Detective Clive works with her and learns the truth later. Her ex-fiance is kept at a distance. Peyton notices her declining interest.
- Inferred: "eating the dead" as a skill-copy mechanic is the best verb in the section: each meal is a temporary build.

**Warm Bodies (U6).**
- Stated (Wikipedia): R eats brains and relives memories (Perry's). Boneys are fully decayed zombies who lead packs and attack both humans and thawing zombies. R's connection to Julie causes speech, dreams, distaste for human flesh and a "cure." Love as cure.
- Inferred: thaw as progression; the stages are emotional rather than numeric.

**Mo Dao Zu Shi (U7).**
- Stated (Wikipedia on the TV adaptation): Wei Wuxian practices demonic cultivation using resentful energy of the dead, after giving up his spiritual power to save his brother. He uses inventions such as the Stygian Tiger Amulet. He is ostracized and feared, dies, and is later summoned back through a ritual into Mo Xuanyu's body. The article does not explain the mechanics. Novel and TV differ in plot (stated: the novel's flashback style and censorship changes).
- Inferred: social cost of the "dark" path is the center of the story, more than any power number.

**Bleach Hollows (U9).**
- Stated: Hollows are former humans not sent on in time who crave souls; Arrancar are humanoid Hollows with Soul Reaper powers. The evolution ladder (Menos, Adjuchas, Vasto Lorde) is recalled, not verified.

### 2.3 What to learn

Steal-worthy:
1. **Three meters that pull on each other** (Vampire): Hunger, Humanity, Masquerade. Feeding raises Hunger relief but lowers Humanity or risks exposure. Torpor is the exit when all three go wrong.
2. **Eat the dead for skills** (iZombie, Warm Bodies). Each corpse is a temporary loadout and a memory clue. It makes work and crime and research all the same verb.
3. **Concealment as a social system** (Skeleton Knight helmet, Masquerade). Disguise strength, who knows, and a threshold event for reveal.
4. **Thaw as progression** (Warm Bodies). The body regains senses and speech as relationships deepen; a "cure" is a decision, not a freebie.
5. **Minions that misread you** (Overlord, as recalled). Delegation with interpretation errors is a rich text event generator.
6. **Retry loop with memory** (Skeleton Soldier). Keep memory but lose the body and the world resets to an earlier state.
7. **Dark path with social cost** (Mo Dao Zu Shi). Power that sets you outside society.

What goes wrong:
- **Overpowered start** (Overlord, Skeleton Knight: nearly invulnerable armor, huge spells). Stakes die, and the story moves to political or comic engines. Inferred: a sim needs the power at the bottom of the ladder.
- **The body becomes a human in disguise too fast.** Skeleton Knight is about hiding the body so he can live a human life (stated: he stays masked, takes a mercenary job). Inferred: the interesting design is the cost and edge cases of the body, such as light, touch, food, ageing, reflection.
- **Tone problems** with violent shock content (Skeleton Knight reviews, stated).
- **Evolution-ladder web novels** (recalled, generic): power creep and repeated "level up, evolve" beats. Inferred: use the ladder for new verbs, not bigger numbers.

Idle and offline time:
- Inferred: undead need not sleep or eat (Overlord, recalled) so offline time is not rest. Better: offline time is **Torpor** (Vampire), where the clock continues and the world moves. A skeleton could do idle "guard duty," "digging," "patrol" while away, with wear.
- A lich or vampire has centuries: offline time can be decades (see Elder Scrolls style "centuries pass" skip; recalled, not verified). The world changes while you wait.

### 3.2 What each source does mechanically

**A Wizard of Earthsea (H1).**
- Stated: Ged's failed spell in a duel with Jasper releases a shadow that attacks him and scars his face. Gensher says it is ancient, nameless, and wants to possess him. It follows him across Earthsea. Ogion urges him to face it. After a chase east with Vetch, Ged names the shadow with his own name and merges with it; he becomes whole and cannot be possessed by anyone but himself. Critics read it as accepting his dark side and mortality.
- True names: knowing a name gives power over a being (stated). Children receive a true name at puberty; sharing it is trust.
- Inferred: a shadow's ending is not victory; it is integration. This matches a sim's "ending" slot.

**Shadow Slave (H3).**
- Shown (Roliascan excerpts): Sunny's Aspect is Shadow Slave, at Divine rank in the later chapters, with Shadow Control, Shadow Step, Shadow Manifestation, Shadow Incarnation, and an innate Shadow Bond. The Aspect strengthens him, his Memories, Echoes and Shadows; later it also reaches followers with the Mark of Shadows (surprising to Sunny). Earlier it reached Nephis only through Shadow Bond. Shadow Control does not strengthen other people's Memories or other living creatures (at the time of chapter 109).
- Premise: shadows serve him; he is the master, not the shadow. So this is the opposite body: you hold shadows.
- Inferred: a "bond" ability that extends to one chosen person shows how a one-person channel is a strong mechanic.
- Source warning: web wikis for this work were spam mirrors. Do not use them.

**Wraith Shadow (H4).**
- Stated: a secondary personality, often played by another player, whose goal is the wraith's self-destruction.
- Inferred: a shadow body could be played from the dark-self side; that is rare and worth trying.

**Dimple and Sai as bound spirits (H5, H6).**
- Stated (see Part 1). Both are tied to one living person and change as the bond deepens.

**Peter Pan, Andersen, Persona (H2, H8, H9).** Recalled, not verified. I list them only as pointers for the next researcher.

### 3.3 What to learn

Steal-worthy:
1. **Bound to one person is a social channel with one edge.** Everything the shadow can do is mediated by the host: the shadow's senses are the host's lights, its verbs the host's body and habits.
2. **Light as a resource.** Inferred: a shadow exists only where there is light and a body to cast it. Night, noon, lamps and darkness are the clock.
3. **Integration ending** (Earthsea). The run can end not in victory but in merging, naming or separating.
4. **Hostile to friendly gradient** (Earthsea, Dimple). The same entity starts as an enemy and ends as a friend.
5. **Bond-as-extension** (Shadow Bond). One person can be strengthened through you.
6. **Dark-self rival** (Wraith Shadow). Needs and risk come from a rival that shares your head.

What goes wrong or is missing:
- In every verified source the shadow is a *threat* or *servant*, not a protagonist. Inferred: there is little precedent for a satisfying 100-hour shadow-as-player life. That is both risk and opportunity.
- A body that cannot act alone is easy to make passive and dull (Lovely Bones problem).
- Becoming "the host's second self" can collapse into just playing the host. Needs a hard mechanical difference such as shape, time of day, and no memory of daylight.

Idle and offline time:
- Inferred: a shadow's offline time is natural: while the host sleeps, you drift; while the host is in lamplight you are sharp. Time of day and light drive output. A day-night schedule is built in.

## Cross-cluster notes

- Anchors and the pull to release recur in all three bodies (Fetters, humanity, shadow naming).
- The **host** appears in ghost (Sai, Dimple), undead (Skeleton Knight disguise), and shadow (Shadow Bond). A single "host/anchor" system could serve all three.
- The **retry loop with memory** (Ghost Trick, Skeleton Soldier) is the best idle-compatible structure in the cluster: runs are cheap, memory persists.
- The **keepsake on release** (Spiritfarer) is a good meta-progression: what your retired run leaves behind.

## The 5 strongest ideas for this cluster

1. **Fetters and Passions as the whole need system** (Wraith). Anchors you must tend, feelings that fuel you, and finishing them ends your run. Works for ghost, shadow and some undead.
2. **The three-meter pull for undead** (Vampire Beast/Humanity/Masquerade plus Torpor as offline time). Hunger, morality and exposure fight each other; Torpor is the safe idle state.
3. **Eat the dead for a temporary build and a clue** (iZombie, Warm Bodies). One verb covers work, crime, research and collections.
4. **Ghost economy of the living's offerings** (Ghost Festival, Obon, hungry ghosts). Your descendants and neighbors feed you; neglect makes you hungry; this ties directly to the family and heirs system.
5. **Release as the win** (Spiritfarer, Lovely Bones, Sixth Sense, Earthsea integration). Endings by fading, naming, or merging rather than getting stronger. Combine with a keepsake that carries to the next run.

Runner-up: the Ghost Trick four-minute rewind as a bounded retry minigame.

## Open questions

- Q3: Do we want the Sixth Sense reveal (you are already dead) as a hidden start type? It requires hiding the starting body from the player.

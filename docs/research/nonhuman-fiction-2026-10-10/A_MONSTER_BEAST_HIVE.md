# Cluster A: monster, beast/familiar and collective/hive starts (2026-10-10)

Research notes and proposals only. Nothing here is an accepted decision.

**Evidence labels.** **shown** = in the work itself, as reported by a page I fetched. **stated** = author, wiki or reviewer says so. **inferred** = my reading. **recalled** = from my own prior knowledge of the work; I did not re-verify it this session, so treat it as lower confidence. Several fetches returned thin or failed pages (Tensura fandom wiki 402, TV Tropes 403, Niche 404, Wikipedia Tines/Children of Ruin pages were short), and I say where that leaves a gap. I did not read any novel in full; web-novel claims rest on summaries and reviews.

Body slots used throughout: verbs, senses, clock, needs, channel, ending.

## 2. What each source does mechanically

### Monster starts

**M1 Spider.**
- Verbs: eat, spin thread, fight, hide, level, evolve, research magic. **shown**
- Senses: skills such as Detection and an Appraisal skill she wastes points on, which later maxes without evolving. **shown**
- Clock: levels heal her fully, so a level-up in a bad fight is a heal. **shown**
- Growth: evolves when she reaches a level cap. Stages named on the page: Small Poison Taratect, Zoa Ele, Taratect, Queen Taratect, Puppet Taratect (operates a mannequin), Arachne (half human), and the title Origin Taratect. **shown**
- Needs: food and survival; the Taboo skill grows when she does unholy acts such as eating a sibling's corpse, and at max level it makes her collapse and gives visions of an apocalypse. **shown** A cost attached to a shortcut is the interesting part.
- Channel: she wants to reach humans, so Arachne is a goal about being able to talk. **shown**
- Multi-mind: Dual Perception splits her into two, then three, then four minds that multitask, with one dedicated to magic study. **shown** This is a body that gains parallel turns.
- Ending: in the anime she survives by moving her consciousness into an egg she had laid. **shown** (The light novel ends at vol. 16, Jan 2022. **stated**)
- Contradiction to our ideas: the system is a game-like layer that god-like administrators grant skills from; it is not a natural process. Her power is partly given. **shown**

**M2 Slime.**
- Predator lets it consume anything and mimic its look and abilities. **shown** Wikipedia only; the pages that would detail Great Sage and stage names failed to load. I have unverified recall of Great Sage as an advisor skill, and a search result said Great Sage later evolves into Raphael with the Harvest Festival as the trigger. **stated (forum commentary)**
- Naming: Veldora names Rimuru, who takes the surname Tempest; names bind and strengthen followers. **shown** (naming as strengthening is **recalled**)
- Human form came from devouring a dead woman, Shizue Izawa; sources disagree on whether Predator also came from her or from Veldora. **shown / disputed**
- Channel: nation building. Creatures of the Great Forest accept Rimuru as ruler once Veldora's aura fades, and neighbors respond with alliances or hostility. **shown**
- Ending: not a death; it becomes a Demon Lord, then more. **stated**

**M3 Re:Monster.**
- Verb: eat to absorb traits (flight, exoskeleton, sentries made from blood). **shown**
- Growth: goblin to Hobgoblin to Ogre and later an extinct Apostle Lord. **shown**
- Heirs: because he has absorbed so much, his children's species are random and some are hybrids. **shown** This is a ready-made heir mechanic.
- Social: the tribe is led by duel; he later forms a mercenary band of people with nowhere else to go, from many races. **shown**
- The level system is barely described on the page I used. **gap**

**M4 Chrysalis.**
- Ant protagonist who levels and evolves and grows a colony. **stated** Genre tags: levels, stats, skills, magic, kingdom, party and army building. **stated** Skill examples at the start are plain verbs like Bite and Dig. **stated**
- Reader reception: one review says the middle gets sloggy and repetitive and lacks viewpoints; another says the stat sheets are a way to examine transformation and identity. **stated** I did not find eating mechanics for this book. **gap**

**M5 Mimic line.**
- Fusion with an insatiable-hunger companion that eats food and people; it later raids a dungeon core and summons familiars. **stated (reviews)** Reviews are mixed: concept praised, execution criticized, and some reviewers found the repeated eating wearing. **stated** Content is explicit and some reviewers object to its treatment of women; I am noting that as a reception fact, not endorsing it as source material.

**M6 Xianxia demon beasts.**
- Demon beasts cultivate like humans, by grade, with a Demon Core that stores energy. **stated (fan wiki)**
- At about grade eight, most face a lightning tribulation, rebuild the body into human shape, and gain full sapience; some variants and odd bloodlines are an exception. **stated**
- In Everlasting the ranks are Great Yao then Yao King, counted partly by tribulations survived; even after 8 tribulations a wyrm may be unable to take human form. **stated**
- The human shape is the reward; it is not the start. That is the opposite of "human in disguise too fast."

**M7 Journey to the West.**
- Demons want the monk's flesh for immortality; sources treat this as a rumor passing between demons, not a rule with a stated dose. **stated** The 72 transformations have no list in the novel. **stated**
- Lesson: the same flesh is a resource for monsters and a person for the monk. Choosing to be the hunter makes you the antagonist of the other story. **inferred**

**M8 Metamorphosis.**
- Body: vermin, often stuck on its back, hurt twice (doorway, thrown apple), later climbs walls and ceiling. **shown**
- Needs: sister feeds him; he prefers rotten food; appetite fades. **shown**
- Channel loss: family hears only noises; he loses job and employer contact. **shown**
- Money: his earnings were the family's income and he was paying his father's debts; afterward the others take jobs and rent rooms. **shown**
- Social: Grete begins as caretaker and ends declaring it is no longer Gregor. Only the charwoman is unafraid. **shown**
- Ending: starved; the family takes a day out. **shown**
- This is the best source for "the body is the same person with the channel cut" and for what the household does when the earner changes.

**M9 Kitsune and tsukumogami.**
- Kitsune: tails count age and power (1, 5, 7, 9 are common counts); a new tail after about 100 years; human form at about 50 or 100; "heavenly fox" at about 1,000 years. **shown (Wikipedia)**
- Transformation needs a prop on the head (leaf, reeds, skull); tells include a fox shadow, a reflection, a showing tail when drunk, and fear of dogs. **shown**
- Fox-wife tale: a man marries a fox unknowingly; a dog or truth exposes it; she must leave. Kuzunoha's son is the astrologer Abe no Seimei. **shown**
- Fox gifts are intangible (protection, knowledge, long life); gifts of wealth turn out to be leaves. **shown**
- Tsukumogami: a tool of about 100 years gains a spirit; households discard old items at year-end cleaning so none reach the age; items thrown away just before the threshold turn angry. Some shrine ceremonies console broken items (the page marks this unsourced). **shown / stated**

### Beast or familiar starts

**B1 Pokemon.** Catching: weaken in battle, then throw a ball (some games skip weakening). **shown** Evolution triggers: level, item, high friendship, and trade for some. **shown** The page treats friendship as only an evolution trigger; the Wikipedia article is trainer-centered. **shown** Creature viewpoint is not in the work. **inferred** That makes it a clean negative example: the creature is a tool.

**B2 Natsume.**
- Names in a book bind spirits; returning a name releases them, drains Natsume's energy and shows him a memory of that spirit's past. **shown**
- Nyanko-sensei guards him in exchange for inheriting the book at Natsume's death, and is openly self-interested, but attachment grows. **shown**
- Structure: standalone chapters, mostly one spirit's request. **shown**
- Strongest source for a familiar's bond as a contract with a quiet end date and a changing motive.

**B3 Watership Down.**
- Culture: El-ahrairah is a trickster hero ("prince with a thousand enemies"); Frith is the creator who promises rabbits will not go extinct; the rabbits believe in El-ahrairah but do not worship. **shown**
- Structure: Chief Rabbit and the Owsla military caste; Efrafa has an enforcement Owslafa; overcrowding can halt reproduction and surplus rabbits found new warrens. **shown**
- Numbers: "hrair" means a thousand and covers anything above four. **shown**
- Needs: safety, food, and does; a warren of only bucks dies out. **shown**
- Ending: old Hazel is visited by El-ahrairah and leaves his body behind to join his Owsla. **shown**
- Fiver's foresight is portrayed as a special gift. **shown**

**B4 Warriors.**
- Prefix often environment or color; suffix changes as rank changes: kit, paw, a unique warrior suffix, star for leaders. **shown**
- Four Clans (plus lost SkyClan) with a warrior code; StarClan is the afterlife, guiding by prophecy and dreams; compared by reviewers to a theocracy. **shown**
- The code changes: it once banned cross-Clan relationships and later arcs accepted them. **shown**
- Recalled (not verified here): medicine cats, nine lives for leaders.

**B5 Tokyo Jungle.**
- 50 breeds and 80 types of animals; more unlock as you play. **shown**
- Packs: small animals can win by grouping if one member survives; herbivores can build packs more easily. **shown**
- Modes: survival with leaderboards, and story missions revealing what happened to humans. **shown**
- Review summary: "eat, sleep, and mate." **stated**
- Ending: a robot dog chooses whether to bring humanity back; yes ends the game, no leads to the true ending. **shown**
- Recalled: lifespans are short and offspring carry on; the page I fetched does not describe hunger, lifespan or generations. **gap**

**B6 Spore.**
- Creature stage: hunger and health, nest as respawn, DNA points from hunting or from socializing (mimicking songs and dances to ally with species). **shown**
- The five stages were criticized as shallow, each a cut-down version of another game. Will Wright said the team prioritized casual players over depth. **stated**
- It is the best-known example of a body that evolves through your choices, and also the clearest warning that breadth with no depth gets shrugged off.

**B7 Chinese beast-taming novels.**
- A master-pet contract binds a beast; beasts with no owner cannot be bound this way (You Cultivate, I Farm). **stated (excerpts)**
- Astral Pet Store: a shop that can cultivate any beast; over 2,000 chapters. **stated (low-quality aggregator)**
- The White Cat's Divine Scratching Post is a romance with a spirit beast and is a weaker fit. **stated**
- I could not confirm the "Black Iron Egg" title from the seed list. **gap**

### Collective or hive starts

**H1 Children of Time.**
- A gene-editing nanovirus intended for monkeys instead uplifts jumping spiders. **shown**
- The spiders hunt cooperatively, build cities and domesticate ants. They pass down genetic memories called Understandings. **shown**
- A satellite intelligence (Kern) transmits to them through crystals; they build a religion around her as the Messenger. **shown**
- Ending: a second nanovirus builds empathy between humans and spiders; the two species mix. **shown**
- Recalled: society is female-dominated, males are second class. Not verified here.
- Mechanically: the memory is inherited, so each new life starts with what the last ones learned.

**H2 Ender's Game.** The Formics assumed humans without a collective mind were non-sentient and realized their mistake too late. **shown** A queen communicates telepathically with Ender. **shown** Ender ends the war by destroying the home planet; later he carries the queen's egg and writes *The Hive Queen*. **shown** The first-person hive voice is only in the sequel; I did not fetch it. **gap**

**H3 Zerg.**
- Units hatch from larva at a Hatchery into forms based on absorbed genetic blueprints. **stated**
- Overlords provide supply, framed in lore as control; losing one costs control points. **stated**
- Only the Overmind and cerebrates are fully sapient; cerebrates cannot disobey; if a cerebrate dies permanently its brood runs mindless. When the Overmind dies the remaining cerebrates die. **stated**
- Kerrigan later replaces cerebrates with brood mothers loyal to her. **stated**

**H4 Antinium.**
- Underground hives, each with a queen and a personality, all sharing the goal of going home. **stated**
- Members of the same hive share memory and thought. **stated**
- Birthers (vats) need a queen to run them; the Twisted Queen designed ones that do not. **stated**
- Earlier hives were only semi-linked and fought each other, queens included. **stated** Post-war, queens are barred from bearing queens. **stated**
- Variants such as the Flying Antinium are hard to reproduce because of the Flying Queen's experiments. **stated**
- One Antinium, Xrn, can do magic. **stated**
- The wiki warns some pages are older snapshots, and spoilers are hidden.

**H5 Tines.** Dog-like creatures whose packs act as one mind; I could not confirm size, sound-based thought, or what happens when a member dies from the page I fetched. **recalled / gap** A Fire Upon the Deep also has Tine factions in medieval-tech conflict (Flenserists). **shown**

**H6 Empires of the Undergrowth.** RTS colony of ants or termites with a lab storyline where unlocked species join your colony. **shown** Rewards scale with mission difficulty. **shown** Pheromones or nest building: **gap**.

**H7 Children of Ruin parasite.** It mimics cells, absorbs memory and learns by homogenizing hosts. **shown** The Kern AI persuades it through a Portiid implant that if everything were the same, nothing would be left to explore, and it accepts a truce. **shown** Octopus minds are decentralized and hard to talk to, and overpopulation fractured their civilization. **shown**

## 3. What to learn for a text idle life-sim

### Steal-worthy mechanics

1. **Cost attached to the shortcut (Taboo, M1).** A power from forbidden eating that bills you later. Fits "gaining power can close a future" in the north star.
2. **Evolution is a threshold, not a number (M1, M6, M9).** A level cap opens a choice of forms. Tribulations and tail ages make the threshold an event with a calendar date.
3. **The human form is a late, costly reward (M6, M9).** Fox tells, prop on the head, tribulation survived. These avoid the "body is a human in disguise too fast" failure.
4. **Fox-wife structure.** A secret that can be found out by an ordinary thing (a dog) is stronger than a stat check. **inferred**
5. **Random heirs from absorbed traits (M3).** Offspring species roll against what you ate.
6. **Names as a mechanical object (M2, B2).** Naming, returning a name, releasing a contract. Book of Friends turns this into slow quiet play.
7. **Contracts with an end clause (B2).** The familiar's price is an inheritance; the motive drifts.
8. **Inherited memory as a bonus on lives (H1).** Each generation starts with the Understandings of the last.
9. **Hive without a sovereign collapses in a particular way (H3).** Cerebrate dies, brood goes feral; supply cap as control. A hive's limit is span of control, not money.
10. **Hive quarrels (H4).** Semi-linked hives fought each other before the war; a "one mind" body can still have a politics inside it.
11. **Tools wake up at 100 years (M9).** Year-end cleaning as a ritual that stops it. Object-adjacent bodies fall into the same engine.
12. **Household as the failing system (M8).** The body is easy; the people around it are the game.
13. **Myth as shared culture (B3).** A trickster story and a promise about not going extinct are what keep the group together. Invented by the player's own warren over time.

### What goes wrong in those works

- **Power creep.** Slime and Re:Monster end in Demon Lord or Lord-tier beings; eating becomes a solved verb. **inferred** Mimic reviewers complain about repeated eating (M5). **stated**
- **Middle slog.** Chrysalis review notes a repetitive middle and too few viewpoints. **stated**
- **Shallow breadth.** Spore is the textbook case (B6). **stated**
- **Creature as tool.** Pokemon's page frames the creature through trainer goals. **shown**
- **Human in disguise.** M1's goal of Arachne and M2's human form arrive early in a long arc; M6's rule puts it later. **inferred**
- **Sovereign convenience.** Zerg cerebrates have no choice; a hive that is all will is not a life. The Antinium's fights show the choice can come back. **inferred**
- **System as gift.** In M1, administrators hand out skills, so a monster's growth can be a human-made system. **shown** In our game, do not let this displace natural evolution.

### Idle and offline time

- A monster's clock can be a growth season. A spider's days between level-ups can be the idle block, with the log reporting which things the monster ate and what threatened it. **inferred**
- Kitsune and tsukumogami lore give a natural calendar: wait years, get a tail. Offline time can cost nothing but also gain nothing. **inferred**
- A familiar's offline days are the master's days. A contract with an end clause keeps ticking. **inferred**
- A hive in offline time should be handled by the colony's own average rule rather than by player decisions. Zerg supply cap and cerebrate break show the failure state (brood goes feral) as an offline consequence. **inferred**
- Generational bodies (Tokyo Jungle, Niche, Watership) fit short sessions well because each life is short. Tokyo Jungle's lifespan details are unverified here. **gap**

## 5. Adjacent bodies and twists our lists miss

1. **Mushishi's mushi as a parasite you are.** Mushi are primitive life forms most people cannot perceive, and stories start when a person gets sick from one. **shown** A body that is the cause of someone's illness, with a "cure" that costs you. Fits next to the symbiont row in the north star.
2. **Fox who must hide a tell.** A body whose game is concealment with three named tells (tail, shadow, reflection) and a dog as the threat. **shown (Wikipedia)**
3. **Tsukumogami as a household tool.** Items woken near 100 years; the year-end cleaning is the player's enemy. **shown**
4. **A name-bound spirit.** The one named in someone's book, who can be called to obey or released. **shown**
5. **A disembodied Understandings lineage.** The long-lived thing is the inherited memory, not the body. **shown (H1)**
6. **Parasite collective.** Children of Ruin's absorbing organism: an expanding mind whose danger is sameness. **shown**
7. **Octopus decentralized mind.** Hard-to-talk-to, overpopulation-prone. **shown**
8. **Egg-saved consciousness.** M1's ending. A save-and-restart as a lore device. **shown**
9. **A displaced earner (Gregor).** Players who start as a person and turn into something are a different body from those born as one. **inferred**
10. **Dead-end deity (Hazel).** Leaving the body for El-ahrairah's Owsla is an ending, not a death. **shown**
11. **Tokyo Jungle's survivor choice.** The final decision is whether to bring humans back. **shown** A body whose ending is a choice about the world.
12. **Cerebrate-less brood.** The failure state of Zerg design as a playable body: a swarm after its mind dies. **stated**
13. **Spore's tribal and civilization stages.** The same species changing its scale of play is a "crossing". **shown**

## 6. Five strongest ideas for this cluster

1. **Evolution as a dated event with a price (A1).** Combine level caps (M1), tribulations (M6) and kitsune tails (M9): a form change you queue, with a shortcut option that carries a Taboo-style bill. Works in idle time because the event has a calendar.
2. **The disguise is the endgame, not the start (A2).** Make human form a late reward with named tells (tail, shadow, reflection, dog) and a fox-wife exposure. Avoids the failure the brief warned about.
3. **Hive as span of control (A3).** Number of bodies is limited by a control resource; losing a controller sends a brood feral (Zerg). Idle play has a built-in failure state, and the hive can quarrel internally (Antinium).
4. **Inherited Understandings as the heir system (A4).** Each generation starts with part of what the last learned (Children of Time); child species roll against what the parent ate (Re:Monster). One mechanic covers monsters, beasts and hives.
5. **Contracts with end clauses (A5).** Natsume's inheritance clause plus Journey to the West's rumor: a familiar is bound for a price, and the price is revealed over time. Names and releases are the moves.

## Part A. Tough As Nails (body state)

**SV1 (shown, `temperature/TemperatureHandler#onPlayerTick`, `api/temperature/TemperatureLevel`).** Body temperature is one of five levels: icy, cold, neutral, warm, hot. It is not a number. Each tick the game computes a target level for the player's spot; the body's current level moves toward it one step at a time, each step after a delay. Default delay is 500 ticks (25 s at 20 ticks per second). When a modifier changes the target, the delay for that change is capped by a per-source value: 50 ticks for armor and mounts, 125 for player-based modifiers, 375 for held items, 20 for food effects. Coming back from an extreme (icy or hot) to a non-extreme target is capped at 250. Changing dimension resets the last-seen level. Mechanism to port: the body lags the world, and what you do (put on a coat, step to a fire, eat soup) shortens the lag, so quick fixes feel quick and weather feels slow.

**SV2 (shown, `temperature/BuiltInTemperatureModifier`, `config/TemperatureConfig`).** The target is built in two stages. Stage one is positional: biome class (five biome tag lists, then the vanilla base temperature as fallback with cutoffs 0.15, 0.45, 0.85 and 1.0), then a night adjustment, altitude, rain, and nearby heat or cold blocks. Stage two is five player modifiers (player-based, held item, armor, mount, internal food effect) applied in an order the config can reorder (a validator requires all five to be present). Environmental modifiers apply only when the spot is above y 50 or at or above the local surface, so caves and cellars read neutral (shown, `getBiomeTemperatureLevel`, `nightModifier`). Defaults: above y 160 colder by one step, below y -32 warmer by one; exposed to rain or snow, one step colder; night makes a non-neutral spot one step colder and a hot spot two steps colder, and leaves neutral spots alone; more heat blocks than cold blocks within 8 blocks of fill raises one step, and the reverse lowers one. Port: a single resolved "felt climate" with a reorderable, additive pipeline, which is also how a world rule or cheat adds a term.

**SV3 (shown, `TemperatureHandler`, `mixin/MixinLivingEntity`, `data/toughasnails/damage_type/*`, `thirst.json`).** Heat is a damage clock: entering hot starts a 500-tick extremity delay, then hyperthermia builds one tick at a time to 140, then 1 damage (half a heart) every 40 ticks; hyperthermia falls 2 per tick when not hot, and fire resistance blocks the build. Cold has no damage type of its own in the data (only `hyperthermia` and `thirst` exist). Instead, icy level after the extremity delay pushes vanilla's freezing counter up, which uses the game's existing freeze damage; an Ice Resistance potion (3 min, 8 min extended) zeroes it. So the two extremes are asymmetric on purpose: heat gives a visible build-up meter, cold borrows an existing mechanism. Creative and spectator players skip the effects.

**SV4 (shown, `thirst/ThirstData`, `thirst/ThirstHandler#onPlayerTick`, `mixin/MixinPlayer#onCauseFoodExhaustion`, `config/ThirstConfig`).** Thirst is a 20-point bar (default 20) plus hydration (default 2.0) plus an exertion counter. Every bit of exhaustion the game already charges for sprinting, jumping, fighting and regenerating is copied to thirst's own counter. When the counter passes 8 (config `exhaustion_threshold`, hunger's own is 4), it drops by 8 and spends 1 hydration; with no hydration left it spends 1 point of the bar (not on Peaceful). So thirst runs at about half hunger's exertion rate, and hydration is a buffer of free "exertion units" that a good drink refills. (Inferred from the two thresholds: a full default meter lasts 2 hydration + 20 bar = 22 units, about 176 exhaustion.) The bar is skipped entirely when `enable_thirst` is false. Port: thirst is not time-based; it is a tax on activity, which maps onto per-verb effort in the project's wear-from-work model (BHC3).

**SV5 (shown, `ThirstData#drink`, `thirst/ThirstHandler#onItemUseFinish`, `init/ModTags$Items`, `data/toughasnails/tags/item/{thirst,hydration,poison_chance}/*`).** A drink is three tags: thirst restored (1 to 20), hydration modifier (0.1 to 1.0), poison chance (25, 50, 75, 100 percent). Drinking adds thirst, capped at 20, and sets hydration to min(old + thirst x modifier x 2, new thirst level); a drink does nothing when the bar is already full. Data defaults: dirty water 1 thirst, 0.1 hydration, 75% poison; vanilla water bottle 3, 0.4, 25%; purified water 5, 0.8, 0%; milk 2, 0.3; apple juice 8, 0.7; melon juice 7, 0.5; pumpkin juice 6, 0.6; cactus juice 7, 1.0; berry and glow-berry juices 6 with 0.2; chorus juice 10 with 0.2; ominous bottle 1, 0.1, always poison. The poison is a 30-second Thirst effect that adds 0.025 exhaustion per tick per level (inferred: 15 exhaustion, about two hydration units). Everything is a tag, so a pack adds a drink by listing it. The tag-per-number encoding is clumsy (an `if` chain per tier, shown); the useful part is the three-number drink record.

**SV6 (shown, `thirst/ThirstHandler#onPlayerUseItem`, `item/EmptyCanteenItem`, `block/entity/WaterPurifierBlockEntity`, `block/RainCollectorBlock`, `data/.../recipe/potion.json`).** Water has a source. Filling a glass bottle from a water block asks the biome: swamp, mangrove swamp and mushroom fields give dirty water; the purified-biome list is empty by default; anything else gives plain water. Canteens (leather, copper, iron, gold, diamond, netherite) have 5, 6, 7, 10, 15 and 25 uses and come in empty, dirty, plain and purified forms; a "water cleansing" enchantment (anvil cost 4, weight 2) makes any fill purified. A rain collector holds 0 to 3 purified fills and gains one on 60% of rain ticks that reach it (30% for snow, shown). The purifier takes a dirty item and a consumable filter and runs 200 ticks by default (400 for the dirty-bottle recipe I opened; I did not open the other purifier recipes). Filter fuel by item (shown, `getFilterDurations`): grass 100, paper 200, gravel 400, any sand 800, charcoal 1600, prismarine crystals 3200, heart of the sea 6400. Port: a water item carries a quality state; source place sets it; a cheap local fix (boil, filter, collect rain) lifts it; the filter is a consumable with a clear scale.

**SV7 (shown, `thirst/ThirstHooksClient`, `ThirstHooks#doFoodDataTick`, `ThirstHandler`).** The consequences are thresholds, not a smooth penalty: sprint is blocked when thirst is 6 or lower (checked client side, flyers exempt); natural healing needs thirst 18 or higher for the slow path (heal 1 per 80 ticks, charging 6 exhaustion to hunger and thirst alike) and thirst 20 with hydration above 0 for the fast saturation path; an empty bar deals 1 damage per 80 ticks, with the same floors vanilla starvation has (cannot kill below 10 health on easy-style rules). Healing therefore costs water as well as food (shown, both exhaustion calls). Flag: the hunger-starvation damage inside `doFoodDataTick` is also tagged with the thirst damage type (shown), so a starving death reads as thirst.

**SV8 (shown, `block/entity/ThermoregulatorBlockEntity`, `temperature/AreaFill`, `data/.../tags/item/thermoregulator_*_fuel`).** A powered block with two fuel slots, cooling and heating, each burning one item for 1600 ticks. Once a second it floods a region from its front face, radius 16, through air, doors and trapdoors, stopping at any sturdy face the flood meets; and only cells below the local roof line count (shown, `isConfined`: the cell must be below the no-leaves surface heightmap). Players within 20 blocks horizontally and 10 vertically are told of it; it is dropped from the player's list past 32 blocks. In its zone, cooling lowers the target two steps, heating raises two, both together make it neutral. A redstone signal disables it. Fuel lists: cooling takes ice, packed and blue ice, snow, snowballs, breeze rods, wind charges, powder snow buckets, ice cream; heating takes coal, charcoal, coal block, blaze rod and powder, fire charge, magma block and cream, lava bucket, charc-os (the mod's own food). Port: shelter is an enclosed space that a fuel-fed device turns into a climate; the roof requirement is the entire definition of "indoors" here.

**SV9 (shown, `TemperatureHelperImpl#armorModifier`, `#handheldModifier`, `#mountModifier`, `#internalModifier`, `data/.../tags/*`).** Heating armor is wool, cooling armor is leaf-woven (both 1 defense per piece, durability multiplier 5). Armor counts pieces and moves the target by pieces divided by two (a full set is two steps), but cannot send a body into an extreme it was not already in (the result is pulled back one step). Held items count one step each (lava bucket warms, powder snow cools). Boats and minecarts force neutral. Internal warmth (charc-os) and chill (ice cream) last 1200 ticks and move the target by one with the same no-new-extreme cap. A chest enchantment (thermal tuning, weight 2) forces neutral. Trim-material lists for heating and cooling exist but ship empty. The caps encode a design choice: gear and food give comfort, not immunity to the worst weather, which only the thermoregulator or the enchantment ends.

**SV10 (shown, `mixin/MixinServerLevel`, `TemperatureConfig`, flags in `TemperatureHelperImpl`).** Grace: the first time a non-creative player joins, they get Climate Clemency for 6000 ticks (5 min), which holds them at neutral; respawn does not re-grant it by default. Three code flags I would not port as intended behavior: (a) in the lingering-wet branch of `immersionModifier` the result of the increment is discarded, so the 40-tick "still wet" cooling after leaving water does nothing; (b) `tryAddHeatExhaustion` adds a positive 0.015 times the hyperthermia fraction to movement speed (ADD_VALUE), which reads as a speed-up although the name says exhaustion (inferred sign slip; vanilla's base speed is 0.1); (c) the starvation damage tag noted in SV7. Label: shown; the intent is inferred.

## Part B. Serene Seasons (calendar)

**SV11 (shown, `season/SeasonTime`, `api/season/Season`, `config/SeasonsConfig`).** One integer, seasonCycleTicks, is saved per world. Year length = 12 sub-seasons x sub-season length x day length; defaults 8 days per sub-season and 24000 ticks per day, so season 24 days and year 96 days (inferred product). Sub-seasons run early, mid, late within spring, summer, autumn, winter, starting at early spring at time 0. A new world starts at a configured sub-season (default 1, early spring; 0 picks at random). Dial: `day_duration` (minimum 20; stated as meant for use with a mod that changes the actual day), `sub_season_duration` (1 or more days), `starting_sub_season`, whitelisted dimensions (default overworld only), `do_season_cycle` game rule, `progress_season_while_offline` (default true). Each sub-season has fixed grass, foliage and birch tints (shown), with winter muted and mid-summer at full vanilla color.

**SV12 (shown, `season/SeasonHandler#onLevelTick`).** The clock does not count its own ticks. Each server tick it reads the overworld day-clock, subtracts the last read, and adds the difference; a negative difference wraps by a fixed 24000 (flag: fixed, not the configured day length, so a backward `/time set` jumps forward and a modified day length would misbehave, inferred). So sleeping through the night, which jumps the day-clock, advances the season by the skipped time. When progress while offline is off and no players are online, the clock holds. A sync packet goes out about once a second (every 20 ticks the loop fires `sendSeasonUpdate`). Port: tie the season to the one world clock, not to a second timer; anything that fast-forwards time must say what it does to the season.

**SV13 (shown, `season/SeasonalCropGrowthHandler#onCropGrowth`, `#applyBonemeal`, `init/ModFertility#isCropFertile`, `config/FertilityConfig`, `data/sereneseasons/tags/{block,item}/*_crops.json`).** The brief asked for growth multipliers; there are none. Fertility is a yes or no per crop per season, from tag lists. A crop is fertile if the current season's list contains it, or if it is in no list at all (unlisted plants are always fertile, shown). Out of season, three modes (`out_of_season_crop_behavior`, default 0): grow slowly (the crop's random tick is cancelled 5 times in 6, so about one sixth as often, inferred), cannot grow, or break (unless in the unbreakable list, which holds saplings, grasses, flowers, onion, cactus and so on). Bone meal follows the same rule and is consumed even when blocked (shown). Data defaults: wheat in summer and autumn; carrots in spring and autumn; potatoes in spring; pumpkin and beetroot in autumn; melon, cocoa, sugar cane and cactus in summer; sweet berries in spring and winter; no vanilla farm crop in winter; nether wart, mushrooms and oak saplings year round. Cold-biome rule: where the vanilla check says the spot is too cold to rain, only the winter list is fertile, whatever the season (shown at `isCropFertile`; the mixin list contains no hook on this call, so it uses static biome temperature, inferred from `sereneseasons.mixins.json`).

**SV14 (shown, `SeasonalCropGrowthHandler#isGlassAboveBlock`, `ModFertility#isCropFertile`).** Escapes from the calendar are physical: any block tagged greenhouse glass within 16 blocks above the crop makes it fertile; below y 48 with no sky view (config `underground_fertility_level`, default 48) crops are always fertile; the blacklisted-biome list (caves, oceans, river, beach, void) ignores seasons; tropical biomes (desert, badlands, savanna, jungle, mangrove, mushroom fields) treat only the summer list as fertile and everything else as dead, so carrots and potatoes cannot grow there at all (inferred from the code path and the summer list). The cost of the escapes is build effort, which is the in-world reason.

**SV15 (shown, `season/SeasonHooks`, `season/RandomUpdateHandler`, `config/SeasonsConfig#DEFAULT_SEASON_PROPERTIES`).** One table row per sub-season: melt chance percent, melt rolls per chunk per tick, biome temperature adjustment, rain delay range, thunder delay range. Defaults: winter melt 0, temperature -0.8, rain every 12000 to 36000 ticks (shorter gaps, so wetter, and as snow); early spring and late autumn -0.25; spring and autumn melt 6.25 to 12.5; summer melt 25 with no adjustment; spring and summer use vanilla's longer rain gap (12000 to 96000, shown as the constants). Temperature adjustment applies only to non-tropical, non-blacklisted biomes whose base temperature is 0.8 or lower, clamped to -0.5 to 2.0; snow forms where the adjusted temperature is under 0.15, so plains (0.8) go to 0.0 in winter and snow, while hotter biomes never do. Melt scans only loaded chunks near players. Weather frequency is enforced by resetting the next-rain timer when it exceeds the season's maximum, and can disable rain (`-1`) for a season. Winter ice and snow generation is a separate toggle. Port: a sub-season table that the world designer edits, not hardcoded weather.

**SV16 (shown, `SeasonTime#getTropicalSeason`, `Season$TropicalSeason`, `SeasonHooks#hasPrecipitationSeasonal`).** Tropical biomes use six phases (early, mid, late dry; early, mid, late wet) computed from the same cycle with an offset formula (shown), so one clock yields two climates. In mid-dry such biomes have no precipitation; in mid-wet they always do. Biomes with no precipitation at all stay dry. Port: a climate profile per region reads the same clock.

**SV17 (shown, TAN `init/ModCompatibility`).** When SS is installed, TAN registers an extra positional modifier: in the open, summer warms the body target one step and winter cools it one; for tropical biomes, mid-dry warms one step and mid-wet cools only a hot target by one step. Note the doubling: SS also lowers the biome temperature in winter (SV15) and TAN's biome class reads the vanilla base temperature, so winter reaches the body through the TAN modifier and not through the SS adjustment, except when SS makes it snow (TAN's rain modifier then calls SS's snow check, shown). Summer warming exists only in TAN's modifier; SS's own table has no summer adjustment. Port: season is a term in the body-climate pipeline, authored once.

**SV18 (shown, `api/season/ISeasonState`, `SeasonChangedEvent`, `block/SeasonSensorBlock`, `item/CalendarItem`, `command/*`, recipes).** What the calendar exposes: day length, sub-season length, season length, year length, ticks into the year, day count, sub-season, season, tropical season. Two events (standard and tropical) fire when the sub-season or tropical phase changes, evaluated at the once-a-second sync. A redstone sensor outputs 1 to 15 across its chosen season (power = min(fraction of season elapsed x 15 + 1, 15), shown) and does nothing outside it; clicking cycles which season it watches. The calendar item shows season and day (recipe: clock surrounded by 8 paper; the sensor adds the calendar, glass, quartz and slabs). Commands get and set the season. The sensor works only in dimensions with sky light. A planning player can read the clock; automation can react to it. Tooltips on seeds and crops list their fertile seasons when the toggle is on (stated, `crop_tooltips`).

## Part C. Farmer's Delight (cooking and the crop loop)

**SV19 (shown, `block/entity/CookingPotBlockEntity`, `crafting/CookingPotRecipe`, `data/.../recipe/cooking/*`, `block/entity/HeatableBlockEntity`).** The pot has six input slots, one display slot, one container slot (bowl, bottle or other), one output slot. A recipe is an unordered list of ingredients, mostly tags (beef stew is a raw-beef tag, a carrot tag and a potato tag; fried rice is rice, an egg tag, carrot, onion; ratatouille uses a "vegetable minus melon" difference ingredient), plus a container, experience and a cook time (default 200 ticks, 100 for three recipes, 400 for one; shown). The result is a fixed dish, no quality roll. It cooks only while the block below is a heat source (a lit stove, magma block, lava cauldron, or tray heat sources) or sits one block above a conductor (hopper) over a source; if the heat is lost, progress falls by 2 per tick, not to zero at once. A finished batch stacks in the display slot (to at least 64), moves to output when a matching container is supplied, and ingredient remainders (buckets, bowls, bottles) eject. Experience pays out with the fractional part rolled. Port: tag ingredients make a recipe a substitution class rather than a fixed item list, which matches "things work by how they work" (AD38): any food of the right kind cooks.

**SV20 (shown, `effect/NourishmentEffect`, `mixin/NourishmentAlwaysEatMixin`, `effect/ComfortEffect`, `client` overlay config).** Nourishment sets the hunger exhaustion counter to zero every tick, so the food bar stops draining for the duration; it pauses while the player is healing from saturation (so healing still spends food), and a mixin lets the player eat even when full. It is a benefit that is the absence of a cost, not a stat bonus. Comfort, the older version (slow heal, 1 health per 80 ticks when no saturation is left), is marked deprecated and slated for removal (stated). Port: a meal's strongest reading is "your drain pauses", which suits a game where the drain is optional and the player should never feel punished for not eating.

**SV21 (shown, `FoodValues`).** Meal duration is the quality ladder: plain cooked rice 600 ticks of Nourishment; bone broth and the plated mid-tier meals 1200; stews, soups and fried rice 3600; the top tier (pumpkin soup, baked cod stew, noodle soup, roast chicken, feast portions) 6000, always with 14 hunger value and 0.75 saturation modifier at the top. Four durations (30 s, 1 min, 3 min, 5 min) tie roughly to ingredient count: 1-2 ingredients, 2-3, 3-4, a feast. Fast foods (cut meat, slices, cookies) eat quickly with no effect; sweets give short speed buffs (cake slice 400 ticks, pie slice 600); fruit and mixed salads give 100 ticks of Regeneration. Vanilla soups gain Nourishment by config. Port: small ladder, longer meal = bigger commitment = longer protection.

**SV22 (shown, `block/entity/CuttingBoardBlockEntity`, `crafting/ingredient/ChanceResult`, `data/.../recipe/cutting/*`, `Configuration`).** A board holds one stack. A tool (knife, axe, shears, shovel, pickaxe by recipe, matched by tag or by an "item ability" such as `knife_dig` or `axe_strip`) processes one item per use and costs one durability. Results are a list with per-result chance; Fortune adds 0.1 to the chance per level (config `cuttingBoardFortuneBonus`), rolled per unit of the result stack. Examples: beef gives 2 minced beef; pumpkin 4 slices; rice panicle rice plus straw; a log gives the stripped log plus bark (axe); a mushroom colony gives 5 mushrooms. A tool can also be carved onto the board as decoration. Port: the same input yields different products per tool, and a skilled or enchanted tool improves odds, not outcomes.

**SV23 (shown, `block/OrganicCompostBlock`, `block/RichSoilBlock`, `block/RichSoilFarmlandBlock`, `Configuration`).** Compost goes through eight stages (0 to 7); each random tick succeeds with chance 0.02 per activator block in the surrounding 3x3x3, plus 0.1 if sky light above is over 12 (0.05 otherwise), plus 0.1 if water is adjacent; success advances a stage, and success at the last stage becomes rich soil. Rich soil boosts the plant above it (or below, for hanging crops) with the effect of a bone meal on a random tick, with chance 0.2 per tick by default (config `richSoilBoostChance`), skipping a listed set (grass, flowers, mushroom colonies, wild crops). Rich-soil farmland is tilled from it, holds moisture 7 if water is within 4 blocks or it rains (otherwise it dries one step per tick), and boosts only at full moisture; it takes reduced fall damage handling (`fallOn` applies the fall without the vanilla trample-to-dirt), and it reverts to plain rich soil if the block above cannot be sustained (shown). Rice and tomato growth each use light of at least 6 and a chance formula; rich soil makes a crop grow in a fraction of the time a plain field would. Port: a compost pile is a slow, local fertility upgrade with its own inputs, and the upgrade is a place, not a stat.

**SV24 (shown, `block/MushroomColonyBlock`, `RichSoilBlock#convertMushroomToColony`).** A mushroom planted on rich soil becomes a colony with four ages; it grows one age with probability 1 in 4 per random tick, but only above rich soil; shears take one age (returns the mushroom, the colony keeps living), a knife takes all ages at once and resets. Bone meal adds 1 or 2 ages. Port: a harvest-without-destroying crop, with two tools offering partial and full yields.

**SV25 (shown, `block/SkilletBlock`, `block/entity/SkilletBlockEntity`, `block/entity/AbstractStoveBlockEntity`).** A skillet on a tray heat source (the block below) cooks one item with a campfire recipe; the time formula takes the original time (600 ticks default), divides to 20% (so 120), rounds to whole seconds, and each Fire Aspect level takes 0.05 off that fraction, clamped to a minimum I did not open. It stops and drops its item if it is waterlogged. A stove has six slots of campfire-style cooking, with per-slot progress that decays by 2 per tick when unlit. Port: a second, quicker small-batch route next to the pot for single items.

**SV26 (shown, `block/FeastBlock`, `item/HorseFeedItem`, `item/DogFoodItem`).** A feast is a block with 4 servings; each right-click with the container item takes a serving as a food item with the top-tier effects, and the block either vanishes or stays as a leftover item. Horse feed heals a tamed horse (or mule, donkey, other listed users) to full and gives Speed II and Jump I for 6000 ticks; dog food heals a tamed dog (or listed wolves) to full and gives Speed I, Strength I and Resistance I for 6000 ticks; both are one use and only on tamed animals (shown, tamed check). Port: shared servings make a meal a household event; animal feed is the same "full care plus timed boost" shape as a person's top meal.

**SV27 (shown, FD `generated/resources/data/sereneseasons/tags/block/*`).** FD ships SS tag files itself: cabbage in autumn and winter; onion in spring and autumn (also unbreakable when infertile); tomato in summer (budding, vine and rope forms); rice in summer and autumn (plant and panicles). A mod's crop calendar is thus a short authored list, with no code and no multiplier. Cabbage being a winter crop is the one place where the calendar gives a cold-season harvest, since SS's own list has no winter farm crop.

## Part D. Couplings between the three (X1)

**X1-a (inferred, SS `SeasonalCropGrowthHandler` hooks vs FD `RichSoilBlock`).** SS intercepts a crop's own random tick and the player's bone meal event. FD's rich-soil boost calls the crop's bone meal routine from the soil's tick, which is neither (shown on both sides). So a rich-soil boost likely reaches an off-season crop, effectively an uncounted second chance. I did not run it; treat as unconfirmed.

**X1-b (inferred from `NourishmentEffect` and `ThirstHooks`).** Nourishment zeroes vanilla's hunger counter every tick but not TAN's separate thirst counter, and the saturation-heal path charges both. So under a long meal effect, hunger holds while thirst keeps draining and healing still costs water. A pack that wants "fed means safe" would have to reset the thirst counter too, an authored choice.

**X1-c (shown, SS `SeasonHandler` vs TAN `TemperatureHandler`).** Both use real ticks differently: the season clock integrates day-clock deltas (sleep moves it), the body-temperature delays count ordinary ticks (sleep does not move them), and TAN's night modifier reads the game time directly. A rested night therefore skips weeks of seasons while the body lags only a few minutes, which matters when sleep is the offline stand-in.

**X1-d (shown, TAN `ModCompatibility`, FD data).** Both compat layers are loose: TAN calls SS functions only when SS is loaded, and FD publishes tag files only. A dependency can be a one-way read of the calendar through a small API, with no shared state.

## Donor questions (default, dial, reason with in-world cost)

All proposals; none is accepted.

- **SV-Q1 body lag (from SV1, SV2).** Default: if a world has weather that matters, body comfort is a few named steps that follow the felt climate with a delay, and prepared gear shortens the delay. Dial: step count, delay length, which modifiers exist, whether it is on at all (the home world defaults to off, per the needs table). Reason and cost: in-world, the body takes time to warm or chill, so the cost of poor preparation is a few quiet minutes of lower comfort, not a death. Pairs with BHC22 (cause recorded: a cold spell, a missing coat).
- **SV-Q2 comfort not immunity (SV9).** Default: gear, fire and food move comfort by steps and cannot erase the worst weather. Dial: a high-tier item or cheat that ends the category. Reason and cost: the strong fix is a built place (the thermoregulator analogue) or a rare item, and it needs fuel or upkeep, so travel in bad weather keeps meaning.
- **SV-Q3 exertion-driven thirst (SV4, SV7).** Default: where thirst exists, it drains from effort already counted by the engine, with a buffer a good drink refills, and warnings come before consequences (low thresholds first, damage last). Dial: threshold, buffer size, which verbs charge it; off in gentle worlds. Reason and cost: the delta over the repo's played-time hunger is that an idling character spends almost nothing, which fits idle play and punishes only a long hard routine. Consequences should be capability thresholds (cannot sprint, slower recovery), not damage, unless a harsh world opts in (HUNGER D1, C3).
- **SV-Q4 water with provenance (SV5, SV6).** Default: water and drink items carry a quality (thirst value, buffer value, sickness chance), set by source, improved by local processing with a consumable. Dial: which sources are bad (swamp, flooded mine, a plague season), how fast filters burn. Reason and cost: a river is not a well; the fix costs a filter, fuel or a collector, and a bad drink makes a named, short illness (BHC22), not a hidden debuff.
- **SV-Q5 shelter as roof plus device (SV8).** Default: "indoors" for comfort purposes means a space under a roof, optionally conditioned by a fueled device that sets a local climate. Dial: radius, fuel list, whether doors count as sealed, the climate step (one or two). Reason and cost: the in-world reason is a stove or cooling stone; the cost is fuel and a built enclosure, which gives homes and caravans a role in a life sim (north star: warmth and shelter).
- **SV-Q6 season fertility (SV13, SV14).** Default: a crop's calendar is an authored list of seasons; out of season the crop grows slowly, not never, and a built shelter (glass, cellar, heated room) lifts the limit. Dial: strictness (slow, none, die), year length, which crops are year-round, which regions have a different calendar. Reason and cost: a greenhouse costs materials and fuel, and winter food depends on stores, trade, or hardy crops, so the season matters to planning. Do not attach seasons to every processing chain (TFC P6); attach them to farms and to the few chains that actually consume weather.
- **SV-Q7 one clock, many climates (SV11, SV12, SV16).** Default: one world year clock, driven by the same time the rest of the game uses, and regions read it through their own profile (temperate four seasons, tropical wet and dry). Dial: sub-season count and length, offline progress (default on for an idle game, or paused if a world prefers). Reason and cost: if sleep, travel and offline time all advance the year, the cost is that a long absence ages the crops and the season; the player sees it through a calendar item and warnings (SO2, TMR7), and a world can stop the clock for a stated reason.
- **SV-Q8 calendar as small API (SV18).** Default: the engine exposes a current-season state, a change event and a few consumers (a calendar note, a sensor-like trigger for routines). Dial: how much a character sees (an almanac skill reads further ahead). Reason and cost: routines can say "plant when spring starts", and the information costs a calendar or a trained reader.
- **SV-Q9 meal as paused drain (SV20, SV21).** Default: in worlds with hunger, a prepared meal's main benefit is that the drain pauses for a duration tied to the meal's ingredient count, and eating is allowed even when full. Dial: durations, whether healing still spends food, stacking (replace, not add). Reason and cost: ingredients are real (farm, cut, cook), and a better meal needs more of them, so food reads as preparation, not a chore, which matches the repo's D2 ("normal unfed play as the baseline").
- **SV-Q10 tag-bag recipes (SV19).** Default: a dish is a class of ingredients (any raw meat, any root, any egg) plus a heat source and a container, with a fixed result. Dial: a quality or skill layer on top (a master cook's pot yields more portions or a longer buff). Reason and cost: lets foraged or traded substitutes work (AD38), while a missing container or heat source is the visible gate.
- **SV-Q11 place-based fertility (SV23, SV24).** Default: soil quality is a built place with its own slow chain (compost stages with activators, water and light), not a global stat; it cannot be trampled once made. Dial: boost chance, which plants are exempt. Reason and cost: composting takes space, inputs and time, and the reward is steady yield, which gives a farm an arc.
- **SV-Q12 shared meals (SV26).** Default: a feast is a multi-serving event that households can share; it gives relationship credit as well as the top meal buff. Dial: servings, whether leftovers keep. Reason and cost: needs the top-tier ingredient set and a container each, so the feast is a decision. Ties to the north star's "households eat together".

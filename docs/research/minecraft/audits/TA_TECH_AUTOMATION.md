# Lane TA: technology and automation mods (audit, 2026-10-14)

Labels: shown = read in data or code; stated = author guide text; inferred = my reading. Source route: public repos cloned at the matching tag or branch head whose mod_version equals the jar (confirmed by reading gradle properties and `git ls-remote`; the `gh` CLI is not installed, so plain git was used). Factory Tech and Prodigy Tech used the sibling decompile output. Private tables: `private/audits/ta/create_mi_pnc.md`, `private/audits/ta/others.md` and, for the pass 2 code reading of decompiled release jars, `private/audits/ta/pass2_code.md`. Minecraft values are reference only; nothing here authorizes implementation.

## Top findings

- TA1 (shown, Embers) Alchemy is a hidden-code puzzle. A world-seed-derived code assigns an aspect to each input slot. A wrong attempt yields a waste item that records right-aspect-right-slot and right-aspect-wrong-slot counts plus what was tried. Failure is the clue source, not a loss. Answers N12 (partly) and is the best "informative failure" pattern in the lane.
- TA2 (shown, Factory Tech) Upkeep is a compounding-risk part, not a timer. Each part has a fixed operation lifetime, then a break roll whose chance multiplies each survived operation. A broken part halts the machine. Parts have quality variants (cheap, short-lived, slow versus long-lived, fast), a spare-part reserve auto-installs, and breaks can return salvage. This is a real replacement choice (answers N29): choose faster-but-brittle against slow-and-durable, and stock spares. It becomes busywork only if the reserve does not auto-install.
- TA3 (shown, Create) One shared budget with a readable cause. Network capacity (declared capacity times rpm) versus summed impact (impact times rpm); overstress zeroes speed. Goggles and the stressometer show impact at current speed. Boilers show the same pattern with three separate caps (heat, tank size, water), engine efficiency = min over those, and each cap is printed. Best "why is it stalled" display in the lane.
- TA4 (shown, PneumaticCraft) Readiness uses operating conditions, not only total energy (answers N11). Air stored is volume times pressure; operations consume air but gate on pressure. Recipes carry minimum pressure, temperature windows (min and max), speed and air multipliers. Over danger pressure while air is still rising, explosion chance climbs with the excess, preceded by a creak; a safety valve vents and clamps. Equalisation splits air by volume, no backflow.
- TA5 (shown, Modern Industrialization) A recipe is a fixed energy bill (eu times duration). Power draw can exceed the recipe rate to finish sooner, ramped by an efficiency counter that climbs by one per finished batch and decays on any under-powered tick. Manual boosts: a catalyst item for timed speed, lubricant at 25 mb per efficiency tick. Tier is gated by hull casing (right-click to upgrade in place) and cable tier (32, 128, 1024, 8192 EU/t). Answers N28: convenience is earned by a ladder of machines (bronze 2 EU/t recipes, then electric), and the guide itself says automate machine parts early so crafting becomes cheap.
- TA6 (shown, Crossroads) Two supports for one process compete through physics. Rotary power is a conserved energy pool (sum of energy, sum of inertia, gear ratios change speed and inertia), heat is a separate conserved quantity with equalisation, and a Stirling engine converts a heat difference to rotary at 0.8 x dT/2000 efficiency. A machine can need both heat tier and spin. That is more than a second fuel currency (answers N10): each side has different loss, storage and failure behavior. Progression is gated by discovering beam alignments (11 colors), then a path choice (alchemy, technomancy, witchcraft), each with a capstone.
- TA7 (shown, Integrated Dynamics and Tunnels) Part state stores evaluation errors, deactivates the part, and re-activates when the offending variable changes. Slot-level messages (invalid value, wrong type) sync to the client. So yes, a player can see the failing predicate's message at the part (answers N32), though not a full trace. Tunnels (N33): the criterion for each import or export is one of: all, amount per tick, single item, list, predicate operator, NBT; stock is readable as variables (network count, max count, per-item count, interface count), so custody and stock predicates are separate readers and writers on a channel, with priority/round robin.
- TA8 (shown, Deep Resonance) One retained object with four numbers (answers N31). Total energy scales with strength and (purity+30)/130; rate with efficiency and (purity+2)/102; low purity raises radiation radius and strength. Preparation: laser converts the crystal to liquid with stats, infusing items add bounded stat bonuses capped by the liquid quality, the crystalizer rebuilds. Generator warnings are plain broadcast messages (no collector, too many, too strong).
- TA9 (shown, Prodigy Tech) Operating conditions are legible: temperature in, temperature out shown in the GUI; machine speed is linear in supplied temperature over required; air only rises, loses temperature per working machine, so stacking order is the puzzle. The book unlocks entries by advancement in order (explosion furnace, aeroheater, grinder, solderer, energion, primordium). Answers N30. Explosion furnace is a one-shot batch where over- or under-damping costs output.
- TA10 (shown, Alchemistry) Conservation is exact only at the nuclear tier: fusion (3481 pair recipes) sums atomic numbers; fission (117) splits one into two. Item tier is not mass-balanced: ores give 32 element units, storage blocks 144, rocks roll a weighted mix (some rolls give nothing). Combiner and compactor reverse selected dissolutions. Answers N34 with a caveat: ChemLib elements are not in the archive, so the element table was not read.
- TA11 (shown) Teaching styles differ: Create uses 179 Ponder scenes plus an advancement chain 35 deep (stressometer at depth 10); PneumaticCraft a Patchouli book of ~230 entries with a programming tutorial and in-world drone debugging; MI a GuideME book (steam, electric, midgame, lategame); Embers a node-tree codex with prerequisites and per-player checkmarks; Prodigy gated Patchouli; Factory Tech plain text pages; Integrated Dynamics a single info book.

### Pass 2 code reading (2026-10-14)

Anchors are class#method in the decompiled jars; file:line detail is in `private/audits/ta/pass2_code.md`.

- TA12 (shown, Create `ScheduleRuntime#tick`, `#tickConditions`) A train schedule is a list of entries, each an instruction plus wait-condition columns. Columns are OR; conditions inside a column run in order (AND). The first column to finish advances the entry. Non-cyclic schedules pause and mark complete at the end; cyclic ones wrap. Failed steps retry after a 40-tick cooldown. Condition kinds: delay, time of day, item or fluid threshold (greater, less, equal; items or full stacks), cargo idle for N ticks, station powered, station unloaded, player aboard, redstone link. Arrival-time boards average the last recorded trip with the live distance estimate, and give up on stays that are not pure delays. Pattern: a readable automation script with OR-of-sequences waits and an honest "unknown" ETA.
- TA13 (shown, Create `Navigation#search`, `Train#getNavigationPenalty`, `SignalEdgeGroup#isOccupiedUnless`, `DestinationInstruction#start`) Station choice is a glob over station names (up to 3 wildcards); the train takes the cheapest path to any match. Cost is distance plus penalties: a station with another train +300, passing a non-target station +50, forced-red signal +400, occupied signal block a weight that grows with how long this train has waited (25 to 200, halving per further block), and half of each other train's penalty on its edges (parked or paused 700, stuck at a signal grows with wait, moving 25 to 50). So several stations with one name spread trains by load without any explicit dispatcher (inferred from the penalties). Limits: 20 bogeys per train, 128-block assembly track, no single-bogey carriages in a multi-carriage train; top speed 28 m/s unfuelled versus 40 fuelled. Stuck trains report a named cause (no conductor, no matching station, unreachable, end of track, crash).
- TA14 (shown, Create `PackageItem#matchAddress`, `DeliverPackagesInstruction#start`, `FetchPackagesInstruction#start`, `LogisticsManager#broadcastPackageRequest`, `PackagerBlockEntity#isTooBusyFor`, `FactoryPanelBehaviour#tickRequests`) Packages carry a text address matched by glob either way. Trains deliver to any station whose postbox address matches a carried package and fetch from postboxes holding packages addressed elsewhere; a fetch with nothing to collect, or a delivery step with no packages aboard, skips instead of stalling, while packages aboard with no matching station stall with a named status. Network orders split across all linked packagers and are refused whole if any packager queue is at its cap (50 player, 20 redstone, 10 restock). A factory gauge counts in-flight promises toward its target, so it never double-orders, orders at most nine stacks per request, and lights the failing input when one network lacks stock. Promises expire after a set time, so a lost package eventually retriggers the order (inferred). This is a custody-and-promise model, the strongest match for YAIRPG supply chains.
- TA15 (shown, PneumaticCraft `DroneAIManager#setActiveWidget`, `#updateWidgetFlow`, `#addJumpBackWidget`, `ProgWidgetConditionBase#getOutputWidget`, `ProgWidgetJump#jumpToLabel`) The drone VM is a one-instruction pointer over puzzle pieces. Action pieces become an AI goal and the pointer waits until the goal ends; pass-through pieces (label, condition, jump, comment) resolve in the same step, with a visited set to stop empty loops. Conditions log true or false to an in-world debugger and jump to the matching label, or fall through when that side has no label. Several labels with one name are picked at random. End of program: stop, or restart from Start or the jump-back stack. A jump-back stack over 100, or an area over 250000 blocks, "overloads" and kills the drone with a chat reason. Area pieces union a whitelist chain minus a blacklist chain across nine shapes (box, line, wall, sphere, cylinder, pyramid, torus, grid, random); block work is ordered closest-first or by Y layer, with an optional max-actions cap. Drones run only above 0.01 bar and burn 1 air per tick flying.
- TA16 (shown, PneumaticCraft `HeatExchangerLogicTicking#exchange`, `#addHeat`, `HeatExchangerLogicAmbient#atPosition`, `HeatBehaviourTransition#tick`) Heat is a capacity and resistance network: per tick, transfer is temperature gap over summed resistance, clamped so neither side overshoots equilibrium; temperature changes by heat over capacity, clamped 0 to 2273 K. Ambient is 300 K with optional biome and depth modifiers. Neighbouring world blocks are heat sources or sinks with a finite budget tracked per position in saved data; exhausting the budget transforms the block (ice turns to water when heated, packed ice when chilled). Finite environmental heat with visible transformation is the reusable idea.
- TA17 (shown, Embers `UpgradeUtilImpl#collectUpgrades`, `#verifyUpgrades`, `#getWorkTime`, `CatalyticPlugUpgrade#getMultiplier`) Upgrades are adjacent blocks, reachable through up to 3 proxy hops, each hop counted as distance. If a machine has more copies of an upgrade than that upgrade's limit, all copies switch off (not just the extras). Upgrades apply in priority order to speed, ember use, fuel use and output; work time is base over the speed product, and speed 0 halts. Bonuses decay with distance (bonus divided by distance x 0.5 past 1) and with more than two copies (divided by count x 0.4). Specific trades: the catalytic plug raises speed, ember use and fuel use by the same factor; the stirling cuts ember use; the attenuator sets speed from a dial (0 pauses); the siphon reverses a charger; bellows force a hearth coil to plain smelting at speed 2; insulation raises max heat and cuts cooling and ember use per distance. The mini boiler turns ember spent into steam (1 mb per ember by default) and can rupture when its gas tank overflows. Embers 1.4.7 has no steam engine: only a declared research field and lang pages exist (shown absence; reason inferred as leftover or add-on content).
- TA18 (shown, MI `ShapeMatcher#rematch`, `#matches`, `#onBlockUpdate`, `MultiblockMachineBlockEntity`, `ElectricBlastFurnaceBlockEntity`, `OverclockComponent#tick`) Multiblocks are fixed templates rotated by controller facing. A rematch checks every position; a hatch counts where the template allows that hatch type and the hatch is not already claimed by another controller. Any block change in a member position, or chunk load or unload, flags a rematch; mismatching block entities are reported to the client for display. First successful build fires an advancement and an FTB Quests hook. Auto-build exists only as a debug command. Some machines cycle shape variants; the blast furnace has one template per coil (cupronickel up to 32 EU/t recipes, kanthal 128), so tier is a physical rebuild. Gunpowder overclock: x2 for 2400 ticks, ticks stack per multiplier, highest multiplier spent first.
- TA19 (shown, Crossroads `BeamUnit#getValuedRGB`, `EnumBeamAlignments#getAlignment`, `#isDiscovered`, `BeamEffect#performTransmute`) A beam is four numbers (energy, potential, stability, void). Its color is the first three scaled so the largest is 255; its alignment is the first of 11 color boxes (in a fixed order, narrow boxes first) that contains it, else void; any void component flips to the void variant. Rare alignments (time, enchantment) need near-exact ratios because their boxes are tight and checked first. Each alignment is discovered per player through an advancement. A hit first tries data-driven block transmutation for that alignment and power, then the alignment's effect (energy heats or, void, chills). Range 16 blocks, one pulse per 4 ticks; a 64000 power limit is declared but unused.
- TA20 (shown, Crossroads `ReagentMap#addReagent`, `#getTempK`, `AlchemyRec#performReaction`) Alchemy is a mixture with one shared heat pool: temperature is total heat over total quantity, and every reagent added brings its own temperature. A reaction checks, in order: charge if required, catalyst present (not consumed), temperature inside its window. Batches run as many times as reagents and chamber space allow, further capped so the reaction's own heat does not push the mix out of its window. Types: normal; precise (wrong ratio destroys the chamber); destructive (damages the chamber per batch); elemental (consumes three base reagents and picks the product from their ratio through the beam alignment table). Data: 51 reactions (36 normal, 7 elemental, 3 precise, 3 destructive, 1 elemental destructive, 1 other).
- TA21 (shown, Integrated Dynamics `OperatorVariableFacade#validate`, `#getVariable`, `LazyExpression#getValue`) A programmed operator card validates before use and reports, as player text: invalid card, variable not in network, cyclic reference, each input's errors, operator type mismatch, output type not matching the slot. Expressions are lazy and cached, rebuilt on error or network change, and an error marks the expression for invalidation when its cause resolves. Extends TA7: errors are caught both at programming time and at run time.

## System patterns across targets
- SP1 Bottleneck is min over named caps and the UI prints each cap (Create boiler, PNC gauge, Prodigy temperature in/out).
- SP2 Failure leaves evidence (Embers waste pins, ID part error text, DR broadcasts, Create overstress effect).
- SP3 Fixed energy bill, variable rate (MI), versus rate-limited transfer with back-pressure (Embers emitter, PNC air).
- SP4 Wear as compounding risk with replaceable quality tiers (Factory Tech) versus none elsewhere; every other mod has no upkeep.
- SP5 Unlock by discovery rather than by points (Crossroads alignments, Create advancements, Embers codex checkmarks).
- SP6 One retained object with a few numbers, edited by consumables up to a cap (Deep Resonance).
- SP7 Load balancing by cost, not by rule: Create trains pick the cheapest of several same-named stations using occupancy penalties (TA13); Create packagers refuse when queues are full (TA14).
- SP8 Promises count as stock: Create factory gauges add in-flight orders to stock and expire stale ones (TA14), avoiding double orders without a central planner.
- SP9 A hard ceiling that fails loudly: PNC drone overload with a chat reason (TA15), Embers all-copies-off over limit (TA17), Crossroads precise reactions that break the chamber (TA20), MI coil tier refusing a recipe (TA18).
- SP10 Classification by ratio, not amount: Crossroads beam alignment and elemental alchemy map component ratios to one of 11 outcomes, with tight boxes checked first (TA19, TA20).

## YAIRPG fit (proposals only, no implementation)
Grep of src/ shows existing owners: `systems_workshop_content.js`, `systems_workshop_runtime.js`, `systems_workshop_ui.js`, `systems_workshop_validation.js`, `field_workshop.js`, `artifact_workshop_*`, `work_ledger_system.js`, `native_craft_transaction.js`, `mixture_rules.js` and `mixture_state.js`, `action_legibility.js`.
- F1 SP1/TA3 stall reasons belong with `action_legibility.js` and the systems workshop UI: show each limiting cap, not one number.
- F2 TA1 informative-failure alchemy fits `mixture_rules.js` (per-save hidden recipe code, feedback counts on the waste result); keep per-world, per the standing no-fixed-once rule.
- F3 TA2 part wear fits `work_ledger_system.js` or the workshop runtime as an optional per-station part with lifetime and compounding break chance; needs a design owner decision before anything is built, and an auto-restock option to avoid chores.
- F4 TA4/TA9 operating-condition gates (min pressure, temperature window) map to station recipes with condition fields rather than just resource totals.
- F5 TA5 fixed-bill/variable-rate can be a station rule: same total work, more throughput if inputs allow, ramp with consecutive batches.
- F6 TA12/TA14 schedules with OR-of-sequence waits and a promise-aware restock gauge fit `work_ledger_system.js` as a standing order primitive (wait until stock or idle, then deliver), with the named-cause status list feeding `action_legibility.js`.
- F7 TA15 a small pointer-and-label program with a visible true/false trace is a candidate shape for companion or worker routines; the overload-with-reason ceiling avoids silent runaway loops. Owner would be the systems workshop runtime; proposal only.
- F8 TA16/TA20 shared-heat-pool mixtures (temperature = heat over quantity, reaction windows, self-limiting batches) extend `mixture_rules.js` and `mixture_state.js` beyond fixed recipes.
- F9 TA17 upgrade falloff by distance and count, plus all-copies-off over limit, is a readable anti-stacking rule for `artifact_workshop_*` modifiers; per the standing rule, limits stay per-world adjectives, not fixed caps.

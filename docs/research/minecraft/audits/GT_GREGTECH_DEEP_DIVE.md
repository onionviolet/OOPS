# GregTech deep dive: mechanisms worth porting (2026-10-23)

Research and proposals only. Nothing here is an accepted decision, and no code was built or run. Source is static reading of two shallow clones (gitignored) under `docs/research/minecraft/private/gregtech-src-2026-10-23/`: `gtceu-modern/` (GregTech CEu Modern 1.20.1, commit 6ac6cdcf, LGPL-3.0) and `gt5u/` (GT5-Unofficial, GTNH, commit e36e0dd7). Each finding names a mechanism to re-implement, not code to copy (LGPL). Prior pass for context: [TA_TECH_AUTOMATION](TA_TECH_AUTOMATION.md), [SYNTHESIS](SYNTHESIS.md). Receiving owners in this repo: [TECHNOLOGY_AND_PROGRESSION](../../../TECHNOLOGY_AND_PROGRESSION.md), [modular production chains](../../../MODULAR_PRODUCTION_CHAINS_CONSIDERATIONS_2026-10-14.md), [MAKING_RULES](../../../MAKING_RULES.md), [ARCHITECTURE_RULES](../../../ARCHITECTURE_RULES.md).

## Top findings

1. **GT1** One material row generates a whole product family. Properties are the single source; recipes, items and fluids derive from them.
2. **GT2** Tier is one ladder of numbers (voltage x4 per step) and a recipe's tier is a hard gate; overclock trades 4x power for 2x speed (or 4x for 4x).
3. **GT3** Heat tier of a coil block both gates a recipe and discounts its power, with a perfect-overclock bonus above the requirement.
4. **GT4** Faults are legible and physical: over-voltage destroys the cable (not the machine), over-amperage heats, a dry boiler explodes when re-watered.
5. **GT5** Ore processing is a graph with a different byproduct slot per stage, so routing choices change the byproduct mix.
6. **GT6** Bedrock fluid veins deplete by operations toward a floor, never to zero.
7. **GT7** Maintenance is a six-slot fault set that blocks the recipe, accelerates rotor wear and is cured by one consumable or the right tools.

## Part A. Material property system

**GT1 (code, `api/data/chemical/material/properties/*`, `data/recipe/generated/*`, `common/data/materials/*`).**
A material is a builder call: components (a formula), a list of property objects, and flags. Properties seen: Dust, Ingot, Gem, Fluid, Ore, Blast, AlloyBlast, Wire (cable), FluidPipe, ItemPipe, Tool, Armor, Rotor, Polymer, Wood, Hazard. Each property has `verifyProperty`, which auto-adds prerequisites (Blast requires Ingot; AlloyBlast requires Blast and Fluid; Ore requires Dust and makes every named byproduct, wash fluid and separation result get Dust or Fluid; Wire requires Dust and adds a foil flag at IV and up for non-superconductors; Item pipe and Fluid pipe on one material throws). A handler per property then emits recipes: `OreRecipeHandler`, `MaterialRecipeHandler`, `WireRecipeHandler`, `PipeRecipeHandler`, `PartsRecipeHandler`, `ToolRecipeHandler`, `DecompositionRecipeHandler`, `RecyclingRecipeHandler`. Examples: Copper is `ingot, liquid(1358K), ore, cable(MV,1A,loss2), fluidPipe(1696K, 6)`; Tungsten adds `blast(3600K, gas MID, EUt override, 1800 ticks, vacuum stats)` and a plasma-proof pipe.
- Port: a material is a short record of typed capability blocks. Each capability has a prerequisite check that adds missing capabilities and a generator that emits that capability's recipes, items and tools. One row yields ingot, plate, wire, pipe, tool head, rotor and recycling, so adding a material costs one row, not forty recipes.
- Hazard note: a material may carry `HazardProperty(trigger, condition, progressionMultiplier, applyToDerivatives)`. Triggers (inhalation for dust forms, skin contact, consumption, any) map to PPE slots; protection is worn gear that is damaged every 100 ticks while used (`HazardProperty$ProtectionType#damageEquipment`).
- Donor question (YAIRPG): default: a material gets its family from capability blocks, not hand-written recipes. Dial: which capabilities a given world's material table allows. Reason/cost: a capability with no prerequisite met is the in-world reason a material cannot be piped or alloyed (it lacks a liquid state or a refined form), so gaps are explained by the world's chemistry, not by a permission list (AD38).

**GT1b (code, `Material#getMass`, `calculateDecompositionType`).** Mass is the amount-weighted mean of component masses (unlisted formula: 98). Mass multiplies into durations: EBF duration = `max(1, mass x blastTemp / 50)`, alloy-blast duration = `mass x blastTemp / 100`, then x outputAmount x 3/4; vacuum-freeze duration = mass x 3; wire-mill duration = mass; ingot-to-block and similar use mass. So a heavier, hotter-melting material is slower everywhere without per-material tuning. A decomposition type is inferred: all-metal components can be centrifuged, anything else electrolysed, unless flagged off.
- Port: derive process time from one intrinsic scalar (mass or hardness) times one process scalar (temperature, hardness). Override fields stay optional for balance exceptions (`BlastProperty` carries EUt, duration, vacuum EUt and vacuum duration overrides, default -1 = derived).
- Donor question: default: durations derive from material mass and process difficulty. Dial: an override per material. Reason/cost: heavier things take longer because the work done is greater; an exception needs an in-world reason (a catalyst, a rare technique).

**GT1c (code, `BlastProperty$GasTier`).** A blast recipe can carry a gas variant at 0.67x duration: gas tiers LOW nitrogen 1000 mB, MID helium 100 mB, HIGH argon 50 mB, HIGHER neon 25, HIGHEST krypton 10 (a noble-gas ladder; rarer gas, less used). Metals at 5000K and up need liquid helium to cool (`processEBFRecipe`: freeze recipe consumes 500 mB He, returns 250 mB).
- Port: an optional consumable that speeds a process by a fixed ratio, with the consumable's rarity rising with the material's difficulty.

**GT1d (code, `AlloyBlastRecipeProducer`, GCYM).** Alloy blast: for any alloy (2+ components, all components dust or at most two fluids), one recipe is generated from its components: inputs are the component dusts, circuit number = component count (gas variant = count + 10), output fluid = 144 mB per input unit, duration = base x outputAmount x 3/4. This collapses mix-then-smelt into one multiblock step.
- Port: a shortcut recipe auto-derived from an alloy's formula, gated behind a higher-tier machine. Early game mixes by hand; late game derives by formula. Cost: the machine, a circuit (configuration) item, and a hotter coil.

## Part B. Tier ladder

**GT2 (code, `api/GTValues`; docs `Gameplay/Electricity/Cables-and-Transformers.md`).** `V[]` = 8, 32, 128, 512, 2048, 8192, ... x4 per tier, fifteen named tiers: ULV, LV, MV, HV, EV, IV, LuV, ZPM, UV, UHV, UEV, UIV, UXV, OpV, MAX (plus MAX+ to index 30). `VA[]` is `V` minus a quarter-ish step (7, 30, 120, 480, 1920, ...): the standard recipe draw, deliberately just under the tier cap so a tier-N machine runs a tier-N recipe at one amp. Derived names: Basic, Advanced, Elite, Ultimate, Epic, Legendary by tier band.
- What each tier gates (code, `GTMultiMachines`, `GTBedrockFluids`, `FluidDrillMachine`, `CircuitRecipes`, `FusionReactorMachine`, material rows): MV bedrock fluid rig and steel-grade casings; HV titanium rig, parallel hatches from EV (`4^(tier-EV)` parallels), cleanroom circuits (phosphorus wafer, HV), EV naquadah boule and wafers, IV neutronium boule and mainframe circuits, fusion reactor LuV/ZPM/UV (casing mk1-3), superconductor cable lines (LV 2A through UHV 24A), UHV+ deeper. A recipe whose EUt tier exceeds the machine tier is cancelled with "insufficient voltage" (`GTRecipeModifiers#ELECTRIC_OVERCLOCK`).
- Tier is carried by one number on the machine, the cable, the hatch, the recipe and the coil, so every compatibility check is one comparison.
- Donor question: default: each progression era has a tier number; machines, conduits, parts and recipes each declare theirs and one comparison gates them. Dial: how steep the step is (GT uses x4 voltage per tier; a YAIRPG era might be x2 or custom per world). Reason/cost: higher tiers cost rarer materials in every part, so the gate is felt as material access, not a level counter.

**GT2b (stated, `Cables-and-Transformers.md`).** Transformers convert 1A at tier N+1 to 4A at tier N (variants 2x, 4x, 16x hi-amp). Diodes limit amps through a block (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 A). Voltage loss is subtractive per block per amp, so moving fewer higher-voltage amps over distance loses less (the doc's worked example: 16A LV over 20 blocks of 16x tin cable loses 68.75%; the same power stepped up to MV over 4x annealed copper loses 15.6%).
- Port: a transport rule where loss is per hop and independent of volume, so concentrating throughput into fewer, higher-grade links beats many cheap ones. Costs: converters at both ends.

## Part C. Recipes, overclock, parallel, modifiers

**GT3 (code, `api/recipe/OverclockingLogic`).** Standard overclock loop: for each spare tier (`OCs = machineTier - recipeTier`, one fewer from ULV), multiply EUt by 4 and duration by 0.5 (non-perfect) or 0.25 (perfect), stopping if EUt exceeds the machine voltage or duration would drop below 1 tick. Sub-tick variants: once duration would fall below 1 tick, each further overclock instead multiplies parallels by 2 (or 4 for perfect) at the same 4x power, capped by the maximum parallel allowed. Fusion uses a half-step logic (EUt x2, duration x0.5). Heating coil overclock: perfect overclocks count = `(machineTemp - recipeTemp) / 900 / 2`, taken first; EUt is also multiplied by `0.95^((machineTemp - recipeTemp)/900)` when the recipe needs at least 900K. EBF machine temperature = coil temperature + 100K per tier above MV.
- Port: speed purchased with power in fixed ratios, with extra headroom (surplus tier, surplus heat) converted into either speed or efficiency. The choice of ratio (2x speed per 4x power versus 4x per 4x) is one boolean per machine class.
- Donor question: default: spare capacity speeds a process by a fixed ratio at a worse-than-linear power cost. Dial: ratio per machine class (standard, perfect, half-step). Reason/cost: you pay in power (the world's real constraint) or in tier materials; perfect classes exist only where a specialist structure (hotter coil) justifies it.

**GT3b (code, `GTRecipeModifiers`).** Recipe modifiers are small functions `(machine, recipe) -> ModifierFunction` composed in a list per machine (`recipeModifiers(a, b, c)`). A `ModifierFunction` scales duration, EUt, content amounts, parallels, or cancels with a reason. Examples: `hatchParallel` (multiplies contents and EUt by the hatch's parallel count, limited by what inputs exist and output space), `batchMode` (if duration < 100 ticks, runs `100/duration` copies as one longer recipe to cut tick load), `ebfOverclock` (cancels if coil temperature too low, with a visible reason), `crackerOverclock` (EUt x(1 - 0.1 x coilTier)), `pyrolyseOvenOverclock` (duration x 4/3 at tier 0, `2/(tier+1)` above), `multiSmelterParallel` (32 x coil level parallels; EUt from parallels and coil discount), `ENVIRONMENT_REQUIREMENT` (duration x(1 + strength x 5 / maxAllowed), cancelled above the limit), `SPOILAGE_TRANSFER` (output keeps the input's spoil progress).
- `ParallelLogic#getParallelAmount` clamps parallels by input availability, then by simulated output space, using a binary search (`adjustMultiplier`). Failures record a reason ("insufficient input: X", "insufficient output: X").
- Port: a pipeline of pure modifiers over a base recipe. Because each modifier either rewrites numbers or cancels with a message, you get tiering, coil bonuses, environment penalties and parallelism without special-casing machines, and the failure reason is always shown (matches the show-the-bottleneck pattern X3 in SYNTHESIS).
- Donor question: default: machine behaviour = base recipe plus an ordered list of modifiers. Dial: which modifiers a given structure carries. Reason/cost: each modifier maps to a part you built (coil, hatch, filter), so "why is it slow" always points at a part.

**GT3c (code, `api/recipe/condition`, `common/recipe/condition/*`).** Conditions are separate objects checked every tick: Adjacent block, Adjacent fluid, Biome, Biome tag, Cleanroom, Daytime, Dimension, EU-to-start, Environmental hazard, Position Y, Raining, Thunder, Research, Vent (muffler front face must be free), plus quest/stage hooks (FTB Quests, Heracles, Game Stages). A failing condition sets the machine WAITING with the reason; the recipe's progress regresses to 1 by default when waiting (`RecipeLogic#regressRecipe`; the large turbine turns that off), and a multiblock that stays underpowered for five attempts goes SUSPEND with a runDelay of `attempts x 60` ticks (`handleRecipeWorking`).
- Port: conditions as small named predicates that attach to any recipe and report a plain-language reason. Environmental conditions (day, rain, altitude, adjacent liquid) are cheap to add and make placement matter.
- Donor question: default: a recipe may carry named conditions; each failing condition shows a reason and pauses the work. Dial: whether progress regresses, holds or ends the batch. Reason/cost: the world's weather, place and time already exist in the idle sim.

**GT3d (code, `api/recipe/chance/logic/ChanceLogic`).** Chanced outputs are out of 10,000. Logics: OR (each entry independent, deterministic share for batches: `times x chance / 10000` guaranteed copies, then roll the remainder), AND (all or none), XOR (one winner), FIRST (deprecated). A per-recipe chance cache stores the failed chance: `newChance/2 + cached` carries over (a pity timer that half-keeps near misses). No tier-based chance boost exists in this version (not found in `api/recipe` or `common`; earlier GT versions had one, inferred).
- Port: byproduct chance with a half-banked pity so long-run yield tracks the stated percentage and a very unlucky streak is smoothed.
- Donor question: default: byproduct odds with a banked pity carry. Dial: carry fraction (GT uses 1/2). Reason/cost: pity softens variance without changing averages, which suits an idle game with offline progress.

## Part D. Multiblocks, hatches, power

**GT4 (code, `common/data/machines/GTMultiMachines.java`, `api/multiblock/*`).** Pattern: a builder lays out slices of characters with predicates per character: `blocks(...)` with min/max counts (`setMinGlobalLimited(9)` casings), `abilities(PartAbility.X)` for hatches, `autoAbilities(recipeType)` which derives needed hatch kinds from the recipe type's IO shape (so the casing slots accept item, fluid, energy hatches automatically), `heatingCoils()` (any coil block, the machine reads the coil type), `air()`, `controller()`. Structure check yields a typed error showing the missing position. EBF: 3x3x4 with invar casings (minimum 9), coils, a muffler on the top, hatches anywhere on the casing shell (`autoAbilities`), maintenance required. Cleanroom is expandable (min depth 3, radius 2; max depth 14, radius 7), has filter casings, a door predicate, bans listed machines inside (generators, miners, muffler machines, primitives), and its clean amount (0-100) must reach 95 for recipes (`CleanroomMachine#CLEAN_AMOUNT_THRESHOLD`).
- Port: a built structure with named part slots; the parts present define the machine's traits (coil -> temperature and discount, hatch -> ability, filter -> cleanliness). The player sees the pattern as a recipe-like card.
- Donor question: default: bigger machines are a layout of typed parts the player builds, and parts carry the numbers. Dial: how strict the layout is (fixed shell, or flexible with counts). Reason/cost: space, materials and the labor of building are the price of throughput.

**GT4b (code, `common/blocks`, `CoilBlock$CoilType`).** Coil ladder, temperature K / level / energy discount: cupronickel 1800/1/1, kanthal 2700/2/1, nichrome 3600/2/2, RTM alloy 4500/4/2, HSSG 5400/4/4, naquadah 7200/8/4, trinium 9001/8/8, tritanium 10800/16/8. `level` x 32 = multi-smelter parallels; `energyDiscount` divides smelter power; pyrolysis oven speed = 75% at tier 0, `50 x (tier+1)`% above; cracker EUt = `100 - 10 x tier`%.
- Port: one part ladder whose single block type feeds several machine formulas, so one upgrade materially improves several machines.

**GT4c (code, `EnergyHatchPartMachine`, `NotifiableEnergyContainer#acceptEnergyFromNetwork`).** An energy hatch has tier, IO direction and amperage. Buffer = V x 16 x amps (input) or V x 64 x amps (output). Feeding a voltage above the receiver's input voltage destroys it by explosion with power `tier + 1` (`GTUtil#getExplosionPower`), if the machine has the explosion trait; the machine accepts amperes up to its input amperage each tick. Weather and fluid-adjacent machines can explode: `EnvironmentalExplosionTrait` rolls 1/1000 per tick when adjacent to fluid, and in rain 1/10 x 1/1000 for explosion, else 1/1000 fire (thunder raises the chance of the explosion path); gated by config.
- Port: a power contract per port (voltage, amps), enforced by consequences instead of silent clamping. Voltage mismatch downward = machine runs slower or waits; upward = loss of the part.
- Donor question: default: over-voltage destroys the weakest part on the path, not the whole base. Dial: severity (part lost, part damaged, jammed until repaired). Reason/cost: the world itself is dangerous to power; a protective part (diode, fuse cable) is the player's tool.

**GT4d (code, `FusionReactorMachine`).** Heat buffer: a recipe has `eu_to_start`; the reactor stores energy in an internal container sized `inputs x 2^(tier-LuV) x 10,000,000` EU, spends it to raise `heat` to the recipe's start value, and idles down 10,000 heat per tick when unused. A later recipe with a lower start value runs without paying again; a higher one tops up the difference. Fusion recipes carry the start value in recipe data, and the fusion tier names scale (mk1 LuV, mk2 ZPM, mk3 UV).
- Port: warm-up as stored state, so switching between related processes is cheap but cold starts and long idle cost a one-time bill.
- Donor question: default: big reactors have a charged state that decays when idle. Dial: decay rate. Reason/cost: heat leaks; running continuously is rewarded, switching is not free.

**GT4e (code, `common/machine/multiblock/steam/*`, `api/machine/steam/SteamBoilerMachine`, `common/machine/multiblock/generator/LargeTurbineMachine`).** Steam age rules: boiler temperature climbs 1 per 12 ticks (24 ticks off-peak), steam appears at 100K and above and scales with temperature / max temperature / 2; no water while hot, then adding water causes an explosion (power 2.0, `hasNoWater` flag). Large boiler: bronze/steel/titanium/tungstensteel with max temperature and heat speed from config, throttle 1-100%, 160 steam per mB water. Steam multiblocks (`SteamParallelMultiblockMachine`): up to 8 parallels, duration x1.5, EUt scaled `0.8888 x parallels` and capped at 32 EU/t, only recipes of tier LV or below (`GTRecipeModifiers`-style cancel), steam as energy at conversion 2 steam per EU. Large turbine: output = V x 2 x rotor power % / 100, rotor speed +1/tick, -3/tick idle, production boost = (speed/maxSpeed)^2, duration x rotor efficiency, rotor damage per cycle = 1 + number of maintenance problems.
- Port: an early power plant with a real hazard (run dry, then refill and it blows), a throttle dial, and a rotor part with spin-up, efficiency and wear. Maintenance feeds rotor wear (couples two systems).

**GT4f (code, `AssemblyLineMachine`, `ResearchRecipeBuilder`, `ResearchCondition`).** Assembly line recipes require ordered item input (each input must sit in its own slot in bus order, `checkItemInputs` matches the Nth recipe item to the Nth bus), plus a research condition: a data item with the recipe's research id, produced by a scanner recipe (60 s, HV default) or research station recipe (LuV default, total CWU = per-tick compute x 4000) from a consumed research item. Data lives on data sticks read by data-access hatches; compute is a separate resource provided by computation hatches.
- Port: a late recipe family unlocked by a physical knowledge item made by consuming a sample, not by a global unlock. Ordered inputs reward a deliberate layout.
- Donor question: default: advanced crafts need a produced, portable data item. Dial: whether data is consumed, copyable, or tied to a location. Reason/cost: the sample you destroy to learn it, plus compute time.

## Part E. Ore, vein and byproduct graph

**GT5 (code, `data/recipe/generated/OreRecipeHandler`).** Stages and exact numbers. Ore block (or raw ore): forge hammer 10 ticks 16 EU -> crushed x(ore multiplier x double-drop); macerator 400 ticks 2 EU -> crushed x2 + 14% byproduct[0] (+ 67% stone-type secondary). Crushed: macerate -> impure dust, wash (water 1000 mB circuit 1 -> purified + 1/3 byproduct[0] + stone dust; 100 mB fast variant circuit 2 8 ticks; distilled 100 mB 200 ticks), thermal centrifuge -> refined crushed + byproduct[1] (x multiplier) + stone dust, chemical bath with the material's `washedIn` fluid (70% byproduct[3], 40% stone). Purified: macerate -> pure dust + 14% byproduct[1]; sift (gem materials): normal chances exquisite 3%, flawless 10%, gem 35%, dust 50%, flawed 25%, chipped 35% (high-output flag: 5, 15, 50, 25, 20, 30). Refined: macerate -> dust + 14% byproduct[2]. Impure dust: centrifuge 200 ticks 4 EU -> dust + 1/9 byproduct[0]. Pure dust: centrifuge 100 ticks -> dust + 1/9 byproduct[1]; electromagnetic separator -> up to two separated materials at 10% and 50% (nugget x2 at 20% when the last separation target is a plain low-temperature metal). Smelting is direct only for materials without a blast property. Raw ore blocks compress 9:1 by hand or machine.
- Byproduct list index = stage: byproduct[0] at crush/macerate, [1] at purified and thermal, [2] at refined, [3] at bath. A material usually names up to four, with the last index clamped to the list length (`OreProperty#getOreByProduct`).
- Port: a stage graph where each route has its own byproduct slot and cost (power, a fluid, time); choosing the route changes what you also collect. The "also collect" side is where specialization lives: a chain that makes copper also makes the sulfur another chain needs.
- Donor question: default: raw material refines through three to five stages; each stage has a different side yield. Dial: how many stages a world has. Reason/cost: each stage costs a machine, a fluid (water, a bath) and time; skipping a stage loses the byproduct, not the main product.

**GT5b (code, `common/data/GTOres`, `api/data/worldgen/*`, docs `Gameplay/Ore-Generation.md`).** Veins are large defined deposits: `clusterSize` uniform int (e.g. 40-52 blocks), `density` 0.15-1.0, `weight` (spawn lottery weight), a layer (stone, deepslate, netherrack, end stone), a height range, biomes, and a generator type. Generators: layered (stacked material layers with weights and sizes), classic (primary, secondary, between, sporadic), veined (ore blocks with weights plus a rare block at a chance and a richness/edge-roundoff curve), dike (height-keyed vertical blocks). Surface indicators (a rock of the vein's main material placed over the vein, with a radius, density and placement) let a player prospect from the surface. Layers differ by depth/dimension, so progression moves the player downward and between worlds.
- Port: a few big named deposits per layer with a material list and a surface tell, not scattered single ores. Weighted lottery per region.
- Donor question: default: a world region has named deposits with surface indicators. Dial: indicator reliability (always visible, only in some biomes, needs a tool). Reason/cost: reading the surface is the cheap prospecting skill; a tool or scan makes it deeper.

**GT6 (code, `GTBedrockFluids`, `BedrockFluidVeinSavedData`, `FluidDrillLogic`, `FluidDrillMachine`).** Bedrock fluid veins: one per 8x8-chunk square, picked by seeded lottery over weights (oil 20, light oil 25, heavy oil 15 with biome bonuses, raw oil 20, natural gas 15, salt water weight 0 but +150..+200 in oceans; nether lava 65, nether gas 35). Yield is a seeded roll in [min, max] (e.g. light oil 175-300 mB per cycle); the vein tracks operations remaining, starting at 100,000. Each drill cycle (20 ticks) depletes with probability `1/chance` by `depletionAmount` 1: MV chance 1 (every cycle), HV 2 (every other), EV 8 (one in eight). Output per cycle = `max(depletedYield, yield x remaining / 100000)` x rig multiplier (MV 1, HV 16, EV 64) x 1.5 when the energy tier exceeds the rig tier. Depleted yield floors at 15-40 mB. The yield is never zero.
- Port: a fixed regional deposit that slowly drains toward a small permanent trickle, with higher-tier rigs trading faster extraction for slower per-cycle depletion (an EV rig yields 64x but depletes 8x less often). The visible total is the floor, not zero.
- Donor question: default: a deposit has a rich phase that decays to a permanent trickle. Dial: depletion curve and floor. Reason/cost: pressure drops; better machines drain slower per unit extracted, so upgrades are both throughput and conservation.

## Part F. Logistics: pipes, covers, cables

**GT7 (code, `common/blockentity/CableBlockEntity`, `EnergyNetHandler`; stated, `Cables-and-Transformers.md`).** Cables: per-material `WireProperties(voltage, amperage, lossPerBlock, superconductor?)`. Over-voltage: the cable's voltage is clamped to its own maximum and the cable heats by `ln(tierDifference) x 45 + 36.5` per transfer (`acceptEnergyFromNetwork`), so the cable is the sacrificial fuse (stated). Over-amperage: heat += `(avgAmps - maxAmps) x 40` per tick; temperature cools by `(T - 293)^0.35` per tick if no new heat; at 1500K an insulated cable loses insulation with 10% chance per tick (becomes bare wire); at 3000K the block turns to fire. Loss: subtracted per block (volt) along the route, the route list sorted by distance, with path max-loss tested. Superconductor wires have zero loss and need a critical temperature (e.g. 78 for early, 30, 5, 3).
- Port: conduit capacity with graduated failure (heat, strip insulation, burn) and a bounded cooling curve. The failure state is visible and fixable and gives the player time to react.
- Donor question: default: overload heats a conduit and degrades it in steps. Dial: cool-down rate and step thresholds. Reason/cost: you pay for thicker, better-made conduit or a diode/splitter; ignoring it costs repairs.

**GT7b (code, `FluidPipeBlockEntity#checkAndDestroy`; `FluidPipeProperties`; stated, `Pipes.md`).** Fluid pipes: material gives max temperature, throughput, channels (up to 9), flags gas-proof, acid-proof (a containment predicate set per fluid attribute), cryo-proof, plasma-proof. A pipe is destroyed or damaged if the fluid is hotter than the pipe allows, is a gas in a non-gasproof pipe (leaks: 10% voided per step plus area heat/hazard), is cryogenic in a non-cryo pipe (shatters), carries an attribute the pipe cannot contain (corrodes), or is plasma in a non-plasma pipe (melts). Each pipe block is a tank of `20 x throughput` mB; every 5 ticks it pushes up to half its capacity to the connections it did not just receive from, split by fullness. Item pipes teleport items along a route limited by the smallest segment's per-second rate, choose the destination by lowest summed priority (small pipes have higher priority than big ones, restrictive variants have 100x), and shutter sides to make them one-way.
- Port: carriers whose material limits the substance, not just the rate: temperature, phase, corrosion. A carrier mismatch ruins the carrier or the cargo.
- Donor question: default: containers and conduits declare what they can hold (heat, phase, corrosive). Dial: which properties apply in a world. Reason/cost: the chemistry of the world; the tell is a leak, a smoking pipe or a shattered one.

**GT7c (code, `common/cover/ConveyorCover`).** Covers are a per-face attachment layer on any machine or pipe: conveyor (rate = `2 x 4^min(tier, LuV)` items/s), pump (fluid; clamped to pipe throughput x 20), robot arm (exact counts), filters (item and fluid), regulators, shutter, storage, voiding, detectors, machine-controller (can prevent power-fail suspension), and wireless/ender variants. Rates scale by tier, so a tier upgrade is a cover swap.
- Port: automation as small attachments with a tier-set rate, instead of whole-machine rewrites.

## Part G. Maintenance, circuits, hazards

**GT8 (code, `MaintenanceHatchPartMachine`; config `maintenanceCheckRate` 1000 ticks default).** Six independent problem bits (wrench, screwdriver, soft mallet, hard hammer, wire cutters, crowbar). Active time accrues; every `rate = checkRate / timeMultiplier` ticks of work, a 1-in-6000 roll (not per tick: per check) breaks one random problem and removes the "taped" state. While any problem exists the recipe is cancelled (`modifyRecipe` returns null) and the recipe is marked dirty. Fix with duct tape (all six at once, from the hatch's tape slot every 20 ticks or the player's inventory) or with the matching tool (damages the tool by 1). Configurable maintenance hatch (HV) shifts `durationMultiplier` in [0.9, 1.1]: lower duration multiplier makes the time multiplier `-20 x m + 21` (faster work, more frequent breaks), higher uses `-8 x m + 9`. The auto-maintenance hatch (`AutoMaintenanceHatchPartMachine`) removes the chore, cleaning hatch is a separate part. Rotor wear = 1 + problem count (see GT4e).
- Port: a maintenance state that blocks work, with two cures (consumable for convenience, tool for thrift) and a buyable trade of speed against fault rate. Late game buys the chore away with a part.
- Donor question: default: big machines need periodic upkeep, cheap to fix, with a convenience cost. Dial: break rate and whether a part can automate it. Reason/cost: the upkeep consumable and attention; automation costs materials.

**GT9 (code, `data/recipe/misc/CircuitRecipes`, `common/data/GTItems`).** Circuit tiers form a chain: electronic (LV, MV), integrated (LV, MV, HV), processor, processor assembly, workstation, mainframe (IV), and higher quantum tiers beyond. Silicon boule (blast 1784K, 9000 ticks MV) -> wafers (16) -> engraved by lens type (red = logic, green = RAM, light blue = CPU, blue = ULPIC) at 900 ticks MV; phosphorus boule (2484K, HV, nitrogen gas) gives 32 wafers and 4x yield per wafer inside a cleanroom; naquadah boule (5400K, EV, argon) 64 wafers, 8x; neutronium boule (6484K, IV, xenon) 96 wafers, 16x, engrave 50 ticks. Cleanroom-made "system on chip" lines raise yield (microprocessor LV: 2 or 3 per craft by hard-recipe config; SoC route 3 or 6). Circuits are consumed as both component and as a configuration slot (`circuitMeta(n)`) that selects among recipes on the same machine. Soldering fluid and a `solderMultiplier` scale tin/lead use by tier. The `harderCircuitRecipes` flag halves outputs.
- Port: a quality ladder where one scarce intermediate (a wafer) has yields that multiply by cleanliness and by boule grade, so the same craft is cheap after the upgrade. Plus a "program number" slot that picks among a machine's recipes without more machines.
- Donor question: default: the pivotal intermediate has grades; a cleaner workshop multiplies its yield. Dial: yield multiplier per grade (GT: x1, x4, x8, x16). Reason/cost: cleanliness is a built room with filters and banned contaminants; the grade needs a rarer input and a hotter furnace.

**GT10 (code, `GTMedicalConditions`, `EnvironmentalHazardSavedData`, `MufflerPartMachine`, `EnvironmentalHazardEmitterTrait`; config `environmentalHazards` default false, decay 0.001 per chunk per tick, spread only above strength 1000).** Pollution is a per-chunk "hazard zone" with a condition type and strength; it spreads to neighbours above 1000, decays slowly, and raises a condition progression for players in it (`progressCondition(strength/1000)`). Each finished multiblock cycle emits through its muffler (`2.5 / max(tier,1)` per cycle for carbon monoxide); a muffler of tier T also recovers waste items with chance `10 x T`% over `(T+1)^2` slots and requires its front face free (`VentCondition`). Recipes in some GCYM mega-multis apply a duration penalty `1 + strength x 5 / limit` (cancelled above the limit). Medical conditions carry symptoms, an idle progression type (heal or none), and optionally an emitted byproduct recipe modifier (chemical burns add acid outputs). Hazardous materials in item form trigger by contact class (inhalation for dusts, skin for dusts and tiny dusts, any for gases).
- Port: an environment ledger per region fed by machine output, damped by upgrades (higher-tier muffler = less emission, better recovery), with a consequence that bites production (slower) before it bites the player.
- Donor question: default: industry leaves a local trace that raises costs for nearby work. Dial: spread, decay and whether the player is affected. Reason/cost: a clean-up part (muffler, scrubber), or relocation.

## Part H. GT5U differences

Source: GT5-Unofficial `src/main/java` (paths under `gregtech/` unless noted), read by a bounded subagent, with spot checks by me (overclock constants, pollution decay, spread and thresholds matched the notes). Full coded notes with extra numbers: `~/Library/Application Support/Claude/side-session-notes/local_ee5cf879-d3b0-42c5-839b-9da58b7cbd29/gt5u_notes.md` (G1-G57, outside the repo). **GT11 (code, `api/enums/GTValues` V[], VP, AatV; stated, quest chapter list).** GT5U has 16 tiers: ULV 8, LV 32, MV 128, HV 512, EV 2048, IV 8192, LuV 32768, ZPM 131072, UV 524288, UHV, UEV, UIV, UMV, UXV, MAX at 2^31-8 (x4 per step). A "practical" voltage `V x 30/32` leaves ~6% headroom for loss. Maximum amps per tier fall as tier rises (LV 67,108,863 down to 1 at MAX, UV 4095, UHV 1023), so top tiers exist effectively as one-amp tiers that cannot be spammed in parallel. Pacing spine = quest chapters, with counts: Stone Age 71, Steam 99, LV 144, MV 142, HV 108, EV 85, IV 130, LuV 77, ZPM 65, UV 42, UHV 33, UEV 54, UIV 46, UMV 44, then endgame 29, with side lines (oil, power, space race 140, nuclear, bees, mass processing) in parallel. Quest count is a rough proxy for content density (inferred): most onboarding at LV and MV, a second bulge at IV.
- Port: a ladder with a spine of authored chapters (one per tier), not a level counter, plus parallel side lines. Cost to note: the density curve is an authored choice; YAIRPG can compress the tiers the player rushes.
- Donor question: default: each era is one tier chapter with side lines. Dial: how many eras and how dense the chapters. Reason/cost: the player only moves up when the previous era's part set exists.

**GT12 (code, `common/tileentities/BaseMetaTileEntity#injectEnergyUnits`, `OverclockCalculator`).** Failure is asymmetric: over-voltage explodes the machine (power rising with tier; `doEnergyExplosion` also explodes if stored EU is at least 1/5 capacity), under-voltage only makes it slower or unable to run. Overclock: EU x4 and duration /2 per step (perfect: /4); OC count = floor(log4(machinePower / max(recipePower, 32))); heat OC: `min(OCs, (machineHeat - recipeHeat)/1800)` steps divide duration by 4 instead of 2, and EU is x0.95 per 900K of surplus; laser OC adds steps costing (4 + 0.3 x (n+1)) each, steeper per step; tier skip lets a machine accept recipes up to one tier above itself by default (`maxTierSkip` 1); sub-tick durations convert to extra parallel instead of being wasted; parallel = min(max parallel, input-limited, EU-limited, output room), with "void protection" refusing to run when outputs would be lost.
- Port: the GT5U calculator is the same family as GT3. The new idea is tier skip (a dial to run slightly-too-big recipes at some price), and void protection as an explicit machine setting.
- Donor question: default: a machine may run a recipe one tier above its own at a penalty. Dial: how many tiers of skip and the penalty. Reason/cost: more power draw and wear; the in-world reason is a crude run past the rating.

**GT13 (code, `common/pollution/Pollution`, `GTProxy` rates, `MTEMultiBlockBase#polluteEnvironment`, `MTEHatchMuffler`; stated, config `Pollution.cfg`).** Pollution is an integer per chunk, processed once per 1200 ticks: x0.9945 decay (about 0.55%/min); spreads only above 400,000, moving diff/20 into each neighbour whose value is below about 5/6 of this chunk's. Thresholds: smog 550,000 (weakness, slowness, mining fatigue; duration min(P/1000, 1000) ticks), poison 750,000 (confusion, poison, blindness, hunger), vegetation 1,000,000 (random blocks per chunk per cycle destroyed: leaves, grass to dirt, crops, plants), sour rain 2,000,000 (cobble to gravel to sand). Hazmat gear blocks the entity effects; creative players are exempt. Rates per second: coke oven 10, charcoal pit 100, primitive blast furnace 200, large boilers bronze/steel/titanium/tungstensteel 200/400/800/1600, EBF 400, large combustion engine 480, extreme combustion engine 3840, implosion compressor 10,000; base gas turbine and diesel generators have a per-tier release multiplier table {0.1, 1.0, 0.9, 0.8, 0.7, 0.6}, and a "throttle" divides release by 24 (wrong, see V3: each circuit step removes one 24th). Multiblocks vent in 10,000 chunks through a muffler; no muffler, or one with a blocked front, stops the machine. Muffler tier >= 2 passes `100 - 12.5 x (tier - 1)`% (tier 9 passes 0). The chunk's pollution is shown by tinting grass, foliage and water. GTNH ships its own values (explosion pollution 333 instead of 100,000; diesel generators 40 instead of 200).
- Port: a regional ledger with smooth decay, diffusion only above a high floor and four stacked thresholds, each adding a new, different harm. Mitigation has three levers (muffler grade, gear, distance).
- Donor question: default: heavy industry builds a local trace that raises friction on nearby living work. Dial: decay rate, spread floor, harm list, whether harm hits the player or only the land. Reason/cost: the cure is a better exhaust part or moving the industry away from the farm and home.

**GT14 (code, `MTEMultiBlockBase#getRepairStatus`, `doRandomMaintenanceDamage`, `MTEHatchMaintenance`; stated, `MachineStats.cfg`).** Maintenance differs from the CEu all-or-nothing model: six tools (wrench, screwdriver, soft mallet, hard hammer, soldering iron, crowbar), each missing tool costs 10% efficiency (efficiency cap `10000 - missing x 1000`); the machine stops only when all six are broken. Roughly every 1000 running ticks, 1/6000 chance one tool breaks (a nag, not a block). Duct tape fixes all six; the auto-maintenance hatch consumes 4 duct tape, 2 lubricant cells, 4 steel screws and 2 HV circuits every 100 ticks. Efficiency itself is a state variable that rises after each cycle and drops 1000 per idle check; a TecTech multi's energy use = base x 10000 / max(1000, efficiency). Controller-slot part wear per 1000 ticks: `partFactor x min(EU/5, EU^0.6)`.
- Port: graded degradation (each fault costs 10%) rather than a switch, so ignoring upkeep is a visible, tolerable tax. Automation is a purchasable part whose upkeep inputs are exactly the chore.
- Donor question: default: neglect costs a fixed percentage per fault. Dial: percent per fault, whether a total failure state exists. Reason/cost: the slow decline is felt in output; the automation part costs the same consumables you would have spent by hand.

**GT15 (code, `api/enums/HeatingCoilLevel`).** Coil heat is `1 + 900 x ordinal`: cupronickel 1801, kanthal 2701, nichrome 3601, TPV 4501, HSS-G 5401, HSS-S 6301, naquadah 7201, naquadah alloy 8101, trinium 9001, electrum-flux 9901, awakened draconium 10801, infinity 11701, hypogen 12601, eternal 13501. Each extra 900K = 5% EU discount, each extra 1800K = a 4x-speed overclock instead of 2x (see GT12). Same shape as the CEu table (GT4b), with 14 steps instead of 8.

**GT16 (code, `tectech/*`, `MTEResearchStation`, `TTMultiblockBase`; `AssemblyLineRecipes`).** Research is a computation resource: required computation for a scan = `researchTime x 2^(tier-1)`; a data feed that drops gets 20 ticks of grace, then 80 ticks of progressive progress loss, then total failure at 100 ticks (the machine still draws energy during the timeout). Any TecTech multi lacking required data stops after a 100-tick timeout ("computation loss"). Assembly line recipe shape: a research item (the previous-tier part) is scanned (1 minute, one tier below); item N is built on the assembly line at tier N-1 power from a scanned item N-1 (LuV motor: samarium magnetic rod, HSS-S long rods, 128 fine wire, YBCO cable, Indalloy, 250 mB lubricant, 30 s). Parametrized multis refuse to run when parameters are outside an acceptable range, with LED states (unused, ok, low, high). The uncertainty hatch holds a 16-cell matrix of random values 0-999 regenerated on mode change; the player sets a selection so the matrix balances (sum of mirrored differences below a limit) to clear the "uncertain" status. Hatch families: laser tunnel energy and dynamo, multi-amp energy and dynamo, wireless energy and data, data in/out and racks (a quantum computer), capacitor.
- Port: computation as a flow resource that can fail with a grace window; knowledge items that need the previous tier's flagship part as the sample; a small puzzle gate on a late structure (balance a hidden vector).
- Donor question: default: late crafts need a "scan" of the previous tier's part. Dial: grace window and penalty for lost data. Reason/cost: a tier-lower power draw and a minute of time per scan; the compute stream must stay connected.

**GT17 (code, `gtnhintergalactic/.../TileEntitySpaceElevator`, `elevatormodules`, `spaceprojects`).** The space elevator is a central structure with 5 motor casing tiers; module slots unlock at {6, 12, 15, 18, 24} slots by motor tier; modules need a minimum motor tier and run only recipes with module tier at or below their own; module overclocking is disabled (fixed parallels: assembler modules 4, 16, 64; pump modules 4, 4 with more recipes, 64). Tiers: miner modules at UV, UHV, UEV; assembler modules UHV, UIV, UXV; pump UHV, UEV, UXV; manager and research modules at UMV.
- Port: a late hub whose upgrades are physical blocks and modules (not menus), with fixed parallels instead of overclock, which keeps endgame throughput legible.
- Donor question: default: the late game is one hub with slots opened by a part grade. Dial: slot counts and parallel per module. Reason/cost: each module needs its own top-tier input and the hub's motor casing.

**GT18 (code, `loaders/postload/chains/*`, `ChemicalRecipes`, `ProcessingOre`, `ProcessingDirty`, `ProcessingCrushedOre`).** Long chains seen end to end:

**GT19 (code, `common/GTWorldgenerator`, `WorldgenGTOreLayer`, `common/UndergroundOil`, `GTUOFluid`).** Ore veins seed per 3x3-chunk cell (only chunks where the chunk coordinate mod 3 is 1 can seed); roll under the dimension's vein chance; vein has weight, density, size in chunks (max 2), 9 stacked layers each with its own ore slot, local density falls with distance from the seed (`density / int(sqrt(2 + dx^2 + dz^2))`).

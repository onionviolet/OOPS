## Top findings

Grouped by mod; cross-mod patterns follow.

### Mekanism

- **TB1** (shown, `UpgradeUtils`, Mekanism upgrade JSON) **Upgrade chips as exponent dials.** M = 10. Speed, energy and chemical upgrades each cap at 8 installs; fraction f = installed/8. Craft time = base x M^(-f_speed). Energy per tick = base x M^(2 f_speed - f_energy). Full speed is 10x faster for 100x the draw per tick (10x per craft); full energy upgrades alone cut draw 10x; both together net 10x speed at 10x draw, so per-craft energy returns to base. Buffer is 4 ticks of use. The electrolytic separator is a deliberate exception (2^n operations per tick), so designers can pick a different curve per machine (shown, `TileEntityElectrolyticSeparator#recalculateUpgrades`). **Mechanism to port:** every speed-up is bought by an offsetting dial that costs a different resource, with a hard cap, so the total ceiling (10x) is knowable up front. Pairs with TB33 and TB34 (Thermal chips) and TB12 (IE overclock).
- **TB2** (shown, Mekanism `recipe/processing/**` JSON) **Ore multiplier ladder where each rung needs a new supply line.** One ore gives 2 dust (enrich), 3 (purify, 1 mB oxygen per tick), 4 (inject, hydrogen chloride per tick), or 5 (dissolve with sulfuric acid, wash with 5 mB water per 1 mB slurry, crystallize 200 mB per crystal, then HCl). Every intermediate (clump, shard, crystal, dirty dust) is a storable item that re-enters the chain at its own stage. Every step runs 200 ticks at 1 mB per tick of reagent. **Mechanism:** processing grade as a ladder: +1 yield per ore per rung, each rung adds a machine and a reagent line, half-products are re-enterable, and the reagent is itself manufactured (TB3).
- **TB3** (shown, Mekanism `chemical_conversion`, `separator`, `oxidizing`, `crystallizing`, `evaporating` recipe JSON) **Reagent supply tree with 10:1 dilution loops.** Flint 1 gives 10 mB oxygen; salt dust 1 gives 2 mB HCl or 15 mB brine; sulfur dust 1 gives 2 mB acid; 10 water gives 1 brine; 15 brine crystallizes back to 1 salt, closing a loop. Losses at each 10:1 step make volume the real cost. **Mechanism:** a tier-gated input is itself a production chain, and the chain has recycling loops, so scale (not unlock) is the limit.
- **TB4** (shown, `MultiblockCache#merge`, `FormationProtocol`) **Rebuild-safe multiblocks.** Re-forming issues a new inventory ID and merges all old caches found on frame blocks; contents that no longer fit are rejected (items drop near the nearest player within 25 blocks; rejected chemicals vent, and radioactive ones become radiation). Capacity scales with enclosed volume (boiler water 16,000 mB per volume cell, steam 160,000). Default bounds 3 to 18 per side; evaporation 4x(3..18)x4, SPS 7^3, fusion 5^3. **Mechanism:** a facility can be rebuilt or expanded without losing contents, but overflow has a real world cost.
- **TB5** (shown, `RadiationUtil#computeExposure`, `RadiationManager`, `RadiationEntity#update`, `GeneralConfig`) **Radiation as a point-source field with log-scale effects.** Sources are chunk-stored points; exposure at a spot = sum of magnitude / max(1, distance squared) within 5 chunks. Magnitude += per-mB radioactivity (polonium 0.05 Sv/h per mB, nuclear waste 0.01, plutonium 0.02) when radioactive chemical is vented. Sources decay x0.9995 about once per second (half-life near 23 min; 1,000 Sv/h clears in about 10 h, matching the stated text). Dose per second = level / 3,600 x (1 - armor shielding); hazmat pieces 0.25 + 0.40 + 0.20 + 0.15 sum to full immunity. Severity is log-scaled from 10 uSv to 100 Sv, then each tick compares severity against a random threshold. Waste barrels shed only 1 mB per second. Meltdown multiplies source radiation by 50. **Mechanism:** a lingering, mappable hazard that comes from how you handled a byproduct (not from an event roll), reduced by additive shielding and time.
- **TB6** (shown, `FissionReactorMultiblockData`) **Risk loop with hysteresis.** Heat added = burn rate x 1,000,000 per mB fuel; damage accrues above 1,200 K by min(T,1800)/12,000 per tick and repairs slowly below; meltdown chance per tick = (damage/100) x 0.001 once damage >= 100 and T >= 1,200; re-enable needs T below 1,200 and damage below 100. Safety margin is coolant throughput. **Mechanism:** a rate dial the player sets against a cooling cap, with slow-accumulating damage that gives warning time.
- **TB7** (shown, `DynamicBufferedNetwork`, `EmitUtils`) **Transmitters as a shared buffer.** Each connected run is one network with one buffer whose capacity is the sum of its pipes' tier capacities; networks holding different chemicals refuse to merge; pulling pipes move min(tier rate, space) per tick; output splits fairly among sinks with leftovers redistributed. Tier tables (mB): chemical tube 4k/16k/256k/1,024k capacity, pull 750/2k/64k/256k; fluid pipe 2k to 128k. **Mechanism:** long lines buffer more, flow is bounded by the number of pulling points, and no sink has priority (contrast AE2 priority, TB23).
- **TB8** (shown, `TransporterPathfinder`, `TransporterStack`) **Item transport as visible cost routing.** Stacks move as entities; A* edge cost = 50 / tier speed (speeds 5, 10, 20, 50); cheapest valid sink wins; colors restrict routes (a colored line only accepts stacks carrying its color). Pull per operation 1/16/32/64. **Mechanism:** route cost from tier, color-coded lanes, and nearest-sink default with round-robin as an option.
- **TB9** (shown, `QIODriveData`, `QIODriveTier`) **Two-axis storage accounting.** A drive caps total item count and distinct types separately (16k/128, 128k/256, 1.05M/1,024, 16e9/8,192); a drive array holds 12; a frequency pools all drives; contents live on the drive item, so removing a drive carries its inventory. A one-item type still costs a type slot, so variety is the pressure. Compare AE2's byte formula (TB22), which blends both axes.
- **TB10** (shown, `TileEntityDigitalMiner`, `MinerEnergyContainer`) **Mining with cost multipliers.** Radius 10 default (cap 32), 80 ticks per block, 40 energy/tick; radius up to the cap and full height each double cost; silk touch x12; filters (item, tag, mod id) with an inverse mode and optional replace-with. **Mechanism:** a user-set scope (radius, depth, filter) priced multiplicatively, so "mine everything" costs far more than "mine one thing nearby".
- **TB11** (shown, evaporation, solar neutron activator, teleporter, SPS, laser code) **Small environmental-rate rules.** Evaporation tower rate = (min(T,3000) - 300) x 0.4 x height/18; 4 sun-facing collectors reach equilibrium 1,600 K above ambient with no fuel. Solar activator peak 64 x (1 + 0.3(0.8 - biome temp) - rain penalty), scaled by sun. Teleporter cost = 100 + 2/block + 1,000 across dimensions, x(1 + riders), nearest same-dimension destination by frequency. SPS holds fractional progress from an energy stream and hurts anything in its death zone, damage scaling with throughput. **Mechanism:** passive production rates tied to build size and biome, travel priced by distance and cargo.

### Immersive Engineering

- **TB12** (shown, `MultiblockProcessor`, `MultiblockProcess#doProcessTick`, `AveragingEnergyStorage`) **Recipe energy as a lump, power sets speed.** Every machine is a queue of processes (recipe, progress); energy per tick = total / duration. A process only ticks when it can draw its energy and its output is accepted; starved machines pause and never lose progress. Crusher and mixer can run up to 4 or 7 extra ticks per tick: the extra ticks climb +1 per tick while smoothed input exceeds draw and fall when it does not, so power supply sets throughput while energy per item stays constant. Queue length and per-tick parallelism are separate knobs (crusher serial; squeezer/fermenter 8). A `minDelayAfter` fraction models physical spacing (the press piston must be free). Contrast Thermal TB35 (brownout resets) and Create/MI in TA5.
- **TB13** (shown, `EnergyTransferHandler#transferPower`, `#burnOverloaded`, `IEWireTypes.EnergyWire`; config text for rates) **Power network with attrition, rationing and instant burnout.** Tiers: copper 16 blocks, 2,048 IF/t, 1.25% loss per 16; electrum 16, 8,192, 0.3%; steel 32, 32,768, 0.08%. Connectors cap at one-eighth of wire rate (256/1,024/4,096), so one wire can carry 8 full connectors. Paths minimize accumulated loss (Dijkstra), losses compound multiplicatively, and loss is linear in length divided by the wire's max length (so steel's text "per 16" is actually per 32; code and config text disagree). When supply is short, every sink is scaled by the same factor (proportional to demand, not distance). Any wire whose flow exceeds its rate for one tick is destroyed (connection removed, coil lost). Transformers are rate limiters and category bridges, not ratio converters. Wires have no storage; capacitors (100k/1M/4M, 256/1,024/4,096 per tick) are separate. **Mechanism:** a tier is (capacity, reach, attrition); overload is punished at the first peak, which makes headroom a design choice.
- **TB14** (shown, `WireDamageHandler#onCollided`) **Hazard proportional to flow.** Bare wires shock: damage = factor x energy available / wire rate x 8 (factors 2, 5, 15), costing the grid energy in proportion; insulated variants are safe.
- **TB15** (shown, `ExcavatorHandler#generatePotentialVein`, `MineralVein#getFailChance`, `ExcavatorLogic#fillBucket`, vein JSON) **Finite deposits measured in attempts, with site-quality rolls.** One vein per chunk at most, from a Perlin threshold (0.9), radius 12 to 43 blocks, 0 to 20% pre-depleted, composition chosen by weighted pick over biomes in the column (cave biomes widen the pool; weights 5 to 25). Yield cap 38,400 buckets; depletion rises by one per bucket whether it produced ore, spoil or nothing, and never replenishes. Per-bucket failure uses two independent rolls (mineral fail chance and a smoothstep of squared distance / radius squared: 0.16 at u=0.25, 0.5 at u=0.5, 0.84 at u=0.75), so sitting at the centre costs nothing and the rim is mostly spoil. Overlapping veins share the wheel in proportion to saturation. A core sample reports remaining yield as (cap - depletion) x (1 - fail). Power 4,096 IF/t continuous (about 7e9 IF per full vein). **Mechanism:** survey, then position, then pay; the expected-yield readout is honest and the counter counts attempts.
- **TB16** (shown, `TemplateMultiblock#form`, `IETemplateMultiblock#disassemble`) **Template build with one trigger.** A multiblock is a structure file; the player strikes the trigger block with a tool; every non-air cell must match (try each mirror) or nothing forms; all state sits on one master; ports are fixed cells (energy in at one, items at another), so layout is forced; disassembly restores memorised original blocks. Contrast Mekanism TB4 (cuboid + rebuild-safe cache) and MI in TA18.
- **TB17** (shown, `FurnaceHandler#tickServer`, `AdvBlastFurnaceLogic#getProcessSpeed`, `CokeOvenLogic`, `DieselGeneratorLogic`) **Speed bought in power, per-item fuel invariant.** Blast furnace: fuel and process both drop by processSpeed, so each preheater (32 IF/t) adds +1 speed (3x max) and fuel per item stays constant. Coke oven needs no fuel: 16 coal to 1 coke in 6,000 ticks plus creosote, batched by min(space, input, recipe count) multiplier, and blocked output aborts the timer to zero. Diesel generator: 4,096 IF/t only while the grid accepts; fuel use 1000/burnTime mB per tick; biodiesel gives 1,024 IF/mB and creosote 82 (12x worse), a one-number efficiency per fuel. External heater: 8 IF/t to heat, 4x startup, +24 IF/t doubles speed at full heat.
- **TB18** (shown, `BlueprintCraftingRecipe`, `ToolUpgrade`, `UpgradeableToolItem#recalculateUpgrades`) **Blueprints as category keys; upgrades as predicates.** A blueprint is one item type with a category string; holding one unlocks every recipe in that category (batch craft = min over ingredients). Common categories craft normally; special ones come from chest loot and villagers. Tool upgrades are items with a tool-set, a max stack and an exclusion predicate (drill damage up to 3 stacked; antenna and tesla mutually exclusive; drill 5 slots) and the effect set is recomputed from scratch on every slot change. The auto workbench mass-produces a held blueprint's recipe (180 ticks, 23,040 IF). Pair with Thermal TB33.
- **TB19** (shown, `WindmillBlockEntity`, `WatermillBlockEntity`, thermoelectric, `DynamoBlockEntity`) **Ambient generation from site geometry.** Windmill needs the whole swept disc clear, counts blocked cells downwind (>100 in a row kills it, a second windmill in the cone adds a large penalty), scales by sails 0.5 to 1.5, weather (storms best) and biome; rechecked every 128 ticks. Water wheel torque is summed from sampled flow cells; more wheels on an axle add power but not speed. Thermoelectric = sqrt(temperature gap)/2 per axis. Dynamos have no buffer, so a full battery wastes the turn. **Mechanism:** site quality as a scan result, slow recheck, wasted output when sinks are full.
- **TB20** (shown, `SorterBlockEntity`, `ItemBatcher`, `IFluidPipe`, pump code) **Kit batching and pressure.** Item batcher releases only when every filter quota is met (a kit for a machine) and raises a signal per satisfied filter; sorter has 6 sides x 8 filters, filtered sides first, random among valid outputs. Fluid pipes carry 50 mB per step unpressurized, 1,000 pressurized (stamped by a powered pump for 250 IF per source pull). Cloche: growth per tick x fertilizer (1.6 / 1.3 / 1.3 / 1.2 / 1.1), one dose 250 mB lasting 6,000 ticks, 8 IF/t. **Mechanism:** logistics primitive of "deliver a complete kit" and pressure as a rate multiplier bought with power.

### Applied Energistics 2

- **TB21** (shown, `GridNode#getMaxChannels`, `PathingCalculation`, `PathingService`) **Channels as tree-shaped bandwidth allocation.** Capacity 8 per link, 32 for dense links (dense cable, controller, quantum bridge, P2P tunnel); a global mode multiplies it x1, x2, x3, x4 or infinite. With a controller, a BFS from controllers drains three queues (dense, normal, other) nearest-first; each device that needs a channel takes one along its ancestor chain only if every checked ancestor has room, otherwise it gets none. So the nearest devices win and far ones starve when a trunk saturates. Without a controller (ad hoc), more than 8 channel-needing devices zeroes the whole network, not just the extras. Layout changes trigger a full reboot. Upkeep = sum of channels passing through each node / 128 AE per tick. Multi-part machines (crafting CPU, spatial pylon cluster) take one channel for the whole cluster. Controllers must be one blob under 7 per axis with no cross-shape. **Mechanism:** bandwidth per link tier, first-come-nearest allocation, a no-infrastructure threshold that hard-fails past a small count, and a global difficulty dial for the multiplier.
- **TB22** (shown, `BasicCellInventory#getUsedBytes`, `AEItems` tiers, STATED `bytes-and-types.md`) **Cell accounting: overhead per kind plus one per unit.** Used bytes = stored types x bytes-per-type + ceil(count / 8) (fluids 8,000 per byte). Tiers 1k/4k/16k/64k/256k use bytes-per-type 8/32/128/512/2,048 and idle drain 0.5 to 2.5 AE/t; the type cap stays 63 items (18 fluids) at every tier, so small cells stay useful. Stack size is irrelevant. Unique-NBT items count as separate types (stated rationale: sync and sort load). Equal-distribution, void (overflow destruction), inverter and fuzzy cards each change the accounting; a non-empty cell cannot go inside another cell. Compare Mekanism TB9.
- **TB23** (shown, `NetworkStorage#insert`/`extract`, `MEInventoryHandler#isPreferredStorageFor`) **Priority storage with asymmetric insert and extract.** Inserts go to the highest priority first (preferred whitelisted or already-holding inventories before the rest); extraction goes lowest priority first, so a high-priority sink fills first and drains last and the main vault stays intact.
- **TB24** (shown, `CraftingCalculation#computePlan`, `CraftingTreeNode#request`) **Plan before you run.** A recursive demand tree is simulated against a copy of stock; a node first draws from stock, then expands patterns by provider priority; sibling patterns are tried one craft at a time in child states and merged only on success; recursion is blocked; the plan reserves peak need (max ever drawn from each parent stack), not net need. Result: a pure function of recipes and stock showing initial extracts, craft counts, byte cost and missing items. If the full amount fails, REPORT_MISSING_ITEMS reruns in simulate mode to list shortfalls (and the plan cannot be submitted), or CRAFT_LESS binary-searches the largest affordable amount. Substitution only among same-amount alternatives. Runs on a worker thread paused every ~100 steps with a small per-tick budget, so huge plans never lag. **Mechanism:** the missing-items list and the auto-scaled request are first-class outputs; planning is incremental.
- **TB25** (shown, `CraftingCPUCalculator`, `CraftingCpuLogic#tickCraftingLogic`, `CraftingCpuHelper#calculatePatternPower`, `CraftingService#findSuitableCraftingCPU`) **Two independent crafting dials.** A CPU is a contiguous cuboid up to 17 per axis: storage blocks add 1,024 x kB bytes each (memory = how big a job), accelerators add co-processor threads (speed). Plan bytes = 8 x requested volume terms + 1 per craft + 8 x tree nodes (comment: crafting memory is deliberately 8x normal storage). Pushes per tick = threads + 1 minus pushes in the last three ticks. The job pulls all its inputs into a private inventory at submit, then only accepts items it is waiting for. Cost per push = input amount / unit AE. CPU choice: filter offline/busy/too small/excluded, then smallest adequate first ("fast last") for machine jobs, strongest for player jobs, with counts of why none fit. **Mechanism:** reservation at start, waiting-for gate, memory and thread as separate upgrade axes.
- **TB26** (shown, `PatternProviderLogic#pushPattern`, `AEProcessingPattern`, `AECraftingPattern`) **Recipe slot attached to a workstation.** 9 pattern slots; push refused while a prior batch still delivers; blocking mode skips a target already holding any of the pattern's inputs; undelivered remainders retry on tick; lock modes (redstone high/low, until pulse, until the result returns). Processing patterns hold up to 81 inputs and 27 outputs; crafting patterns carry a "may substitute" flag. Very close to an idle-game assembly-line input buffer.
- **TB27** (shown, `IOBusPart#getOperationsPerTick`, `TickManagerService`) **Cards buy integer throughput; polling backs off when idle.** Speed cards give 1, 8, 32, 64, 96 operations per run (fluid op = 125 mB). Poll interval moves between per-device bounds (e.g. import 5 to 40 ticks): faster by 2 when work was found, slower by 1 when not, idle jumps to the max, urgent to the min. Interface keeps stock levels (pulls to maintain, pushes surplus back, requests crafting for shortfall); level emitter turns a stock threshold into a signal and can mark a machine-based supply as "craftable" for the planner.
- **TB28** (shown, `SpatialPylonService`, `SpatialIOPortBlockEntity#transition`, `SpatialStorageCellItem`) **Pocket space priced convexly with a build-quality discount.** Cell tiers 2, 16, 128 per axis; a cell binds to its size at first use. Efficiency = min(1, pylon blocks / max(6, 3/8 x shell area)). Energy = floor((outer volume x 1250) ^ (1 + 0.35 x (1 - efficiency))) (multiplier 1250 and exponent 1.35 are config defaults, shown). Example by arithmetic: 4^3 outer at full efficiency 80,000 AE, at zero efficiency about 4.2 million. The whole cost is pulled in one operation, entities inside travel too. **Mechanism:** a stored space priced by volume, cheaper when the apparatus is well built.
- **TB29** (shown, `P2PTunnelPart`, `MEP2PTunnelPart`) **Short-code wormholes.** One base item; type is set by "attuning" (right-click with a trigger item, which keeps frequency and direction); frequency is a 16-bit code; one input, many outputs; ME tunnels split channels rather than duplicate, take 1 channel on the home grid and carry 32 on the far side, and cannot nest (prevents exponential compression). Costs: idle 0.5 to 2 AE/t plus a 0.025 AE tax per item, per 125 mB, and 2.5% of energy moved.
- **TB30** (shown, `MolecularAssemblerBlockEntity`, `InscriberBlockEntity`) **Convex speed cost versus time-only cards.** Assembler cards 0 to 5 give progress/tick 10, 13, 17, 20, 25, 50 at energy tax 1.0 to 5.0, so energy per craft is 100, 130, 170, 200, 250, 500 for 10 down to 2 ticks. Inscriber: cards cut time only (116 to 20 ticks) while energy stays near 2,000 per craft. Two designs of "speed upgrade" in one mod.
- **TB31** (shown, `BuddingCertusQuartzBlock`, `GrowthAcceleratorBlockEntity`, `MeteoriteStructure`) **Self-decaying crystal farm and a once-per-region find.** Budding block: 1 in 5 per random tick tries one face; each growth gives a 1 in 12 chance to downgrade (flawed, chipped, damaged, plain); flawless never decays and cannot be crafted. Accelerator pokes adjacent random ticks every 10 ticks for extra power. Meteorites: spacing 32 chunks, separation 8, radius 2 to 8, uniform quality pick for budding blocks, a Mysterious Cube holding the only source of inscriber presses (an iron block clones a press without consuming it). Charger recipes consume the full 1,600 AE buffer as a discrete gate. Progression (inferred): meteorite, then presses and budding blocks, charger, accelerators, silicon and processors, cells and controller, pattern provider and CPU, then spatial.
- **TB32** (shown, `AEConfig`, `EnergyService`, `VibrationChamberBlockEntity`, `WirelessAccessPointBlockEntity`) **Shared power with a burst limit and brownout hysteresis.** 2 FE = 1 AE; one pool per network with no rate limit; small buffers (25 AE per node, 8,000 per controller, 200,000 energy cell, 1.6M dense); a tick cannot spend more than the buffer holds, so big operations need cells or the network browns out and reboots; powered state needs 30 consecutive good ticks to turn on but drops immediately. Vibration chamber throttles itself between 4 and 40 AE/t by whether the grid accepts power, ramping over 100 ticks. Wireless range = 16 + boosters^1.5 blocks; draw = 8 + boosters^(1 + boosters/64) AE/t; drain on the terminal grows with distance. Annihilation plane cost = (1 + hardness + drop count) x enchant factors. No security-terminal code exists in this tree (recorded absent).

### Thermal (Expansion, Core, CoFH Core)

- **TB33** (shown, `AugmentDataHelper`, `ThermalAugmentRules`, `AugmentableBlockEntity#updateAugmentState`, `MachineProperties#finalizeAttributes`) **Chips as typed numeric bags with per-stat stacking rules.** An augment is a type tag plus named float mods; the machine recomputes every derived stat from the slot list on each change. Rules differ per stat: base/RF/fluid mods take the highest value (a tier chip never stacks with itself); machine speed and output mods add from 1.0; energy, XP and catalyst-use mods multiply; all clamped 0 to 100. Slots: machines 4, dynamos 4, devices 3, storage 3, tools 4, hard cap 9. Type exclusivity: machines deny dynamo/area/potion chips, RF/fluid/filter chips are one per machine, unique chips cannot repeat. Dismantling keeps augments, energy and side settings by config flag. Chips can sit in carried items too (one model for stations and gear; Tinker Bench). **Mechanism:** compose boosts by stat family, recompute rather than mutate.
- **TB34** (shown, `TCoreItems#registerMachineAugments`, `MachineBlockEntity#finalizeAttributes`) **Every boost carries a compounding tax.** Speed chip: +100% throughput, energy per craft x1.1. Output chip: secondary +15%, energy x1.25. Efficiency chip: speed -10%, energy x0.9. Catalyst chip: catalyst burns 20% less, energy x1.25. Tier upgrade I/II/III sets BaseMod 2/3/4 (max-folded) and scales speed, buffer and transfer together without changing energy per craft. Total RF per craft = recipe cost x product of energy chips x catalyst energy mod; time = RF / per-tick draw.
- **TB35** (shown, `MachineBlockEntity#processStart`/`processTick`, `ThermalMachineConfig`) **Fixed energy bill, variable draw, leftover carries.** Machine base draw 20 RF/t (crucible 80, pyrolyzer and crystallizer 5), 50,000 RF buffer, input cap 10x draw. If the buffer cannot pay one tick the machine stops and loses progress (brownout resets; contrast IE TB12 which pauses). Overspend on a finishing cycle carries into the next process; an idle machine refunds it. Changing inputs, catalyst or output space mid-run aborts the process.
- **TB36** (shown, `BaseMachineRecipe#getOutputItemChances`, `CatalyzedMachineRecipe`, catalyst JSON) **Catalyst as a five-number record and "has a chance" as the switch.** Catalyst = {primary x, secondary x, energy x, minimum chance, burn chance}. A recipe output with no listed chance is locked at 100% and ignores every modifier; one with a listed chance (even 1.0) is modifiable. Final chance = max(chance x mods, floor); above 1 gives guaranteed copies plus a fractional bonus roll. Each craft burns a catalyst with probability use x chip mod. Examples: pulverizer basalz powder 1.25 / 3.0 / 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.50 (cheap, great, burns half the time); smelter rich slag 2.5 / 2.25 / 1.75 / 0.10 / 0.60; insolator phytogro 3.0 / 3.0 / 0.8 / 0.75 / 0.75. Worked by arithmetic: iron ore at the pulverizer with basalz powder yields 2.5 dust, 30% nickel, 60% gravel for 1,000 RF (base: 2 dust, 10%, 20%, 4,000 RF).
- **TB37** (shown, recipe JSON `machines/*`, `DEFAULT_ENERGY` constants) **One recipe schema and a self-feeding by-product loop.** All machines read {ingredients, outputs with optional chance, energy or energy_mod, xp}. Ore gives a bulk primary plus a long tail (pulverizer iron ore: dust chance 2.0 plus 10% nickel plus 20% gravel); ingot-to-dust conversions cost half energy so only the ore tier multiplies. Rich slag from ore smelting is itself a smelter catalyst; gravel pulverizes into sand and 20% flint, and flint is a catalyst; mob-rod powders are catalysts. Chance above 1 is a yield dial (copper ore smelting 5.0). Alloys use counts (lumium 3 tin + 1 silver + 2 glowstone for 4 ingots, 12,000 RF).
- **TB38** (shown, `DynamoBlockEntity#calcEnergy`, fuel JSON) **Demand-following generators with a fuel catalogue.** Output per tick = min(processTick, floor + processTick x (1 - buffer fill)), floor 10% of base; fuel is consumed only as fast as RF is produced, so a full downstream makes fuel last. Dynamo chips: output +100% at energy x0.9 (faster but 10% less total per fuel), fuel chip +10% per fuel. Fuel values (total RF per unit): coal 24,000, coal block 240,000, coke 40,000, lava 100,000 per bucket, diamond 500,000, netherite coin 320,000, cake 25,600. Unlisted fuels get a derived price: burn ticks x 10 RF; food = nutrition x 1,600 with x2 for only-beneficial effects, x4 for saturation > 1; enchanted items = (sum of enchant minimum costs + triangle number) x 8,000. **Mechanism:** auto-pricing unlisted items from their existing attributes.
- **TB39** (shown, `DeviceBlockEntity`, device block entities) **Ambient devices: no power, timers and neighbour validity.** Tree extractor yield = fluid amount x base mod x booster x sqrt(logs x leaves / (min logs x min leaves)); cycle 500 ticks, slowed by (2 + other extractors on the trunk)/2, up to 3x; boosters (bone meal x1.5 for 8 cycles, phytogro x2 for 16). Fisher: needs a water patch, timer 4,800 ticks minus 20 per water tile, divided by 3 in oceans, 2 in rivers, 2 more in rain; bait multiplies rolls and burns by use chance. Rock gen result from adjacent block + block below. Soil infuser (the only RF device) charges soil to 5 levels; each soil tick gives growth up to 5x. Potion diffuser uses 25 mB per 60 ticks with amplifier capped at 3. **Mechanism:** a co-location penalty (shared trunk) is a cheap anti-stacking rule; sqrt size scaling.
- **TB40** (shown, `IReconfigurable`, `Reconfigurable4WayBlockEntity`, `EnergyCellBlockEntity`, `HoldingEnchantment`) **Face roles and separate capacity/throughput stats.** Per face: none, input, output, both, accessible; round-robin transfer of up to 64 items or 1,000 mB per face about once a second and after each completed craft. Redstone control gates only the next start. Energy cell: 1,000,000 RF, 1,000 per tick in and out; RF coil chips trade storage against transfer (x4/x4, x6/x2, x2/x6, one per cell); Holding enchantment (max IV) adds capacity (+50% per level) but never transfer. Machine XP is banked as an expected value with random rounding of the fraction. Redprint copies settings between blocks. Features can be base or item-unlocked via one config flag.

## Cross-mod patterns

- **SP-B1 Three families of "speed" upgrade.** Exponential dial with offset (Mekanism TB1), lump energy with power-sets-speed (IE TB12, MI in TA5), convex card cost (AE2 assembler TB30) versus time-only card (AE2 inscriber), versus chip-tax (Thermal TB34). All keep a cap and a printed cost.
- **SP-B2 Failure policies differ and each is legible.** Pause with no loss (IE), reset on brownout (Thermal), all-zero past a threshold (AE2 ad hoc channels), instant destruction on first overload (IE wires), meltdown with warning time (Mekanism), reboot on layout change (AE2).
- **SP-B3 Capacity and throughput are separate stat families.** Mekanism tier tables (throughput outruns capacity), Thermal Holding versus RF coils, AE2 bytes versus types versus channels, IE capacitor storage versus rate.
- **SP-B4 Plan, reserve, run.** AE2 peak-reserved simulation and job inventory (TB24, TB25) complements Create's promise model (TA14): both avoid double orders and mid-run surprises.
- **SP-B5 Finite sources measured in attempts.** IE depletion counts buckets (TB15), AE2 budding blocks decay per growth (TB31), Mekanism waste sheds 1 mB per second (TB5).
- **SP-B6 Co-location penalties and placement scans.** Thermal shared-trunk slowdown, IE windmill wake, AE2 controller cross-shape ban, Mekanism boiler layout rules: stacking is discouraged by geometry rather than caps.
- **SP-B7 Unlock by held item or found item.** IE blueprint categories, AE2 presses only from a meteorite find and then cloneable, Thermal side-config/redstone/XP gated by chips, AE2 attunement by trigger item.

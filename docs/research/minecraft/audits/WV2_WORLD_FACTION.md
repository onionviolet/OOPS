## Top findings

1. **WF1** A village is a layout list of (plan, role) slots, and growth picks the next building by weight = slot priority x tier multiplier (centre/start/player 6, core 4, secondary 2, extra 1), one tier at a time.
2. **WF2** Building cost is computed from the plan's block list, not written by hand, with a free-block list (dirt, water, flowers, paths) and wood folded to logs.
3. **WF3** Reputation has two layers (per village and per culture), the culture layer gets one tenth of every village gain, and both add together for gating.
4. **WF4** Trade is a per-culture catalog (selling price, buying price, reserve, target stock, minimum reputation) plus per-village-type price overrides, and a donation mode pays 4x reputation.
5. **WF5** Travelling merchants move stock between villages with `relation >= 0`, a different village type, within 2000 blocks, and prefer destinations that need what they carry.
6. **WF6** Village-to-village relations drift nightly, and a player can nudge them with a capped currency (5 diplomacy points) scaled by a log of reputation.
7. **WF7** Vampirism makes faction a rewrite of rules: one blood meter replaces hunger, sun, water and garlic become environment damage, and level thresholds phase the penalties in (weakness 2, nausea 3, burn 4).
8. **WF8** Level-up requirements are different physical acts per faction (altar rituals and blood versus tables and trainers), and lord levels require killing the opposing faction in bulk.
9. **WF9** Crafting stations are gated by skill, not by recipe (a hunter without the weapon-table skill cannot use the table).
10. **WF10** Refinements are rolled stat bundles with built-in trade-offs (a buff paired with a penalty), sold or earned through tasks.

## Part A. Millenaire: culture-generated villages and their economy

### A1. Culture as a data pack

**WF11 (shown: `cultures/<c>/` tree, `docs/content-guide.en.md`, `CultureLoader`).** A culture is a directory: `culture.json`, `traded_goods.json`, `reputation.json`, `culture_reputation.json`, `buildings/<category>/`, `villagers/<category>/`, `villages/`, `shops/`, `namelists/`, `quests/`. Seven shipped: norman, byzantines, seljuk, indian, japanese, mayan, inuits (directory listing). Norman alone has about 440 files in `buildings` (houses 109, townhalls 97, marvel 180, lone 60, walls 55, special 47). Overrides merge by rule per file family: replace-first-alphabetical for plans, villagers and villages; append for traded goods, name lists and sentences; a `_disabled.json` lists IDs to drop. No hot reload (shown: content guide §1.3).
- Port: culture = one folder of typed records with an explicit merge rule per record family, so a new world's people can be added or an old one patched without touching others.
- WQ1 default: a world's inhabitants are a culture record (buildings, trades, names, attitudes). Dial: how many record families a world must supply (a minimal culture may only supply names and goods). Reason/cost: a missing family falls back to a neutral default and shows as "unknown customs" in play, so thin cultures cost a flatness the player can see.

### A2. Village types and growth

**WF1 (shown: `villages/*.json`, `VillageGrowthManager#getAllCandidates`, `TIER_MULTIPLIERS`).** A village type has `weight` (spawn likelihood), `biome_tags`, `radius`, and a `layout` of `{plan, role}` entries. Roles are `centre`, `start`, `core`, `secondary`, `extra`. Example `norman/agricole`: centre manor, start (farm, grove, lumberman hut, well, large orchard), core (bakery, carpenter, cattle farm, church, farm, fountain, sheep and chicken farm, tavern, market), secondary (glassblower, forge, guard house, inn, pig farm, presbytery, quarry, chicken farm). Growth runs every 20 ticks per village (`GROWTH_TICK_INTERVAL`). It finds the lowest tier with unbuilt slots (order start, player, core, secondary, extra) and only offers that tier's slots. Candidate weight is `max(1, slot priority x tier multiplier)`, multipliers `centre 6, townhall 6, start 6, player 6, core 4, secondary 2, extra 1`. A weighted pick chooses one. Limits: `max_simultaneous_constructions` per village type (default 1, `VillageTypeLoader`), `max_count` per plan, a `never` list per village type (norman `agricole` bans armoury, weaver house, watchtower, fountain, small orchard), and a count rule so the same plan cannot exceed its layout copies.
- If the picked project is unaffordable it is stored as the pending project and an affordable fallback is launched (`tryAffordableFallback`), so one missing material does not stall the village. When no candidate exists the village sleeps for 600 ticks (`NO_PROJECTS_CACHE_TICKS`); a failed placement cools down 1600 ticks.
- Upgrades are the same machinery: each building plan has `variants` and numbered `levels` (forge has levels 0-5, farm 0-4, guard house 0-3), each level with its own `priority` (forge level 0 priority 800, level 1 priority 80, so a new forge is far more likely than upgrading one). Level conditions use tags (`requiredTags`, `requiredParentTags`, `forbiddenTagsInVillage`, `requiredVillageTags`, `checkBuildConditions`), so a upgrade can need "a forge exists in the village".
- Port: a settlement grows by tiers (founding, core, secondary, extras) and by weighted pick among what the tier still lacks, with upgrade priority numbers far below new-build priority numbers so upgrades are rarer.
- WQ2 default: settlement growth is a weighted pick within the lowest unfilled tier, one project at a time. Dial: simultaneous projects (1 for a hamlet, more for a town), tier weights, upgrade-to-new ratio. Reason/cost: growth waits on material stock, so a settlement stuck at "core" shows which good is missing (see A3), which makes player supply a lever.

**WF2 (shown: `BuildingCostCalculator#computeCost`, `BuildingPlanSetLoader` line 231).** Required resources per level are computed from the plan's NBT block list. Free blocks: air, dirt family, farmland, water, lava, snow, clay, grass, 20+ flowers, redstone parts, cake, nether wart, and the mod's path and dirt-wall blocks. Wood is converted to planks then to logs (`ceil(planks / 4)`); a generic "any wood" plank item stands in for any species so a village builds with local timber; glass panes are folded to blocks (`ceil(panes x 6 / 16)`); roof-tile slabs are counted as halves (`ceil(halves / 2)`, minimum 1). Stairs, slabs and fences map to their base material.
- Port: a build bill is derived from the plan's content with a free list for terrain filler and a "any local wood" wildcard. This is the same idea as MineColonies' material buckets (WC54) but with an explicit free list, which is what lets a plan be authored once and costed automatically.
- WQ3 default: every structure derives its bill from its parts, with a world-set free list. Dial: free list (a desert world frees sand, an ocean world frees water). Reason/cost: free terrain filler keeps bills from being dominated by dirt, while paid materials (stone, glass, metal) tie growth to trade.

**WF12 (shown: `VillageSpawnQueue`, `MillenaireServerConfig`).** Defaults: minimum village spacing 500 blocks (range 300-1000), minimum lone-building spacing 500, village to lone-building 250, spawn protection 250, `generateHamlets=false`. Biome match is by biome tag per village type (index built per tag). "Progressive completion" (stated): villages far from spawn start with up to 25% of their buildings done, ramping from 2000 to 10000 blocks. Villager children cap `maxChildren` 10 (range 2-20). Villages keep ticking only inside `keepActiveRadius` 200 of a player; relations, trade and diplomacy use `backgroundRadius` 2000.
- Port: far-from-start settlements arrive partly built, so exploration meets mature places, and near-start ones grow in front of the player. Both come from one distance ramp.
- WQ4 default: initial maturity of a generated place rises with distance from the starting area. Dial: ramp start, ramp end, maximum share. Reason/cost: mature places hold more goods and demand, so a long trip pays and a near place can be shaped by the player.

### A3. Villagers, goals and the production loop

**WF13 (shown: `villagers/normalvillagers/*.json`, `goal/impl/*`, `gathering_type/*.json`).** A villager type lists `goals` (strings), `tags` (`chief`, `religious`, `helpInAttacks`), `required_goods` (what the household keeps: farmer 100 wheat seeds, smith 10 iron ingots and 2 white wool), `collect_goods`, `bring_back_home_goods`, a tool class, `experience_given`, and a name list. Goals are global templates in `gathering_type/*.json` with a handler (`crafting`, `smelting`, `chopping`, ...), `priority`, `priorityRandom`, an inventory penalty (`priorityInvPenaltyItems/Base`), scan and batch radii, cooldown, `minimumHour` and `maximumHour`, and stock limits (`buildingLimit` per item, `townhallLimit` per item). Examples: `craft_bow` (priority 50, one wool in, one bow out, building limit 2 bows, hours up to 12500); `cook_iron_ore` (priority 1000, raw iron to ingot, townhall limit 256 ingots); `chop_trees` (priority 125, logs limit 4096 in the townhall, one chopper per building).
- Production balance is by stock limits: a worker stops producing when the building or townhall holds the limit. There is no money in production, only inventory.
- The carry loop is three small goals: `get_goods_for_household` (priority = missing goods x 20, blocks others until the home has its `required_goods`), `get_resources_for_shops` (priority = items waiting x 5), `deliver_resources_shop` (priority = carried items x 10). Throttle 2000 ticks unless more than 16 items are waiting. So a smith without iron makes the iron smelter's output priority rise.
- Port: workers pick actions by numeric priority from stock and need, with per-item caps that stop overproduction, and with household needs that outrank shop work.
- WQ5 default: a worker's day is a priority list recomputed from stock gaps. Dial: caps per item and place, hour windows. Reason/cost: caps leave surplus to trade rather than burying the economy, and hour windows tie work to the day cycle already in the sim.

### A4. Prices, shops and stock

**WF4 (shown: `traded_goods.json`, `TradeGood`, `TradeMenu#resolvePrice`, `executeBuy`, `executeSell`).** A good has `selling_price`, `buying_price`, `reserved_quantity`, `target_quantity`, `auto_generate`, `min_reputation`, `foreign_merchant_price`, `category`. Selling price is what the village charges; buying price is what it pays. Village types override both per good (`selling_price_overrides`, `buying_price_overrides`: norman agricole sells bread 7, pays 5; cider 60/50; a Norman sword is bought at 4106). Money is three coins: denier 1, silver 64, gold 4096 (`MoneyHelper`). Shop profiles list what a building `sells`, `buys`, `buys_optional` and `deliver_to`. Trade actions come in 1, 8, 64. A buy is limited by money, stock (unless `auto_generate`) and space; a good with `min_reputation` is locked below that combined reputation. Villages hold stock, so a player selling wood fills the village's stock and the stock then feeds its construction (A2).
- Port: a world's economy as one catalog with buy/sell asymmetry per good and per place, a reputation lock on valuable goods, and village stock that is the same stock construction consumes.
- WQ6 default: price is a catalog number per good with place overrides, and place stock doubles as construction supply. Dial: spread between buy and sell, which goods are reputation-locked. Reason/cost: selling a village what it lacks speeds its growth and earns reputation, so supply is both profit and a favour.

**WF14 (shown: `LocalMerchantHelper#attemptMerchantMoveFromInn`, constants).** Each inn holds a travelling merchant. After at least 2 nights (`MIN_NIGHTS_BEFORE_MOVE`) the merchant picks a destination among villages with relation >= 0, a different village type, within 2000 blocks, and an inn that "needs" any item the merchant carries. A destination with no merchant is added three times to the target list (triple weight); one with a merchant needs more than 1 night or a forced swap. If nothing needs the stock, after more than 3 nights (`NIGHTS_BEFORE_BACKUP`) any such village is a backup. The merchant moves permanently, carries its stock, and the inn logs the arrival.
- Port: trade routes as a character who moves goods to where they are needed, only along friendly relations, with demand deciding the destination. No route graph is needed.
- WQ7 default: a trader links two friendly places that need each other's goods. Dial: distance cap, minimum nights, relation threshold. Reason/cost: a war or bad relation removes the trader, which is how diplomacy shows up in shelves and prices.

### A5. Reputation, diplomacy and language

**WF3 (shown: `Village#adjustReputation`, `getCombinedReputation`, `PlayerCultureReputation`).** Each player has a reputation per village (unbounded integer) and per culture (clamped -640 to 4096, `CULTURE_MIN/MAX_REPUTATION`). Buying adds `totalCost` to village reputation; selling adds `totalRevenue` (4x when donating). Each village gain also gives the culture `amount / 10`, with the remainder rolled randomly (e.g. +3 gives 30% chance of +1). Gating uses `village + culture`. Labels are per-culture thresholds (norman: -1024 public enemy, -256, -64, 0 stranger, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192 friend of the village, 16384 ally, 32768 one of us, 131072 natural leader). Culture labels: -640 "scourge", 0 neutral, 256 decent, 512 good, 1024 excellent, 2048 stellar. Three cultures at -640 grants an achievement (`pendingAttila`). Reputation is paid out in spending, so a loyal customer is a rich one.
- Port: standing as spending, not as kill counts or quest ticks, with a culture-wide echo so a good name carries to a neighbour's village of the same people.
- WQ8 default: standing with a place rises with what you trade there, and one tenth echoes to its people. Dial: echo ratio, donation multiplier, caps. Reason/cost: echo makes reputation travel but never as fast as local, so knowing a people still costs time in each of their towns.

**WF6 (shown: `VillageDiplomacyHelper#performNightlyDiplomacyDrift`, `DiplomacyHelper#performDiplomacy`).** Each night, each relation (range -100 to +100) has a 1 in 10 chance to change by 10 to 19. Direction odds: below -90 never improves; -90 to -50 improves 30%; -50 to 0 improves 40%; 0 to 50 improves 60%; 50 to 90 improves 70%; above 90 always improves. A player with positive combined reputation and a diplomacy point (5, refilled for each player per village) can praise or slander another village: effect = +/-10 x coefficient x (0.8 to 1.2), coefficient = `(ln(rep) / ln(32768) x 2 + floor(rep / 32768)) / 3`, which is about 0.44 at rep 1024 and 1.0 at 32768 (I spot-checked the integer division: it adds 1 only at the cap). Each nudge uses one point.
- Port: a slow random drift that favours the extremes (friendships harden, feuds persist), and a player lever that is rate-limited and scaled by earned standing.
- WQ9 default: place-to-place relations drift on a bias that makes extremes sticky. Dial: change size, drift odds, player points per night. Reason/cost: a nudge needs standing and uses a scarce currency, so brokering peace is work.

**WF15 (shown: `PlayerCultureReputation.LANGUAGE_*`, `TradeMenu` calls to `addLanguageKnowledge`).** Items traded add to a per-culture language score (beginner 100, moderate 200, fluent 500). Shown: the score exists and trades feed it; stated: it controls how much of villagers' speech the player understands (`languageLearning` config; the speech effect itself I did not trace).
- WQ10 default: trade volume with a people teaches their tongue. Dial: thresholds, what the tongue gates (dialogue, prices, quests). Reason/cost: learning needs repeated trade, so a newcomer is charged in mistrust before fluency.

### A6. Quests, marvels, raids, player control

**WF16 (shown: `quests/<culture>basic/*.json`, `Quest`, `QuestInstance`).** A quest has `key`, `chancePerHour` (abbot books delivery 0.1), `maxSimultaneous`, `minReputation` (192), global and player tag requirements and bans, `villagerDefs` (a villager type plus a relation such as `nearbyvillage`, so the second NPC lives in a neighbour village), and `steps`, each with `duration` (hours), `requiredGoods`, `rewardGoods`, `rewardMoney`, `rewardReputation`, `penaltyReputation` (32 for a failed delivery), success and failure tags for villager, player and global scopes, `relationChanges` and `bedrockBuildings`. Quests are offered by a chance per hour when conditions hold, never picked from a menu.
- Port: quests as chains of timed, tag-gated steps whose failure has a reputation price, offered by chance when a trusted NPC and a neighbour exist.
- WQ11 default: a task chain has a time limit per step and a standing cost on failure. Dial: chance per hour, standing floor, penalty size. Reason/cost: a deadline plus a penalty makes accepting a task a commitment, not a free option.

**WF17 (shown: `MarvelManager`).** Marvel villages grow one huge building over many levels (norman abbey has about 180 plan files). Completion is tested every 200 ticks when the marvel building's level is the last. Constants: donation ratio 0.5, luck effect 12000 ticks. Players who helped (tag `normanmarvel_helper`) get the reward when it completes. A shared long project that players and villagers build together.
- WQ12 default: a landmark is a slow community project with named helpers. Dial: levels, donation ratio, reward. Reason/cost: it ties many nights of play to one visible result.

**WF18 (shown, with a gap: `MillenaireServerConfig` raids block; `Village#isUnderAttack`).** Config comment says "Raid settings (combat AI not yet implemented, values reserved)"; `raidingRate` default 20 (% per night, TODO), `banditRaidRadius` 1500. `isUnderAttack()` returns `false`. So in this port, village raids and bandit raids are not implemented. Only `carries_raid` and `isRaider/isDefender` tags in data and the military panel exist. Do not mine this port for raid numbers; MineColonies (WC16) is the stronger donor.

**WF19 (shown: `BuildingPurchaseService`, village type `player_controlled`, `player_buildings`).** A player can buy a building for a village (`price` in deniers, `reputation` minimum, must be in the village type's `playerBuildings`, not already built or bought). Buying sets a bought flag, clears the pending project and the village builds it by the normal growth rules (player tier weight 6). A `controlled` village type (norman "Seigneurie", weight 0, `playerlargefort` centre) is the player's own holding with the same growth machinery.
- WQ13 default: a player funds a place's next building instead of building it. Dial: price, reputation floor, which buildings are buyable. Reason/cost: money buys intent, but villagers still need the materials, so wealth cannot skip supply.

### A7. What is new relative to MineColonies (WC12-WC19, WC51-WC55)

- Culture-generated variety: seven culture trees with mixed layouts, not one colony style (WF11, WF1).
- Price as catalog plus place overrides, with reputation locks and donation (WF4); MineColonies has request resolvers but no player-facing market.
- Reputation as spend (WF3) and a diplomacy budget (WF6); MineColonies' happiness (WC15) is internal to one colony.
- Traders carrying stock by demand (WF14); MineColonies has no inter-settlement trade.
- Weighted tier growth with cheap upgrades (WF1) versus MineColonies' player-driven work orders (WC52).

## Part B. Vampirism: a faction that rewrites the rules around one player

### B1. Structure

**WF20 (shown: `ModFactions`, `faction/` and `faction-api/` projects).** Two layers. `faction-api` is a generic faction framework (playable faction, skill trees and segments, actions, tasks, lord levels, minions, refinements, village banners, totems, faction-specific tags). `vampirism` registers two playable factions on it, VAMPIRE and HUNTER. Each: max level 14 (`REFERENCE.HIGHEST_*_LEVEL`), max lord level 5, an extension (Dracula for vampires, Marshall for hunters), a task master entity, a village banner, totem blocks, village guards (an entity tag), a bad-omen effect and a minion entry. Vampires also get a refinement item set (amulet, ring, obi belt). Per-faction tags (`registerFactionTags`) assign biomes, point-of-interest types, villager professions, entity types, default targets, tasks, books, food data components and "actions disabled by effect" to each faction. Stated by the framework: leaving a faction needs `canLeaveFaction()` and optionally kills the player (`leaveFaction(die)`, damage 10000); PvP can be restricted to between factions (`factionPvpOnlyBetweenFactions`).
- Port: a faction is a record of tags and hooks, not a class hierarchy. A new faction supplies its level cap, its task master, its enemies by tag and its skill trees, and the framework supplies the rest.
- WQ14 default: a faction = tag set (friends, enemies, food, places) plus level cap plus skill trees plus tasks. Dial: whether leaving is possible and what it costs. Reason/cost: leaving is possible but costs the faction's level and skills (shown: `setFaction` resets level to 1 on change unless the update specifies one), and the in-world reason can be a rite, an oath or a death.

### B2. Levels: different acts per faction

**WF8 (shown: `VampireLeveling`, `HunterLeveling`, `getRequiredStructurePoints`).**

Vampire infusion rows (`AltarInfusionRequirements(target, pureBloodLevel, pureBloodQuantity, humanHeartQuantity, vampireBookQuantity)`): L5 (0,0,5,1), L6 (0,1,5,1), L7 (0,1,10,1), L8 (1,1,10,1), L9 (1,1,10,1), L10 (2,1,15,1), L11 (2,1,15,1), L12 (3,1,20,1), L13 (3,2,20,1), L14 (4,2,25,1). The altar also needs "structure points" around it: `8 + (54 - 8) x t / 5` with `t = (target - 4) / 2` (integer division), giving 8 at level 5, 17 at levels 6-7, 26 at 8-9, 35 at 10-11, 44 at 12-13 and 54 at 14. (The L5 hunter row's pure-blood slot is -1, meaning none.) Each table step yields a numbered intel item the trainer needs. A vampire at levels 1 to 2 can also level passively by blood (100 for level 2, 175 for level 3; config `vpPassiveLevelingEnabled`; shown: `canLevelPassively` is `0 < level < 3`).
- Port: advancement as distinct physical rites per path: the vampire path spends blood and body parts at a built structure, the hunter path spends metals and trophies at a table and trainer, and both want the other faction's drops (pure blood, fangs, hearts).
- WQ15 default: each path levels by acts that fit its fiction, each requiring the other path's drops at higher levels. Dial: numbers per level, structure size, passive early levels. Reason/cost: needing the enemy's drops makes the two paths depend on conflict, and structure points make the altar a place you build up.

**WF21 (shown: `data/vampirism/factionapi/tasks/*.json`, 49 files).** Tasks are data: requirement list (item, entity-type count, stat count) plus reward (item, refinement, lord level), with an `unlocker` (level or exact lord level). Lord level tasks (shown): vampire lord 1 needs 32 gold, 25 infected creatures, 5 pure blood level 4, 3 captured villages; lord 2: 48 gold, 30 hunters, 5 pure blood 4; lord 3: 48 gold, 30 hunters, 5 pure blood 4 (read: `vampire_lord3`); lord 4: 5 advanced hunters, 64 gold, 10 pure blood 4; lord 5: 6 captured villages, 64 gold, 50 infected creatures, 20 pure blood 4. Hunter lord 1: 32 gold, 50 vampires, 3 captured villages; lord 2: 32 gold, 50 vampires; lord 4: 64 gold, 75 vampires; lord 5: 6 captured villages, 64 gold, 100 vampires. Side tasks reward gold, emeralds (10 for one capture), a chainmail chestplate (20 skeletons), random refinements (10 advanced hunters plus 2 gold), minion upgrades and equipment, and the oblivion potion (resets skills).
- Port: faction goals as small data records (counted kills, counted stats, items) that unlock the next, which fit a world that only needs a new JSON file per faction goal.
- WQ16 default: rank-ups beyond the base levels are tasks whose cost names the opposing group. Dial: counts, village captures required. Reason/cost: a count of kills or captures is a visible, finite price that shapes who the player fights.

### B3. Skills

**WF22 (shown: `ModSkills`, `HunterSkills`, `factionapi/skill_tree/*`, `skill_segment/*`, `FactionSkillPointProvider`).** Skill points = `max(0, level - 1) x factionSkillPointsPerLevel` (default 2.0), so level 14 gives 26 points; lord levels give the same per lord level in separate trees (vampire: `level`, `lord`, `dracula`; hunter: `level`, `lord`, `marshall`). A skill has a cost of 1 to 3 points, a parent list inside its segment and a tree. A tree unlocks by a predicate (`player_faction`). Sample costs: vampire night vision 2, fledgling 2, regeneration 2, rage 2, advanced biter 1, sword finisher 3, dark blood projectile 2, blood charge 1, freeze 2, sunscreen 2, attack speed 2, speed 2, blood vision 2, garlic vision 1, jump 2, teleport 3, summon bats 1, neonatal decrease 1, DBNO duration 1. Hunter: hunter disguise 1, weapon table 2, basic alchemy 1, crucifix wielder 1, garlic diffuser 1, purified garlic 2, awareness 2, brewing skills 2 each, master brewer 3, potion resistance 2, crossbow technique 2, dual wielding 2, artisan craftsmanship 3, minion stats 3. A skill is one of: an action unlock (`actionSkill`), a permanent attribute modifier (`attribute`), an on-enable and on-disable hook (`onEnable`, `onDisable`), or an unlock list (`unlocks`). Config can unlock everything at max level (`factionUnlockAllSkills`).
- Port: a skill is a small typed effect (action, attribute, hook, unlock) in a tree segment, so a world can add a faction's tree as data. Point income is a flat per-level number, with costs 1-3 so a build has about 10 to 15 skills at level 14.
- WQ17 default: skill trees are data segments; points come from level with a per-faction rate. Dial: points per level, cost range, whether a late-game cap unlocks everything. Reason/cost: a build is a committed choice, undone by a costly reset (the oblivion potion task needs a blood bottle), which gives specialisation weight.

**WF9 (shown: `WeaponTableBlock` line 71, `HunterSkills.WEAPON_TABLE`).** The weapon table's menu check reads `HunterPlayer...isSkillEnabled(WEAPON_TABLE)`. Hunter stations (weapon table, alchemical cauldron under `basic_alchemy`) are therefore skill-gated, not recipe-gated. The table also takes lava as a fuel (a `LAVA` block state, `MAX_LAVA`).
- Port: a skill that opens a station is the cheapest gate for a crafting family, and the station's fuel (lava) keeps it a place.
- WQ18 default: a station opens by a skill and burns a fuel. Dial: which stations are gated. Reason/cost: without the skill the table is just a block, so the player visibly lacks the knowledge, not the materials.

### B4. Blood, thirst, sun and garlic

**WF7 (shown: `BloodStats#onUpdate`, `addBlood`, `addExhaustion`; `VampirePlayer#levelChanged`).** The vampire's hunger bar is forced to 10 (`foodStats.setFoodLevel(10)`) and its exhaustion is moved into blood exhaustion, multiplied by the `BLOOD_EXHAUSTION` attribute (config default factor 0.7). Maximum blood: 20 at levels 1 to 3, 26 at 4 to 6, 30 at 7 to 9, 34 at 10 to 13, 40 at 14. A drop of 1 blood happens when exhaustion passes 4 (6 in vampire biomes), after saturation is spent. Healing: if blood is at maximum and saturation > 0, every 10 ticks heal `min(saturation, 6) / 6 x (1 + level/maxLevel x 0.5)`; if blood >= 18, heal at 80 ticks; otherwise 0.5x at 300 ticks. At 0 blood, every 80 ticks a "no blood" effect (150 ticks) unless health is low on Normal/Hard. `addBlood(amount, saturationModifier)`: saturation += blood added x modifier x 2, capped at current blood. Blood is not consumed in Peaceful unless `bloodUsagePeaceful`.
- Port: replace hunger with a second meter whose burn rate depends on place (vampire biomes burn slower), whose size grows with level, and which controls healing. One meter then ties hunting, resting and healing together.
- WQ19 default: a faction meter replaces hunger, with a level-scaled cap and place-dependent burn. Dial: cap per level, burn per place, healing rate. Reason/cost: cheaper at home (vampire biome slows the burn) and costlier abroad, which pulls the player toward faction land.

**WF23 (shown: `VampirePlayer#handleSunDamage`, `isBurningInSun`, `BalanceConfig`, `onPlayerLoggedIn`).** Exposure counter `ticksInSun` rises by 1 per tick of direct sun and falls by 1 otherwise, capped at 100 (`SAFE_SUN_TICKS`). Penalties are phased by vampire level: weakness (152 ticks, every 150 ticks) from level 2; nausea (180 ticks every 300 ticks, if more than 50 ticks exposed and no sunscreen) from level 3; damage from level 4 once at 100 ticks, every 40 ticks, equal to the `SUNDAMAGE` attribute (config default 7) x the environment attribute `SUN_INTENSITY` at the player's position. Shade, roofs, water (4 water source blocks above blocks sun, `sundamageWaterblocks`), the umbrella item in hand, and the mist-form skill block it; sunscreen amplifier >= 4 (or any with `sunscreenBuff`) caps exposure at 50 ticks. Instant death by ash is optional (`sundamageInstantDeath`, default off). On login, a vampire gets 200 ticks of sunscreen amplifier 4, so a rejoin in daylight is safe for 10 seconds. A killed vampire in sun turns to ash instead of the normal death animation. Water: a vampire in flowing water gets weakness scaled by `level / maxLevel x 3` unless it has the water-resistance skill (checked every 16 ticks).
- Port: a weakness as a ramp (a few seconds of grace, then escalating penalties by rank), with named counters (umbrella, roof, water, mist) and an environment value (sun intensity) so storms or deep forest change it. Level phases keep new members playable.
- WQ20 default: a faction weakness is exposure to one environment value, with a grace timer and rank-phased penalties. Dial: grace length, damage, minimum rank per penalty, counter list. Reason/cost: each counter costs a skill, an item or a route choice (roofs and tunnels), so the weakness shapes travel, not just numbers.

**WF24 (shown: `Helper#getGarlicStrengthAt`, `DamageHandler#affectVampireGarlicAmbient`, `FleeGarlicVampireGoal`).** Garlic is a per-chunk strength (none, weak, medium, strong; `getStrengthAtChunk`) set by garlic diffuser blocks and a hunter skill line (`garlic_diffuser`, `purified_garlic`, `garlic_diffuser_improved`), with fuel from a data map (`garlic_diffuser_fuel`). A vampire inside a garlic chunk takes damage every 17 ticks (offset 7) scaled by strength (the damage formula itself I did not read), and mobs flee garlic (`FleeGarlicVampireGoal`). A `blood_vision_garlic` skill lets vampires see garlic. A hunter-built diffuser is a defensive area, and a fuel cost makes it a recurring chore.
- WQ21 default: a faction's bane is a chunk-sized field that the opposing faction can build and fuel. Dial: field size, strength steps, fuel cost. Reason/cost: it forces a vampire to scout, and a hunter to supply fuel, so defence is upkeep.

**WF25 (shown: `ExtendedCreature#onBite`, `data_maps/entity_type/entity_blood.json`, `item_blood.json`).** Every creature has a blood value from a data map (cow 10, horse 13, camel 15, villager 15, pig 5, sheep 5, goat 7, cat 3, rabbit 2; chicken and fish-like creatures 0; 29 entries). A bite takes `max(1, maxBlood / 2)` (vampire player biters: `maxBlood / 6`), or all if it is the rest; advanced biter leaves 1 and 25% of the time doubles the haul; children give a third; blood 0 kills the victim (`noBlood` damage). A villager that is awake when bitten raises "villager hurt" reputation with the biter. Items carry blood too (raw beef 200, pork 100, human heart 200, weak heart 100; cooked meat 0). Blood bottle heals a minion `amount / 3`.
- Port: a creature's blood is a number in a table; one bite takes a fraction; side effects hit social standing in a given place.
- WQ22 default: feeding takes a fraction of a stored value from a source and costs standing where witnesses are awake. Dial: fraction, source table, witness rule. Reason/cost: sleeping prey is safer and children yield less, so the cheap meal is also the risky one.

**WF26 (shown: `InfectionStatus#checkStatus`, `vpSanguinareAverageDuration` 900 s).** Infection (Sanguinare) is a timed stage machine on any biten entity: at progress <= 0.5 hunger is added, at <= 0.25 sun sensitivity, at <= 0.1 "exposed", with a 2% per check nausea chance. At the end the target becomes a vampire. Duration is randomised 0.5x to 1.5x the average. Players can be turned only if config allows (`playerCanInfectPlayers`, `mobBiteInfection`).
- WQ23 default: conversion is a timed, staged, cure-able process with visible stages. Dial: duration, who can be infected. Reason/cost: the stages give a window for a cure, so becoming a vampire is a choice that can be interrupted.

### B5. Items, refinements, minions

**WF10 (shown: `ModRefinements`, `ModRefinementSets`, task `random_refinement1`).** A refinement is one attribute modifier or skill upgrade with magnitudes: armor 0.3, 0.5, 1.0; negative armor -1, -2, -3; speed 0.025, 0.05, 0.075; and similar sets for health, damage, attack speed. Sets bundle them with rarities: common single-buff sets and "buffed" sets (a skill upgrade plus a stat), "debuffed" sets (the same upgrade plus a penalty), and uncommon sets of the form buff-with-penalty (`armor2_n_health2`, `health2_n_damage1`, `damage2_n_speed1`), then higher tiers (`armor3_n_health3`). The three slots are amulet, ring and obi belt. Rewards come from tasks (e.g. 10 advanced hunters and 2 gold for a random refinement).
- Port: gear modifiers as a rarity table of paired buff/penalty bundles that point at the player's skills (a refinement for freeze duration only matters to a player with freeze).
- WQ24 default: a refinement roll pairs a gain with a cost, higher rarity widening both. Dial: slots, rarity table, penalty share. Reason/cost: the penalty is the in-world price of power, which makes the best roll a build choice.

**WF27 (shown: `VampireMinionEntity`, `LordPlayer#getMaxMinions`, `ServerConfig.minionPerLordLevel`).** Minions per player = `lordLevel x minionPerLordLevel` (default 1 per lord level, so 5 at the top). Minion stats are levelled stat tracks (inventory 2 levels, health 3, strength 3, speed 3), set by attribute from base plus per-level constants, and `x1.2` with the hunter skill `hunter_minion_stats_increase` (or `hasIncreasedStats`). Upgrades are items with a min and max level matched to the minion's faction, so a vampire minion cannot take a hunter upgrade. Minions have tasks (follow, stay, collect resources, defend; `MinionTasks`), and offline owners slow resource collection (`minionOfflineResourceCooldownMultiplier`). Binding a vampire minion needs a task (kill 4 advanced hunters, 5 barons, 32 gold) at lord level 1.
- Port: followers as a capped, levelled, faction-bound resource unlocked by rank, with upgrades as items, and a cheaper offline mode.
- WQ25 default: follower count = rank x a per-world rate; stats grow by tracks. Dial: rate, tracks, offline slowdown. Reason/cost: followers fight and carry for the faction, so a rank-5 player runs a small household that needs upkeep (blood, gear).

### B6. How rules differ by faction

**WF28 (shown across B1 to B5).** Same world, different rules:

| Rule | Vampire | Hunter |
|---|---|---|
| Hunger | Replaced by blood (WF7) | Normal |
| Sun | Ramped damage (WF23) | None |
| Garlic | Damaged (WF24) | Builds and fuels diffusers |
| Water | Weakness in flowing water (unless skill) | None |
| Level acts | Altar rituals, blood, hearts (WF8) | Trainer and table, metals, fangs (WF8) |

Neutral NPCs react by faction tags: hunter and vampire villages have faction guards, captured by the player via totems (stat `capture_village`, `win_village_capture`). I did not read the capture code.

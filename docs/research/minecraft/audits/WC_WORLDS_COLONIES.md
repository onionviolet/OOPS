## Top findings

### Terrafirmacraft (N13)

- **WC1 Place-shaped professions come from three data layers, not one.** Shown: tool/alloy tiers are fixed (anvil tier 0 stone, 1 copper, 2 bronzes, 3 wrought iron, 4 steel, 5 black steel, 6 red/blue steel; an anvil works its own tier and welds one tier above). What differs by place is which ores, rocks, woods and climate you have, so which rung you can reach first. Answer to N13: yes, geology makes the same profession differ by place, because tier access depends on local ore, but the *rules* of the profession are global.
- **WC2 Alloys are ratio windows, not recipes.** Shown: bronze needs 88-92% copper with 8-12% tin; brass 88-92% copper with 8-12% zinc; bismuth bronze 50-65 copper, 20-30 zinc, 10-20 bismuth; black bronze 50-70 copper plus 10-25 silver and 10-25 gold; steel family needs 3-4 inputs in windows (weak steel 50-70 steel, 15-25 nickel, 15-25 black bronze). Players tune by the mixed amounts. A mixture-window mechanic with visible ratio bounds is the reusable part (YAIRPG already has `mixture_rules.js`).
- **WC3 Iron is deliberately a process, not a smelt.** Shown/stated: all iron ore melts to cast iron; a bloomery (15 in-game hours, 2 charcoal plus 100 mB per bloom) makes a bloom that must be worked twice at an anvil into wrought iron; steel needs a blast furnace making pig iron, then anvil conversion to high-carbon steel, then steel. Black/red/blue steel need weak-alloy ingots welded to a lower steel ingot, then worked.
- **WC4 Anvil work is a small skill minigame with a quality output.** Shown: 278 anvil recipes, each with a tier and 3 ordered rules (for example hit last, punch second-last, upset third-last). Stated: a progress marker must land on a target while the last three actions match the rules; overshooting destroys the ingot; working needs the metal hot enough ("can work") and reheating is allowed. Efficient forging gives a four-step quality bonus (poor, well, expert, perfect) carried into the finished tool. Heat bands are named by color (480 faint red to over 1500 brilliant white).
- **WC5 Food spoils on a date, nutrition tracks recent meals.** Shown (code): default decay is 528000 ticks (22 game days at 24000 ticks per day) times a per-food modifier times a server multiplier. Modifiers across 148 foods run 0.1 to 99999 (effectively never) with most between 0.25 and 5; cooked meat has a smaller modifier than raw. Stated: sealed large vessels double lifetime, firepit cooking gives 1.33x, grill 1.66x, salting and vinegar pickling extend it, chests attract rats. Five nutrients (fruit, vegetables, protein, grain, dairy); nutrition is a weighted average over a rolling window of recent hunger worth of meals (window set in config), blank slots fall back to defaults (0.5, dairy 0). Stated: full nutrition raises max health, poor lowers it; death resets nutrition. Answer to the scout's "no hunger death" caveat: stated text says empty food or water slows you and causes damage, so it is not painless; check the config before claiming otherwise.
- **WC6 Crops are climate-band gated.** Shown: each crop has min/max hydration and temperature with a wiggle range (barley -8 to 26 C, hydration 18-75). Stated: each crop has one favored nutrient (N, P, K) that speeds growth and raises yield; consuming it replenishes the others a little. This is a seasonal/regional crop calendar that doubles as a place identity.
- **WC7 Local-material tool age.** Shown: knapping patterns exist per rock class (44 rock, 26 clay, 3 fire-clay, 6 leather, 8 goat horn) so the first axe head differs by stone category (igneous extrusive, intrusive, metamorphic, sedimentary tags).
- **WC61 Short months, and time pauses when nobody is online.** Shown (pass 2): default 8-day months and 96-day years; new worlds start June 1 of year 1000; the calendar only advances while players are logged on (`ServerCalendar#onOverworldTick`, `ServerCalendar#setPlayersLoggedOn`; defaults stated in `CommonConfig`).
- **WC62 Temperature is four stacked terms.** Shown (pass 2): position temperature = a worldgen average that bands along one axis (about -20 to 30, coasts pulled toward 5) + a season term (month cosine with a 12 to 18 amplitude that varies by band) + a daily term (seeded noise of about plus or minus 3 and a small noon peak) minus altitude (0.16 per block above sea level, capped near 18); underground trends to a steady 15 (`OverworldClimateModel#getTemperature` and helpers, `RegionGenerator#baseNoise`). This is the code behind WC6's crop windows.
- **WC63 Rain is one world event filtered by local wetness.** Shown (pass 2): annual rainfall (0 to 500, banding along the other axis) sets a local threshold; a global rain event must exceed it for rain to fall there, and it falls as snow below 0; climate labels follow Koppen-like thresholds on average temperature and rainfall (`WorldTracker#isRaining`, `Climate#getPrecipitation`, `KoppenClimateClassification#classify`).

### Productive Bees (N14)

- **WC8 Breeding is a lookup of parent pairs, so matching is the game.** Shown: 109 `bee_breeding` recipes of the form parent1 + parent2 -> offspring (for example magmatic + water -> breeze, lava + water -> obsidian, farmer + rancher -> cupid, lumber + rancher -> farmer). It is not stochastic genetics for the type. Answer to N14: yes, it demands matching, but it is a recipe graph, and the part that behaves like genetics is the *attribute* layer below.
- **WC9 Attribute genetics are a separate, transferable layer.** Stated: attributes are productivity (normal, medium, high, very high), weather tolerance (none, rain, any), behavior (diurnal, nocturnal, metaturnal), endurance (weak to strong; chance to keep stinger) and temper (passive to hostile). Genes are bottled from a bee, centrifuged into samples, combined (same genes raise purity) and fed on a honey treat to another bee with a chance set by purity. Tolerances are earned by leashing a bee out in bad weather or the wrong time of day, with death risk until adapted.
- **WC10 Four acquisition routes besides breeding.** Shown: wild nests spawn by biome and block (35 `bee_spawning` recipes), nests can be repopulated with honey treats, `bee_conversion` turns one bee into another by feeding a tagged item (50 gem conversions), and flower requirements can be blocks, tags, entities or fluids (lava bee, amber bee encases entities). Solitary nest bees do not live in hives; hive inhabitants do (hives hold 3 bees, 5 with an expansion box).
- **WC11 Output is a table per bee, scaled by productivity.** Shown: `bee_produce` recipes give a base comb plus chance extras (pollen at 0.15). Compat-driven: most produce/breeding rows depend on other mods and are guarded by existence conditions, so the core graph is small (about a dozen rows at the root).
- **WC64 Trait inheritance is a bounded roll with a ratchet.** Shown (pass 2): for productivity, endurance, behavior and weather tolerance the child rolls uniformly between its parents' ranks and keeps the better of that and its own fresh roll; temper takes the calmest of both parents and "normal", so breeding never makes bees angrier (`BeeHelper#setOffspringAttributes`, `BeeAttributesHandler`, `GeneValue`). This replaces the earlier "unread `GeneAttribute`" gap.
- **WC65 Traits also move by exposure and by gene items.** Shown (pass 2): leashed bees out in rain or at night gain tolerance or night activity with a 10% chance per 1337-tick check; honey treats calm temper 5% of the time; extracted genes carry 15 to 54% purity, combine by summing toward 100, and apply with purity-percent odds; productivity rank multiplies hive output by about 1.3x to 2.2x (`EventHandler#onEntityTick`, `HoneyTreat#applyGenesToBee`, `CombineGeneRecipe#mergeGenes`, `AdvancedBeehiveBlockEntity`; defaults stated in `ProductiveBeesConfig`). Confirms the stated mechanics in WC9.

### MineColonies (N16)

- **WC12 Requests resolve down a priority chain.** Shown (pinned source): resolver priorities are building 200 (its own stock), warehouse 150, crafting 125, retrying 50, player 0. A request first tries the requesting building's inventory, then warehouse stock, then crafter workers (public or private), then retries (max 3, every 1200 ticks), and finally appears to the player. Answer to N16: a worker that lacks a tool or input creates a typed request (for tools, with a required level from the target block), couriers carry deliveries, and the player is the last resolver. Builder-style compound requests spawn child requests for the remainder.
- **WC13 Tools are gated by hut level.** Shown: a worker pulling a block that needs a higher pickaxe level than the building's max equipment level raises an "important" alert asking for a higher-level building rather than a tool.
- **WC14 Citizen skills cap by home hut level.** Shown: 11 skills (Athletics, Dexterity, Strength, Agility, Stamina, Mana, Adaptability, Focus, Creativity, Knowledge, Intelligence), each with a complementary and adverse pair; jobs name a primary and a secondary skill (farmer-type roles use Dexterity/Agility style pairs, teacher Knowledge/Mana). XP stops once skill level reaches (home level + 1) x 10 unless the home is at the cap. Intelligence growth is capped at happiness x 9.
- **WC15 Happiness is a weighted average of named factors.** Shown: school 1, security 4, social 2, mystical site 1, food 3 (static weights); homelessness 3, unemployment 2, health 2, idle-at-job 1, slept-last-night 1.5 (time-based, decaying through complain then demand thresholds). Result is 0-10, multiplied by a research bonus; factors at exactly 1.0 are skipped. Food factor: dietary diversity and quality measured against home level, so higher homes demand more varied and better food. Security is guards versus workers (target ratio about 1 guard per 1.33 workers, capped at 2x).
- **WC16 Raids are adaptive and paced.** Shown: defaults min 10 nights, average 14 nights between raids; forced after average+2; raid difficulty per colony 1-14, starts 7, +1 after a raid that killed under 5% of max citizens, falls if losses exceed 15% and delays the next raid by 40% of the average; minimum colony "raid level" 75; five raider cultures (barbarian, pirate, Norse, Egyptian, Amazon) chosen by biome with a 1-in-2 chance to ignore biome; a "raid without death" happiness bonus exists. Server config can disable raids.
- **WC17 Research is paid in items, gated by buildings.** Shown: 217 research nodes (combat 68, technology 76, civilian 69, unlockable 4) in a parent tree; costs are item stacks (for example 128 books), requirements are building levels (159 "building", 23 "single-building"), five nodes have exclusive children; effects are named multipliers (leveling, teaching, block-place speed). Research is done by a researcher worker in a university-type building.
- **WC18 Narrative is a quest schema with triggers.** Shown: quests have parents, random rarity, state triggers (building type and level), citizen triggers (job, child), dialogue objectives with branching answers, and a reputation trigger; 15 tutorial and 10 general quests plus 3 diseases (curable with listed foods).
- **WC19 Housing sets population.** Shown: initial citizens 4, max 250 per colony; citizens leave/grow with beds, children grow in homes (reproduction manager).
- **WC51 Build orders are gated by builder tier, distance and research.** Shown (pass 2): a hut upgrade is refused unless some staffed builder hut is at least the target level, a builder is within 100 blocks (or the player picks one), the colony owns every chunk the footprint touches, and any hut research is above the current level; the builder hut can always lift itself one level, which is the bootstrap (`AbstractBuilding#requestUpgrade`, `WorkOrderBuilding#canBuildIgnoringDistance`, `BuildingBuilder#canBeBuiltByBuilder`, `WorkManager#addWorkOrder`).
- **WC52 Work queue: claimed, then priority, then age.** Shown (pass 2): each colony tick drops orphaned orders, sorts claimed first, then higher priority, then older, and gives each to the first idle qualifying builder; manual-mode builders only take what the player assigns (`WorkManager#onColonyTick`, `WorkManager#tryAssignWorkOrder`, `IWorkOrder#WORK_ORDER_COMPARATOR`).
- **WC53 Builders work in fixed stages.** Shown (pass 2): new huts run clear, solid, weak solid, water clear, air clear, decorate, spawn; upgrades skip clearing; removals drain water then remove; one position per step; a missing item pauses the builder and files a request; an item neither held nor listed forces a material rescan (`AbstractEntityAIStructure#structureStep`, `#loadStructure`, `AbstractEntityAIStructureWithWorkOrder#loadRequirements`).
- **WC54 Materials arrive in inventory-sized buckets.** Shown (pass 2): the bill is scanned in four passes (solid, weak solid, deco, entities) and split into buckets that fit the worker's inventory minus 9 slots; the builder requests the current and next bucket, so deliveries stay one wave ahead (`AbstractEntityAIStructureWithWorkOrder#requestMaterials`, `BuildingResourcesModule#checkOrRequestBucket`). Feeds the WC12 request chain.
- **WC55 Build speed scales with skill and research.** Shown (pass 2): each placement waits 15 ticks scaled by 10 / (skill / 2 + 10), reduced by a placement-speed research; builder mining is halved; a finished build gives 8 XP; level 4+ builders clear hostile mobs from the site daily (`AbstractEntityAIStructure#structureStep`, `EntityAIStructureBuilder#getBlockMiningTime`, `#killMobs`, `AbstractEntityAIStructure#completeBuild`).
- **WC56 Hut levels are separate blueprints; cost is the schematic.** Shown (pass 2): standard huts max at level 5; each level loads its own schematic and the materials are that schematic's blocks; sample counts in one style grow from about 740 to 1340 blocks (builder) and 890 to 3830 (town hall); styles differ about 3x for the same hut (`WorkOrderBuilding#create`, `AbstractBuilding#getMaxBuildingLevel`; counts from blueprint data, raw totals not upgrade deltas).
- **WC57 Guard huts scale reach and toughness per level.** Shown (pass 2): each hut level adds 2 max health, 3 vision (base 15) and 30 patrol radius (base 50); barracks towers hire one guard per level and cannot outlevel their barracks (`AbstractBuildingGuards#getBonusHealth`, `#getBonusVision`, `#getPatrolDistance`, `GuardConstants`). Inferred: gear tiers unlock by level band (gold at 1-2 up to diamond at 4-5).
- **WC58 Guard tasks: patrol, hold, follow, mine patrol, rally.** Shown (pass 2): patrol waits for the whole hut's squad at each point; hold returns near a post; follow keeps 6 or 15 blocks and teleports past 30; a rally overrides everything; chase limits differ by task (hold 10, plus 20 for knights; patrol 80; follow and rally 30) (`AbstractEntityAIGuard#decide`, `#patrol`, `#follow`, `#getPersecutionDistance`).
- **WC59 Guards pick targets from a threat table.** Shown (pass 2): new threats score up to 7 for closeness and up to 2 for low health; attacks on the guard or a citizen add more; a broad scan runs every 4 seconds; a raider sighting redirects nearby guard huts' patrols to it (`ThreatTable#addThreat`, `TargetAI#searchNearbyTarget`, `CombatUtils#notifyGuardsOfTarget`).
- **WC60 Guard failure states have character.** Shown (pass 2): idle guards can doze off (chance falls with Adaptability and a research) for about two minutes and must be woken; with retreat on and research, guards under 20% health run home and regenerate; wounded guards hit twice as hard (`AbstractEntityAIGuard#shouldSleep`, `#wakeUpGuard`, `#shouldFlee`, `#regen`, `MeleeCombatAI#getAttackDamage`).

### Vault Hunters (config and guide only)

- **WC20 Run structure.** Shown/stated: one run = one crystal-forged vault, timer 30000 ticks (25 min) base in `vault_timer`; layout starts at a portal room (the exit), rooms branch four ways; objective rolled by level (levels 0-9 only Monolith; 10-19 Monolith/Elixir; 20-49 adds Scavenger; 50+ adds Hunt the Guardians), or forced with a crafted seal. Exiting early forfeits the objective but keeps what you carried.
- **WC21 Crystal modification is risk budgeting.** Stated: modifications cost experience levels and raise "instability"; catalysts add +5% instability per modifier; curse chance 75% when instability changes; motes reveal or remove curses; a chaos catalyst applies 30 random modifiers. Levels above the vault level (by 4) reduce experience and loot quantity.
- **WC22 Persistence between runs.** Shown/stated: vault level 0-100 (tnl table starts 1000 and grows), points for talents, abilities and expertises (expertise points every 5 levels), mod-gating researches bought with knowledge stars and never unlearnable, five saved skill builds (swap costs a share of regret orbs), prestige power tree (cost in knowledge and regret points, gated by greed tier), god reputation (100 points total), bounties, gear levels (gear protects only if you meet its level), ascension score to tier and embers tables.
- **WC23 Death handling.** Shown: a "downed" bleed-out timer by difficulty (120 s easy-ish down to 30 s hardest, cumulative 10% reduction) with a 5 s revive channel and 30% health on revive; stated: an Afterlife modifier on unmodified crystals prevents item loss, which implies item loss on death otherwise (inferred).
- **WC24 Research as a mod gate.** Shown: 59 research entries gate whole mods by restricting use, crafting and block interaction; groups raise each other's cost (storage group +0.24 per unlock), so unlock order matters.

### Roguelike Dungeons

- **WC25 Depth-banded theme and difficulty with weighted loot tiers.** Shown: default dungeon runs 11 level bands from Y=50 to -50 (10 blocks each) using a theme accordion (oak, spruce, dark oak, stone, crumbled, mossy, tiled slate); five difficulty steps; gear material is rolled from a per-difficulty weight table (armor easiest: wood 300, stone 80, copper 40, iron 20, gold 5, diamond 2, netherite 1; hardest: 1,5,10,50,5,10,1), and loot rules assign per-chest-type provider counts (block 8, food 5, ore 5, supply 4, junk 6, one precious per chest) per difficulty. Rooms use weighted-random picks with "sometimes" and "once" flags.

### Blightfall

- **WC26 Taint world as a restoration campaign with a reputation gate.** Stated (quest text): you are colonists from a starship on an alien world covered in spreading purple biomass (taint). Shown (pass 2, decoded quest file and scanner code): a "Mission Outlook" reputation controls whether the supplier will release goods (WC45); badges at 20%, 50% and 100% purification per named zone are printed by a scanner that measures progress against a baseline (WC43); a Dawn Machine, Dawn Totems, Ethereal Blooms, a purity wand focus and silverwood splash potions cure taint at different scales (WC42).
- **WC27 Quest-giver cast delivers progression.** Shown (pass 2, decoded): ten quest sets, each a named NPC (Instructor, Supplier, Geologist, Scout, Paranormalist, Colonist, Xenobiologist, Engineer, Black Market, Dark One); the Geologist asks for samples of newly found alien metals; the Supplier's requests unlock ship supply throttled by outlook. Hardcore Questing config: 2 starting lives, quest book lost on death, lives max 255.
- **WC28 Pack-level gates by script.** Shown: CraftTweaker scripts add research and recipes (tier six altar and Dawn tools gated by 50-point aspect costs; a taint egg gated by warp and 128 vitium). Nether access is replaced by a netherless module.
- **WC38 The Dawn Machine is a chunk-at-a-time cleaner with aspect upgrades.** Shown (pass 2, `DawnMachineTileEntity#func_145845_h`, `#getNewCleanseCoords`, `#getAerCost`): a 3x3 silverwood multiblock with four spouts, each face taking one of ten essentia types. Unless a redstone signal pauses it, it cleans one chunk every 12 ticks, or every 4 with Machina. Without Ordo it picks random chunks; with Ordo it sweeps a spiral. Without enough Aer it stays within 4 chunks of itself (a 9x9 chunk area); with Aer it reaches anywhere on the 113 x 136 chunk map, paying max(8, square root of chunk distance) per jump. Cognitio lets it reroll up to 10 empty chunks per cycle. The machine force-loads its own chunk and its current target.
- **WC39 Power is a discount, not a requirement.** Shown (`DawnMachineResource` enum, `DawnMachineTileEntity#getDiscount`, `#haveEnoughFor`): every action has an aspect cost (8 for routine burns, 256 for Ordo, Cognitio and Machina, 16 for curing aura nodes), and each essentia unit is worth 4 to 8192 internal units depending on aspect. If the machine also holds that action's listed RF, Botania mana or Blood Magic essence, each one it can pay halves the cost, so up to 8x cheaper. RF enters from below (cap 1.28M), mana through a spark from nearby pools (cap 1M), blood from a ritual that needs eight Ethereal Blooms and moves up to 500 LP per refresh (`RitualDawnMachine#performEffect`). Players see a green, yellow and red glow tied to stored RF (80k, 40k, 20k thresholds).
- **WC40 Purification is a biome rewrite; blocks then rot on their own.** Shown (`DawnMachineTileEntity#cleanseBiome`, `#cleanseSingleBlock`; Thaumcraft `BlockTaint#func_149674_a`, `BlockTaintFibres#func_149674_a`): the free core act resets every taint, eerie or magical-forest column to the biome the world generator would have placed there. Aspects then pay for extras: Ignis burns crusted taint to flux goo (Vacuos makes it air), turns tainted soil back to dirt (Herba makes it grass) and removes fibres; Vacuos clears goo; Arbor plants a biome-matched sapling on heavily crusted columns; Auram turns tainted aura nodes normal; Sano turns six tainted animals and three tainted Custom NPC types back to vanilla. Thaumcraft's own rules finish the job: outside the taint biome fibres delete themselves, crusted taint becomes goo at 1 in 20 per random tick and tainted soil becomes dirt at 1 in 10. So the biome flag is the true state, and blocks are its lagging symptom.
- **WC41 Blightfall runs taint spread about 200 times hotter than default.** Shown (`BlockTaintFibres#taintBiomeSpread`, `Config.taintSpreadRate`, pack `Thaumcraft.cfg` `biome_taint_spread=1`): on each random tick a taint block with 2 or more adjacent taint blocks converts a neighbouring column to taint biome with chance 1 in (rate x 5). The default rate is 200 (1 in 1000); Blightfall sets it to 1 (1 in 5). Inside the biome, fibres climb onto logs and leaves (2+ taint neighbours) and soil (3+), and swarmers spawn when none is within 16 blocks. Standing on taint poisons players 1 in 100 per contact tick. The pack's coremod softens edges: fibres cannot taint sand or Botania flowers, and aura nodes only generate in ocean biomes (`BlightCoreCoremod` editor list).
- **WC42 Cleansing tools form a scale ladder.** Shown: the purity wand focus cleans (2p+1)^2 columns for 10 Terra plus 15 Ordo vis per column area, where p is its enlarge rank, and cures a tainted node for 10,000 Terra plus 15,000 Ordo (`ItemPurityFocus#onFocusRightClick`, `#getBigVisCost`, `#onFocusBlockStartBreak`). A silverwood splash potion resets biome within radius 5 and clears taint within radius 3 (`EntitySilverPotion#func_70184_a`). An Ethereal Bloom resets one column every 20 ticks, up to 7 blocks away on each axis (`TileEtherealBloom#func_145845_h`). A Dawn Totem resets a random 3x3 patch within 25 blocks every 20 ticks and catches up on time spent offline at up to 5 patches per tick (`DawnTotemEntity#func_145845_h`, `#func_145829_t`). A Pure Daisy is hooked to reset a nearby column at 1 in 35 per tick (`BiomeCleanseSupport#cleanseBiomeAroundTile`). The Dawn Machine cleans whole chunks. Each step up trades setup cost for area and automation.
- **WC43 Progress is measured against a baseline, by zone.** Shown (`MapScanner#scanSomeChunks`, `RegionMap#calculateCompletion`, `TileEntityIslandScanner#updateZoneCompletion`): the scanner samples a fixed 1760 x 2520 block region every 2 blocks, one 5-chunk strip per tick (about 2.5 minutes per full pass), and counts a sample as tainted if any of its 2x2 columns holds taint or fibres at any height. Samples map to 24 named zones from a painted region image. Zone progress is (clean now minus clean at baseline) / (total minus clean at baseline), clamped 0 to 1, and the baseline ships as a config file. Bronze, silver and gold badges print at 0.1999, 0.4999 and 0.9999; a global badge prints at 0.9999 overall; a three-block gauge shows 43 stages. This corrects WC26's "20/50/100%" to code values.
- **WC44 Quest graph: ten NPC trees joined through the Scout.** Shown (own parser of the HQM 4.4.5-blight binary format, `Quest#loadAll`; all 217,577 bytes consumed): 420 quests in 10 sets (Colonist 82 is the largest), 513 prerequisite edges, of which 142 cross between NPCs (Scout to Colonist 32, Scout to Geologist 15, Instructor to Supplier and Scout 12 each). 50 roots, longest chain 21 (Purify Floating Islands III). Task mix: 206 hold-item, 114 hand-in, 52 location, 43 reputation threshold, 16 kill, 5 death, 0 craft; 400 quests have one task. 112 quests hide once available; 33 use "m of n parents" (for example 7 of 11 alien-metal finds unlock the Scout's floating islands). Option links make the team-registration chain mutually exclusive. 66 quests ask for zone badges. Lottery bags ship 18 tiers (seed, sapling and animal rarities; five machine grades from "Disappointing" to "Jackpot!!").
- **WC45 Mission Outlook is a spendable supply budget with a lose line.** Shown (decoded rewards and reputation tasks; `QuestTaskReputation.ReputationSetting#isValid`; `QuestingData#die`): outlook starts at 0 ("Concerned") with named markers from Cancelled (-150) to Near Assured (250). Supplier requests are cooldown-repeatable purchases paid in outlook (copper -2 every 2 hours, a machine -24 every 6 hours, the one-time power plant -20) and unlock by tier (copper and tin at Fair 30, silver and blaze rods at Good 60, nickel and platinum at Very Good 90, the power plant at Excellent 120). Emergency food stays available until outlook falls to In Peril. Purification badges pay +15, +20 and +30 per zone (+1430 across 66 quests), and the all-gold badge pays +1000. Each death costs 10 outlook, baked into the pack's HQM build, and early death quests refund a little. "In Danger" fires at Near Cancellation, and "YOU LOSE." fires at Cancelled, turning the world into sandbox play (stated). A second reputation, the Dark One's Impression, gates Blood Magic slates and tools the same way.
- **WC46 Consequences push curing over killing.** Shown: a player who dies while taint-poisoned leaves an "Infected <name>" hostile (20 HP) at the death spot (`PlayerZombieEventHandler#playerDeathEvent`); poisoned animals and villagers drop nothing when killed (`PurityFocusEventHandler#onEntityDrop`); rotten flesh gives no food (`FoodHandler`). Purple Panic patches the world on server start by version, and it scans Custom NPC dialogue records for one of three ending dialogues to place a "Victory" structure at the matching site (`PurplePanicMod#onServerStarted`). Inferred: the pack has three alternative endings chosen through NPC dialogue, and the world remembers which one you took.

### Betweenlands (N39)

## System patterns across targets

1. **Visible ratio windows** (TFC alloys, VH crystal instability, MineColonies food factor diversity vs home level): inputs are tuned against a shown band rather than a fixed recipe.
2. **Capacity caps tied to a place object** (citizen skill by home level, VH gear by level, TFC anvil tier): the thing that stores or houses you limits the ceiling.
3. **Priority-chain fallbacks ending with the player** (MineColonies resolvers, Dimensional Doors link-or-limbo).
4. **Risk budgets with an opt-in pressure dial** (VH instability and curses, MineColonies raid difficulty adapting to losses, Roguelike depth bands).
5. **Local rules that rewrite imported items** (Betweenlands weakness/rot/taint, VH item blacklists, Blightfall netherless): worlds assert their own law on outside gear with a toggle and a whitelist.
6. **Exclusive branches in talent graphs** (Prominence, VH talent exclusions).
7. **Reputation as supply valve** (Blightfall mission outlook as a spendable budget with a lose line, WC45; VH god reputation; MineColonies happiness feeding research).
8. **True state plus lagging symptoms** (Blightfall biome flag versus taint blocks that rot once the flag flips, WC40; TFC climate terms behind crop windows, WC62): fixing the underlying state is cheap per unit, and visible cleanup follows on its own.
9. **Progress measured against a starting baseline** (Blightfall scanner zones, WC43): percent restored ignores land that was already clean, so every zone starts at 0%.
10. **Story gates on power growth** (Prominence campaign level caps that delete excess XP, WC49; Blightfall supply tiers by outlook, WC45; MineColonies builder tier gating upgrades, WC51).
11. **Scale ladder of the same verb** (Blightfall wand, potion, bloom, totem, machine, WC42): one action, five tools trading setup cost for area and automation.

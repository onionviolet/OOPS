## Top findings

### Terrafirmacraft (N13)

- **WC1 Place-shaped professions come from three data layers, not one.** Shown: tool/alloy tiers are fixed (anvil tier 0 stone, 1 copper, 2 bronzes, 3 wrought iron, 4 steel, 5 black steel, 6 red/blue steel; an anvil works its own tier and welds one tier above). What differs by place is which ores, rocks, woods and climate you have, so which rung you can reach first. Answer to N13: yes, geology makes the same profession differ by place, because tier access depends on local ore, but the *rules* of the profession are global.
- **WC2 Alloys are ratio windows, not recipes.** Shown: bronze needs 88-92% copper with 8-12% tin; brass 88-92% copper with 8-12% zinc; bismuth bronze 50-65 copper, 20-30 zinc, 10-20 bismuth; black bronze 50-70 copper plus 10-25 silver and 10-25 gold; steel family needs 3-4 inputs in windows (weak steel 50-70 steel, 15-25 nickel, 15-25 black bronze). Players tune by the mixed amounts. A mixture-window mechanic with visible ratio bounds is the reusable part (YAIRPG already has `mixture_rules.js`).
- **WC3 Iron is deliberately a process, not a smelt.** Shown/stated: all iron ore melts to cast iron; a bloomery (15 in-game hours, 2 charcoal plus 100 mB per bloom) makes a bloom that must be worked twice at an anvil into wrought iron; steel needs a blast furnace making pig iron, then anvil conversion to high-carbon steel, then steel. Black/red/blue steel need weak-alloy ingots welded to a lower steel ingot, then worked.
- **WC4 Anvil work is a small skill minigame with a quality output.** Shown: 278 anvil recipes, each with a tier and 3 ordered rules (for example hit last, punch second-last, upset third-last). Stated: a progress marker must land on a target while the last three actions match the rules; overshooting destroys the ingot; working needs the metal hot enough ("can work") and reheating is allowed. Efficient forging gives a four-step quality bonus (poor, well, expert, perfect) carried into the finished tool. Heat bands are named by color (480 faint red to over 1500 brilliant white).
- **WC5 Food spoils on a date, nutrition tracks recent meals.** Shown (code): default decay is 528000 ticks (22 game days at 24000 ticks per day) times a per-food modifier times a server multiplier. Modifiers across 148 foods run 0.1 to 99999 (effectively never) with most between 0.25 and 5; cooked meat has a smaller modifier than raw. Stated: sealed large vessels double lifetime, firepit cooking gives 1.33x, grill 1.66x, salting and vinegar pickling extend it, chests attract rats. Five nutrients (fruit, vegetables, protein, grain, dairy); nutrition is a weighted average over a rolling window of recent hunger worth of meals (window set in config), blank slots fall back to defaults (0.5, dairy 0). Stated: full nutrition raises max health, poor lowers it; death resets nutrition. Answer to the scout's "no hunger death" caveat: stated text says empty food or water slows you and causes damage, so it is not painless; check the config before claiming otherwise.
- **WC6 Crops are climate-band gated.** Shown: each crop has min/max hydration and temperature with a wiggle range (barley -8 to 26 C, hydration 18-75). Stated: each crop has one favored nutrient (N, P, K) that speeds growth and raises yield; consuming it replenishes the others a little. This is a seasonal/regional crop calendar that doubles as a place identity.
- **WC7 Local-material tool age.** Shown: knapping patterns exist per rock class (44 rock, 26 clay, 3 fire-clay, 6 leather, 8 goat horn) so the first axe head differs by stone category (igneous extrusive, intrusive, metamorphic, sedimentary tags).

### Productive Bees (N14)

- **WC8 Breeding is a lookup of parent pairs, so matching is the game.** Shown: 109 `bee_breeding` recipes of the form parent1 + parent2 -> offspring (for example magmatic + water -> breeze, lava + water -> obsidian, farmer + rancher -> cupid, lumber + rancher -> farmer). It is not stochastic genetics for the type. Answer to N14: yes, it demands matching, but it is a recipe graph, and the part that behaves like genetics is the *attribute* layer below.
- **WC9 Attribute genetics are a separate, transferable layer.** Stated: attributes are productivity (normal, medium, high, very high), weather tolerance (none, rain, any), behavior (diurnal, nocturnal, metaturnal), endurance (weak to strong; chance to keep stinger) and temper (passive to hostile). Genes are bottled from a bee, centrifuged into samples, combined (same genes raise purity) and fed on a honey treat to another bee with a chance set by purity. Tolerances are earned by leashing a bee out in bad weather or the wrong time of day, with death risk until adapted.
- **WC10 Four acquisition routes besides breeding.** Shown: wild nests spawn by biome and block (35 `bee_spawning` recipes), nests can be repopulated with honey treats, `bee_conversion` turns one bee into another by feeding a tagged item (50 gem conversions), and flower requirements can be blocks, tags, entities or fluids (lava bee, amber bee encases entities). Solitary nest bees do not live in hives; hive inhabitants do (hives hold 3 bees, 5 with an expansion box).
- **WC11 Output is a table per bee, scaled by productivity.** Shown: `bee_produce` recipes give a base comb plus chance extras (pollen at 0.15). Compat-driven: most produce/breeding rows depend on other mods and are guarded by existence conditions, so the core graph is small (about a dozen rows at the root).

### MineColonies (N16)

- **WC12 Requests resolve down a priority chain.** Shown (pinned source): resolver priorities are building 200 (its own stock), warehouse 150, crafting 125, retrying 50, player 0. A request first tries the requesting building's inventory, then warehouse stock, then crafter workers (public or private), then retries (max 3, every 1200 ticks), and finally appears to the player. Answer to N16: a worker that lacks a tool or input creates a typed request (for tools, with a required level from the target block), couriers carry deliveries, and the player is the last resolver. Builder-style compound requests spawn child requests for the remainder.
- **WC13 Tools are gated by hut level.** Shown: a worker pulling a block that needs a higher pickaxe level than the building's max equipment level raises an "important" alert asking for a higher-level building rather than a tool.
- **WC14 Citizen skills cap by home hut level.** Shown: 11 skills (Athletics, Dexterity, Strength, Agility, Stamina, Mana, Adaptability, Focus, Creativity, Knowledge, Intelligence), each with a complementary and adverse pair; jobs name a primary and a secondary skill (farmer-type roles use Dexterity/Agility style pairs, teacher Knowledge/Mana). XP stops once skill level reaches (home level + 1) x 10 unless the home is at the cap. Intelligence growth is capped at happiness x 9.
- **WC15 Happiness is a weighted average of named factors.** Shown: school 1, security 4, social 2, mystical site 1, food 3 (static weights); homelessness 3, unemployment 2, health 2, idle-at-job 1, slept-last-night 1.5 (time-based, decaying through complain then demand thresholds). Result is 0-10, multiplied by a research bonus; factors at exactly 1.0 are skipped. Food factor: dietary diversity and quality measured against home level, so higher homes demand more varied and better food. Security is guards versus workers (target ratio about 1 guard per 1.33 workers, capped at 2x).
- **WC16 Raids are adaptive and paced.** Shown: defaults min 10 nights, average 14 nights between raids; forced after average+2; raid difficulty per colony 1-14, starts 7, +1 after a raid that killed under 5% of max citizens, falls if losses exceed 15% and delays the next raid by 40% of the average; minimum colony "raid level" 75; five raider cultures (barbarian, pirate, Norse, Egyptian, Amazon) chosen by biome with a 1-in-2 chance to ignore biome; a "raid without death" happiness bonus exists. Server config can disable raids.
- **WC17 Research is paid in items, gated by buildings.** Shown: 217 research nodes (combat 68, technology 76, civilian 69, unlockable 4) in a parent tree; costs are item stacks (for example 128 books), requirements are building levels (159 "building", 23 "single-building"), five nodes have exclusive children; effects are named multipliers (leveling, teaching, block-place speed). Research is done by a researcher worker in a university-type building.
- **WC18 Narrative is a quest schema with triggers.** Shown: quests have parents, random rarity, state triggers (building type and level), citizen triggers (job, child), dialogue objectives with branching answers, and a reputation trigger; 15 tutorial and 10 general quests plus 3 diseases (curable with listed foods).
- **WC19 Housing sets population.** Shown: initial citizens 4, max 250 per colony; citizens leave/grow with beds, children grow in homes (reproduction manager).

### Vault Hunters (config and guide only)

- **WC20 Run structure.** Shown/stated: one run = one crystal-forged vault, timer 30000 ticks (25 min) base in `vault_timer`; layout starts at a portal room (the exit), rooms branch four ways; objective rolled by level (levels 0-9 only Monolith; 10-19 Monolith/Elixir; 20-49 adds Scavenger; 50+ adds Hunt the Guardians), or forced with a crafted seal. Exiting early forfeits the objective but keeps what you carried.
- **WC21 Crystal modification is risk budgeting.** Stated: modifications cost experience levels and raise "instability"; catalysts add +5% instability per modifier; curse chance 75% when instability changes; motes reveal or remove curses; a chaos catalyst applies 30 random modifiers. Levels above the vault level (by 4) reduce experience and loot quantity.
- **WC22 Persistence between runs.** Shown/stated: vault level 0-100 (tnl table starts 1000 and grows), points for talents, abilities and expertises (expertise points every 5 levels), mod-gating researches bought with knowledge stars and never unlearnable, five saved skill builds (swap costs a share of regret orbs), prestige power tree (cost in knowledge and regret points, gated by greed tier), god reputation (100 points total), bounties, gear levels (gear protects only if you meet its level), ascension score to tier and embers tables.
- **WC23 Death handling.** Shown: a "downed" bleed-out timer by difficulty (120 s easy-ish down to 30 s hardest, cumulative 10% reduction) with a 5 s revive channel and 30% health on revive; stated: an Afterlife modifier on unmodified crystals prevents item loss, which implies item loss on death otherwise (inferred).
- **WC24 Research as a mod gate.** Shown: 59 research entries gate whole mods by restricting use, crafting and block interaction; groups raise each other's cost (storage group +0.24 per unlock), so unlock order matters.

### Roguelike Dungeons

- **WC25 Depth-banded theme and difficulty with weighted loot tiers.** Shown: default dungeon runs 11 level bands from Y=50 to -50 (10 blocks each) using a theme accordion (oak, spruce, dark oak, stone, crumbled, mossy, tiled slate); five difficulty steps; gear material is rolled from a per-difficulty weight table (armor easiest: wood 300, stone 80, copper 40, iron 20, gold 5, diamond 2, netherite 1; hardest: 1,5,10,50,5,10,1), and loot rules assign per-chest-type provider counts (block 8, food 5, ore 5, supply 4, junk 6, one precious per chest) per difficulty. Rooms use weighted-random picks with "sometimes" and "once" flags.

### Blightfall

- **WC26 Taint world as a restoration campaign with a reputation gate.** Stated (quest text): you are colonists from a starship on an alien world covered in spreading purple biomass (taint). A "mission outlook" tier (such as on probation, near cancellation, very good) controls whether quest-givers will release supplies; badges (bronze 20%, silver 50%, gold 100% purification) are printed per named biome from a scanner and raise outlook. Shown (jar class list and lang): a Dawn Machine multiblock, RF charger, totems of dawn, ethereal blooms, purity wand focus and silverwood splash potions cure taint at different scales; a baseline scan of taint is stored and compared by biome zone.
- **WC27 Quest-giver cast delivers progression.** Stated: named NPCs per discipline (instructor, scout, xenomorphologist for metals, black-market trader, magic and tech specialists); samples of newly discovered alien metals are requested for rewards; resource requests unlock supply from the ship, throttled by outlook. Hardcore Questing config: 2 starting lives, quest book lost on death, lives max 255.
- **WC28 Pack-level gates by script.** Shown: CraftTweaker scripts add research and recipes (tier six altar and Dawn tools gated by 50-point aspect costs; a taint egg gated by warp and 128 vitium). Nether access is replaced by a netherless module.

### Betweenlands (N39)

- **WC29 Answer to N39: foreign gear is locally degraded, by explicit rules.** Shown (code): in the dimension, overworld tools, swords and bows are "weakened" to 0.3x break speed and swing speed unless whitelisted; vanilla torches and fire-starting tools are blocked; overworld food rots, overworld potions taint, and fertilizers are blacklisted; each has a config toggle and a gamerule, plus whitelist/blacklist lists. Native tools can corrode (six stages; speed multiplier falls to 0.3 at max corrosion), and the corrosion applies to Betweenlands items implementing the corrodible interface, not to foreign gear (inferred from the interface use).
- **WC30 Player decay replaces hunger-as-only-pressure.** Shown: decay 0-20; walking, rain, being in water (x2.75) and getting hit add to an acceleration meter; every 4.0 consumes a saturation point then adds one decay; decay 10/13/16 applies slowness I/II/III; max health scales down with decay level. Base speeds by difficulty 0/0.0025/0.0033/0.005 per tick; armor reduces gain up to 100%. Native food heals decay.
- **WC31 Food sickness is per-food repetition fatigue.** Shown: thresholds 50/110/180 hatred points (fine, half, sick), +5 per eating; when sick, 2/3 of the hunger and decay healing is removed, with a 25% chance to keep one point.

### Dimensional Doors (N40)

- **WC32 Access rules.** Shown: three pocket kinds by dimension data: dungeon pockets, personal (private) pockets, public pockets, plus limbo. A private pocket is created per player UUID on first entry, regenerated if all entrances are gone, and dye items thrown at the door recolor and expand it (config private size 2, public 1, grid 32, max 15). Rift links are restoring targets: a link that makes its target lazily, then remembers it (retained place), and invalidates when a rift is deleted. Dungeon targets use depth and coordinate factors (max dungeon depth 50, depth spread 20) so farther links lead to deeper pockets. Failure sends to limbo, which decays surroundings and returns you within a config distance (0 to 5000); "hardcore limbo" off by default.
- **WC33 Pocket decay.** Shown: rift decay spreads with chance 1.0 and delay 40 ticks, decaying into air by default; doors close behind by default.

### Prominence II (N45)

- **WC34 Two-tier talent system on one skill engine.** Shown (Puffish Skills config): one default "Talent Tree" (49 node types, 176 placed nodes, exclusive-root so you pick one of six starting paths: archer, rogue, fire, arcane, frost, protector; 32 spendable points; XP curve 220 x 1.125^level with a level limit of 32, sourced from kills) and 13 locked per-artifact trees (7 spendable points each, XP 100 x level, no built-in XP source, so XP comes from the pack's own mod), each named for a legendary weapon (for example Thunderwrath with nodes for artifact damage, a warrior's-devotion damage and infusions that are exclusive "path to corruption" vs "path to Etyr"). One artifact "Granny's Pan" is a joke tree with 0 nodes and a 35,000,000 XP level.
- **WC35 Exclusive branches are the design signal.** Shown: connection types "normal" and "exclusive" in each tree; picking one excludes the rival path (infusion choices in the story are mirrored by exclusive nodes).
- **WC36 NPC campaign is a quest-chapter chain with bespoke dialogue.** Shown: 31 FTB Quests chapters; the main chapter has a locked-by-level entry ("Unlock 51-65 Leveling"), lore books, boss kills, structure visits, XP-level payments and item tasks; story chapters are titled with named NPCs (S'kellak, Gu'mas, Mirgaff, Alm, Radin) that also appear as voiced dialogue assets in the Prominent mod, which also ships dialogue JSON (guard, guild leader, relic recovery), lottery tables for artifact/trinket tiers T1-T4 and vendor trade lists split into "past" and "present" eras. Era split of vendors is a content pattern worth noting (inferred as a time-state shop swap).
- **WC37 Appearance is separate from stats.** Shown: a transmog config and a transmogging vendor in both eras; trims mod set (allthetrims, bettertrims).

## Donor questions answered

- **N13 (TFC):** Local geology can make a profession differ by place via which ores, rocks and climates gate which rung of the tier ladder you reach first (WC1), but process rules are global; retain comfortable ordinary play only if hunger/thirst damage is tuned down (WC5).
- **N14 (Productive Bees):** Meaningful matching exists as a parent-pair recipe graph plus transferable attribute genes with purity rolls (WC8, WC9); solitary nest bees versus hive inhabitants are distinguished by data (WC10).
- **N16 (MineColonies):** Requests resolve building, warehouse, crafter, retry, player in priority order; tool needs carry a level requirement; couriers deliver (WC12, WC13).
- **N39 (Betweenlands):** The local rule affecting imported equipment is tool weakness to 0.3x speed, torch/fire-tool blocks, food rot and potion tainting, each toggleable (WC29).
- **N40 (Dimensional Doors):** Access is door-linked with lazy generation and remembered links, private per-player pockets re-created if lost, and limbo as failure space (WC32).
- **N45 (Prominence II):** The specific progression consumer is the Puffish Skills trees: one shared exclusive-root talent tree plus per-artifact trees, with the story gated by chapter locks (WC34-WC36).

## System patterns across targets

1. **Visible ratio windows** (TFC alloys, VH crystal instability, MineColonies food factor diversity vs home level): inputs are tuned against a shown band rather than a fixed recipe.
2. **Capacity caps tied to a place object** (citizen skill by home level, VH gear by level, TFC anvil tier): the thing that stores or houses you limits the ceiling.
3. **Priority-chain fallbacks ending with the player** (MineColonies resolvers, Dimensional Doors link-or-limbo).
4. **Risk budgets with an opt-in pressure dial** (VH instability and curses, MineColonies raid difficulty adapting to losses, Roguelike depth bands).
5. **Local rules that rewrite imported items** (Betweenlands weakness/rot/taint, VH item blacklists, Blightfall netherless): worlds assert their own law on outside gear with a toggle and a whitelist.
6. **Exclusive branches in talent graphs** (Prominence, VH talent exclusions).
7. **Reputation as supply valve** (Blightfall mission outlook, VH god reputation, MineColonies happiness feeding research).

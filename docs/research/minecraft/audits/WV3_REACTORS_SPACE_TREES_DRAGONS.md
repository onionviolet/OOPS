## Findings

### Bigger Reactors (reactor simulation)

Held-audit overlap: GT (boiler, large turbine), Mekanism (fission, turbine blades 28) and TA already cover steam and turbine ladders. What follows is what those do not hold: the radiation-ray heat model, fertility, self-braking, the resonance curve and waste-gates-the-next-tier.

- **W3-01** (shown, `BaseReactorSimulation#tick`, `FullPassReactorSimulation`, `TimeSlicedReactorSimulation`, `Config`) **There is no meltdown; the core brakes itself.** Neutron intensity is multiplied by `1 - 0.95 exp(-10 exp(-0.001 x 1.2 x Tc))`: about 1.0 at 20 C, 0.95 at 1000 C, 0.62 at 2000 C, 0.28 at 3000 C, floor near 0.05 to 0.12. Fuel absorption falls the same way. No explode or melt code exists (negative grep over the Java). Overheating costs yield and fuel efficiency, never the save. Arithmetic on the formula is inferred.
  - *Q (default, dial, reason):* Default: a heat-driven machine loses output as it runs hot and never destroys itself. Dial: steepness and the heat scale per world (a hearth versus a star-core); worlds with a destroy-on-overheat canon opt in with an in-world reason. Reason: AD38 says things work by their mechanisms and "idle, absent for hours" must not punish. In-world cost: fuel burnt per useful unit rises when hot, plus the cooling you must feed.
- **W3-02** (shown, `FullPassReactorSimulation#setupIrradiationTick`, `Config`) **Geometry as superlinear output.** Intensity = `((raw^1.05)/N)^1.05 x N` with N control-rod columns and raw = `(fuel + waste/100) x 0.1`. Big, tall, many-column cores beat flat pancakes. Fuel burn per rod per tick = `0.0007 x effectiveRaw / fertility`. Size limits are 3x3x3 to 128x192x128 (reactor), 5x4x5 to 32x192x32 (turbine). Worked values are inferred.
  - *Q:* Default: workshop efficiency comes from three or four shape numbers (columns, height, absorber ratio) with a mild exponent (about 1.05), no block grid. Dial: exponent from 1.0 (flat) to 1.2, size cap per world. Reason: the household can show "layout" as one number set (UR rules: no new UI per feature). In-world cost: materials, floor space, upkeep surface.
- **W3-03** (shown, `BaseReactorSimulation#tick`, `fertility()`, `Config`) **Fertility is a log-scaled "burn-in" that divides fuel cost.** Fertility gain comes from neutrons absorbed by fuel; displayed value is `log10(f) + 1`; decay per tick is `max(0.1, f/20)` while active and the denominator is x200 (4000) while idle. Fuel used per rod is divided by it. Gain per neutron is `fuelRadScalingMultiplier x 4000/max(1, totalStored)`, so full big cores gain less per neutron (note: 4000 is the per-rod capacity, shown). Continuity is rewarded; stop-start is punished (idle decay is 200x slower, so a paused core keeps most of its seasoning, a detail that softens the punishment).
  - *Q:* Default: a "seasoning" stat grows with absorbed output, decays fast when running and near-frozen when idle, and divides input cost. Dial: gain rate, floors, idle multiplier. Reason: gives long idle runs a visible compounding reward without a timer. In-world cost: someone must keep the process fed and tended; a cold start loses the bonus.
- **W3-04** (shown, `ModernTurbineSimulation`, coil JSON) **Two-lever turbine with a resonance curve.** Steam energy is `latentHeat x turbineMultiplier` (water: 4 x 2.5 = 10 per mB); blade count adds capacity quadratically (`n(n+1)/2` per arm); coil efficiency 0.33 (iron) to 1.5 (unobtanium) and an exponent `bonus`; grid efficiency peaks at 900 and 1800 RPM, dips to about 0.5 near 636 and 1273 RPM, and is zero past about 2457 RPM; drag is `(rpm x 0.0005)^2` per mass. Passive reactors output x0.5 with a 0.2 transfer factor and need no supply; active plus turbine is about 5x passive per unit heat (inferred).
  - *Q:* Default: a safe low-tier passive route plus a staged active route (heat to steam to motion to power), each stage with a multiplier, and a resonance sweet spot. Dial: per-stage multipliers, number of resonance peaks, which stage is optional. Reason: several upgrade levers, and "tune it" becomes a player verb at almost no UI cost (one slider, one readout). In-world cost: coolant supply, one more stage to maintain, drag losses that grow with speed squared.
- **W3-05** (shown, `ReactorAccessPortTile`, recipe JSON, `D/recipes`) **Waste is the next tier's material.** Burnt fuel returns 1:1 as waste (cyanite); turbine casing, shaft and blades need cyanite ingots; terminals need blutonium; the best coil and exchanger need ludicrite. Run the first machine to unlock the second. The Cyanite Reprocessor is present in recipes and lang but its Java is fully commented out in this commit (shown), so the dust plus sand shortcut is the only other route. Blutonium is identical to uranium as fuel (shown).
  - *Q:* Default: a by-product of tier N is an ingredient of tier N+1, with one expensive shortcut. Dial: waste ratio and whether a shortcut exists. Reason: forces the first system to run before the second exists (PROGRESSION_GATE_RULES: gates should be earned loops, not counters). In-world cost: storage and ejection chores, plus a hazard if the waste sits.
- **W3-06** (shown, `D/ebcr/moderators/**`) **Material-property table as the tuning surface.** Every interior block has four numbers: absorption [0,1], efficiency [0,1], moderation >= 1, conductivity >= 0 (air 0.10/0.25/1.10/0.05; graphite 0.10/0.50/2.0/2.0; lead 0.75/0.75/1.75/1.5; vibranium moderation 8). Coils carry a triple (efficiency, extraction, bonus exponent). A new world is a new table.
  - *Q:* Default: a few data rows per material, no code per material. Dial: which materials exist per world and the numeric ranges. Reason: matches the "mechanisms, not allowed-outcome lists" rule (AD38). In-world cost: sourcing the rare material and its side effect (a strong absorber starves the fuel; a strong moderator barely absorbs).
- **W3-07** (shown, `TimeSlicedReactorSimulation`, `Config#useFullPassSimulation=false`) **The default simulation is time-sliced.** One rod column and one y-level is sampled per tick, so large reactors integrate from a stochastic slice. A cheap fidelity dial worth copying for any heavy simulation in an idle loop (the owner would be the runtime rules, AR1-AR16).
- **Contradictions kept (WPR20):** the neighbour-rod absorption bonus is `insertion x 0.001` on the CPU paths but `0.01` on the GPU paths, so the "+50%" reaches only +5% on CPU (shown; reading it as a bug is inferred). Lang says dangling coils must sit in the 8 blocks nearest the shaft, but `dangling_coil` exists only in lang, never in Java, and the layer multiplier is `2/(distance+1)` (stated against shown). `ChannelToChannelHeatConductivityMultiplier` is configured and never used. `reactor2` is a stub (`ReferenceSimulation.tick()` is empty).

### Powah (tiers, reactor, energizing)

Held-audit overlap: Mekanism and AE2 tier ladders and Thermal augments are held (TB). The reactor here is the new piece.

- **W3-08** (shown, `DefaultEnergies`, `PowahConfig`) **Two multiplier rows run the whole mod.** Active machines scale 1, 4, 10, 40, 100, 400, 2000 across seven tiers (starter to nitro); passive sources use a gentler 1, 3, 5, 10, 20, 40, 100. Generation, capacity (x1000 of production), transfer (x4), batteries (x4000 of reactor production), cables, rods and ender channels (1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 12) all derive from them. Fuel energy per item is tier-independent (`energy_per_fuel_tick` 30); a higher tier only burns the same stored energy faster (shown; guidebook agrees, stated). Tier N+1 is crafted from the tier N machine plus a tier material and capacitor, so each upgrade consumes the old unit.
  - *Q:* Default: one curve per family, derived from a table, passive families on a flatter curve. Dial: curve shape per world, which families use which. Reason: one table gives consistent pacing and keeps passive sources relevant late (answers the M23 question "how do tiers scale without a trivial max-out": by putting the scarce thing in fuel logistics, not in the tier). In-world cost: fuel hauling must scale with tier or the machine idles; each upgrade melts down the lesser device.
- **W3-09** (shown, `ReactorBlockEntity#calcProduction`, `#calcConsumption`, `#processTemperature`; data maps) **Cooling is the resource, not an alarm.** calc = `(temp/1000 x 0.98/2) x (redstone ? 1.4 : 1)`; output = `(1 - calc) x (fuel/1000) x baseGen / (no carbon ? 1.2 : 1) / (no redstone ? 1.4 : 1)`; burn per tick = `(1 + 0.25 x tierIndex) x calc`; target temperature = `700 + 180 coal + 120 redstone`, divided by `|coolant temp| + 1` when a solid coolant is active. Solid coolants: dry ice 712 units at -32, blue ice 568 at -17, packed ice 192 at -8, snowball 12 at -3; one unit per 40 ticks. Inferred examples at starter: uranium only with water gives 0.39 of base and 28.5k FE per fuel item; with dry ice 0.59 and 1.42M FE per item (about 50x). The tank's water is never drained and adds no cooling by itself (shown); only solid coolant divides the temperature, and only while the tank holds some fluid.
  - *Q:* Default: output falls as heat rises, boosters raise heat and remove small penalties, coolant strength is the divisor, and hotter runs burn more fuel per tick. Dial: coolant table, booster temperatures, burn growth per tier. Reason: a safe-and-slow versus hot-and-fast tradeoff with no explosion. In-world cost: a cold supply chain (ice biomes, dry ice ore, power for the energizing recipe) and a slow temperature ramp (+1 per tick).
- **W3-10** (shown, `EnergizingOrbBlockEntity`, `EnergizingRodBlockEntity#postTick`, recipe JSON) **Expensive-to-restart crafting.** The orb's buffer equals the recipe's energy; changing any input slot resets progress to zero; rods in a range-4 cube feed it after a 20-tick warm-up that decays at 1 per tick when starved. Recipe costs: niotic crystal 300k from a diamond, spirited 1M from an emerald, nitro crystals 20M from a nether star chain, dry ice 10k from 2 blue ice, uraninite 25k to 100k per ore. Nothing else gates the next tier except these crafts.
  - *Q:* Default: premium crafts are a committed energy bill that is lost if interrupted. Dial: warm-up length, reset rule, range. Reason: makes big crafts a deliberate act (and naturally idle-shaped: queue, feed, wait). In-world cost: wasted power on interruption, and a big supply line.
- **W3-11** (shown, `PowahBaseEnderBlockEntity#onSlotChanged`, `EnderNetwork`) **A wireless bank bought with sacrificed batteries.** Per player: 12 channels; a cell or gate selects one; dropping a battery into the extender slot adds its capacity and charge to the channel and consumes it; tier limits channel count. Capacity starts at zero and caps at 9e18.
  - *Q:* Default: shared storage is a pool built from consumed crafted items. Dial: channels per tier, consume versus return. Reason: a clean "bank" mechanic for an idle game. In-world cost: the batteries are gone and one owner holds the pool.
- **W3-12** (shown, `ThermoBlockEntity`, `MagmatorBlockEntity`, `SolarBlockEntity`, data maps) **Site-placed passive power.** Thermo = `heat/1000 x max(1, -coolantTemp/2) x passiveRate`, heat read from the block below (magma 800, lava 1000, blazing block 2800), 1 mB coolant per 40 ticks; solar produces flat rate only in daylight (`skyDarken < 4`, the hours of that window unverified); magmator drains 100 mB when its buffer empties (lava is 10,000 FE per 100 mB).
- **Contradictions kept:** discharger capacity and transfer arguments are swapped (shown), so a starter discharger has capacity 1,000 and transfer 1,000,000; battery item transfer equals its capacity (shown); `(int)` truncation in the thermo formula; nitro-tier flows above about 2.1e9 per operation are clamped by `Ints.saturatedCast` in several paths (shown).

### Industrial Foregoing (machine progression, work areas, chains)

Held-audit overlap: TB covers Mekanism ore multiplication and Thermal augments; WV2 covers Mystical Agriculture crops. Chores that run while the household is idle, work-area sweeping and the plastic bootstrap are new.

- **W3-13** (shown, `ModuleCore` frame recipes, `GR/dissolution_chamber/*`) **Four frames, each unlocked by a different mid-chain consumable.** Pity (iron, redstone block, logs), Simple (plastic plus 250 mB latex), Advanced (plastic plus 500 mB pink slime, which comes from killing animals), Supreme (plastic plus 135 mB ether gas, which needs a living Wither beside a fluid laser). Every machine names the frame it needs. The bootstrap is Pity frame, then Fluid Extractor and Latex Processing (neither needs plastic), then dry rubber, smelted 1:1 into plastic (inferred cost per plastic: 750 mB latex, 500 mB water, about 2,000 FE).
  - *Q:* Default: each tier opens a new resource loop, not bigger numbers. Dial: which machine sits where; material amounts (250 / 500 / 135). Reason: matches PROGRESSION_GATE_RULES "earned loop" gates. In-world cost: the whole loop (animals, a Wither) must be built and fed first.
- **W3-14** (shown, `IndustrialMachineTile#canAcceptAugment`, `item/addon/*`) **One augment per type, tier index not stacking.** Speed (tier 1 or 2: 2 or 3 progress per tick), efficiency (bar x0.9 or x0.8), processing (repeat the work 2 or 3 times, each repeat charging its own energy), range (radius = tier + 1, 12 tiers, each tier needs a named material from cobblestone up to emerald). Two quirks: efficiency only shortens the progress bar, so a working machine that pays per action saves no energy; the processing addon on working tiles repeats `work()` but only the first call sets the next cycle length (shown/inferred through the missing Titanium base).
  - *Q:* Default: upgrades are a menu with one slot per type, not a stack. Dial: tier counts and stored values. Reason: removes exploit stacking at zero rules cost. In-world cost: each addon is a latex-bath craft (1000 mB) plus a mid-grade material.
- **W3-15** (shown, `RangeManager`, `IndustrialAreaWorkingTile#getPointedBlockPos`, `#increasePointer`) **Work area is (2r+1)^2, swept one tile per cycle.** No addon is a single tile; tier 0 range addon gives 3x3 (9), tier 3 gives 9x9 (81), tier 11 gives 25x25 (625), one layer thick. Pointer machines (breaker, sower, gatherer) act on the pointed tile per cycle and advance, so full-area time is tiles x cycle. Entity machines (crusher, slaughter, sewer, duplicator) scan the whole box at once and extend 3 high.
  - *Q (answers M6):* Default: a home's chore machine covers a footprint that grows with a range tier and sweeps it over time. Dial: radius per tier, cycle time, thickness. Reason: gives estate and household chores a legible "reach versus time" dial that fits an offline accumulator. In-world cost: tier 11 means 625 cycles per sweep unless paired with speed, and each ring costs a rarer material.
- **W3-16** (shown, `FluidExtractorTile#work`, recipe JSON) **Free at base rate, expensive to accelerate.** Powered (500 FE per action) yields 3x per action and returns a 0.4 follow-up (about 40-tick cycles); unpowered yields 1x at a 100-tick cycle with no energy. Each action has a 1% chance to advance a per-position counter held in an in-memory map (not saved); the 8th success converts the log and pays a bonus of 200x powered or 1x unpowered (oak: 400 mB versus 2 mB). Expected oak totals (inferred): about 5,200 mB latex for 400,000 FE powered, about 1,600 mB unpowered over about 80,000 ticks.
  - *Q:* Default: a gatherer always works, and spending power multiplies a payoff that arrives at the end of a long streak. Dial: break chance (1%), hits needed (8), multipliers. Reason: the world supplies slow free income and rewards investment, with no lockout. In-world cost: the tree is consumed (stripped) and the streak counter does not survive a reload.
- **W3-17** (shown, `BioReactorTile`) **Variety-quadratic fuel.** With `e` distinct valid item slots (up to 9) and water, one cycle (100 ticks, 400 FE) yields `((e-1) x 40 + 93) x e + 2` mB of biofuel: 95 for one input, 521 for three, 3,719 for nine (413 per item at nine). Distinct item kinds only (tags: crops, dyes, mushrooms, seeds, saplings, skulls, sweet berries).
  - *Q:* Default: output grows with the variety of inputs, not the bulk. Dial: constants 40 and 93, slot cap. Reason: rewards a varied larder or herb stock, which suits the life-sim direction. In-world cost: nine distinct supplies and equal water per cycle.
- **W3-18** (shown, `MobCrusherTile`, `SlaughterFactoryTile`, `SewerTile`, `MobDuplicatorTile`) **Mob and essence economy with a conversion loop.** XP is 20 mB essence per point; the crusher kills the first mob per cycle (100 progress, 50 FE), baby animals excluded; the duplicator charges `health x 12` mB essence per clone (a 20 hp zombie is 240 mB, which is 12 XP-equivalents), 5,000 FE per cycle, refuses above 32 mobs of the class in its box, and spawns 1 to 4. The slaughter factory pays by the victim's current health h: animals give meat h and pink slime 20h; non-animals give meat 20h and slime h. The sewer also drains the standing player's total experience into essence (shown). Imprisonment tool: ghast tear plus 4 plastic, blacklists ship empty.
  - *Q:* Default: copying a creature costs essence proportional to its life (health x factor), paid from XP that other chores produce. Dial: factor, copy cap, retry limit. Reason: a closed loop that cannot print value out of nothing (ties to ECONOMY_RULES). In-world cost: it needs a killing chore feeding it, plus ethical and reputation weight where the world has any (a captured named creature is not a farm animal).
- **W3-19** (shown, laser/ore base/fluid base/fermentation/sieve JSON and tiles) **Weighted resource table steered by lenses.** Ore selection: every matching recipe has a rarity entry by depth, biome and dimension; first matching entry wins (list order, not best weight); weight is the entry weight plus 8 per lens slot holding the recipe's catalyst; one weighted pick over all valid recipes. Example weights: iron 20 at depths 5 to 68; diamond 4 at 5 to 16; redstone 28; ancient debris 1. The multiplication ladder: 100 mB raw-ore fluid fermented at x2 (100 ticks), x3 (900), x4 (2,400, pink slime) or x5 (6,000, ether gas), then sieved with sand at 1 dust per 100 mB. About 100 drill cycles at 1,000 FE per ore (inferred; the exact bar semantics are in Titanium).
- **W3-20** (stated, `CHANGELOG.md` 3.6.2, lang) **Black hole units are gone from this commit.** Only item models and book text remain (a unit holds 2,147,483,647 of one item; a controller holds 9 units); there is no capacity formula or behavior to read. The question "what do black-hole units teach" must be answered from the replacement mod (Functional Storage), which is not cloned. Wave four candidate.
- **Contradictions kept:** many `@ConfigVal` comments disagree with the default values (mob duplicator comment 400 FE versus value 5,000; fluid extractor comment 10 FE versus 500; latex unit comment 400 versus 20; pitiful generator comment 400 versus 30). Trust values, not comments. The mob duplicator's `exactCopy` toggle is dead while the config is false. Ether gas needs a living Wither, and each fluid operation costs it 5 hp (shown).

### Extended Crafting (crafting tiers, singularities)

Held-audit overlap: TB (AE2 crafting CPUs and patterns) holds automated crafting; none of the held audits reads tiered grids or singularities.

- **W3-21** (shown, `ShapedTableRecipe#getTier`, `ShapelessTableRecipe#getTierFromSize`, `ExtendedCraftingInventory:86`) **Recipes classify their own tier by size.** Grids are 3x3, 5x5, 7x7, 9x9. Shaped recipes with JSON tier absent take tier 1 when width and height are both under 4, 2 when under 6, 3 when under 8, else 4. Shapeless recipes use ingredient count: under 10, under 26, under 50, else 4. A recipe with an explicit `tier` must equal the table's tier exactly (not "at least"). Tier 0 recipes fit any table large enough. One table type, one UI shape per tier, no per-tier UI work (answers M8).
  - *Q:* Default: recipe complexity maps to workbench tier by a bounding-box or count rule, with an explicit override for flavor. Dial: thresholds and slot counts per world. Reason: tier follows what a recipe is, not a hand-written list (AD38). In-world cost: you must own the bench that fits the work.
- **W3-22** (shown, recipe JSON) **Each bench is built from the previous bench.** Table pattern `BAB/CIC/BSB`: four tier components, one tier catalyst, two of the previous tier's table, one tier storage block, one black iron slate. Component = slate + luminessence + 2 tier material (iron, gold, diamond, emerald for tiers 1 to 4); catalyst = plus shape of 4 components around a black iron ingot. An Ultimate table consumes two Elite tables, so cost roughly doubles per tier (inferred).
- **W3-23** (shown, `AutoTableTileEntity`, config) **Delegated crafting is priced by time and draw.** Auto tables run 500 FE per tick regardless of tier; craft time is 20 x tier ticks, so FE per craft is 10k, 20k, 30k, 40k; capacity doubles per tier (500k to 4M); 100 FE per inserted item; 3 saved recipes per table. Higher tiers cost more per craft, not less. The basic auto table also runs vanilla recipes; higher ones do not.
- **W3-24** (shown, `CompressorTileEntity`, `DynamicRecipeManager#makeSingularityRecipe`, `ModSingularities`) **A singularity is a typed, tinted, bulk commodity.** One item with a data id; 19 default types (nine vanilla materials plus ten `#c:ingots/` tags: aluminum, tin, bronze, silver, lead, steel, nickel, electrum, invar, platinum); defaults 10,000 items and 5,000,000 FE at 5,000 per tick (1,000 ticks, 50 s) with a required catalyst (the ultimate catalyst, not consumed). A type is one JSON record (two colors, optional ingredient, count, enabled, in_ultimate flag); a tag with no members drops the recipe. No compressor or combination recipe JSON ships; only the dynamic singularity ones exist.
  - *Q:* Default: any stockpile can be compressed into one typed item for a fixed batch cost and a catalyst, defined as data. Dial: count and power per type, enabled flag, tag-based ingredient so each world's metals join. Reason: turns huge stockpiles into one idle-friendly object (inventory and ledger relief), and new worlds add types without code. In-world cost: batch time and a one-off energy bill; the catalyst proves you are equipped.
- **W3-25** (shown, `UltimateSingularityRecipe`) **Collection-completion capstone.** One of every enabled `in_ultimate` type, capped at 81 (the 9x9 grid), at an Ultimate table exactly. Default cost is 19 supply chains (inferred: 190,000 raw items, 95M FE). `the_ultimate_ingot` has no craft route in the repo (shown), so the top end relies on modpack recipes.
- **W3-26** (shown, `EnderCrafterTileEntity#getAlternatorPositions`, `progress()`; `FluxCrafterTileEntity`) **Helpers shorten a wait; the bill is placement.** The ender crafter has no energy: 60 s base, minus 1% per Ender Alternator in a 7x7x7 cube, floor 20 ticks (342 positions, so the floor is reachable). The flux crafter pays total energy once (400,000 for a flux star) and N alternators just divide the wait. The combination core uses a flat 7x7 square at its own height (49 pedestals, recipes capped at 48), 5M FE capacity, 500 FE per tick default.
  - *Q:* Default: ambient helpers shorten a ritual at a linear rate with a hard floor. Dial: effectiveness, floor, reach shape (cube versus flat). Reason: spending space and materials on helpers is a build decision that costs the household room, not a counter. In-world cost: the helpers must be built and kept; the combination ritual locks its site for up to thousands of ticks.
- **Contradictions kept:** compressor `inputLimit` defaults true at construction but false when the NBT key is missing; the same for auto-table `running`; the shipped default catalyst is `ultimate_catalyst`, but the book says "usually" a catalyst while the codec makes it required (shown against stated).

### Ad Astra (rockets, planets, life support)

Held-audit overlap: WV2 survival coupling covers hazards by food and thirst; none holds off-world gating. GT and TB hold oxygen only as a reagent.

## Considerations (WPR14, subtle interactions)

- **C1 Idle and offline.** Most of these loops are tick-by-tick (reactor, oxygen refresh, egg exposure). The idle model needs closed forms: the reactor's steady state (heat divides by coolant), exposure banks (count ticks, hatch at a threshold), and flood fills as a stored "claimed cells" integer recomputed on structure change. W3-07 shows a donor already accepting approximate sampling.
- **C2 Time units.** Dragon stages are 25 MC days each (600,000 ticks); a whole life to day 125 is 3,000,000 ticks, about 41 real hours at 20 tps (inferred). The idle game's day length decides whether that is a season or a campaign; keep it a per-world dial.
- **C3 Sealed secrets.** The bestiary design (W3-44) is a discovery route; any world secret it reveals stays under the sealed-secrets rule and is referred to by ID only.
- **C4 Companion welfare.** The mob duplicator and the corpse harvest turn creatures into inputs. For worlds with named or sapient creatures, flip those defaults (W3-18, W3-42): the essence cost becomes a person-sized cost with an in-world agent who objects.
- **C5 Bypasses.** Ad Astra lets creative, spectator and op 2 skip the tier gate. Weibao is essentially the only player (AD22), so an "always allowed" bypass for the player's own character is only a story dial, not an anti-cheat concern.
- **C6 Content budget.** Tables of 19 singularity types or 21 tree species are data, not code; porting 5 of each as a first slice is enough.

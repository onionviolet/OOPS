# WV2: ritual magic cost models (Blood Magic, Astral Sorcery, Forbidden Arcanus), 2026-10-23

Research and proposals only. Nothing here is an accepted decision. No code was built or run; every number comes from static reading of three inert shallow clones under `docs/research/minecraft/private/search-2026-10-23/<slug>/src/` (gitignored, each with a `RECEIPT.txt`). Port mechanisms, not code or assets.

Labels: **shown** = read in code or data (class#method or file), **stated** = author or docs text, **inferred** = my reading. Donor questions follow AGENTS.md: default, dial, reason (the in-world cost). Tables and tiers are palette, not spec.

## What the earlier audits already cover (so this file adds only the delta)

- `SR6` Psi overdraw and `SR3` Hex overcast pay the body only after a gate or a warning. Blood Magic differs: the body is the default overdraft with no gate (RM3).
- `SR13`/`SR18` Goety soul energy and `SR16` ElementalCraft metered flows cover "a pool filled by acts". Blood Magic's new part is that the pool is a persistent per-person account shared by every bound object, with tiered buffers that cap it (RM1, RM2).
- `SRP8` "upkeep only when work happens" (ElementalCraft shrines, Malum rites). Blood Magic rituals and toggled sigils charge a fixed tick upkeep while on, so they are the counterexample (RM6, RM7).
- `SR8` Occultism pentacle tiers and `SR11` Malum altar cover "bigger footprint, higher rank". Blood Magic adds a runes-as-dials altar (RM4) and Forbidden Arcanus adds a self-upgrading station whose upgrade needs a full tank (RM25).
- `MR14` Thaumcraft aura sets refill by moon phase. Astral Sorcery's moon phase decides which constellations exist tonight (RM13).
- `MR3`/`MR8` gamble and warp debts. Astral Sorcery artifacts (RM20) are a third shape: a decaying stability ladder with a puzzle exit.
- `WC39` "power is a discount, not a requirement". Forbidden Arcanus enhancers look like a discount but lower only the gate (RM26).

## Top findings

1. **RM1** Blood Magic's soul network is a per-player integer account (LP) that every bound item reads and writes by owner id, with a 16-entry ticket log. It is not stored in any item.
2. **RM3** At this HEAD a sigil use does not fail for lack of LP (shown by the return path). The shortfall is paid in HP, 1 damage per 100 LP unpaid, rounded up. Toggled sigils therefore do not switch off by themselves (inferred).
3. **RM4/RM5** The altar is a dial panel. Tier is a footprint; runes are counted modifiers with explicit formulas (speed +20% per rune, efficiency x0.85 per rune, capacity +20% per rune, and so on). A starved recipe loses progress, so it can regress.
4. **RM6/RM7** Rituals are fixed-rate upkeep. At low LP they nauseate the owner and skip a pulse rather than stopping. The activation crystal tier gates nothing at this HEAD.
5. **RM9/RM10** Demon will is a per-chunk typed resource that crystals drain at 45 will per growth unit, and it opens ritual behaviors above a 50 threshold.
6. **RM11** Living armor has a point budget of 100 (300 evolved) and negative-cost downgrades that fund extra points.
7. **RM12** Astral Sorcery's starlight yield multiplies crystal quality, a per-constellation moon-phase factor, a night ramp and a structure bonus, then loses 8% per missing purity tier.
8. **RM15** Player attunement is a once-per-life choice (unless a consumable undoes a perk), gated by night, sky, a discovered constellation and 800 ticks of standing still.
9. **RM17** Perk experience comes from five behavior roots and is rate-limited by a diminishing multiplier, so grinding one act decays.
10. **RM21/RM22** Forbidden Arcanus rituals pay four typed essences up front and fail destructively if pedestals change mid-ritual.
11. **RM26** FA enhancers lower the gate for starting a ritual, not the amount spent.
12. **RM27** The player's aureal pool is a regenerating personal resource (1 or 2 per armor piece per 5 s) that wands spend, 5 per shot.

## Part A. Blood Magic (1.20.1, 3.3.8-50)

Paths are under `src/main/java/wayoftime/bloodmagic/` unless a data path is given.

**RM1 (shown, `core/data/SoulNetwork`, `BMWorldSavedData`, `ItemBloodOrb`).** A soul network is one record per player id: `currentEssence` (int LP), `orbTier`, and an `EvictingQueue` of the last 16 tickets (each ticket names what spent or added LP). Binding an item stores the owner id, not an amount, so a hundred sigils draw from one account. `add(ticket, maximum)` returns 0 when the account is at or above the maximum, otherwise adds up to it. The maximum passed in by the altar is the capacity of the orb in the altar slot times an orb-rune factor (`BloodAltar#updateAltar`, `orb.getCapacity() * orbCapacityMultiplier`), so the account can only be filled as high as the best orb you filled it with (inferred from that call; account-wide cap code not otherwise seen).
- Orbs (`BloodMagicItems`): capacity and fill per tick for weak, apprentice, magician, master, archmage = 5,000/2, 25,000/5, 150,000/15, 1,000,000/25, 10,000,000/50. Altar fills the account by fill rate x (1 + speed bonus) per tick while an orb sits in the altar and fluid remains.
- Port: a person-level account of one typed currency, with a bound-object list that reads it. A bigger vessel is a bigger ceiling, not a bigger income.
- Donor question: default: one personal LP-like account shared by every bound tool. Dial: ceiling per vessel tier; what a ticket log shows. Reason/cost: a vessel is made from a rare material by an altar (RM5) and filled by blood, so the ceiling is paid in blood and rarity before it is spent.

**RM2 (shown, `ConfigManager`, `ItemSacrificialDagger`, `PlayerSacrificeHelper`).** Income routes: dagger stab = config `sacrificialDaggerConversion` (100) x 2 LP per use and 1 heart of damage; self-sacrifice with incense = (health above 10% of max) x conversion x (1 + incense x scaling, scaling 1.0), leaving the player at 10% health and zeroing incense; sacrifice of other mobs = default 25 LP per HP lost (config, per entity overrides, 0 blacklists). Altar rune bonuses add +10% per sacrifice rune and +10% per self-sacrifice rune (RM4).
- Port: the cheap income is your own life and others' lives, with a safety floor at 10% health. The multiplier is a per-world adjective (incense).
- Donor question: default: bodily income exists. Dial: LP per HP, the floor fraction, the incense multiplier. Reason/cost: it is paid in health and takes time to prepare incense; a world can set the floor higher than 10% for frailer bodies.

**RM3 (shown, `SoulNetwork#syphonAndDamage`, `#hurtPlayer`; `ItemSigilToggleable#inventoryTick`).** `syphonAndDamage` takes the ticket amount from the account if enough; if the account cannot cover it (`drainAmount <= 0`) or an event asks for damage, it calls `hurtPlayer`. That deals 1.0 `SACRIFICE` damage (invulnerability frames reset each hit) once for a ticket under 100 LP, or `ceil(amount/100)` times for 100 or more, none in creative. The method then returns success `true` regardless. Spot check (second read): the return value is built after `hurtPlayer`, with no failure branch except an event cancel or the client side.
- Consequence (inferred from the above): sigil use does not fail for lack of LP. The debt is paid in HP, so a 1,000 LP lava sigil unpaid costs 10 hits. Toggleable sigils call `syphonAndDamage` every 100 ticks and only switch off when it reports failure, which at this HEAD happens only on an event cancel, so an active toggled sigil with an empty account keeps draining health.
- Contrast with SR6/SR3: Psi and Hex gate or announce the bodily surcharge; here it is the default overdraft.
- Port: a currency whose zero balance converts to injury at a published rate. Use it as the failure branch of an account, not as a surprise.
- Donor question: default: an empty account turns spending into injury at a fixed rate. Dial: LP per hit (100 here), whether toggles auto-cancel, a lock-out below a health floor. Reason/cost: the player sees their own health fall; the in-world agent is the binding itself, which draws on the owner.

**RM4 (shown, `altar/AltarTier`, `altar/AltarUtil`, `BloodAltar#updateAltar` lines 470-495).** Six altar tiers are pure footprints. Tier 2 adds a ring of 8 runes (4 upgrade slots) below. Tier 3 turns all 8 inner runes into upgrade slots and adds 4 corner pillars (3 out) capped with glowstone plus 20 outer rune slots. Tier 4 adds bloodstone-capped pillars at 5 out and 28 rune slots. Tier 5 adds beacon-type blocks at 8 out and 52 rune slots. Tier 6 adds crystal-capped pillars at 11 out and 76 rune slots. Upgrade slots total 28, 56, 108, 184 for tiers 3 to 6 (my count from the component lists). Rune effects, as formulas (`updateAltar`, n = rune count of that type):
- Speed: consumption per tick x (1 + 0.20 n).
- Efficiency: decay when starved x 0.85^n (RM5).
- Sacrifice and Self-sacrifice: +0.10 n on those income routes.
- Capacity c and Augmented Capacity a: capacity multiplier (1 + 0.20 c) x 1.075^a; tank = 10,000 mB x multiplier, buffer 1,000 mB x multiplier.
- Displacement: input/output rate 20 per tick x 1.2^n.
- Orb: orb capacity x (1 + 0.02 n).
- Acceleration: charge frequency = max(20 - n, 1) ticks.
- Charging: rate = 10 n x (1 + speed/2); max charge = 1,000 mB x max(0.5 x capacity multiplier, 1) x n.
- Rune blocks also come in a second grade (`blood_rune_*_2` recipes), whose level counts I did not read (inferred as higher count per block).
- Port: a station's tier is a shape the player builds; its dials are counted blocks with a stated formula each, so the player can plan a build to a number. Different dials trade differently (speed spends more per tick, efficiency spends less on a stall, capacity just holds more).
- Donor question: default: station tiers are footprints and dials are counted add-ons with visible formulas. Dial: the multipliers and how many slots each tier opens. Reason/cost: space, the rare block for each rune grade, and the time to fill the altar from blood.

**RM5 (shown, `BloodAltar#startCycle`/`#update`; `data/.../recipes/altar/*.json`).** An altar recipe has `altarSyphon` (LP total), `consumptionRate` and `drainRate` (per tick) and `upgradeLevel` (minimum tier, zero-indexed: the code compares `recipe.getMinimumTier() <= altarTier.ordinal()`, so `upgradeLevel` 1 means tier 2). Progress rises by consumption x (1 + speed bonus) per tick while fluid lasts, capped by `syphon x stack size`; with no fluid and no charge, progress falls by `efficiency x drainRate` per tick (it regresses and does not freeze). A stack crafts as a batch: needed LP = syphon x count, per-tick rate unchanged. Examples (syphon, consume, drain, min level): weak orb from diamond 2,000/5/1/0; apprentice from a redstone block 5,000/5/5/1; magician from a gold block 25,000/20/20/2; master from the weak shard 40,000/30/50/3; archmage from dungeon metal 80,000/50/100/4; demonic slate 15,000/20/20/3; ethereal slate 30,000/40/100/4; dagger of sacrifice from an iron sword 3,000/5/5/1; life essence bucket 1,000/5/0/0.
- Port: a crafting step that pays its cost over time, that rolls backward when starved, and that rewards stacking (one LP price per item). Higher drain rates mean unattended starvation hurts more at the top end.
- Donor question: default: costly crafts accrue progress and decay without supply. Dial: decay rate per recipe. Reason/cost: a stalled procedure loses ground, which is why the supply line, not the recipe, is the build.

**RM6 (shown, `ritual/Ritual`, `types/*`, `common/tile/TileMasterRitualStone`).** 32 ritual classes. Each declares an activation cost (paid once from the owner's account at activation, skipped when the stone resumes from a redstone pause: `TileMasterRitualStone#activateRitual` guards on `!isRedstoned()`), a refresh cost, and a refresh time (default 20 ticks). Activation costs range from 500 (water) to 1,000,000 (condor); examples: speed 1,000, zephyr 1,000, crushing 5,000, green grove 5,000, lava 10,000, harvest 20,000, crystal split 20,000, regeneration 25,000, well of suffering 40,000, full stomach 100,000, standard dungeon 150,000, meteor 250,000. Refresh cost per pulse: water 25, harvest 20 per harvested crop (times harvests), lava 500, regeneration constant, crystal split 1,000, well of suffering 2 x count, jumping equals the jump height. The cost is charged inside each ritual, not in the stone's tick, and a ritual that cannot afford a pulse calls `causeNausea()` (confusion for 99 ticks) and returns (shown for regeneration, harvest, green grove, lava). If the layout breaks, `performRitual` stops the ritual with `BREAK_STONE`, which clears the ritual and needs a fresh activation (and cost). A redstone signal stops it with `REDSTONE`, which keeps the ritual.
- Port: a standing effect with an activation fee, a pulse fee, a graded refusal (nausea and skip) and a pause that keeps the fee paid.
- Donor question: default: a standing ritual costs a fee to start and a fee per pulse. Dial: pulse length, fee per pulse, what a failed pulse does to the owner. Reason/cost: the owner's account drains whether or not anyone is nearby; low funds cost the owner's comfort, not the ritual.

**RM7 (shown by absence, grep of every `super("..."` in `ritual/types`; `util/helper/RitualHelper#canCrystalActivate`).** Every ritual passes crystal level 0 at this HEAD, including meteor, condor and the two dungeon rituals; the check `ritual.getCrystalLevel() <= crystalLevel` is therefore always true for any crystal. The activation crystal tier (weak, awakened, creative) gates nothing in this version. The dungeon rituals have a config to make them creative-crystal-only (`makeDungeonRitualCreativeOnly`). Older versions gated by crystal tier (stated by my memory of 1.12, not seen here), so do not assume the gate exists.
- Donor question: default: ritual power is bought with LP and layout, not with a license item. Dial: add a license tier per world. Reason/cost: a world that wants gating can price it in a rare item, which this mod's own data no longer does.

**RM8 (shown, `ritual/EnumRuneType`, `AreaDescriptor`, `Ritual#canBlockRangeBeModified`).** Layouts use seven rune colors (blank, water, fire, earth, air, dusk, dawn). Every ritual defines named editable regions with a maximum volume and horizontal/vertical radius (meteor's item range: volume 27, radius 10), and the maximum is queried with the current demon will (`getMaxVolumeForRange(range, willConfig, holder)`), so a will-fed stone can allow a larger area (override behavior per ritual not read; inferred).
- Port: effect range is player-set within a cap, and the cap can depend on a second resource.

**RM9 (shown, `demonaura/*`, `common/tile/TileDemonCrystal`, `will/DemonWillHolder`, `ConfigManager`).** Demon will has five types (raw/default, corrosive, destructive, vengeful, steadfast) stored per chunk and dimension. A demon crystal cluster of up to 7 crystals (`crystalCount < 7`) checks once per second: if the chunk holds at least 0.5 of its own type, growth progress per second is `(1/200) x sqrt(will / 200) x speedModifier`, and the chunk loses 45 will per progress unit (same type; a catalyst can lower the rate). A non-default crystal with no matching will falls back to raw will at 0.6 speed and 90 will per progress. Gem capacities: petty 64, lesser 256, common 1,024, greater 4,096. Config threshold `min_<type>_will` = 50 for each type, the level at which the matching ritual behavior opens (e.g. regeneration's corrosive branch checks `corrosiveWill >= corrosiveWillDrain`). Sources: monster souls dropped when a soul-snared mob dies (amount = random() x (level+1)^2 x 4 + 1, `WillHandler`), and sentient weapons; sinks: crystals, rituals, tartaric gems.
- Port: a second, local, typed currency that lives in the land, is drained by growth and rituals, and refills by killing. The square-root yield makes the first points of will cheap and the next costly.
- Donor question: default: a land-borne typed resource fed by deaths and drained by growth. Dial: threshold to unlock behaviors (50 here), the exponent on growth. Reason/cost: killing near the site feeds it, and every crystal costs 45 per unit of progress, so a rich site is a worked site.

**RM10 (shown, `recipes/soulforge/*.json`).** Soul forge recipes carry `drain` (per tick, 2.5 to 4.0 in samples) and `minimumDrain` (the gem must hold at least this much, 80 for aug shaped charges, 300 for anointed variants). That is a gate on carried will plus a rate. Ritual-inventing example: anointment recipes combine an item with an anointment item, drain 4.0, minimum 300.
- Donor question: default: a recipe asks for a stored minimum and a drain rate from a carried vessel. Dial: both numbers. Reason/cost: the vessel must have been filled by deaths first.

**RM11 (shown, `data/bloodmagic/living_armor/*.json`, `ConfigManager`, `core/living/LivingStats`).** Living armor has a point budget: `default_upgrade_points` 100, `evolved_upgrade_points` 300. Each upgrade is a list of levels with `xp` (use needed to unlock that level) and `cost` (points). Health: xp 80, 200, 340, 540, 800, 1,600, 2,800, 5,000, 7,600, 10,000; cost 5, 12, 20, 35, 49, 78, 110, 160, 215, 320 (sum 1,004); bonus 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 26, 32, 38, 44, 50. Downgrades have negative cost, for example quenched costs -100 (so it adds 100 points). 24 upgrades plus 9 downgrades exist as files.
- Port: a build has a fixed budget, upgrades unlock by use and are bought with the budget, and voluntary handicaps raise the budget.
- Donor question: default: a body-build budget with use-unlocked upgrades and handicaps that refund points. Dial: budget per stage (100 then 300 here), refund size. Reason/cost: a handicap is a visible permanent weakness (quenched, melee decrease), so the extra points are paid for on the character.

**RM12b (shown, `common/item/sigil/*`, `ConfigManager` sigil_cost).** Sigil LP per use or per 100 ticks when toggled: bloodlight 10, air 50, void 50, magnetism 50, fast miner 100, frost 100, water 100, green grove 150, suppression 400, lava 1,000, teleposition 1,000 (config defaults). The config comment says the toggled upkeep is drained every 5 seconds, which matches the 100-tick check in code.
- Port: tools priced by the minute while on and by use otherwise. The price table is a per-world dial.

## Part B. Astral Sorcery (1.21.1, 2.0.3). Facts and formulas, paraphrased

Paths are under `src/main/java/hellfirepvp/astralsorcery/common/` and `src/generated/resources/data/astralsorcery/`.

**RM12 (shown, `tile/network/FocusCrystalSourceNode#produceStarlight`, `crystal/CrystalPropertyCalculator#getStarlightFocusRate`, `util/level/DayTimeHelper`).** The yield of a starlight source is a product:
`rate = [1 + (size x 0.04 + cut x (0.06 if size >= 2 else 0.03) + focus x 0.08) x (1 + constellationTier x 0.4)] x distribution(constellation, moon phase) x nightRamp x (1 + 0.08 x validLayers)`.
`nightRamp` is 0 for the first half of the day length (default 24,000 ticks, `GeneralConfig.dayLength`, range 1,000 to 400,000), rises over the first one-seventh of the night, holds 1, and falls over the last one-seventh. "Night" is a ramp value of 0.45 or more; "day" is 0.05 or less. A crystal's matching constellation tier multiplies the whole crystal bonus by (1 + 0.4 x tier).
- Transmission (`starlight/transmission/StarlightTransmissionChain#buildChain`, `CrystalPropertyCalculator#getStarlightTransmissionLoss`): a node keeps `1 - 0.08 x max(5 - purity - focus, 0)` of the incoming amount, the remaining amount splits equally among outgoing links, and branches below 0.001 are cut. A focus crystal links at most 16 blocks.
- Port: income as a product of independent readable factors: item quality, a world-state schedule, a placement bonus. Each factor is a separate dial, so a world can strip one (no night ramp, for example).
- Donor question: default: a passive source's income multiplies quality, schedule and structure. Dial: each factor and its steepness. Reason/cost: the schedule is the sky, so waiting for night is the cost; quality is paid in rare crystals; the structure is paid in blocks.

**RM13 (shown, `constellation/level/ConstellationHandler`, `MoonPhase`).** Eight moon phases (full, 3/4, 1/2, 1/4 waning, new, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 waxing). Constellations without a special show-up rule are assigned by a shuffled world-seed random to 5 active phases from the 8, filling free slots first. A constellation's strength on a given night is `clamp(1 - 0.5 x phaseDistanceToNearestActivePhase, 0, 1)`, so a constellation is full on its 5 phases, half one phase away and absent two phases away. The active list refreshes once per day. Focal-point angles are random in 0 to 90 degrees with a minimum 8-degree separation (200 attempts). Because the seed is the world's, each world's sky differs (inferred from seeded random; the seed source `ctx.getEffectSeed()` was not traced).
- Port: a calendar that decides which sources exist tonight, deterministic per world and learnable. It is a schedule, not a random roll, so players can plan around it.
- Donor question: default: sources are available on a fixed per-world cycle with a soft fall-off. Dial: cycle length, number of active phases, fall-off per step (0.5 here). Reason/cost: the cost is time and waiting; a world with no sky can use another clock (tides, seasons, spirit festivals).

**RM14 (shown, `tile/TileLightwell`, `data/.../recipe/lightwell/*.json`).** The lightwell turns a catalyst item into liquid starlight (or water, lava) once per tick: gain = `sqrt(max(1, totalCrystalTiers)) x productionMultiplier` mB, with the fraction rolled as a chance. Catalyst shatter chance per tick is `1 / (1 + 1000 x m / shatterMultiplier)` with m = total tiers, so a better crystal both yields more and breaks less often. Multipliers (production, shatter): rock crystal 0.14, 0.15; celestial crystal 0.22, 0.15; aquamarine 0.35, 0.15; resonating gem 0.5, 0.12; ice into water 1.0, 0.04; magma block into lava 0.8, 0.05. The tank holds 2,000 mB, outputs only on the bottom face, and the catalyst is broken at once if the block above is not air. The class never checks sky or time, unlike the source nodes (shown by absence).
- Port: a catalyst that generates a liquid with sub-linear yield in its quality and a breakage clock. Quality helps twice.
- Donor question: default: a catalyst makes a resource slowly and eventually breaks. Dial: yield exponent (0.5 here), breakage constant. Reason/cost: the catalyst is a rare crystal and the stream is slow; replacing catalysts is the upkeep.

**RM15 (shown, `recipe/attunement/PlayerAttunementRecipe`, `tile/TileAttunementAltar`).** Player attunement starts only when: the altar's structure stands and sees the sky, it is night, an active constellation is matched by blocks placed at that constellation's positions around the altar (`AttunementConstellationFinder`, active tonight per RM13), the nearest player within 1 block is alive, not sneaking, not a fake player, not already attuned, has reached the second research tier or later, and has discovered that constellation, and the constellation has a root perk. It runs 800 ticks during which the player is made invulnerable and moved onto the altar. On finish the player is attuned to that constellation. One attunement per progress record (`!prog.isAttuned()`); perks can be removed by spending a perk nullifier item (RM17).
- Port: a once-per-life identity choice with gates in time (night), place (structure), knowledge (discovered) and posture (stand still). The choice is not reversible by waiting; its reset is a consumable.
- Donor question: default: a lasting affinity choice requires a scheduled window, a built place and prior discovery. Dial: whether it is once or once per era; the reset cost. Reason/cost: you lose a night and the build; the cost of changing your mind is a craft item, not time.

**RM16 (shown, `research/ResearchTier`, `datagen` research files).** Five research tiers (shimmer, illumination, resonance, luminance, radiance), 53 research node files with connection lists. Altar recipes are named by these tiers (luminance and radiance altars; radiance needs the armara focus constellation item and a base focus shatter chance of 1.0, so that item is consumed on every successful craft). Altar recipes also carry a 3x3 grid, a 5x5 relay pattern of pillars, a duration (100 ticks base), per-recipe lumen requirements, and optional focus constellation. 94 altar recipe files exist.
- Donor question: default: higher-tier crafts demand a matching focus item that may be consumed. Dial: shatter chance per recipe (0.0 for most, 1.0 for the radiance altar). Reason/cost: the focus is a rare crafted piece and the shatter chance turns it from a tool into a component.

**RM17 (shown, `research/PlayerPerkData`, `perk/PerkLevelManager`, `perk/tree/perk/root/RootPerk*`, `perk/DiminishingMultiplier`, `network/play/PktRequestRemovePerk`, `config/server/PerkConfig`).**
- Levels: perk level cap 30 by default (config range 1 to 100). The experience for level i costs `150 + 100 x floor(1.2^i)` more than level i-1; total through level 30 is 144,900 (my sum of the formula). Available perk points = level + free tokens - allocated perks.
- Gain per event is capped at 8% of the current level's span, so one burst cannot skip a level.
- Five root perks, one per attunement, each fed by a different behavior: Aevitas by placing blocks (hardness x 6, capped 200), Armara by damage taken (min(damage, 8) x 5; x0.25 with no entity source; x0.05 when the combat timer has run long), Discidia by damage dealt (min(damage, 60) x 4), Evorsio by mining (break speed x 5), Vicio by distance moved (x 0.01). Each is multiplied by a root multiplier, a diminishing multiplier and player perk-effect attributes.
- Diminishing multiplier (defaults regain 0.01 per 5 ticks, loss 0.01 per event, floor 0.01; roots may add a chunk tracker with a per-chunk cap): if at least 5 ticks have passed since the last event it regains 0.01 per 5 ticks, otherwise it loses 0.01. So events faster than one per 5 ticks drain the multiplier toward 0.01, and a slower pace restores it.
- Attribute limits clamp the final value of each perk attribute (`PerkAttributeLimiter`).
- Removing a perk requires `mayRemovePerk` and consuming a perk nullifier item, made at the altar.
- Port: progression by doing, per root, with soft anti-grind that is time-based and not a hard cap; levels are cheap-then-steep (1.2 growth); respec costs a crafted item.
- Donor question: default: each affinity levels from the behavior it embodies, with a diminishing multiplier on repeat spam. Dial: root events, caps, regain and loss rates, level cap. Reason/cost: you progress by living that way; farming one act decays unless you pace it.

**RM18 (shown, `lib/LumenAS`, `data/.../lumen_binding/*.json`, `recipe/lumen_crystallization/*.json`, `crystal/CrystalPropertyCalculator`).** 2.0 adds lumen, a typed substance with eight base types by meaning (virel life, solyn light, nullae emptiness, caldor order, hyle matter, dynamis energy, aion time, akasha space), a prismatic type, and one lumen per constellation (14 binding files). Crystallizer recipes consume a fixed lumen amount per operation (1,400 for aion) and turn a resonating-gem catalyst into a typed gem with shatter multiplier 3.0, or fill a typed lumen crystal at shatter multiplier 0. Binding a lumen to armor slots gives effects such as boots (aion): +5% movement speed, -4% cooldown reduction, with a lumen usage type that depends on movement; potion variants last 6,000 to 12,000 ticks. Effect strength scales with crystal attributes: (1 + 0.08 x (channeling + purity)) x (1 + 0.6 x lumenTier) for generic effect rate, and (1 + 0.11 x channeling) x (1 + 0.4 x lumenTier) for binding.
- Port: a bound consumable fuel whose rate is the product of generic and typed crystal quality, consumed by the type of action it supports (moving, fighting).
- Donor question: default: a worn binding consumes a typed fuel by the action it enables. Dial: consumption per action; typed multiplier (0.4 to 0.6 per tier). Reason/cost: the fuel is refilled at a crystallizer using lumen; the cost is the time and the lumen supply.

**RM19 (shown, `recipe/focal`, `data/.../recipe/focal_transmutation/*.json`, `infusion/*.json`, `liquid_interaction/*.json`).** Starlight does three more jobs. Focal transmutation changes world blocks inside a focal area over 800 ticks and requires focused starlight (sand to clay, diamond ore to emerald ore, sea lantern and end stone and cake targets listed), with output blocks chosen by weight. Focal combine merges dropped items over 400 ticks (the wand: 2 raw marble, 2 aquamarine, 1 ender pearl). The starlight infuser transforms an item next to a fluid source over 200 ticks, with a per-recipe fluid consumption chance (0.01 for a glass lens, 0.03 for a resonating gem, inferred as a per-tick roll). Liquid interaction: 6 mB liquid starlight with 6 mB lava, each consumed with chance 0.5, drops an aquamarine shale item. Lightwell recipes (RM14) and liquid-starlight recipes (grow crystal size, merge crystals, form clusters, fill and engrave lumen) share the same liquid.
- Donor question: default: a world-altering transformation requires a focused source and a long window. Dial: window length, weighted outputs. Reason/cost: it is slow and tied to a placed focus, so it changes a site, not the player.

**RM20 (shown, `artifact/ArtifactStability`, `ArtifactStats`; `data/.../artifact_conditions`, `artifact_effects`, `artifact_penalties`).** Artifacts are items whose effects are partial: they pulse and carry conditions drawn from 32 environmental conditions (in nether, in stronghold, near ores, hit by fire, near zombies, near candles, near an anvil and so on), 4 effects (regeneration plus saturation, resistance, speed, random animals) and 3 penalties (random mob spawns, random move, random teleport of 5 to 10 blocks). Stability ladder: inert, active, unstable (delay x0.8), volatile (delay x0.3, effect chance x1.5), critical (delay x0.05, effect chance x2.5, can break), stable (can be crafted into a result). Stats: stability gets worse once 4 pulses, 2 empty triggers or 2 partial triggers accumulate, and the artifact can break when empty plus partial triggers reach 20 (only at critical). Condition selectors carry conflict lists. Trigger code (`ActiveArtifactTrigger`) was not read, so what a "partial trigger" means exactly is inferred as "some but not all conditions met".
- Port: a discovery puzzle where an unstable item wants its hidden conditions met, escalates penalties as it fails, and can be rescued by finding the right conditions; success freezes it into a stable component.
- Donor question: default: a relic with hidden activation conditions that worsens when it misfires. Dial: ladder steps, thresholds (4, 2, 2, 20), whether break is recoverable. Reason/cost: the relic damages a site's quiet (mob spawns, teleports) when it fails; fix by research and travel, not currency.

## Part C. Forbidden Arcanus (26.1.2, 2.8.0-beta.2). Facts and formulas, paraphrased

Paths are under `neoforge/src/main/java/com/stal111/forbidden_arcanus/common/` and `neoforge/src/generated/resources/data/forbidden_arcanus/forbidden_arcanus/`.

**RM21 (shown, `essence/EssenceType`, `block/entity/forge/HephaestusForgeTier`, `ritual/Ritual`).** Four essences: aureal, ectoplasm, blood, experience. The forge holds all four, capped by tier (aureal / ectoplasm / blood / experience): tier 1 = 1,000 / 10 / 10,000 / 900; tier 2 = 3,000 / 50 / 15,000 / 1,350; tier 3 = 5,000 / 100 / 30,000 / 2,500; tier 4 = 10,000 / 500 / 50,000 / 5,000; tier 5 = 20,000 / 1,000 / 100,000 / 7,500. A ritual has 1 to 8 typed inputs (item and count) on pedestals, one main ingredient, requirements (essence set, tier predicate, required enhancers), a magic-circle visual type, and a duration (default 500 ticks). 22 rituals ship in data.
- Port: a station with four typed tanks capped by tier and a recipe form that is a bill of items on pedestals plus a bill of essences.
- Donor question: default: a ritual recipe is item bill plus typed-tank bill plus tier. Dial: tank caps by tier, ritual duration. Reason/cost: items come from the world and the tanks fill over time (RM23), so a ritual is an outcome of preparation.

**RM22 (shown, `HephaestusForgeServerData#startRitual`, `#onDataChanged`, `#failRitual`, `#resetRitual`, `ritual/Ritual#checkIngredients`, `ActiveRitualData#isStillValid`).** Ingredient matching is exact: after each input is matched, any leftover non-empty pedestal stack invalidates the ritual. Essences are removed at the start. During the 500-tick run any change to pedestals or main slot makes the ritual fail: the main item is thrown out on the ground, all pedestal items are cleared, and the essences already spent are not returned. Success clears the pedestals and replaces the main slot with the result.
- Port: the commitment point is the start; interference afterward costs everything except the main item. A cheap-to-state, expensive-to-violate rule.
- Donor question: default: a started rite cannot be touched and punishes interference by loss of inputs and spent fuel. Dial: which losses (pedestals, fuel, main item) and the duration. Reason/cost: the in-world agent is the unfinished working itself collapsing; the cost is everything staged on the pedestals.

**RM23 (shown, `block/entity/forge/tick/CollectBloodTickEffect`, `event/PlayerEvents`, `item/ModItems` essence values, `item/AurealTankItem`, `item/EctoplasmExtractorItem`).** Essence enters by routes:
- Blood: once per second the forge checks living entities within 5 blocks, and for every entity whose health fell since last check it adds `loss x 20` blood. This applies to any living entity in range, with no owner check seen (the player included; inferred from the filter).
- Aureal: items: aureal bottle 35, splash aureal bottle 30; thrown bottles act on ambient or creature mobs that are not already marked aureal (`AurealHelper`, `ThrownAurealBottle`). The player has an aureal pool (RM27).
- Ectoplasm: soul 1, corrupt soul 1, enchanted soul 10, ectoplasm bottle 21; an extractor item takes 21 per use from valid blocks into an item store.
- Experience: condensed experience 91; vanilla experience bottles carry an essence value by data component.
- Storage items: aureal tank item holds 100 by default up to 3,000; utrem jar 10,000 (default fallback).
- Port: four income channels, each a different behavior (hurting things nearby, tending creatures, collecting souls, harvesting experience).
- Donor question: default: each essence has its own physical source and its own carrier item. Dial: amount per carrier. Reason/cost: blood is paid in damage near the altar, so a busy battlefield becomes a fuel line.

**RM24 (shown, `ritual/*.json`).** Cost samples (essences aureal / ectoplasm / blood / experience; tier): eternal stella 82 / 1 / 1,000 / 0 (tier 3); quantum catcher 200 / 5 / 1,200 / 155 (any tier); quantum injector 5,000 / 50 / 3,000 / 1,060 (tier 3); boss catcher 500 / 10 / 7,500 / 1,200 (tier 4); Draco Arcanus helmet 820 / 6 / 475 / 420 through chestplate 1,300 / 9 / 625 / 760 (tier 2); Tyr helmet 4,820 / 40 / 5,930 / 2,450 through chestplate 6,300 / 60 / 8,080 / 3,420 (tier 4). Armor chain: netherite piece plus dragon scales and stygian steel makes Draco; Draco plus aquatic and golden dragon scales makes Tyr.
- Port: a transmute-in-place ritual (`transmute_input` result type) whose output takes the main item's slot and form, making an equipment ladder where each rung consumes the previous.

**RM25 (shown, `ritual/upgrade_tier_2..5.json`).** The forge upgrades itself by ritual. Main ingredients: edelwood planks, then chiseled polished darkstone twice, then a stellarite block; circle type `upgrade_tier` (`upgrade_final_tier` for tier 5); `match_tier_exact`. Costs: tier 2: 500 aureal, 10 ectoplasm, 6,000 blood; tier 3: 1,000 / 50 / 9,000 (forge tier 2); tier 4: 2,000 / 100 / 12,000 (forge tier 3); tier 5: 5,000 / 500 / 20,000 (forge tier 4). Each upgrade's ectoplasm cost equals the current tier's ectoplasm cap (10, 50, 100, 500), so a full ectoplasm tank is required to upgrade (read from the two tables; the equality is a pattern across all four, inferred as intended). Input items rise from arcane crystals plus spawner scrap to stellarite pieces and runes to sculk catalysts, dark nether stars and dragon scales.
- Port: a station that has to be filled to the brim to level up, so upgrade timing is a preparation goal.
- Donor question: default: the station tier is raised by the station, paid in a full rare tank. Dial: upgrade list and costs. Reason/cost: the rare channel (ectoplasm) is the throttle.

**RM26 (shown, `item/enhancer/EnhancerDefinition`, `essence/EssenceSet#applyModifiers`, `Ritual#canStart`, `HephaestusForgeServerData#startRitual`, `EssenceAccess#removeEssences`, `EssenceStorage#setAmount`).** The forge has 4 enhancer slots (`EnhancerResourceHandler(4, ...)`). Seven enhancer definitions: artisan relic (experience requirement x0.75, also in the clibano), crimson stone (ectoplasm x0.5, soul duration x1.3), crescent moon (aureal x0.9 for ticks 0 to 12,999 and 23,000 to 23,999, x0.99 for 13,000 to 22,999), soul crimson stone (all four x0), and three marker enhancers with no effect (divine pact, elementarium, maledictus pact) that rituals can require (sea prism, smelter prism, terrastomp prism and whirlwind prism need elementarium; the soul binding crystal needs divine pact).
- Key spot-check (second read): `canStart` applies the modifier list to the recipe's essence set and compares with `hasMoreThan` to decide whether the ritual can start. `startRitual` then removes `ritual.requirements().essences()`, the unmodified set, through `removeEssences`, which calls `addEssence(type, -amount)`, and `EssenceStorage#setAmount` clamps the result to 0. So an enhancer lowers what you must have stored to begin, but the bill drawn is the full recipe amount, limited to what is in the tank. Soul crimson stone means you can start with empty tanks and the ritual drains whatever is there (to zero of each type up to the recipe amount). I did not see a second modifier pass at removal. This is shown by reading, not by play, and the note is that the intent may be a discount; treat it as a possible bug. (Reading is of a beta.)
- Port: the safe form is "a discount that lowers the bill". If a world wants "a bar to start", model the two numbers separately.
- Donor question: default: enhancers change the bill. Dial: bill versus gate as two separate multipliers. Reason/cost: a gate-only discount rewards hoarding an empty-looking tank; a bill discount rewards good prep.

**RM27 (shown, `event/PlayerEvents`, `event/DataComponentEvents`, `item/MagicWandItem`, `essence/EssenceCost`, `essence/EssenceCosts`, `essence/storage/EntityEssenceAccess`).** Players carry an essence pool through an entity capability. Every 100 ticks the player gains aureal equal to their `aureal_regeneration` attribute. Armor sets that give it (per piece, added values): Draco Arcanus helmet 1, chestplate 2, leggings 1, boots 1 (total 5 per 5 s); Tyr helmet 2, chestplate 4, leggings 2, boots 2 (total 10 per 5 s). Items declare an `ESSENCE_COST` component: a wand shot costs 5 aureal and needs a charge duration; a quantum catcher costs aureal equal to the target's health. The player's pool cap was not found in the files read.
- Port: a regenerating personal pool tied to a worn set, spent by named item costs, so equipment sets the sustainable rate.
- Donor question: default: a worn set sets regen, an item declares its cost. Dial: regen per tier, cost per item. Reason/cost: the armor itself is a ritual product (RM24), so regen is bought with the same essences it later spends.

**RM28 (shown by absence, grep of code, data and `changelog.txt`; `lang/en_us_old.json`).** No corruption meter, corruption item or corruption risk exists at 2.8.0-beta.2. The word appears only in item and block names (corrupt soul, corrupted pixie, corrupted arcane crystal, corrupted crystal obelisk, a corrupti dust tag). The changelog file in the repo is an unrelated Forge mod-list changelog. Earlier versions are not available here, so any claim that corruption existed in older versions is not supported by this clone. The risks that do exist are RM22 (interference), RM23 (blood collected from anything hurt nearby), and the preview status of wands.
- Donor question: default: risk is carried by the process (interference loses inputs), not by a persistent taint meter. Dial: add a taint meter per world. Reason/cost: a world that wants corruption should name its agent (a demon, a curse) and a cost (a loss that persists), not borrow the label.

**RM29 (shown, `recipe/apply_modifier/*.json`, `item_modifier/*.json`, `recipe/essence_transform/*.json`).** Six item modifiers (aquatic, demolishing, eternal, fiery, magnetized, soulbound) are applied through a smithing template plus an addition item that is itself a ritual product (eternal stella makes eternal; soul binding crystal makes soulbound). Eternal removes damage and max_damage components and rejects incompatible items and enchantments. Four essence-transform recipes spend essence directly on an input (growing edelwood: 76 ectoplasm). Wand materials are data (21 files with accuracy, damage, projectile speed) and compose parts such as pommel and transition.
- Donor question: default: a lasting item property is applied with a ritual-made reagent. Dial: incompatibility lists. Reason/cost: the reagent is rare and the property can be exclusive with others.

## Proposals for YAIRPG (palette, not spec)

- **RMY1 Bound account with an injury overdraft** (RM1, RM3). Owner: resources/body in the simulation contracts. A person-level account of one currency, with all bound objects reading it; at zero, spending converts to injury at a world-set rate. Pair with a visible ticket log of the last N spends.
- **RMY2 Counted-block dial panel** (RM4). Owner: modular chains and home buildings. A station's footprint sets its tier, and counted add-on blocks adjust named dials with a printed formula.
- **RMY3 Starved progress regresses** (RM5). Owner: crafting and making. A long craft accrues and decays when its supply stops, at a recipe-set rate.
- **RMY4 Standing rite with graded refusal** (RM6). Owner: sites and exploration. An activation fee, a pulse fee, and a low-funds branch that costs the owner comfort and skips one pulse.
- **RMY5 Product-of-factors income** (RM12). Owner: world cards. Income = quality x schedule x structure; every factor is a per-world adjective and can be omitted.
- **RMY6 Deterministic per-world calendar of sources** (RM13). Owner: world cards and skills. Which source exists tonight follows a learnable per-world cycle with soft fall-off.
- **RMY7 Behavior-rooted progression with a time-based diminishing multiplier** (RM17). Owner: skills and talents. Five roots, each fed by a distinct act, with a regain/loss multiplier instead of a daily cap.
- **RMY8 Relic stability ladder with hidden conditions** (RM20). Owner: discovery policy. A relic misfires, worsens along a named ladder, and stabilizes into a component when its hidden conditions are found.
- **RMY9 Full-tank upgrade** (RM25). Owner: home domain. A station levels up only when its rarest tank is full, making upgrade timing a goal.
- **RMY10 Separate the gate and the bill** (RM26). Owner: making systems. Two numbers per recipe: what must be present to start, what is spent.

## Top findings

**SR1 (Hex): one typed stack machine, the cast is the program.** SHOWN. Patterns (hex-grid strokes, 186 registered actions) run left to right against a stack of typed values (number, vector, bool, entity, pattern, list, null, garbage). Action names encode stack shape (reflection pushes 1, purification 1 to 1, distillation 2 to 1, exaltation 3+ to 1, decomposition 1 to 2, gambit is anything else). Patterns can be pushed as values and evaluated later, so a spell can be saved, edited as a list and cast by an item. Whole spells are plain data: a list of patterns on a focus, scroll or casting item.

**SR2 (Hex): failure is graded, typed and mostly mild.** SHOWN. Each mishap has a distinct consequence: invalid pattern pushes garbage; too few values pads garbage; wrong type replaces the bad value; out-of-range yanks the held items toward the target; divide by zero costs half remaining health; wrong block gives a harmless explosion; too many operations ("lost in thought", cap 100,000) removes air; saving another player's reference removes sight for about a minute. No mishap destroys long-term progress.

**SR3 (Hex): overcast to health is a gated late power, not the default.** SHOWN. Media is spent per action, whole items at a time, from dust (1 unit), shard (5), crystal (10); phials give exact change; casting items run on an internal battery. When inventory media runs short, the shortfall is paid from health at 1 hp per dust-equivalent (10,000 raw units per hp, minimum half a heart) only after the player has failed to cast a great spell from a scroll once (advancement). A second advancement is "opened eyes" (any overcast). The third, "enlightenment", needs a cast that uses at least 0.8 hp while leaving the caster alive. The 14 great spells (lightning, flight, lava, greater teleport, greater sentinel, rain on/off, brainsweep, battery crafting, 5 potion buffs) require enlightenment and have world-seeded random stroke patterns, found only on ancient scrolls or from a wandering trader. Without enlightenment the spell refuses, with a hint. This is a gate sequence: fail, learn, pay with the body, then see.

**SR4 (Hex): power comes from knowledge, supply and circuits, not levels.** SHOWN. There are no levels. Growth comes from recipes for media storage (focus, spellbook of 64 pages, battery phial), automated circuits (impetus, slates, directrix) and the economy of finding amethyst. Impetus and directrix blocks are made by consuming a villager with the matching profession (mindflay), which changes the activation condition (right click, redstone, look, bound player). Range is a 32 block "ambit"; a bound player reference acts as an exception.

**SR5 (Psi): a spell is a 9x9 data-flow grid with five compile-time limits.** SHOWN. Piece kinds: selectors, constants, operators, tricks, connectors. Evaluation: tricks top to bottom, left to right; each piece evaluates its inputs first and only once per cast. The compiler sums five stats: complexity (all non-debug pieces), potency, cost, projection (trick count) and bandwidth (grid span). The CAD (device) caps three of them: core (complexity 14 to 36, projection 1 to 7), assembly (potency 100 to 350, efficiency 70 to 95 percent) and socket (bandwidth 5 to 9, 4 to 12 bullet slots). Cost is not capped; it is paid from a personal pool.

**SR6 (Psi): cost is bodily and has a cliff.** SHOWN. Player pool 5000, regeneration 25 per tick. Real cost is spell cost divided by assembly efficiency, times the bullet modifier. Spending past zero draws the CAD battery first (100, 200, 400 overflow), then health at 1 per 125 excess (1 per 50 while loop casting), and the player is locked out until the pool fully refills. Trick costs scale with the chosen number: explosion potency 70 and cost 210 per power point (minimum 0.5), smite 100/400, a fixed top tier trick 250/3000.

**SR7 (Psi): vocabulary unlocks by use.** SHOWN. 29 piece groups; the default unlock is "the group's main piece executes once", with an advancement variant. Tutorials teach the first handful. This is use-to-learn, no currency.

**SR8 (Occultism): ritual = pentacle tier + bowl contents + activation book + optional sacrifice.** SHOWN. 184 ritual recipes, 13 ritual types (craft 57, summon 31, summon with job 26, wild summon 16, upgrade 15, familiar 15, spirit-name craft 9, others). Four pentacle footprints scale with spirit rank: 9x9 foliot, 13x13 djinni, 17x17 afrit, 21x21 marid (chalk colour diversity and candle and skull counts also rise). The activation book is bound to the same rank (book of binding, bound foliot, djinni, afrit, marid). Eldritch and wild contact pentacles are separate. The ritual runs for 40 to 160 seconds, consumes ingredient bowls one at a time, can be interrupted, and shows grey then purple particles to signal requirements met. Afrit and marid essence (needed for the top chalks) comes only from summoning that spirit unbound and killing it.

**SR9 (Occultism): job spirits are workers with ranks, time limits and custody objects.** SHOWN for tier numbers, STATED for behaviour. A "summon with job" ritual produces a spirit with a fixed job (crusher, smelter, crystallizer, farmer, lumberjack, cleaner, transporter, machine operator, traders, gambler, weather, day or night). Processing jobs scale by rank (table SR9a). The summoner holds a Book of Calling: it sets work area (16, 32 or 64 blocks), deposit target and facing, and can be re-crafted and re-bound by sneak use if lost. Guide text says workers pause for minutes after clearing their area; code shows a shorter backoff (SR21): a lumberjack that finds no tree waits 30 s, a farmer 20 s, and reassigning the work area clears the wait. Trade spirits live 3,600 s, gambler 1,800 s, clear-weather 15 s; weather and time spirits do one change and vanish. Dimensional miner spirits (crafted into an item, not roaming) have a fixed durability measured in operations (SR9b).

**SR10 (Occultism): familiars are owner support at three levels, stored in jewellery.** STATED. A familiar ritual needs a possession pentacle and a sacrifice of a species group; 15 familiar recipes. Levels: default, upgraded (via the blacksmith familiar), iesnium (via an anvil). A Familiar Tablet configures which effects run. A Familiar Ring stores one (Glove stores 6) and applies passive buffs while worn; releasing it makes the releaser the new master (tradable). Some abilities need the familiar in the world (beaver chops grown trees, bat hunts bats); the ring keeps only the passive part.

**SR11 (Malum): spirits are typed material; infusion consumes them to transform one carrier.** SHOWN. Eight spirit types (aerial, aqueous, arcane, earthen, eldritch, infernal, sacred, wicked). 746 entity entries in spirit data map each mob to 1 to 3 spirit types; scythe kills drop them. The spirit altar takes 1 input item, up to 9 spirit slots, extras from pedestals within 4 blocks. 144 infusion recipes name item count plus spirit counts, e.g. an inlay needs 8 aerial and 4 arcane plus 2 weave and 2 refined soulstone. Crafting takes about 300 ticks divided by speed; runewood obelisks (up to 4, +25% each) and pylons (+50% each) speed it. Other machines: crucible focusing (14 recipes, tool durability cost, 300 to 2,700 ticks), soul binding (36, up to 32 of every spirit), runeworking (24), void favor (13), tool repair with spirits (35).

**SR12 (Malum): totem rites are ordered spirit stacks.** SHOWN in the 1.8.2 jar (SpiritRiteTypeBuilder#minorTotemRite, #majorTotemRite, #specialTotemRite; SpiritRiteType#matches). 26 rites, each an ordered pattern of 3 to 5 spirits on a totem base, matched bottom pole first; minor rites are arcane plus two of the element, major rites eldritch, arcane plus two, the two special rites five arcane. A soulwood (corrupt) base must match the rite's corrupt flag and gives the paired variant. Pulse, range and spark details are SR20. The last spirit names the element. Effects: radial buffs to creatures (+40% mining speed at the level II the rite applies, jump height, a stone ward damage cut; see SR20 for the ward's odd formula) or a moving "locus" that works blocks (smelts, grows, breaks, forms cobblestone, reverses gravity). The base is a persistent installation, not a consumable.

**SR13 (Goety): equipment-gated spell slots, soul energy pool, servant caps.** SHOWN (code and data; book text only for lich form and research). Every focus item is bound to exactly one spell object at registration (ModItems focus entries; 119 focuses); a wand or staff holds one focus at a time and casts that spell. Staves are element specialisations: when the staff's element matches the spell (Spell#rightStaff) about 40 spell bodies give a bonus, e.g. vexing summons 3 vexes, or 3 to 5 with the matching staff. Cost, cooldown and summon mechanics in code are SR18 and SR19. Casting spends Soul Energy from a totem or similar store filled by kills; enchanted focuses double the cost. Costs are config defaults (examples: magic bolt 8, vex 18, fang 8, sword 16, meteor shower 64, vanguard 24, blackguard 48, killing 444). Servant spells have a per-spell cooldown ("summon down", 100 to 400 ticks) and a per-spell cap (vex 8, zombie and skeleton 32, wraith 6, reaper 8, phantom and vanguard and blackguard 16, hoglin 4, ice golem 2). Summoned servants have limited lifespan; crowns make some permanent; robes let them draw on soul energy to heal; ritual-summoned versions have no lifespan. 337 altar rituals (203 craft, 54 enchant, 36 summon or convert, 19 locate) are tiered by 15 "craft types" (magic 85, necroturgy 48, forge 43 ...) that name the altar's required structure, and 47 recipes need a named research unlock.

**SR14 (Roots Classic): ordinary plants become a consumable spell powder.** SHOWN. 22 flower or plant components, each a mortar recipe (flower plus string, feather and a berry-type item for the dandelion), each with a mana cost from 6 to 36. Modifiers are plain dusts: glowstone adds potency, redstone adds efficiency, gunpowder adds size. The mortar yields a powder item carrying component id plus the three counts. A staff loads it (65 uses base, 15 extra per efficiency); per cast mana cost is cost divided by (efficiency + 1). Max mana starts at 40. Rituals (12 types, bowls plus incenses) make tools, armor and standing stones.

**SR15 (Totemic): group performance is a three-state machine with per-instrument caps.** SHOWN. A totem base idles in effect mode. Playing a first selector instrument opens a 60 second selection window; two instruments in order select one of 13 ceremonies (e.g. flute then drum = fertility). Startup then needs total music of 4,500 to 15,060 units, but each instrument has its own per-instrument cap (flute 3,000, drum 3,300, wind chime 1,500, rattle 3,300, eagle bone whistle 3,600, jingle dress 1,500), so at least two instrument types must contribute; per-play amounts are small (120 to 360). A startup time limit (20 to 32 s, difficulty adjusted) fails the attempt. On success an effect runs for a fixed time (ceremony specific). After a ceremony or failure the totem returns to idle. What remains: carved totem poles give continuous potion-like boons, and idle music drains by 10 to 60 per second (3 wind chimes hold about two thirds full), so effects need upkeep. Ceremony bodies and carving numbers are SR23.

**SR16 (ElementalCraft): elements are metered flows into placed shrines.** SHOWN. Four elements; shrines draw from a 10,000 element buffer, each with a rate, period and range (growth shrine 50 per 20 ticks range 4; lava shrine 5000 per 1200 ticks; vacuum 5 per tick range 10). 22 upgrades modify capacity, consumption, speed, range and strength by multipliers, with per-upgrade caps (silk touch 1, fortune 3, optimization 2). Runes (13) trade element preservation against speed or luck. Spells (20) cost element, have cooldowns and weights. Tools take one infusion each (40 infusion definitions: enchantments, cost reductions, attribute bonuses).

**SR17 (Biomancy): a living tool has its own fuel store.** SHOWN. Living swords, claws and armor hold Nutrients; each action (sweep, dig) spends some; with none, the action refuses. Refill by nutrient paste, a self-feeding enchantment (draws from the hotbar every 1.5 s when damaged) or parasitic metabolism (draws from the player's food bar when healthy). Machines (bio-forge, decomposer, digester, bio-lab) spend nutrients per recipe (decomposer 441 recipes, digester 124, forge 91, brewing 15). Forge items cost 1 to 250 nutrients. Serums are single-use body changes: ageing, rejuvenation, enlargement, shrinking, cleansing, frenzy, absorption, insomnia reset.

### Pass 2 additions (code read, 2026-10-14)

**SR18 (Goety): soul cost is a stack of multipliers, and summoning again too soon is taxed.** SHOWN (ISpell#SoulCalculation, ISpell#SoulCostUp, DarkWand#SoulUse, SEHelper#decreaseSouls). The spend for one cast is the config cost, then: times (summon-down level + 2) if a summon spell is cast while the caster still has the summon-down effect; minus 15% for any magic robe; minus the element robe's discount; divided by 1.5 in the element's favoured place or times 1.5 in its disfavoured place (frost in snowy biomes, wind high in open sky versus deep underground, storm under open sky in thunder, geomancy deep versus high, nether in the Nether, necromancy under a bright moon or near necromancer structures, void in the End, others by biome tags); times an attribute discount; then doubled if the focus is enchanted. Equipment soul discount is applied twice, once when the wand computes the price and again when souls are removed (INFERRED to be unintended). Souls come from the player's bound soul store if active, else from a Totem of Souls. Cooldown is per spell across every focus carrying it, so a duplicate focus does not bypass it. Casting near villagers who can see you lowers their opinion of you. Each summon cast raises the summon-down level by one (cap 4) and resets its timer (x1.5 with an enchanted focus); servants summoned while summoned-down arrive at half health with weakness and a "sapped" penalty and damaged gear, and do not get the potency buff. A summon above the per-spell cap is refused; sneak-casting instead recalls (teleports) your existing servants of that kind.

**SR19 (Goety): expired servants bleed, crowns and robes change upkeep.** SHOWN (MobUtil#getSummonLifespan, IOwned lifespan tick and #lifeSpanDamage, IServant#healServant, ServantUtil#healServant). Spell-summoned servants live 30 to 119 s (random) times a duration factor (1, plus duration enchant levels and 1 more for an enchanted focus). When the timer runs out they do not vanish: the timer resets to one second and they take 1 damage each time, so they bleed out and can be kept alive by healing. Wearing the crown that matches the servant's element switches the lifespan off; taking the crown off resumes it. Robes let damaged servants heal a fixed amount at a fixed rate, each heal paid in owner soul energy, with a 5 s no-heal window after being hurt.

**SR20 (Malum): rites pulse every 5 s and locus rites fire a routable spark.** SHOWN in 1.8.2 (TotemBaseBlockEntity#serverTick, #updateRite; SpiritRiteEffect#getCooldown; SpiritRiteEntityEffect#triggerRiteEffect; SpiritRitePotionEffect#applyEffect; BlockRiteEffectActivatorEntity; RiteAnchorBlockEntity#travel). Activating a base assembles poles one per second, matches a rite, then fires every 100 ticks for as long as the totem stands; nothing is consumed and a second use turns it off. Radial rites reach 8 blocks and touch at most 2 random targets per pulse: aura rites give level II for 150 s, refreshed only when under 10 s remain; corrupt "empowerment" rites raise matching effects already on a target to level III for 300 s. Locus rites send a spark from the base in the facing of the first pole; it affects up to 8 blocks by default (16 or 32 when upgraded) and has four tiered attributes (speed, potency, impact, distance) with 4 upgrade slots. Rite anchors on its path change it by spirit: aerial, aqueous, earthen, infernal each raise one attribute; sacred resets the block count (up to 4 times); wicked resets it but lowers a tier; eldritch duplicates the spark (up to 4 generations); anchors can also turn it. An unweaver deletes it. Stone ward, as written, multiplies incoming damage by 0.1 per level (cap 0.5), with the factor doubled when wearing no armour, so level II leaves 20% damage with armour and 40% without; the 1.9.0 branch is the same. This reads like an inverted "reduce by" (INFERRED), and contradicts pass 1's "20% reduction".

**SR21 (Occultism): spirit expiry, custody record and job loops.** SHOWN (SpiritEntity#aiStep, #die, #dropEquipment; BookOfCallingItem#inventoryTick; SummonRitual sets max age from the ritual's spirit_max_age; CrusherJob#onInit, #update; NearestTreeSensor and NearestCropSensor; LumberjackJob#onChangeWorkArea; PausableGoal). Age counts in seconds; past max age the spirit dies and is removed. The held work item is inventory slot 0 and the death drop routine spills the whole inventory plus the filter item. A tamed spirit's death is written to a shared death register; once a minute the Book of Calling checks it, marks itself spirit-dead and drops the binding, but the book survives. Processing jobs work from the held stack: the crusher picks up only items with a recipe whose tier window includes its rank, crushes a batch sized by rank, multiplies output by rank (unless the recipe opts out), drops results tagged so it will not re-grab them for a delay, or inserts directly into an inventory under a dimensional extractor, and drops anything it cannot process. Idle backoff: lumberjack 30 s and farmer 20 s after finding nothing, scans every 5 s, cleared by changing the work area; deposit goals back off 2 s, the storage fallback retries x 10 s, measured in real time rather than game ticks.

**SR22 (Hex): cost curves make potency the expensive axis.** SHOWN (OpPotionEffect#execute, OpExplode, OpAddMotion, OpBlink, OpFlight, great/OpTeleport, great/OpLightning, OpMakePackagedSpell entries in HexActions, brainsweep recipe data). Potion spells cost base x seconds x potency squared, and the great-spell buffs (regeneration, absorption, haste, strength) use potency cubed: ten seconds of regeneration III costs 270 dust, ten seconds of weakness II costs 4. Impulse costs the square of the push length in dust; blink 2.5 dust per block; explosions 3 dust per strength point (cap 10) plus 1 with fire; flight 2 dust per second or per block of radius. Flat great spells: greater teleport 100 dust, lightning 15, create lava 10, rain 10, clear rain 5. Making casting items: cypher 10, trinket 50, artifact 100 dust. Brainsweep (villager to circuit block) is 100 dust per recipe except the allay quench at 10. Paid from the body at the default rate, a teleport would need 100 hp, so overcast only patches small shortfalls.

**SR23 (Totemic): ceremony bodies and carving numbers.** SHOWN (ModContent ceremony and carving registrations; each ceremony class; PotionTotemEffect#getAmplifier; TotemEffectApiImpl#getDefaultRange). Most ceremonies are one-shot: war dance (strength II and speed II, 180 s, players within 8), depths (water breathing II, 180 s), rain and drought (6,000 ticks of weather), buffalo dance and eagle dance (convert one nearby cow or parrot, keeping its health ratio), flute infusion (upgrade flutes on the ground or in nearby inventories), cleansing (cure weakened convertible mobs), sun dance (regeneration IV 15 s and absorption V 300 s; during startup it costs every dancer within 8 one hp per second, never below 1). Timed ones: fertility 20 s (one sapling per second turns cedar; animals and villagers breed from dropped food), zaphkiel waltz 45 s (frequent growth ticks within 6), animal growth 45 s (age up young animals, hatch turtle eggs), danse macabre 45 s (rotten flesh nearby rises as buffed zombies), baykok summon (spawns the boss after 4 s). Carvings each give one effect (bat slow falling, wolf strength, horse speed, and so on); every 4 s nearby players get it with 1 s lingering; level rises by one per two repeats of the carving plus one when stored music is at least three quarters full; range is 5 blocks plus 1 per quarter of stored music plus 1 at full pole height (5 to 10).

**SR24 (ElementalCraft): sources exhaust and refill only when empty, pipes cap the flow.** SHOWN (SourceElementStorage#extractElement, #checkExhausted; SourceBlockEntity#serverTick; source trait data; ElementPipeTransferer; SimpleElementTransferPathfinder; AbstractShrineBlockEntity). A natural source holds 500,000 to 1,000,000 element and starts full. Once drained to zero it gives nothing until it has refilled completely, at 50 to 200 per tick, and it only refills while exhausted. Artificial sources hold a quarter, never refill and break when empty. Rarer traits (extraction speed, preservation, day or night bias) only roll beyond 100, 500 or 1,000 blocks from spawn. Pipes move 5, 25 or 100 per tick by tier and a path moves the smallest budget along it; extractors pull 5 or 25 per tick. Shrines spend element only on a period where the action actually happened; an idle shrine costs nothing. Upgrades multiply consumption, period, range, capacity and strength.

**SR25 (Biomancy): machines share one loop where hunger undoes progress.** SHOWN (MachineBlockEntity#serverTick, #hasEnoughFuel; FuelHandlerImpl; Nutrients). Decomposer, digester, bio-forge and bio-lab each hold up to 1,000 nutrients and refuel from their fuel slot every 8 ticks (nutrient paste 3, bar 27). A job starts only if the output fits and fuel covers the whole cost; while it runs, progress rises one per tick when fuel covers the cost and falls two per tick when it does not; the full cost is paid at completion and a redstone pulse fires. Decomposer outputs are random within ranges; in this decompile the result appears to be rolled separately for the fit check and the craft (INFERRED; possibly a decompilation artefact).

## Donor question answers

**N03 Malum. Could an infusion change one particular carrier and its supported function?** Yes. SHOWN: the altar is a one-input device (slot 0) that rewrites that one carrier using typed spirit currency and optional pedestal extras; the output is a new item with a different function (calx, hex ash, inlay, tainted rock, totem base). Spirits are consumed, not retained. Totem combinations are not infusions: they are ordered stacks of spirit blocks matched against 26 rites (SR12) that apply buffs or a roaming effect while the totem stands. A runeworking table (24) and tool repair (35) cover per-tool changes without the altar. Reusable idea: an input carrier plus a quantity vector of typed reagents, with speed modifiers from nearby buildings.

**N04 Occultism. Distinguish an actor's support, task eligibility and custody from a generic automation bonus.** Support: familiars (SR10) give owner buffs and some world actions; workers (SR9) give a specific job output. Eligibility: a job spirit only picks up items it has a recipe for, and the tier gates which recipes (crusher tier ranges per recipe; marid can crush the echo shard, djinni crystallizers deconstruct amethyst and quartz blocks; recipes carry min and max tier). Custody: the Book of Calling is the proof of assignment (work area, deposit target, filters); the spirit's inventory is also reachable by transporter spirits; the owner tag decides who gets the output; time limited spirits expire (1,800 or 3,600 s); on expiry the spirit dies, its whole inventory including the held work item is dropped (SpiritEntity#dropEquipment SHOWN; that death runs the drop path is INFERRED from vanilla), the job is removed and the Book of Calling is marked spirit-dead within a minute (SR21). Interruption: ritual interruption is explicit in code; a spirit killed in the field loses the job; resurrect rituals exist for familiars (2 recipes) and spirits (resurrect pentacle). These are per-actor objects, not a global production multiplier.

**N06 Roots Classic. Can ordinary fieldcraft prepare a consumable magical method?** Yes (SR14). Flowers and kitchen items in a mortar make a powder that stores which spell it is plus three small counts; a staff then spends mana to cast it and burns one of its fixed uses. Classic is the old design (one mana pool of 40, not Roots 2 to 4). Failure and recovery: the powder is only a recipe; casting fails only for lack of mana; staves lose uses and are crafted again.

**N07 Psi. Compare target selection and composition with Hex; could known building blocks offer menu-based combinations?** Hex composes by sequence (stack, strokes, implicit order, targets arrive as values from earlier patterns: entities, vectors, raycasts). Psi composes by wiring (grid with named input sides, selectors supply targets, operators transform, tricks act). Hex errors show at execution; Psi errors show at compile (stats) and as runtime fizzles. Psi costs are visible before casting; Hex costs are discovered per action as they happen. Psi's compiled spell is a stored item (bullet); Hex's is a list value. A menu (radial) selection from loaded bullets exists in Psi: the compile step is where combinatorial menu building lives, and the caps (SR5) are the budget. Reusable idea: known verbs plus slots with a visible budget; composition builds once, then fires from a short menu.

**N08 Goety. How do equipment, servant role and finite support produce a distinct expedition method?** Equipment decides which spells are available (focuses in the wand or staff, a focus bag when many), robes and crowns change servant behaviour (healing from soul energy, no time limit), the soul totem is the finite pool (filled by kills, reserve for revival and for lich form), servants have per-type caps and cooldowns so you cannot flood. A servant keeps fighting hostile creatures and is told to follow, wander or stay with the wand. Servants stored in a tesseract pause their lifespan (STATED). Code adds two expedition rules: repeated summoning inside the summon-down window raises soul cost and weakens new servants (SR18), and expired servants bleed out slowly rather than vanish, so healing gear stretches an expedition (SR19). Result: expeditions are planned by pre-loading focuses, topping the soul pool and deciding which capped servant roles to field, not by raising stats.

**N09 Biomancy. Could a living tool have its own substrate, condition and useful transformation?** Yes (SR17). Substrate: Nutrients meter in the tool. Condition: actions refuse at zero. Transformation: forge crafts a living tool from organic parts; paste and food refill it; enchantments change how it is fed. The machines process organs and food into more usable parts (decompose, digest, brew). Serums are separate one-shot changes to a creature.

**N36 Totemic. Can supported group participation change an effect and what remains after performance stops?** Yes (SR15). Participation: several players or blocks can feed music (drums, chimes, flutes, rattles) into the same totem; the ceremony needs more than one instrument type because of per-instrument caps; one player alone with one instrument cannot reach the target. After the effect time ends the totem returns to idle; what remains is the pole's carved boons and a decaying music meter that scales those boons (SHOWN in PotionTotemEffect: effect level rises by one for every two repeats of the same carving, plus one more while stored music is at least three quarters of the 7,680 maximum). Redstone output follows startup progress.

## System patterns across targets

- **SRP1 Gate by failure or ritual, not by points.** Hex requires failing a great spell then nearly dying; Occultism requires killing an unbound spirit for the next chalk; Psi unlocks by executing; Ars spends XP at a table. Four different unlock currencies for one kind of verb.
- **SRP2 A visible budget beats an invisible one.** Psi shows five stats before casting; Totemic shows a music bar; Hex hides cost until the action runs.
- **SRP3 Pay the body only after the player is warned or has chosen.** Hex overcast is gated; Psi overdraw is lethal but announced.
- **SRP4 Persistent installation versus consumable.** Totem, pentacle, altar, shrine stay; powder, serum, bullet are spent. Both exist in every magic mod here.
- **SRP5 Actors have caps, lifespans and a custody token.** Goety caps and summon-down, Occultism calling book and age, Ars helper limits.
- **SRP6 Modifier-by-sideways-items.** Roots uses dusts, ElementalCraft uses runes, Malum uses obelisks and pylons: cheap extra ingredients tune a base effect.
- **SRP7 Penalise repetition instead of forbidding it.** Goety's summon-down raises cost and weakens new servants; Hex charges impulse again on a target already pushed this cast; Totemic caps each instrument. A soft tax keeps options open where a hard once-limit would not.
- **SRP8 Upkeep only when work happens.** ElementalCraft shrines and Malum rites cost nothing when idle; Biomancy machines pay on completion; Goety robes charge per heal. Idle cost is zero; ongoing cost tracks real output.

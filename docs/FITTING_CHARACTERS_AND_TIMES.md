## Three questions before picking anything

1. **Does the world allow it?** Its own rules first (rule 2); book worlds
   follow their book (rule 3). If not, is there a carrier (rule 5) or is
   this an outlier (rule 12)?
2. **Does the character have it?** Their body, power, knowledge, reach,
   personality and history. A shy healer doesn't open domains; a bored
   god doesn't send seven omens.
3. **Is this the moment?** Where the player is in the life and the run,
   what just happened, what is due to happen (natural course), and how
   much spectacle has been spent lately (effect budget).

If all three say yes, it fits. If one says "almost", the gap is a story:
the world bends, the character strains, or the moment is early and
foreshadowed.

## Fit by stage of a life or run

Illustrative; worlds and characters shift these.

| Stage | What usually fits | What usually waits |
|---|---|---|
| **Arrival / childhood** | Ordinary people, small trials (rites, exams), quiet adjectives, rumors of greater things | Gods acting, domains, branches |
| **Finding a footing** | Rivals, institutions, first technique, first adaptive enemy, first meta-character sighting (R1) | Run-ending threats except where clearly eligible |
| **Rising** | Real trials and tribulations, named rivals with their own growth, perfect-fight rewards, odd-record reactions | Cosmic gaze unless provoked |
| **Prominent** | Fame-driven attention, institutional politics, world-class foes, omens beginning (if a cause exists) | |
| **World-class** | Wars, lineages, natural-course disruption, heirs, god-like rivals climbing | |
| **God-like** | Other god-likes, cosmic treaties, looking at other screens, descending to live small | Ordinary-scale threats as real danger (they become story, not combat) |
| **Ending / afterward** | Legends, epitaphs, branches saved, heirs inheriting, atlas | |

## Fit by character role

| Role | Typical picks |
|---|---|
| **Nobody** | Ordinary trials, rumors, being beneath notice, small adjectives, witnessing big events |
| **Rival** | Adaptation to you, perfect fights against you, their own trials, climbing tiers, reach R3 if it fits |
| **Mentor** | Trials they set, projected selves, stale foreknowledge, a past you can learn |
| **Institution figure** | Grades, exams, courtroom domains, licenses, policy reactions to odd records |
| **Meta character** | Reach ladder, UI tricks within the effect budget, a relationship that grows |
| **God-like** | Cosmic scopes, omens, avatars, few appearances, personality-driven verdicts |
| **The player** | Any of the above over a life, which is the point |

## Pacing and density

- **Spread the big things.** One headline consideration per stretch of
  play (a new adaptive foe, a trial, a meta-character visit), not five.
- **Let systems echo.** An adjective learned in a trial reappears on an
  item; a rival's adaptation shows up again years later.
- **Foreshadow up the ladder.** Each tier's signature should be glimpsed
  before it arrives (rumors of domains before the first domain).
- **Leave quiet stretches.** Calm makes the next moment land (restraint
  X5).
- **Keep the ordinary useful.** Late-game characters still need food,
  friends and a place; small systems stay alive (vision: ordinary skills
  stay useful late).

## Checks that something "makes sense"

- **Cause:** can you say why this happens here, now, to this character?
- **Cost:** what does it cost the one doing it, at their scale?
- **Counter:** does an answer exist somewhere, for a real reason?
- **Knowledge:** who knows what, and could they be wrong (rule 6)?
- **Aftermath:** what remains afterward, and who remembers?
- **Tone:** does it fit the world's voice (grim, comic, gentle, cosmic)?
- **Scale:** do the consequences match the actor's capability?

## More to consider

- **Mixing palettes.** A trial that uses adaptation, staged with a
  meta-character watching, in a world off its natural course. Combinations
  are where the surprises are; keep each piece's cause clear.
- **Characters change tier mid-story.** A nobody who becomes a rival, a
  rival who ascends, a god who descends. Their picks change with them.
- **Same consideration, different worlds.** A perfect fight is a system
  reward in the Crucible, a clan dream-gift elsewhere, a scholar's
  analysis in the Frontier. Choose the source that fits the world.
- **Generated characters** use the same checks: the generator (E8B, E3)
  picks from the palette by world, role and stage.
- **Reuse before inventing.** If an existing consideration fits, use and
  adapt it before adding a new system.
- **Future worlds** start with the three questions and this table, then
  add their own rows.
- **Record what was chosen.** When a world picks a consideration, note it
  in that world's docs with why it fits, so later work stays consistent.
- **Unfit is a choice too.** Leaving a strong idea out of a world that
  doesn't suit it keeps worlds distinct (the vision asks for mechanically
  distinct worlds).

### Worked walkthroughs

**1. An adaptive beast in the Frontier.**
- World: the Frontier has no adaptation law. Fits as an outlier (rule 12)
  or as something carried in through a rift (rule 5).
- Character: a beast, not a sage. Coarse adjectives: *slow*-learning,
  *forgetful* (decays over days), *visible* wheel.
- Moment: after the player has a few weapons to switch between, so the
  counter (novelty) is actually available.
- Checks: cause (a rift stain on its hide), cost (it starves if it fights
  too long), counter (burst, switch, flee and return), aftermath (a hide
  that keeps one resistance as a crafting material).

**2. A courtroom domain in the JJK world.**
- World: canon has a trial domain; fits directly.
- Character: an institutional sorcerer, lawful, tired, fair.
- Moment: after the player has a real record worth judging, ideally with
  an odd record or two.
- Checks: cause (a complaint, an execution order), cost (domain burnout
  for the judge), counter (evidence, silence, a technicality), aftermath
  (confiscation, a title, a grudge or a friend in the court).

**3. A god-like gaze in Fog City.**
- World: distant terrors fit; restraint in presentation fits the tone.
- Character: something alien; omens that only make sense afterward.
- Moment: only after the player went digging where they were warned not
  to (cause), with several quiet sessions before.
- Checks: cost to the god is nothing; the cost to the world is a district
  that stays *wrong*; counter is to stop digging, or a sealed relic;
  aftermath is a scar on the title screen and a new rumor.

**4. A meta character's first reach to the tab.**
- World: one where the UI is part of the fiction (status windows, a
  narrator).
- Character: the Lodger, after months living in the inventory.
- Moment: when the player returns after a long absence (an idle-game
  natural moment).
- Checks: cause (the relationship milestone), cost (none, or it leaves the
  inventory messier), counter (ask it to stay inside), aftermath (it does
  it again only on anniversaries).

### Anti-patterns to watch for

| Anti-pattern | What it looks like | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Everything everywhere | Every world has domains, adaptation and gods | The "unfit is a choice" rule; distinct worlds |
| Spectacle inflation | Big effects every session, so none land | Effect budget, restraint rules |
| Unearned power | A god-like with no road, no cost, no cause | The makes-sense checks |
| Tier soup | Nobodies and gods mixed with no sense of scale | Fit by stage; relative consequences |
| Power creep without contrast | Every new thing stronger than the last | Rule 11: somewhere you're nobody; ordinary stays useful |
| Copy-paste characters | Same personality with a new skin | The actor shapes the act; personality-driven picks |
| Lore drift | A world forgets what it chose earlier | Record what was chosen, per world |
| Dead ends | A failed trial or broken branch that leaves nothing | Aftermath check: something always remains |

### A fit card (template)

When a world or character takes a consideration, record it in that
world's docs:

```
Consideration: (which doc and section)
World fit:     (its rule, a carrier, or an outlier)
Character:     (who, tier, personality, relevant adjectives)
Moment:        (stage of life or run, trigger, what came before)
Cause / cost / counter / aftermath:
Presentation:  (rarity tier, X2 ladder)
Status:        proposal | selected | implemented | verified | accepted
```

### Over long play

- **Worlds age.** A consideration that fit in one era may not in the next
  (timelines, powers age with history). Revisit fit cards per era.
- **Revisiting worlds.** Coming back after years should show change:
  rivals grown, gods quieter or louder, the adaptive beast's descendants.
- **Generational fit.** An heir faces a world shaped by the parent; the
  right considerations for them differ from the parent's.
- **Prestige layers.** A new run may unlock considerations the first run
  only glimpsed (the first milestone keeps one prestige layer; more come
  later).

### Player agency

- **The player chooses the pace.** Provoking, avoiding and preparing
  should shift when things arrive (cause and omen rules).
- **Options, not rails.** A fitting moment offers a choice, not a forced
  outcome, except where a world's harshness is the point.
- **Readable fairness.** The player can learn why something happened,
  even if only afterward.

### Emotional shape

- **Arcs per consideration.** A meta character: curiosity, trust, mischief,
  loss. A trial: dread, struggle, relief, change. A god: rumor, fear,
  encounter, aftermath.
- **Contrast of moods.** A heavy event followed by small warm life.
- **Personal stakes over spectacle.** The most memorable moments involve a
  person the player cares about.

### Surprise versus fairness

- Surprise comes from combinations and from characters acting on their
  own goals.
- Fairness comes from causes, counters and foreshadowing.
- Both together: the player didn't see it coming, but can see afterward
  why it came.

# World cards (proposal)

*Status: PROPOSAL, not accepted. Drafted 2026-10-08 by Claude Code at
Weibao's request ("O1, write the world cards"). The format comes from
[LIFE_SIM_NORTH_STAR.md](LIFE_SIM_NORTH_STAR.md#every-idea-somewhere-not-everywhere-proposal-2026-10-08).
Book-inspired cards use working names and absorb mechanics, not prose,
names or characters (see the north star on copyright). A direct adaptation
would live in a private pack.*

## The card format

| Field | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Premise | One paragraph: what the world is and who rules it |
| Signature laws | Optional: things only this world does, or does best |
| Absences | Optional: things impossible here by default, each with a costed way around |
| Dials | Shared mechanics with local settings |
| Display | How powers and stats are shown here |
| Growth | Which growth families are on, and the local tier names |
| Stance | How the world treats natives, travelers and deviants |
| Scarcity | What is rare and fought over |
| Deck | The world's own events, people and small touches |
| Doors | How you get in and out, and what crosses |
| Places | Smaller cards that override a few dials |
| Swap test | Why play here is different from anywhere else |

Dials used below: **danger** (how lethal ordinary life is), **death**
(unconsciousness, injury, final, or revival), **time ratio** (against the
multiverse clock), **fusion pressure**, **carry in/out**, **stat source**
(use-raised, assigned points, or both), **display** (prose, descriptors,
numbers).

Every absence below, including "no revival", is a default. A cheat, a
ritual, a rift or a world law can get around it, at a cost.

## 1. The Frontier (home world, vanilla YAIR)

**Premise.** The vanilla YAIR world, as a branch dimension: a village, a
forest, a frontier, and the quiet life of someone who woke up there. It is
the default origin and the first campaign. "Home world" only means the
first world the player lived in; it doesn't have to be the source or origin
world, and it can be as deep as any world.

**Signature laws.**
1. **Everything you do is a skill.** Doing is growing; the skill list is
   the biography.
2. **The calendar matters.** 30-day months, seasons and night already shape
   work and danger.

**Absences.** None fixed. Defeat is unconsciousness, as now, and no world
will notices you at first; what else the Frontier holds is open.

**Dials.** Danger low to medium. Death: unconsciousness by default; true
death only in a chosen harsh life. Time ratio 1. Fusion pressure none.
Carry: generous in, generous out (it's home). Stat source: use-raised only.
Display: vanilla numbers, as now.

**Growth.** Use, breakpoints, branches and insight. Tier names: Novice,
Apprentice, Advanced, and past Advanced into Master and Grandmaster (USER
VISION L706). No fixed cap; what lies past Grandmaster is open.

**Stance.** Neutral. Later, if a barrier to an expansionist world thins,
the Frontier starts to feel pressure.

**Scarcity.** Good iron, a real teacher, books, money, time.

**Deck.** Seasons and festivals, a bandit winter, a wandering peddler with
gossip from far away, the hidden-strength elder, a child who follows you
around, a barn fire, a first snow, a stranger who knows your name.

**Doors.** The awakening (origin, mysterious). Rifts that open late in a
life. Paradise-style recruitment. What crosses into the Frontier follows
the edge's carry dial.

**Places.**
- *The village:* safe, social, and where your home accumulates.
- *The deep forest:* danger up, rare herbs, an old woodcutter.
- *The old ruin:* the first place a concept can be sensed, faintly.

**Swap test.** The only world where ordinary life is the point and nothing
is trying to make you a hero. Leaving it should feel like a real change.

## 2. The Crucible (inspired by *A Novel Concept*)

**Premise.** A universe where seven primordial Concepts have egos and feed
on captured civilizations. Newcomers arrive after a Tutorial into one hostile,
energy-rich world inside it. Factions compete for an eighth throne whose
conditions nobody fully knows.

**Signature laws.**
1. **Arrival is survival.** The environment (energy, heat, gravity, a
   living ecology) is the first enemy. Food, shelter and resistances matter
   more than fighting.
2. **Understanding shapes reality.** Concepts are gained by epiphany,
   bloodline, resources or fragments, and climbed by stage (Affinity,
   Harmony, Symphony, Unity). Specialize, fuse, or invent a new one.
3. **Growth is tested.** System rewards and tribulations tie each step up
   to a consequential trial.

**Absences.** No default revival (it is an origin gift, not a world law).
No unconditional right to leave. A higher Concept does not grant lower ones.
No fixed limit on Concept seats.

**Dials.** Danger very high. Death final for most; revival only by gift,
token or rewind. Time ratio varies by trial. Fusion pressure high for
related Concepts (two held apart by will, three or more risky). Carry in:
knowledge, some items. Carry out: conditional, with allowed time away.
Stat source: **both** (points from levels plus tempering and use).
Display: system numbers for stats, stages in words for Concepts.

**Growth.** Concepts by tier (which) and stage (how deep). Titles and merit
trees for firsts and feats. Skills based on real-world knowledge. Body
tempering and bloodlines as separate tracks.

**Stance.** The world will is neutral to hostile; the Concepts themselves
are actors with goals, so a patron Concept can favor you and leash you.

**Scarcity.** Compatibility, knowledge, a safe workspace, water, a teacher,
a compatible Concept fragment, survival time. The eighth throne is the one
true singular seat.

**Deck.** A suspected poisoned flower, a rival champion's trace, a seed
needing a home, a mentor reading a damaged spear, a merchant who pays for a
story with a lesson, a recipe the locals have never tasted, a weather or
corruption signal rising, a tribulation you can delay but not cancel.

**Doors.** The Tutorial gate in. Small rifts to neighboring worlds with
conditional, timed absence. Shop items that promise travel (listed is not
the same as working).

**Places.**
- *The refuge:* a defended home, the only place with low danger.
- *The wilds:* full danger, best resources.
- *A trial space:* time ratio up, items suppressed, mind at risk, body
  death blocked.

**Swap test.** The only world where surviving the environment is the first
skill, and where understanding, not training, is the main way up.

## 3. The Gu Wilds (inspired by *Reverend Insanity*)

**Premise.** A harsh cultivation world where power lives in Gu, small
living creatures you raise, feed and refine. Clans hold the mountains;
the heavens favor the righteous and grind down deviants.

**Signature laws.**
1. **Power is a living thing you keep.** Gu must be fed; starve them and
   they die or rebel. Your strength is your stable.
2. **Recipes, not grinding.** Gu combine into killer moves through recipes
   you find, buy, steal or invent.
3. **Heaven's will is real.** Luck and fate are forces with favorites.

**Absences.** No skill grows from use alone (Gu grow by refining and
feeding). No revival. No free help from strangers.

**Dials.** Danger high. Death final. Time ratio 1. Fusion pressure: by
recipe only. Carry in: knowledge only (your Gu don't survive the trip
unless you research how). Carry out: Gu as goods if you can feed them.
Stat source: aptitude at birth plus Gu. Display: descriptors (aptitude
grades, essence colors).

**Growth.** Ranks of cultivation set by aptitude and essence. Gu ranks.
Killer moves as fusion. Immortal Gu are each one of a kind, but many kinds
exist.

**Stance.** Suppressing toward deviants and outsiders who break the order;
favoring toward its chosen. Both shift with deeds.

**Scarcity.** Gu food, rare Gu, aptitude, primeval essence, inheritance
caves.

**Deck.** A clan assessment of children's aptitude, a Gu that won't eat, an
inheritance cave rumor, a merchant caravan, a clan feud, a beast tide.

**Doors.** Hard to enter, harder to leave with anything living.

**Places.**
- *A clan village:* order, duty, and the assessment ritual.
- *The beast mountains:* wild Gu, wolf packs, danger.

**Swap test.** The only world where your power is alive and can die of
neglect, and where the heavens openly take sides.

## 4. The Fog City (inspired by *Lord of the Mysteries*)

**Premise.** A gaslit city of factories, churches and secret societies.
Power comes from potions; each potion is a role you must live to digest.
Knowing too much drives you mad.

**Signature laws.**
1. **Acting digests power.** You grow by living your potion's role, not by
   training.
2. **Knowledge is dangerous.** Certain names and truths cost sanity to
   hold.
3. **Pathways are fixed.** Each pathway climbs by sequence; neighboring
   pathways can swap only at certain points.

**Absences.** No mixing pathways freely. No safe divination. No open use of
power (the churches hunt the unregistered).

**Dials.** Danger medium in daylight, high at night and in the occult.
Death final, with rare strange exceptions. Time ratio 1. Fusion pressure
none. Carry in: knowledge; potions only if the ingredients exist. Carry
out: knowledge, with a risk that the madness comes too. Stat source:
sequence plus ordinary training. Display: sequence name in words, sanity as
shifting descriptors that tint the log.

**Growth.** Sequences from 9 to 0. Acting as the main growth family. Top
seats are scarce (one Uniqueness plus a few Sequence 1 characteristics per
pathway), which makes a real singular-seat race.

**Stance.** Neutral, with powerful hidden watchers. Outsiders who show
future knowledge get noticed.

**Scarcity.** Formulas, ingredients, a sane mind, a trustworthy
organization.

**Deck.** A newspaper with an odd ad, a club meeting in a back room, a
dream that repeats, a church inspector at the door, a smog week, a market
for beyonder items.

**Doors.** Easy to arrive, hard to arrive unnoticed.

**Places.**
- *The docks:* cheap rooms, smugglers, rumors.
- *The cathedral district:* safety, surveillance.
- *Above the fog:* where watchers live.

**Swap test.** The only world where pretending to be someone is how you
grow, and where reading the wrong thing can ruin you.

## 5. The Dreaming (inspired by *Shadow Slave*)

**Premise.** A world where a Spell pulls people into a nightmare realm and
judges them. Survivors gain an Aspect, a Flaw, and a place in a harsh new
order. Sleep is never safe.

**Signature laws.**
1. **Aspect and Flaw.** Every power arrives with a matching flaw.
2. **The Spell judges.** It sets trials, grants Memories and Echoes, and
   enforces rules nobody can argue with.
3. **Two realms.** The waking world and the dream realm, with travel
   between them through sleep and gates.

**Absences.** No safe sleep. No power without a flaw. No escaping the
Spell's trials.

**Dials.** Danger extreme in the dream realm, medium awake. Death final.
Time ratio differs between realms. Fusion pressure none. Carry: Memories
cross between realms; bodies don't always. Stat source: rank plus soul
cores. Display: rank names and rune-like descriptors.

**Growth.** Ranks of Awakened, Ascended and beyond, each a trial. Memories
(items) and Echoes (summons) as separate tracks. Flaws grow with power.

**Stance.** The Spell is impartial but merciless: a referee, not a patron.

**Scarcity.** Soul shards, a safe anchor in the dream realm, a citadel to
belong to.

**Deck.** A first nightmare, a citadel recruitment, a clan's offer, a
Memory that whispers, a gate opening in a city.

**Doors.** Sleep and gates. Leaving the world entirely is the hard part.

**Places.**
- *The waking city:* clans, academies, politics.
- *The dream realm:* ruins, horrors, citadels as anchors.

**Swap test.** The only world where every strength is also a weakness by
law, and where going to sleep is going to war.

## 6. The Paradise hub (inspired by *Reincarnation Paradise*)

A hub, not a world. Hubs use the same card plus a **charter** (from the
hubs-and-heavens proposals).

**Premise.** A residence between worlds that recruits the dead and dying as
contractors and sends them on missions into other worlds.

**Charter.**
- **Home:** a room or house, upgraded by rank.
- **Eligibility:** recruited at death or by contract.
- **Ranks and permissions:** missions, shops and worlds unlock by rank.
- **Shops and currency:** points from missions; goods, skills and
  blessings for sale; bought skills still need a foundation.
- **Duties:** mandatory missions on a schedule; penalties for refusal.
- **Return:** a fixed timer pulls you back from each mission world.
- **Enforcement:** contractors who break rules lose points, rank or life.
- **Leaving, switching, defecting:** unknown in the source, so ours to
  author. Proposal: possible, expensive, and remembered by both hubs.

**Rivals.** Several paradises compete, each with its own tactics: one
follows an arena champion, one hoards resources, one sends disciplined
squads.

**Swap test.** The only place that is a job, a home and a battlefield at
once, and where other travelers are your coworkers and competitors.

## 7. Bay City (original superpower world; mechanics informed by Worm and Super Powereds)

**Premise.** A coastal city a generation after powers first appeared.
Heroes are a licensed public service, villains run territory like
businesses, and a few ancient disasters arrive every few years that nobody
can stop alone.

**Signature laws.**
1. **Powers arrive in a crisis.** Your worst day shapes your power; a
   second crisis can expand it. Training improves control, not whether you
   have it.
2. **Everyone is rated.** A public agency rates every known power by what it
   does, and people plan around your rating.
3. **The truce.** When a city-ending threat comes, heroes and villains fight
   together under a code; breakers get hunted by both sides.

**Absences.** No cultivation or concepts. No revival. No safe anonymity once
unmasked.

**Dials.** Danger medium, spiking during disasters. Death final. Time ratio
1. Fusion pressure none (powers don't merge, but teams combo). Carry in:
your skills and knowledge; foreign powers are rated as unknown and feared.
Carry out: your power may reread as a talent, a curse or nothing. Stat
source: trained stats plus a power rating. Display: ratings in public
categories, descriptors for control.

**Growth.** Control, creative uses, gear, teammates, second awakenings.
Bought powers from a secret catalog, on installments or favors, with a small
chance the power goes wrong. Inventor powers make devices only you can
maintain.

**Stance.** Neutral world; strongly opinionated institutions.

**Scarcity.** Identity, trust, a sponsor, licenses, a power that fits your
needs.

**Deck.** A bank job gone wrong, a talk show invitation, an unmasking threat,
a gang's recruitment pitch, a rookie team's first patrol, a disaster
warning, a funeral where nobody gives a eulogy, a password check.

**Doors.** Disasters sometimes come from elsewhere; so do the catalog's
powers.

**Places.**
- *The academy:* training, classmates, a hidden program for people whose
  powers don't work right (the Super Powereds premise, as inspiration only).
- *The docks:* gang territory with its own rules.
- *Agency headquarters:* licenses, ratings, surveillance.

**Paths that shine.** Hero, villain, rogue for hire, inventor, information
broker, agency bureaucrat, journalist, ordinary citizen who survives.

**Swap test.** The only world where your power is public knowledge others
plan around, and where your enemies may save your life during the truce.

## 8. Salvage Reach (original tech world; mechanics informed by The Legendary Mechanic)

**Premise.** A frontier planet on the edge of a galactic civilization, full
of wrecks from an old war. Corporations, militias and drifters salvage,
build and sell. Above it, a ranked galaxy of civilizations decides what
the planet is worth.

**Signature laws.**
1. **Four gates for every machine:** know it, get the blueprint, build it,
   operate it. Each is separate.
2. **Power lives outside the body.** Your strength is your machines,
   workshops and supply lines, so it can be stolen, broken or mass-produced.
3. **Repetition stops paying.** Doing the same task gives less and less;
   variety and invention keep paying.

**Absences.** No body cultivation or concepts. No revival except clones and
backups. No free energy: every machine has a power budget.

**Dials.** Danger medium, high in the wrecks. Death final unless you paid
for a backup body. Time ratio 1. Fusion pressure: blueprint combination
only. Carry in: knowledge reread as blueprints; magic weakened or rechanneled
into devices. Carry out: machines, if their fuel and parts exist where you
go. Stat source: trained stats plus implants. Display: numbers and specs.

**Growth.** Blueprints from missions, reverse engineering, combining and
invention; failed prototypes count toward success. Classes gated by
knowledge, not money. Production: workshops, assembly lines, machine squads
with a control limit, then fleets and supply lines. AI partners with their
own minds.

**Stance.** Neutral; the galactic ranking ignores the planet until it
produces something worth taking.

**Scarcity.** Rare parts, power cores, classified knowledge, skilled hands,
safe workshops.

**Deck.** A wreck that just surfaced, an encrypted weapon that destroys
itself if forced, a buyer wanting a demonstration, an EMP that fries your
own gear, a corporate audit, a supply ship that doesn't arrive, a machine
that develops a personality.

**Doors.** Spaceports and jump gates, licensed and taxed; wormholes nobody
controls.

**Places.**
- *The scrapyards:* cheap parts, danger, rival salvagers.
- *The corporate enclave:* jobs, licenses, classified knowledge.
- *Orbit:* the galaxy's gaze.

**Paths that shine.** Engineer, salvager, merchant, mercenary, factory
owner, AI whisperer, smuggler.

**Swap test.** The only world where your power can be repossessed, and where
you get stronger by building, not by training.

## 9. The Red Basin (original harsh world; mechanics informed by Kenshi)

Draft from the Kenshi second pass (2026-10-08), condensed.

**Premise.** A dry basin around a dead industrial sea. Well-owning houses
control water and tolls, a fabrication league controls parts, and a
religious march distrusts mechanical bodies. You arrive with no title, and
strong people already exist who don't scale to you.

**Signature laws.**
1. **Your body is infrastructure.** Injury changes what work you can do;
   parts need fitting, upkeep and supply; skill and installed capability
   are separate.
2. **A settlement is an address.** A roof, a well and workers change who
   notices you; tax, provisions and ritual visits are separate deals.
3. **Power vacuums have inhabitants.** Removing a ruler changes shops,
   workers and roads, and news spreads unevenly.

**Absences.** No native magic, resurrection or limb regrowth. No stat
points. Imported abilities may still work by their own mechanism.

**Dials.** Road danger high; wounds, capture or true death possible with no
guaranteed rescue; time ratio 1.

**Growth.** Use, work, teaching and research. Local titles (Learner, Hand,
Specialist, Master) describe craft, not strength.

**Doors.** In through a derelict transit lock; out through a rebuilt
regulator, a caravan lock or a compatible ability.

**Places.** A cistern ward, a solder district, a pale causeway, a quiet
quarry.

**Swap test.** The only world where your body is a supply chain and being
nobody is the default.

## How you grow in each world (proposal, 2026-10-08)

Weibao asked for the systems and progression of each world. Each block
gives the ladder, the daily loop, breakthroughs, limits and ways past them,
what a long life there looks like, and what carries in and out. Book-based
points come from the audits where they exist; the rest are from memory of
the books and marked (memory). Everything is a proposal.

### The Frontier

- **Ladder.** Skills only: Novice, Apprentice, Advanced, then Master and
  Grandmaster (new above vanilla). No realms, no system.
- **Daily loop.** Vanilla play: work a profession, train, gather, craft,
  fight on the frontier. Routines form from repetition and run idle.
- **Breakthroughs.** Skill breakpoints change what a skill does (Woodcutting
  "Read the grain"). Branch choices around mid levels. Insight from an
  elder or a book unlocks research projects.
- **Limits and ways past.** No fixed cap. Grandmaster is the highest named
  tier so far; growth past it can come from another world, a rare
  tribulation, an heir who inherits the branch you skipped, a cheat, or
  something the Frontier itself holds.
- **A long life here.** Youth learns fast; middle age builds a trade, a
  home and a family; old age teaches and is the hidden-strength elder for
  someone else.
- **Carry.** In and out per the edge's carry dial: skills, items, people
  who come with you, and knowledge when something carries it.

### The Crucible (from the A Novel Concept audit)

- **Ladder.** Several separate tracks that add together:
  - **skills** with levels and rarity, which branch into high, ideal or
    specialist versions; each level adds fixed stats;
  - **Concepts** by tier (which one) and stage (Affinity, Harmony,
    Symphony, Unity);
  - **titles and merit trees** for firsts and feats;
  - **talents** (rare, often won in trials);
  - **body tempering, bloodlines and race tier** (race tiers raise stat
    thresholds);
  - **Domain and Masteries** as higher control layers;
  - **Potential** as a growth currency.
- **Daily loop.** Survive the environment, gather, build the refuge, train
  skills, study a Concept, prepare for the next tribulation.
- **Breakthroughs.** Crossing stat thresholds raises your tier and triggers
  a tribulation, personalized and harder each time. Surviving it gives
  picks, more if you survived several at once.
- **Limits and ways past.** Stats grow steadily while tribulations scale
  faster (a soft cap). Ways past: better picks, delaying a tribulation,
  bloodline purification, race change, a new Concept, inventing a skill.
- **A long life here.** Years of preparation between tribulations; a
  refuge that becomes a home; rivals from other civilizations who become
  allies or enemies; an eighth throne nobody understands.
- **Carry.** In: knowledge, some items. Out: conditional, with a permitted
  absence and a return deadline.

### The Gu Wilds (memory of Reverend Insanity)

- **Ladder.** Cultivation ranks set by aptitude and essence. Gu ranks
  separately. Immortal Gu at the top, each one of a kind.
- **Daily loop.** Feed and care for your Gu, gather their food, refine new
  Gu, earn clan contribution, avoid or exploit clan politics.
- **Breakthroughs.** Rank breakthroughs need essence, the right Gu, and
  often a risk. Killer moves come from recipes combining several Gu.
- **Limits and ways past.** Aptitude limits you from childhood. Ways past:
  rare Gu that change aptitude, forbidden methods, stealing an inheritance,
  a recipe nobody else has.
- **A long life here.** A child assessed and ranked, a youth climbing the
  clan, an adult running a household of Gu, and the slow choice between
  the clan's order and your own way.
- **Carry.** In: knowledge only. Out: Gu as goods, if you research how to
  keep them alive elsewhere.

### The Fog City (memory of Lord of the Mysteries, plus the audit's rechecks)

- **Ladder.** Pathways with sequences from 9 (lowest) to 0. Each sequence
  is a potion with a role name.
- **Daily loop.** Hold an ordinary job as cover, act your role to digest the
  potion, gather ingredients, attend a secret club, avoid church notice.
- **Breakthroughs.** Drinking the next potion once the last is digested.
  Drinking early risks losing control.
- **Limits and ways past.** Ingredients and formulas are scarce; top seats
  are limited per pathway. Ways past: swapping to a neighboring pathway at
  certain points, rituals, a patron, finding a seat's holder.
- **A long life here.** A clerk with a secret, an investigator, a member of
  an organization, and one day someone powerful enough to be watched.
- **Carry.** In: knowledge; potions only if the ingredients exist. Out:
  knowledge, and maybe the madness.

### The Dreaming (memory of Shadow Slave)

- **Ladder.** Ranks gained by trial: dormant, awakened, ascended and
  beyond. Aspect and Flaw at awakening. Soul cores and shards.
- **Daily loop.** In the waking world, train and belong to a clan or
  academy. In the dream realm, hunt, survive, collect Memories (items) and
  Echoes (summons).
- **Breakthroughs.** Each rank is a nightmare trial set by the Spell.
- **Limits and ways past.** The Flaw grows with power. Ways past: Memories
  that cover a flaw, allies who cover it, a rare trial that rewrites it.
- **A long life here.** Survive the first nightmare, find a citadel, rise
  in a clan or alone, and decide what to protect in two worlds at once.
- **Carry.** Memories cross between realms; bodies don't always.

### The Paradise hub

- **Ladder.** Contractor rank, gained by mission points and reviews.
  Points buy skills, items and blessings; bought skills need a foundation.
- **Daily loop.** Rest and shop at home, take missions into other worlds,
  return on a timer.
- **Breakthroughs.** Rank promotions open new worlds and shops.
- **Limits and ways past.** Mandatory missions and rank gates. Ways past:
  standout missions, a rival hub's offer, defecting.
- **A long life here.** A career: rookie contractor, veteran, team leader,
  maybe a hub figure, with coworkers as friends and rivals.
- **Carry.** The hub lets your mission gains come home, within its rules.

### Shared rules across all worlds

- **Your skills cross when something carries them** (the soul, a cheat, a
  record, an item, a teacher, the world's rules), and each world reads them
  through its own system: a Frontier skill becomes a Crucible skill with levels, a Gu
  recipe ingredient, or a role you can act.
- **Each world has its own ladder** by default. Conversion between ladders
  is a dial, not a ban (corrected 2026-10-10; see
  [verse equalization](CHEAT_COSMOLOGY.md#verse-equalization-converting-between-systems)).
- **Arriving gives a small set of entry picks** (an origin, a perk, an
  item), as in Jumpchain.
- **A ladder may have a cap and ways past it.** Caps are optional per
  world. If one exists, it has priced ways past it; another world is one
  common way, and others exist.

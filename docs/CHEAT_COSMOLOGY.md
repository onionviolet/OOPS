## Tools this uses (already in the north star)

- **Scopes, as relationships, not a ladder:** a world; a cluster joined by
  routes; a multiverse; what lies between multiverses (power-scaling
  findings).
- **Eight axes:** effect, targets, reach, contact, speed, defenses,
  authority, preparation.
- **Evidence:** feats (seen, with observer and conditions), statements
  (claims), anti-feats (failures that reveal limits), outliers (one-offs,
  not repeatable by default).
- **Compatibility modes in a new world:** Works, Reread, Rechannel,
  Weakened, Blocked.
- **Meeting outcomes:** ignore, override, cancel, counter, translate,
  trade, merge, devour, infect, cascade.
- **Tier words** (Knack, Gift, Golden finger, Fate-class, Origin-class) are
  vocabulary for scope, not a rank.

## The key to portability: what backs a cheat

Whether a cheat works outside its world mostly depends on what holds it
up. This is the first question for every placement.

| Backing | What holds it up | Outside its home world | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soul | Bound to the soul | Travels with the soul; parts that need the old body weaken in a new one | Regression, reserve-body reincarnation, the A.I. Chip (fused with its host's soul) |
| Body | Bound to the body, bloodline or constitution | Travels with the body; lost when you change bodies | Aspects that become part of you; bloodlines |
| Item | An artifact, Gu, chip or bead | Travels if carried; can be stolen, broken or blocked at a border | Spring Autumn Cicada, the bead |
| System | A system of some level (S1 to S7) | Works where that system reaches; outside it, frozen, reread or cut off | Solo Leveling's system, Paradise abilities |
| World law | The home world's own rules | Stays home; elsewhere it's Blocked | Mother of Learning's loop, Circle of Inevitability's reset |
| Patron | A god, sponsor or star | Works where the patron reaches; whether it can be taken back depends on the terms (see patron gifts below) | Omniscient Reader's sponsors, wish givers |
| Knowledge | What you know | Always travels; its relevance decays in a different world or timeline | Legendary Mechanic's game memory, the reader's foreknowledge |
| Concept or meta | Above the worlds | Works almost everywhere; rare and attracts attention from above | The Fourth Wall |

A cheat can have more than one backing. Each part travels by its own
rule: the Fated Villain's knowledge travels, while its system follows the
system's reach.

## The placement card

Every cheat gets the same card. It describes the cheat; it doesn't score
it.

1. **Identity:** book, holder, family (time, copy, multiply, space,
   knowledge, fate, body, system, summon, analysis), tier word.
2. **Mechanism:** trigger, input, output, fuel, rate.
3. **Axes:** a sentence for each of the eight axes that apply.
4. **Backing:** from the table above.
5. **Scope reached by feats:** world, cluster, multiverse or between,
   counting only what was shown, not what was claimed.
6. **Feats, statements, anti-feats, outliers,** each with a source line.
7. **Home world:** what it does under its native rules and what supports
   it there.
8. **Outside:** the default compatibility mode for each kind of
   destination, and why (backing, fuel, anchor, body).
9. **Meetings:** what it overrides or is countered by; how observers see it.
10. **Growth and ceiling,** and the way past the ceiling.
11. **Outside checks:** who notices and pushes back.
12. **Absorption:** which forms it can take in our game (origin, cheat card,
    world law, path, event, enemy, place, item, rumor) and an original
    variant for the public build.

## Simple cheats

The web-novel staples. Each is one rule, and the story lives in what it
multiplies and who notices. Backing is usually a system, so outside
behavior follows the system's reach.

| Simple cheat | Rule | What makes it busted | Its lever (what limits it) | In an idle game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multiplier return (10x, 100x, 10,000x) | What you give returns multiplied | Generosity becomes an investment | Input: real things given to someone who accepts; gifting to yourself fails; who notices the source | The bottleneck moves to finding worthy recipients |
| Sign-in or check-in | A reward for signing in each day, bigger at significant places | Free daily power | Rate; access to the places | Natural daily rhythm; drives exploration |
| Auto-cultivation | Grows while you sleep or are away | Never stops growing | Offline rules; what it can't do (relationships) | It is the idle mechanic, made into fiction |
| Effort always pays | Every action gives guaranteed progress, no plateaus | No bottlenecks | Time; varied practice still matters | Removes walls, so pacing comes from content |
| Perfect comprehension | Understand any technique on seeing it | Learns everything | Access to see it; capacity; the body to use it | Turns exploration and observation into progress |
| Copy or plunder | Take skills from targets or kills | Builds a huge kit | Slots, targets, daily caps | Rivals and enemies become shops |
| Gacha or lottery | Points buy random rewards | Rare jackpots | Luck; points | Fits idle loops; risk of feeling empty if overused |
| Life simulator | Simulate a path before living it; keep one reward | Previews choices safely | Cost per simulation; simulation errors | Fits event cards: preview a branch |
| Daily intelligence | One piece of intel a day | Always a step ahead | Vague, one per day | Fits the Notebook and rumor system |
| Choice system | Rewards for picking at key moments | Turns decisions into loot | Only at moments it chooses | Fits event cards |
| Disciple return | Your disciples' growth returns to you | Teaching is power | Disciples need care and loyalty | Fits followers |
| Territory or building system | Building grants rewards | Base growth snowballs | Materials, defense | Fits home and hub building |
| Pocket farm with fast time | A field where time runs faster | Endless harvests | Size, seeds, upkeep | Fits crafting and gathering |
| Game UI (The Gamer) | Stats, levels, a calm mind, healing by sleep | Everything is measurable and trainable | Rate; what the UI can't see | Already the base game's feel |
| Inventory space | Storage outside the world | Logistics vanish | Capacity; living things | Fits trade and smuggling |
| Points from emotion | Earn points when people are shocked, angry or humiliated | Rewards provoking people | Reputation cost; people avoid you | A social path with fallout |
| Trash talent that evolves | An F-rank talent that is secretly top tier | Underdog arc | Hidden condition to evolve | Fits hidden constitutions |
| Item duplication | Copy items a number of times | Infinite resources | Rate; item grade limits | Economy pressure; markets notice |
| Max talent at birth | Everything at the top grade | Fast at everything | Jealousy, attention, no struggle | A "god start" difficulty mode |

**Rules for simple cheats:**
- **Multiply one input, then move the bottleneck.** A simple cheat should
  change what's scarce, not erase scarcity. 100x return makes recipients
  scarce; sign-in makes reachable places scarce.
- **Pure "number goes up" stays available** (everything possible,
  reasonably): a god-start or 10,000x mode exists, with world pushback
  (attention, suppression, envy) as the reasonable part.
- **The absolute-word default applies:** one sentence plus an exclusion list.
  "Everything you give returns tenfold, except what you give to yourself."
  Exceptionless versions stay available as rare finds.
- **Who backs it decides travel:** a 100x system that is S2 in one world
  may not reach the next world.

## Placing new cheats as book worlds arrive

Not a ranking: an intake that places each cheat on the card above.

1. **Extract** each cheat from the book with its feats, statements,
   anti-feats and outliers, cited by chapter or note line.
2. **Classify** its family and backing.
3. **Place** its scope by feats only.
4. **Write home and outside behavior,** marking inference.
5. **Check meetings** against cheats already placed: which overrides,
   counters or translates which, decided case by case.
6. **Find its siblings:** most new cheats are a variant of a placed one (one
   trope, many implementations). Record it as a variant with what differs.
7. **Decide absorption forms** and an original variant name.
8. **Re-place when better sources arrive;** a placement can move when a new
   feat or anti-feat appears.

Later, the cards can become data (a cheat registry the game reads), so a
world's suppression or an observer's skill acts on a specific axis or
backing, not on a score.

## Absorbing tropes properly

Builds on [using tropes](LIFE_SIM_NORTH_STAR.md#using-tropes),
[tropes done differently](LIFE_SIM_NORTH_STAR.md#tropes-and-ways-to-do-them-differently-claude-2026-10-08)
and [one trope, many implementations](LIFE_SIM_NORTH_STAR.md#one-trope-many-implementations-proposal-2026-10-08).
The method:

1. **Name the promise:** what fantasy the trope gives readers. (100x
   return: kindness rewarded and numbers exploding.)
2. **Collect implementations:** at least three sources if possible, with
   their feats and limits.
3. **Find the engine:** the verbs and numbers that deliver the promise.
4. **Find what breaks it:** the costs books skip and readers' complaints
   (stakes collapse, repetition, replaceable side characters).
5. **Choose forms and pacing:** rumor, event, place, item, then cheat, path
   or world law.
6. **Set dials and fit:** backing, compatibility modes, which worlds it
   suits.
7. **Keep subversions as variants,** not replacements.
8. **Add one small touch** that makes it feel lived.
9. **Acceptance test:** the promise is felt in the first session, it is
   still interesting at hour 50, and it doesn't erase other paths.
10. **Attribution:** inspiration in the public build, direct adaptation
    only in private packs.

**Worked example: 10,000x return.**
- Promise: giving pays back enormously.
- Engine: a gift action with a multiplier and a recipient check.
- What breaks it: self-gifting loops; nothing is scarce; recipients
  become props.
- Forms: a rumor of a merchant whose gifts come back; an item that returns
  one gift tenfold; then the full system as a rare cheat.
- Dials: multiplier, eligible recipients (must accept, must be a real
  relationship), cooldown, what can't be multiplied (lifespan, cheats).
- Subversions: the return comes from the recipient's own future; it
  multiplies grudges too; the system is a debt collector.
- Small touch: recipients start bringing you gifts, hoping it works both
  ways.
- Acceptance: early gifts feel magical; at hour 50 the game is about whom
  you choose to help, not the number.

## Verse equalization: converting between systems

Weibao: "We will need to convert somehow, maybe a concept of verse
equalization?" In power-scaling communities, verse equalization means
putting characters from different settings on equal footing for a
matchup. Here it becomes a family of in-world mechanisms. Who imposes one
(a world will, a barrier, a hub, a tournament, a cheat, the player's
difficulty choice) decides which applies.

| Mode | How it converts | Feels like | Who might impose it |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | Ladders don't compare; compatibility modes apply (Works, Reread, Rechannel, Weakened, Blocked) | Foreignness, surprise | The default for most worlds |
| Reread | The destination's system relabels you in its own terms | "Your cultivation shows as level 40" | Any world with a system |
| Treaty rates | Two specific systems agree on an exchange table | Like currency exchange; can be wrong or outdated | Hubs, empires, trade guilds |
| Hub standard | One institution's scale rates everyone it touches | A universal rank as an in-world claim, not engine truth | Paradise-style hubs, star streams, ranking boards |
| Level sync down | You're capped to the local ceiling | The MMO level sync; fair fights with old skill | Tournament worlds, suppressing barriers, trials |
| Level sync up | You're raised to the local floor | A weak visitor survives a strong world | Benevolent hubs, tutorials, protective patrons |
| Normalize | Everyone gets the same stats; only skill, knowledge and choices matter | A pure contest | Gauntlets, arenas, dream worlds |
| Proportional | You keep your relative standing: top 1% at home is top 1% here | Your rank carries, your numbers don't | World wills that respect achievement |
| By feats | Converted from what you've shown, not claimed | Fair but needs evidence | Scholars, judges, bounty boards |
| Absolute | A true universal scale exists in this cosmology | Clean comparisons everywhere | A cosmology card can declare one |

**How it fits:**
- **Several modes can stack in one place:** a hub standard for pay and
  rank, level sync for its arena, none in the wild.
- **Equalization can be partial:** stats sync, but cheats keep working; or
  cheats are sealed (a gauntlet), but skill stays.
- **Cheats can resist or exploit it:** a cheat that ignores sync, a
  proportional world that rewards being top somewhere small, a treaty
  rate that's wrong in your favor.
- **The rate itself is a market:** brokers, scholars and smugglers profit
  from knowing which conversion is wrong.
- **Engine shape (proposal):** each world system exposes a reading of a
  character; converters map one reading to another; when no converter
  exists, compatibility modes apply. A universal rank is one optional
  converter that a cosmology or institution can supply.

## Patron gifts: what can and can't be taken back

Weibao: "the patron can give stuff that can't be taken back, but there
are also stuff that can." The same spectrum applies to systems, hubs and
anyone who grants power.

| Terms | Can it be taken back? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Gift | No. Once given, it's yours | A blessing absorbed into your body; a skill taught; an awakened bloodline |
| Digested | Starts as a loan, becomes yours once mastered | Digesting a potion by acting its role (Lord of the Mysteries) |
| Matured | Becomes yours after a set time or a deed | A probation period; a trial passed |
| Lease | Lasts for a term or while terms hold | Hub contract powers; a sponsor's deal |
| Loan | Revocable at will | Channelled divine spells; sponsor powers (Omniscient Reader) |
| Lien | Yours, but the patron keeps a cut or a claim | Tithes on what it earns; a favour owed; a power the patron can borrow back |
| Leash | Yours, but removing it harms you | A gift woven into your soul, so revoking it wounds |
| Burdened loan | Comes with a burden that grows | Bestowed power as a loan with a burden (Circle of Inevitability, `research/novels/full-book/circle-of-inevitability/WORLD_BRIEF.md:33`) |
| Bound item | Bound to you; can't be traded, refunded or exported | Paradise shop purchases (`research/novels/hubs-and-heavens-2026-10-08/books/paradise.md:31`) |

**What else matters:**
- **Who else can take it:** a third party may sever, steal or seal a gift
  even when the patron can't (fate severing, a seal, a thief).
- **When the patron dies or falls:** the gift stays, goes wild, fades, or
  passes to the patron's heir or killer.
- **Leaving the patron's reach:** some gifts keep working, some go dormant
  until you return.
- **Reading the contract:** terms may be hidden or misleading, which links
  to the observation contest (reading a contract is an observation).
- **On the placement card,** every granted cheat records its terms and
  who can revoke, sever or steal it.

**Decisions (Weibao, 2026-10-10):**
- **Both winner rules stay.** "The higher tier wins" (north star) and
  "case by case, by mechanism" (this file) are both available. A world,
  place or scenario picks which applies. Neither is the engine default.
- **Time collisions depend on how each time cheat works.** No blanket
  "two time cheats cancel". Cancel stays as one option a world can
  declare.

Codex's O2 (world-chosen tie-break) and O3 (record reset order and what
each side keeps) fit these decisions. The ideas below are Claude's
proposals.

### Time cheats by how they work

| Kind | What actually moves | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Mind sent back | Only the holder's will or memory; the world replays | Spring Autumn Cicada, regression on death |
| World recreated | A region is rebuilt from a snapshot; marked souls keep memory | Mother of Learning's loop |
| Checkpoint | The holder returns to a saved point; the world may or may not | Déjà vu checkpoints, Returner's Clockwork, The Perfect Run |
| Rate change | Time runs faster or slower in a space | The bead's practice room, pocket farms |
| Local reset | One thing resets on a schedule; gains stay | Circle of Inevitability's daily body reset |
| Branch | A copy splits off; both may continue | Save files, timeline splits |

**How kinds meet (options, picked per pair):**
- **Mind sent back vs world recreated:** the rewinder lands in a world
  that is already looping, or the loop's snapshot already contains the
  rewinder's earlier self, who now remembers nothing. Either is a story.
- **Nested resets:** the larger domain wins the order; the smaller loop
  restarts inside each larger reset. A loop inside a loop is a dungeon.
- **Two regressors in one world:** each sees the other's changes as
  surprises. Spotting the deviations is how you find a fellow regressor.
  A rival regressor races you on knowledge.
- **Same moment triggers:** earlier anchor first, or the timeline splits
  (cascade), or the world declares "one clock only" and both cancel.
- **Rate change vs reset:** does time spent in the bead count for the
  loop? Each world answers; the answer is a discovery.
- **Time leaves marks:** every reset leaves a scar observers can read
  (the god in You Have Died Again notices variance). Scars are clues,
  rumors and a way for hunters to find you.

### Both winner rules in play

- **A world dial:** hierarchy worlds (cultivation heavens, Paradise hubs)
  use tier wins; mechanism worlds use case by case; mixed worlds let tier
  set the default and a shown counter beat it.
- **Tier as weight, not verdict:** in mixed worlds the higher tier gets a
  bonus in the contest, and preparation or a counter can overturn it.
- **The rule is a discovery:** the player learns which rule a world uses
  by watching a meeting or losing one, and it goes in the Notebook.
- **The rule can change:** a world will wakes up, a hub imposes its
  standard, a war breaks a hierarchy.
- **Law domains:** some cheats or places switch the rule locally (inside
  this array, mechanism decides; inside this palace, rank decides).

### Targets, labels and custody (from F2, F3 and F5)

- **Failures teach targets:** a cheat's valid targets start partly
  unknown. A failed attempt reveals one ("it eats living skills, not
  shadows"). Anti-feats become play, not just notes.
- **Every strong cheat has a known weakness rumor:** the Fourth Wall
  leaks to a foreign gaze; Gluttony choked on one power. Rivals buy and
  sell these rumors.
- **Custody isn't ownership:** a thief can hold a soulbound item without
  the bond. It returns, refuses or works at half strength, per item.
  Lending is a separate, friendly verb.

### What these worlds add to the cosmology

- **CO1 Rewind-capable worlds.** Re:Zero adds a time kind our table lacks:
  a **patron-held checkpoint** whose placement belongs to someone else.
  It sits between "checkpoint" and "mind sent back". If the patron link is
  cut, the checkpoints reportedly go erratic (wiki, partly confirmed). That gives a clean rule for
  cross-world travel: carry Return by Death to a world the patron can't
  see, and it degrades instead of vanishing.
- **CO2 Owned small worlds.** Toaru's Academy City is a sealed, designed
  experiment with a planner inside it. That suggests a **cosmology
  type**: a world whose scope is set by an in-world owner (a planner, an
  unaware god as in Haruhi, a hub). Owned worlds can be entered,
  measured and escaped, and the owner notices deviations. This is the
  "small world that is the playground of a being" idea; Toaru is the
  strongest sourced fit.
- **CO3 Two systems in one world.** Toaru's esper and magic sides
  conflict (espers are hurt by casting). That is our compatibility modes
  inside one world, not only at borders. A player with two systems
  could pay that cost anywhere two systems overlap.
- **CO4 A world with a built-in hub standard.** Slime's Existence Points
  and the Voice of the World make an in-world absolute scale. Under
  [verse equalization](#verse-equalization-converting-between-systems)
  that is an Absolute mode that a world, not the engine, declares.
- **CO5 Contract cheats with a grantor in reach.** Geass and Code are
  the clearest leash-type gifts we have: power grows, control decays, and
  the grantor's terms surface later. The **Failed One** state is a good
  fit for a player who nearly succeeds.

### Meetings worth testing

Picked per pair, as the meeting decisions above require.

- **Imagine Breaker vs Return by Death:** does touch cancel a patron's
  checkpoint on the holder? The answer depends on whether the rewind is
  "an effect on him" or "an effect on the world". A world declares it.
- **Geass command vs Return by Death (I):** "die" makes him rewind; the
  Geass user's world resets too and they don't remember. Absolute
  obedience loses to a power that resets the order.
- **Gluttony vs naming:** Re:Zero eats names; Slime grants power through
  names; Shadow Slave forces True Names; LOTM uses honorific names.
  **Names** become a cross-world axis: a name-eater meeting a named
  monster either eats its evolution or chokes on its lineage.
- **Vector reflection vs holder-specific Authorities:** reflection works
  against what it can calculate. Authorities that rewrite rules, not
  vectors, should pass through (the shown anti-feat pattern).
- **Great Sage vs the A.I. Chip:** siblings. Two analysis cheats in one
  party can share data or compete for it.

- **N1 Names as an axis.** Add "name" as a ninth axis or a target type:

- **N2 The taboo as a world law.** Re:Zero's "can't tell anyone" fits

### Names as an axis (A1, proposal)

Five placed worlds treat names as power, so the placement card gets a
ninth axis, **name**: which of a target's names a cheat reads, uses,
gives, eats or forces. It builds on "which name" (LS4 in
[means and appraisal](MEANS_AND_APPRAISAL.md)), "defines or names" (I10
in [deep concepts](DEEP_CONCEPTS.md)), the `████` true-name display in
[presentation](PRESENTATION_AND_FOURTH_WALL.md) and soul/true-name
custody in [rivals and fate](RIVALS_IDENTITIES_AND_FATE.md).

**Kinds of name** (a character can have several):

| Kind | What it is | Placed examples |
|---|---|---|
| Registered | What records and institutions call you | Toaru's Bank database (memory), Re:Zero's registered true-name change (0436) |
| Given | A name someone bestowed, which may carry a bond or evolution | Slime's naming (costs the namer magicules, evolves the named) |
| True | The name that lets someone command or bind you | Shadow Slave's True Name, earned in the First Nightmare |
| Honorific | A title the world's powers recognize and answer to | LOTM's honorific names, which reach their bearer when recited |
| Remembered | The name as it lives in other people's memory | Re:Zero's Gluttony eats this; Julius stays capable but forgotten |
| Magic | A chosen name that states a purpose | Toaru's magic names, declared before killing intent (memory) |
| Hidden or lost | A name no one can say | The Nameless One (Planescape); `████` in our UI |

**Operations on names:** read (appraise), give (bond, evolve, claim),
eat (erase recognition or memory), force (command through a true name),
recite (call a bearer's attention), change (re-register, take a new
name), hide (shield), restore (bring an eaten name back).

**How it fits the card:**
- **Backing:** a given name rides the lineage (Slime); a true name rides
  the soul; a remembered name lives in other people, so it is the only
  name that can be attacked without touching you.
- **Outside its world:** names travel with the soul or the records that
  hold them. A remembered name doesn't travel; a new world simply never
  knew you, which is a soft version of Gluttony's eating.
- **Meetings (picked per pair):** a name-eater against a named monster
  may eat the evolution or choke on the lineage behind it. A forced true
  name against a holder with no true name yet does nothing, which makes
  "haven't earned a true name" a defense. Reciting an honorific name in
  a world that doesn't know the bearer may reach nobody, or something
  else that answers to it.

**More to consider:**
- **NA1 Names as a life-sim verb.** Naming a pet, a child, a sword, a
  shop or a technique (the naming idea in
  [presentation](PRESENTATION_AND_FOURTH_WALL.md)) could carry a small
  bond in worlds where names have weight, and nothing in worlds where
  they don't.
- **NA2 Being forgotten as a state, not death.** Re:Zero's victims keep
  their skills but lose their place in other people's lives. A quest
  line to be remembered again is a relationship game, not a combat one.
- **NA3 Names as a currency.** Fame is a name others remember. A world
  could let you spend your name (be forgotten) to pay for a miracle.
- **NA4 Naming rights.** Who may name what is a social rule: elders name
  children, discoverers name places, a monarch names nobles. Breaking it
  is a crime in some worlds.
- **NA5 The Notebook knows names.** A name you haven't learned shows as
  `████`. Learning a true name is a discovery route, which suits sealed
  secrets.

## Alchemy and crafts in the cosmology (proposal, Claude, 2026-10-10)

Weibao: "What about alchemy and more? more to consider?" Source: the
[alchemy scan](research/alchemy-2026-10-10/SCAN.md) (14 kinds, sourced
from search summaries, chapter pages, Wikipedia and UESP; Fandom wikis
were blocked and no primary text was read), plus
[Noita substitution](research/noita/ALCHEMY_AND_SUBSTITUTION.md), the
[FMA digest](research/power-systems/wiki-digests/DIGEST_fullmetal-alchemist.md)
and the Battle Through the Heavens cases in
[cultivation paths](RESEARCH_CULTIVATION_PATHS.md). The game's existing
Alchemy skill keeps its ordinary meaning.

### A craft has four parts, and each has its own backing

This is the scan's strongest finding, and it holds for every craft, not
only alchemy.

| Part | What it is | Backing | Travels between worlds? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recipe | The method | Knowledge | Always, but may be wrong in a world with different properties |
| Material | Ingredients, ore, herbs, souls | Item or the local world | Only what you carry; local materials must be found again |
| Fuel | What powers the change: fire, qi, mana, tectonic energy, a life flow | World law or local supply | Breaks first. Missing fuel means Blocked or Weakened |
| Operator | Who can do it: skill, aptitude, a trait, a system | Body, soul or system | With the body or soul; a system operator follows its system |

**Same art, different fuel.** FMA's Amestris and Xing share technique
but draw on different energy (scan 1.1). So one recipe card can carry a
per-world fuel field: qi in a cultivation world, mana elsewhere, ordinary
fire in a mundane one. Porting a craft means finding a local fuel, which
is a quest, not a block.

**What travels best:** internal alchemy (neidan, the body as furnace)
needs only a body and a method. External alchemy (waidan) needs common
materials and a crucible. Heavenly flames and pill lightning carry world
law and block hardest. Discovery maps (Skyrim tasting, Noita seeds) are
knowledge of one world, so a traveler arrives with hypotheses, not
recipes.

### Where alchemy sits: skill, cheat and source of cheats

- **As a world skill:** the 14 kinds are palettes for each world's own
  craft (exchange in an FMA-like world, furnaces in xianxia, ratio mixing
  in a merchant world).
- **As a cheat:** Perfect crafting is already in
  [the cheat library](CHEAT_LIBRARY.md). Wave cheats add analysis-driven
  crafting (Great Sage working out a full potion; Slime) and simulation
  alchemy (Toaru's Ars Magna, which turns thought into reality but needs
  more time than a human life to complete; scan 1.2).
- **As a source of cheats:** the Philosopher's Stone is an item cheat
  with a hidden payer (souls inside it). On the
  [patron gifts](#patron-gifts-what-can-and-cant-be-taken-back) table it
  is a gift whose cost someone else already paid, and finding out who is
  a discovery.

### Meetings worth testing

- **Equivalent exchange vs the 10,000x return cheat:** a world law that
  demands equal payment meets a cheat that pays out ten thousand times.
  Either the world bills someone else for the difference (a hidden payer
  again), or the cheat is Blocked there, or the world takes notice.
- **Gluttony vs a homunculus:** a being made by alchemy may have no
  remembered name to eat, or only its maker's memory of it.
- **Name axis:** some worlds may need a true name to transmute a living
  thing. Alchemy then depends on the name axis above.
- **Body-furnace cultivator vs a toxicity world:** a neidan body may burn
  off pill poison that ordinary drinkers accumulate.

### More to consider

- **AL1 Every craft uses the four-part card.** Smithing, enchanting,
  formation arrays, cooking, medicine, Gu refining and tailoring share
  recipe, material, fuel and operator. One model, many crafts.
- **AL2 Alchemy as a reason to travel.** A recipe needing a material
  from one world forces a trip; a potion holding stored fuel (bottled
  qi) carries one world's power into another, until it runs out.
- **AL3 Exchange-rate markets.** If a world's transmutation has a
  visible rate, brokers sell knowledge of where the rate is wrong, like
  the treaty rates under verse equalization.
- **AL4 Tolerance as a body budget.** Witcher toxicity and pill poison
  give the life-sim body a limit on consumables, opt-in per world.
- **AL5 Lineage alchemy.** Einzbern-style multi-generation projects run
  on the life-sim clock: your grandchild finishes the Great Work.
- **AL6 Living inputs.** Homunculi, Gu and stones made of souls bridge
  to the nonhuman fiction research and raise the question of who
  consents.
- **AL7 Careers.** Apothecary, pill refiner, guild examiner, ingredient
  forager, tester (someone who tastes unknown potions for pay), recipe
  thief, counterfeiter.
- **AL9 Small worlds are simulable.** Ars Magna needs a model of the
  whole world, which takes centuries for ours. A small owned world (CO2)
  or a pocket realm might be small enough to model in one life, so
  simulation alchemy is weak at home and strong in a pocket world.
- **AL8 Thresholds, not tiers.** Small numeric gaps that unlock large
  effects (the scan's purity and threshold examples) give crafting
  breakpoints worth chasing.

Engine shapes (four-field recipe, property axes, tolerance meter,
discovery log, stage runner, trait inheritance, fuel adapters, body
furnace, digestion) are the scan's P1–P9, all proposals.

### Memory entitlement: a shared rule across five worlds

Re:Zero (only Subaru's mind carries over), Steins;Gate (memory tiers),

proposal: every mind has a **memory entitlement** per kind of change.

| Tier | Keeps | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Full | Everything | Reading Steiner, Subaru |
| Traveler | Own memory, because they were moved, not rebuilt | Suzuha, a time leaper |
| Fragment | Dreams and déjà vu that grow when prompted | Steins;Gate's lab members, Endless Eight's brigade |
| None | Only the new history | Most people |
| Edited | A memory someone else wrote | Geass victims, Gluttony's witnesses |

Prompting a fragment into recall is a relationship scene (Steins;Gate's
S4), which suits the life-sim: helping a friend remember a world that no
longer exists.

Each world declares its mode, and the player finds out by watching a

### Slime web novel: what the full read adds (2026-10-11)

From the [Tensura deep dive](research/novels/tensura/DEEP_DIVE_2026-10-10.md)
(private; 304 chapters read in full from the Japanese original, 70
spot checks). Its corrections to the wiki audit: naming has different
payers and bonds and doesn't always make a subordinate; Existence Points
are a rough in-world estimate, not an exact scale (which softens CO4);
how long awakening sleep lasts varies; fusing skills can lose functions.

- **Names:** the WN strengthens the Given-name kind but shows a given name
  needn't create domination. Record the relationship separately:
  equality, protection, patron service, refugee naming, family naming,
  stabilization.
- **Crafts:** the four-part card fits closely (herb quality and
  magicule-rich water change yield; a maker's technique changes gear of
  the same grade). Add tools, workshop capacity and distribution as
  extra fields. A finished potion carried to Earth still works there,
  even though nobody can make one on Earth.
- **Memory entitlement:** Chloe keeps the history she traveled or
  retried; Hinata gets fragments through a soul link; Memory Orbs hold
  copied, editable memories of the dead, which don't prove the soul
  survived.
- **History pushback:** ordinary Tensura history adapts through changed
  causes; it does not rebuild prevented disasters. Chloe's journey adds a
  **protected observation** mode: the past she has already confirmed is
  fixed, while a specific present window can change.
- **HS9 Wrong public history.** Historians misattribute the sea's
  changed ecology, citizens credit Masayuki for others' deeds, and a
  foreign court builds royal legitimacy on a visitor's offhand remark.
  The world reacts to accounts that are wrong, which makes historians,
  teachers and record keepers real life-sim careers.

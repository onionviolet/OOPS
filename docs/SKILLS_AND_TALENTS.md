## What counts as what

The words overlap in fiction. A world picks its own names; the engine
keeps these roles apart because they behave differently.

| Kind | What it is | How you get it | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skill | A competence that grows with use | Practice, teaching | Woodcutting 40 |
| Technique | A named procedure inside a skill | Learned, invented, copied | Read the grain |
| Talent (aptitude) | Learns some things faster | Born, awakened, bought | Quick hands: crafting XP +30% |
| Talent (faculty) | A verb or sense you otherwise lack | Born, awakened, transformed | Perfect pitch, mana sight |
| Trait | A standing fact about you, good or bad | Birth, life events | Night owl, scarred lungs |
| Perk | A small rule bend picked at a milestone | Level, class, title | Second wind once a fight |
| Quirk | A harmless or double-edged habit | Life record | Hums while working |
| Title | Recognition that opens doors and may grant effects | Deeds, witnesses | Giant slayer |
| Class or profession | A bundle that shapes growth | Choice, guild, system | Herbalist |
| Constitution or bloodline | Body-level foundation with stages | Birth, rare treasure | Ice-vein body |
| Affinity | Ease with an element or concept | Birth, exposure | Fire affinity |
| Oath or vow | A self-imposed limit that pays power | Swearing it | Never retreat: +defense, can't flee |
| Flaw | A weakness tied to a strength | Comes with power | Aspect and Flaw (Shadow Slave) |

**Rules of thumb:**
- Aptitude speeds learning; it never grants current mastery.
- A faculty lets you try; skill decides how well.
- A title is a social fact first. Effects are optional per world.
- One thing can play two roles: Perfect memory is a faculty and also
  speeds Literacy, so it's both.

## Skill families beyond appraise and observe

Each row is a palette. Vanilla YAIR seeds are in **bold**; everything
else is a candidate for a world that fits it.

### Knowing and sensing (appraisal's siblings)

| Skill | What it does | Variations |
|---|---|---|
| **Perception** | Notice what's there | Peripheral, detail, pattern |
| Identify | Name a thing and its basic use | By item, monster, plant, person |
| Analyze | Explain how something works | Mechanism, spell structure, fighting style |
| Insight / Read people | Mood, intent, lies | Tells, micro-expressions, aura colors |
| Lie detection | Flags falsehood, not the truth | Hears a "wrong note"; fooled by sincere errors |
| **Presence sensing** | Feel living things nearby | Hostility only, killing intent, life force |
| Danger sense | A warning before harm | Vague dread, a direction, a countdown |
| Tracking | Follow trails | Footprints, scent, mana residue, money trail |
| Mapping / Cartography | Builds a map as you walk | Auto-map, minimap, sense of north |
| **Spatial awareness** | Know where everything is | 360 degree, through walls at high tiers |
| Eavesdropping | Catch speech at range | Lip reading, through doors, in crowds |
| Hindsight / Retrocognition | See what happened here | Minutes back, years back, one object's history |
| Foresight / Precognition | Glimpse what's next | Seconds in combat, dreams of days, odds |
| Divination | Ask the world a question | Yes/no, omens, tarot draw, astrology |
| Sense weakness | Finds the soft spot | Armor seam, emotional wound, structural flaw |
| Value sense | Knows price and demand | Local market, rarity, "underpriced" glow |
| Linguistics / Comprehension | Understand languages | Read only, speak, animals, monsters, code |
| **Night vision**, dark sight | See without light | Heat vision, echolocation, soul sight |
| Status window | See your own numbers | Partial, delayed, lying, others' too |

to seeing rules (E7). Lie detection to Insight to reading fate.
Danger sense to Foresight to choosing among futures.

### Meta and rule-level (rare, cheat-adjacent)

Skills about skills. Most worlds keep these rare, costly or impossible.

| Skill | What it does | Usual price |
|---|---|---|
| Mimicry / Copy | Learn a technique by seeing it | Needs the learning rule, compatible means, practice |
| Steal / Plunder | Take a skill from someone | Their consent, death, or a corruption cost |
| Skill fusion | Merge two skills into one | Lose both; result may be unique |
| Skill evolution | Pick a branch at a breakpoint | Other branch closed for this life |
| Skill transfer, teach-by-touch | Hand a skill over | Teacher loses it or ages |
| Skill slots, skill swap | Load and unload skills | Swap time, loadout limits |
| Comprehension boost | Learn everything faster | Rare talent; hated by rivals |
| Skill sealing | Lock someone's skill | Contact, a seal item, a duel loss |
| Skill grafting | Bolt a monster's skill on | Mutation, rejection |
| Rewrite / Edit | Change a skill's description | Cheat tier only |

## Talents in more depth

### Aptitudes (learn faster)

- Broad: Genius, Quick learner, Late bloomer (slow then fast).
- Narrow: Sword prodigy, Born cook, Green thumb, Ear for languages.
- Conditional: learns faster at night, while starving, near water, under
  a teacher, alone.
- Negative: Clumsy hands (crafting XP slower), Tone deaf.

### Faculties (new verbs or senses)

- Senses: perfect pitch, eidetic memory, synesthesia (see sound as
  color), mana sight, smell emotions, see ghosts.
- Body: ambidextrous, double-jointed, night eyes, iron stomach,
  no need for sleep, cold-blooded.
- Mind: parallel thought, speed reading, lie-proof mind, perfect time
  sense, sense of direction.
- Odd: luck halo, animal speech, plant empathy, death aura, machine
  sympathy.

### Constitutions, affinities and bloodlines

- Constitutions: Pure yang body, Ice-vein body, Hollow body (empty but
  vast capacity), Poison body, Heaven-defying meridians, Crippled
  meridians (a trope start that can be fixed).
- Affinities: one element strong, rare dual or "chaos" affinity, no
  affinity (sometimes a hidden gift).
- Bloodlines: dormant, awakening in stages, purity that can rise,
  ancestor memories, a bloodline enemies can sense.

### Talent states (from granular progression)

Dormant, awakening, usable, controlled, mastered, evolved. Not every
talent needs every state; a world can skip straight to usable.

## Variation axes

Any skill or talent can vary along these. Two characters with "Fire
bolt" can differ on every axis. Reuse E8F adjectives for the words.

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| Activation | Passive, active, toggle, reactive, channeled, ritual, conditional |
| Cost | Stamina, mana, health, time, sanity, lifespan, money, items, memories, a cooldown |
| Target | Self, one, area, item, place, concept |
| Range | Touch, sight, sense, anywhere you've been, cross-world |
| Scaling | Level, stat, concept stage, followers, wealth, belief |
| Growth | Use, insight, research, kills, eating, acting the role, never |
| Rank or quality | Common to unique; F to S; iron to diamond; per world |
| Source | Learned, innate, granted, stolen, item-bound, title-bound, borrowed, inherited |
| Ownership | Yours, on loan, shared (party), held by an item |
| Visibility | Public, hidden, disguised, unknown even to you |
| Reliability | Always, chance, mood-bound, needs a ritual |
| Drawback | None, cost on use, flaw always on, corruption over time |
| World fit | Full, weakened, changed, sealed, banned, illegal |
| Form | Weapon form, element form, beast form, style |

**Variation stacks are where builds come from.** "Reactive, costs
memories, hidden, item-bound" is a different ability from the same
effect as "active, costs mana, public, learned."

## Getting, losing and changing them

**Ways to gain:**
- Practice and use (vanilla).
- A teacher, a manual, a sect, a school.
- Watching (Mimicry, or ordinary learning by observation).
- Epiphany on a life event, a near-death, a tribulation.
- Eating, absorbing, bonding, contracts.
- Items: skill books, scrolls, crystals, gear that grants a skill while worn.
- Titles and achievements.
- Rebirth with memory: knowledge returns, practice doesn't. (2026-10-18 refinement: understanding carries and
  fluency regrows faster in the new body; see
  [SLC28–SLC30](SKILLS_AND_LEARNING_CONSIDERATIONS_2026-10-18.md#slc-e-carrying-to-another-life-or-world)
  and [SLR20–SLR22](DOMAIN_RULES.md#carrying-across-lives-and-worlds).)
- Shops, systems, gods, bargains.

**Ways to lose or weaken:**
- Rust from disuse (optional per world).
- Injury: a crippled arm, broken meridians, a burnt-out channel.
- Seals, curses, contracts, being stolen from.
- Aging, illness, madness.
- World rules: a skill sealed when you cross a barrier.
- Trading it away on purpose.

**Ways to change:**
- Branching evolution, fusion, specialization, broadening.
- Inventing a new concept when stuck (A Novel Concept).
- World travel: same skill, new meaning (Woodcutting becoming sword
  intent in a cultivation world).
- Corruption or purification.

## Little stuff

- A skill's name changes as it grows ("Woodcutting" to "Grain reader").
- Failed attempts at an unlearned skill still give tiny XP and a log
  line ("You fumble the lockpick. You understand locks a bit better").
- Skills can have flavor text written by your past self if you've had
  it in a past life.
- NPCs notice your high skills: the smith comments on your calluses.
- A skill used in an unusual way logs it ("You used Cooking to clean a
  wound"), and repeated odd use can seed a branch.
- Some skills have a signature tell others can read (sword style,
  spell accent), tying into appraisal on both sides.
- Talents can be wrong about you: a "talentless" test result in a world
  whose test can't measure your kind of gift.
- Hobbies: low-stakes skills like Gardening, Painting, Card games that
  feed relationships and mood, not power.

## From the bottom and from the top (2026-10-10)

Weibao: "Working from the bottom to consider subtleties but also at the
top? More to consider?" The bottom is where most people in a world live;
the top is where skill stops looking like skill. Both need texture, and
the middle needs to connect them.

### At the bottom: untrained, novice, ordinary

**Untrained use.**
- Anyone can try most things badly. An untrained attempt has a real
  chance, a fumble table and a tiny lesson, not a greyed-out button.
- Some things can't be tried at all without a means: you can't "try"
  mana sight without the faculty. The line between "bad at" and "can't"
  is a world decision, stated per skill.
- Default competence comes from life: a farm child has Farming 3 and
  Animal handling 2 at start without ever choosing them. Origins seed
  skills (see [origins](LIFE_SIM_NORTH_STAR.md#origins-and-starts)).

**Novice texture.**
- **Confident wrong reads.** Low Perception or Identify can be sure and
  wrong. The Notebook corrects it later "in a different hand" (E7).
- **Beginner's luck.** An optional, small, early streak that teaches the
  wrong lesson: the first gamble wins.
- **Bad habits.** Self-taught practice can lock in a flaw (a sloppy
  grip) that caps the skill until a teacher fixes it, at a cost of
  temporary levels. Unlearning is its own short project.
- **Not knowing what you don't know.** A novice sees one skill; only
  practice or a teacher reveals that it has branches, techniques and a
  ladder above. The skill window grows as you understand it.
- **Fear and nerves.** Low skill under pressure drops further. A
  first real fight, first sale, first surgery feels different from
  practice, and the log says so.
- **Cheap failures.** Early mistakes waste a log, burn a meal, cut a
  thumb. They should be funny or human, not punishing.
- **Tools matter more at the bottom.** A good axe doubles a novice's
  output and adds little to a master's. A master with a bad tool is
  still good.

**Ordinary life at low levels.**
- Most NPCs plateau between 10 and 30 in their trade and live fine.
  Skill 20 is a career, not a failure. The economy prices that.
- Apprenticeship is a real path: years of chores, room and board, then
  journeyman papers. It trades time for a safe, guaranteed ladder.
- Guild secrets: some techniques are never taught outside the guild;
  learning them elsewhere is theft, with consequences.
- Licenses: practicing Medicine or Alchemy without papers can be
  illegal in an orderly world and normal in a frontier one.
- Hobbies and side skills: a guard who whittles, a smith who sings. They
  give NPCs texture and give the player low-stakes skills that feed mood
  and relationships.
- Small wins should be visible: the first edible bread, the first clean
  cut, a neighbor asking you to fix their fence.

**Plateaus.**
- Every skill has soft walls where XP keeps coming but nothing new
  happens. Each plateau should name its way through: a teacher, a
  better tool, a harder target, an insight, a different world.
- Some people never pass a plateau, and that's a valid life.

### In the middle: competence and reputation

- **Recognized skill.** Around journeyman level, people start to know
  you for it. Reputation and skill can drift apart: famous and mediocre,
  or brilliant and unknown.
- **Specialization pressure.** Breadth stays useful, but the world now
  offers branches, guild ranks and exclusive teachers.
- **Teaching starts paying.** Teaching an apprentice gives a small XP
  return and a follower. Explaining forces understanding.
- **Measuring skill.** Exams, ranking tournaments, guild tests, sect
  evaluations, licenses. Tests measure what the test can see: a talent
  outside the test's schema reads as zero.
- **Claimed versus real skill.** People lie about skill. Means and
  appraisal decide who can tell.

### At the top: masters, apex and beyond

**What mastery looks like.**
- **Less, not more.** A master's action log gets shorter: one cut, one
  word, one stitch. Effortlessness is the tell. Text VFX can do this.
- **Returning to basics.** Masters often go back to the first technique
  and find it deeper. The humble starter skill keeps being useful (the
  woodcutter's first swing).
- **Breadth through depth.** At the top, a skill leaks into others: a
  master cook understands alchemy, a master swordsman reads
  calligraphy. This is the concept ladder (Skill to Intent to Concept).
- **The gap is unreadable from below.** Novices can't tell a master
  from a journeyman. Only near-peers see the difference. Masters
  recognize each other at a glance.
- **Hiding mastery.** Masters can choose to look ordinary (hidden
  strength, the sweeping monk).
- **Style becomes signature.** A master's work is recognizable; forgers
  copy it; students inherit its tells.

**What the top costs and changes.**
- **No teachers left.** Above a point, progress needs invention,
  research, rivals, other worlds or the world itself as teacher.
- **Few rivals.** Masters seek worthy opponents, or the skill stalls.
  A rival master is a resource as much as a threat.
- **Obsession.** Mastery can eat a life: neglected family, lost
  friends, a single-minded trait. Optional per character.
- **Boredom and ennui.** A master who has done it all may want to start
  a new skill from zero, which is a fresh bottom.
- **Fragility.** One injury to a master's hand costs more than to a
  novice's. Peak performance is narrow.
- **Aging.** Physical skills peak and decline; knowledge and judgment
  keep climbing. An old master trades speed for reading.
- **The world reacts.** Masters get noticed by sects, kings,
  tribulations and jealous cheaters. A world can test anyone reaching
  its cap.

**The ceiling and past it.**
- **Caps per world, never absolute.** The vanilla `max_level` (default
  60 in `src/models/skill.js`) is one world's ceiling. Each has stated
  ways past (north star limits rule): a breakthrough, another world,
  fusion, a sacrifice, a teacher who broke through.
- **Above the cap is a change of kind.** Past the ceiling, a skill
  stops adding numbers and starts touching rules: Woodcutting cuts
  curses, Cooking feeds souls, Haggling bargains with fate.
- **Founding.** The top of a skill is founding a school, a style or a
  concept others can learn. Your technique enters the world's teaching
  pool under your name and outlives you.
- **Legacy and lineage.** Heirs, students and later lives can inherit
  the ladder you built, so the top of one life is the start of another.
- **Skill as identity.** At Unity stage (A Novel Concept), the skill
  changes who you are. Losing it is losing part of yourself.

### Symmetry: the same subtleties on both sides

- **Enemies and NPCs have bottoms and tops too.** A bandit fumbles; a
  bandit chief may be a hidden master. Same rules, same tells.
- **Observers at both ends.** A novice observer misreads a master as
  ordinary; a master observer reads a novice's whole history from how
  they hold a knife.
- **Teaching both ways.** A master can learn from a novice's odd idea;
  unusual use (the Little stuff rule) can come from anyone.

### More to consider

- **Context modifiers.** Skill is never alone: tool, terrain, weather,
  fatigue, mood, hunger, injury, audience and morale all shift it.
  Keep them few and readable.
- **Teamwork skills.** Some skills only exist between people:
  formations, duets, combo attacks, a kitchen brigade. Group skill
  can exceed any member's.
- **Skill across bodies.** Transmigration, possession, a new species or
  a child body: knowledge comes along, but strength, reach and reflexes
  don't. "Knows how, body can't" is a great early tension.
- **Skill and time.** Long lives allow deep mastery; short lives push
  breadth or legacy. Time skips and offline progress should respect
  plateaus, not blow through them.
- **Skill distributions per world.** An orderly world has many
  journeymen and few masters; a wild one has many untrained and a few
  monsters. The shape of the curve is part of a world card.
- **Prestige and rebirth.** What carries over at each level of reset:
  knowledge, a percent of levels, a talent, a title, nothing. Bottom
  and top both change under each rule.
- **Skill economy.** Skill books, teachers' fees, guild dues, licenses
  and stolen techniques all have prices that should match how rare the
  skill is in that world.
- **Negative space.** What a character can't do is as defining as what
  they can: an archmage who can't cook, a warrior who can't read.

## Ways to grow, and what a level means (2026-10-10)

### Growth models

Each world picks one or more as its native model. A character can carry
a model from another world, and the two can clash.

### What a level means, per world

The number is the same; the meaning isn't. A world card should state
which of these its levels are.

| Meaning | A level is... | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| L1 Competence | How good you are at a task | Linear, everyone gets better slowly |
| L2 Power | Raw strength; level 50 kills level 10 | Gaps are absolute; a world of hard tiers |
| L3 Permission | What you're allowed to use or enter | Gates, licenses, a world of locks |
| L4 Capacity | How much you can hold (slots, mana, seats) | Breadth versus depth tradeoff |
| L5 Status | Social rank; nobles care, monsters don't | Levels matter in court, not in a fight |
| L6 Age or time | Years spent, not skill shown | Old weak masters, young strong upstarts |
| L7 Purity | How close you are to a source (bloodline, concept) | Dilution, refining |
| L8 Debt | How much the system has invested in you | High levels owe service, get audited |
| L9 Visibility | How loud you are to the world | Higher levels draw tribulations and hunters |
| L10 Distance | How far from human you've gone | Power costs identity |

**Subtleties:**
- **Same number, different worlds.** Level 40 in a soft home world can
  equal level 8 in a harsh one, or not convert at all. Conversion is a
  world-barrier rule, not a fixed ratio.
- **Levels can lie.** A level shows what a system counts, not what you
  can do. A level 3 with a cheat beats a level 30. Means and appraisal
  decide who sees the truth.
- **Level gaps can be absolute or soft.** Absolute (L2) worlds make
  underdog wins impossible without a cheat; soft worlds let skill
  and preparation close gaps.
- **Leveling can have a direction.** Some worlds level you toward a
  role (LotM pathways), so growth is also drift.
- **Some worlds have no levels at all,** only stories and reputation.
  The UI then shows impressions, not numbers.
- **Visible versus hidden progress.** A system world shows XP bars; a
  martial world shows only "your stance feels steadier."
- **Clashing models.** Bringing a G2 system into a G9 cultivation world
  can mean the locals see you skip bottlenecks and call it demonic, or
  that your levels stop counting there.

## Level scales: when is a number "OP"? (2026-10-10)

Weibao: "Types of levels and more? From 100 being OP to 1000 being OP
and more?" A level's meaning (L codes) is one question; its **scale**
is another: how big the numbers get, how they space out, and where
"strong" sits. Claude's first pass from general knowledge; the Codex
audits queued below will check it against sources.

### Scale shapes

| # | Shape | What "OP" looks like | Known examples (memory) |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Small cap | 10 or 20 is the top; every level is huge | D&D 1 to 20, many tabletop games |
| S2 | Hundred cap | 99 or 100 is the top; 50 is veteran | RuneScape 99, many MMOs, YAIR's 60 default |
| S3 | Soft thousand | No hard cap; 1000 is legend, 100 is a captain | Many LitRPGs (Primal Hunter grades) |
| S4 | Uncapped and inflating | Numbers run to millions; only relative size matters | Disgaea 9999, Prestige Grinding's hidden Body Level |
| S5 | Named realms | Few named steps, each a wall | Cultivation realms, Cradle, Naruto ranks |
| S6 | Letter grades | F to S, then SS, SSS, EX | Solo Leveling hunters, many manhwa |
| S7 | Metals and colors | Iron, bronze, silver, gold, diamond | HWFWM, Lone Wanderer's cores |
| S8 | Ordered sequences counting down | 9 is weakest, 0 is a god | Lord of the Mysteries |
| S9 | Tiers with sub-levels | Tier 3, stage 7; realm, then early/mid/late/peak | Most xianxia, A Novel Concept tiers |
| S10 | Logarithmic | Each step is ten times the last | Power-of-ten bonuses (DIE. RESPAWN. REPEAT.) |
| S11 | Percentile | Only your rank against everyone counts | Top-1% survival stories |
| S12 | No scale | Reputation, titles and stories only | Worm, most mundane worlds |
| S13 | Nested scales | Levels inside grades inside realms inside world tiers | Big xianxia, multiversal LitRPG |
| S14 | Two scales at once | A small visible number over a huge hidden one | Prestige Grinding (class 49, body 8 million) |

### What makes a number OP

- **Spacing, not size.** If each level is linear, level 1000 is ten times
  level 100. If each is exponential, level 101 already outclasses 100.
  The same "1000" can be a slight edge or a god.
- **The local ceiling.** OP is relative to the highest common level in
  that world. Level 100 is OP in a world where most people stop at 20,
  and ordinary where cities are full of 300s.
- **The gap rule.** Some worlds say one realm higher always wins (hard
  gaps); others let skill, gear and tricks cross several levels.
- **The population curve.** How many people sit at each level: a
  pyramid (few at the top), a plateau (most stuck in the middle), or a
  cliff (a few monsters and everyone else). The world card should say.
- **Where the player starts.** Starting at 1 of 100 feels like a climb;
  starting at 1 of 10,000 feels like an ant. Both can work.
- **The rate.** A thousand levels in a year is grind; ten in a lifetime
  is legend. Idle games especially need the rate to stay fun offline.

### Crossing scales between worlds

- **No universal exchange rate.** A world-barrier rule decides how a
  level from one world reads in another (north star, barriers).
- **Ways to convert:** by power (equal strength), by percentile (same
  rank in each world), by realm (named step to named step), by what the
  number counts (L codes), or not at all (your numbers vanish and only
  your skill stays).
- **Small-scale visitors in big-scale worlds** look weak on paper and
  may be strong in fact, and the reverse. Appraisal decides who notices.
- **Scale shock** is a story beat: a level 99 hero arrives where
  children are level 300, or a "mere" Tier 2 wipes out a city of
  level-500s.

### Scales inside one character

- **Different skills, different scales.** Cooking can cap at 100 while
  Swords runs uncapped. Each world, and even each skill, can pick.
- **Character level versus skill level versus realm.** Three numbers
  can disagree: a level 10 with Swords 90 in the first realm.
- **Personal ceilings** (L20) put a scale inside each person: one
  person's 100 is another's 30.
- **Hidden scales.** A character may not know their own true number.

### The idle-game angle

- **Prestige layers are scale resets.** Each layer can restart the
  numbers with a multiplier (S4) or move you to a new named realm (S5).
- **Number fatigue.** Huge numbers stop meaning anything; named steps
  and titles keep the top readable (the audits found the top usually
  stops being a number).
- **Display choice:** show the number, a named step, or an impression
  ("strong for this valley"). A world card can pick, and appraisal
  level can change what you see.

## More to consider: the life of a level (2026-10-10)

Weibao: "More to consider?" Angles not covered above. Claude's
proposals from general knowledge; examples are memory unless marked.

### The moment of leveling

- **What a level-up does:** nothing visible, a full heal, a choice, a
  fanfare, or a world announcement ("world first"). Each world picks.
- **When it lands:** instantly, after sleep or meditation to consolidate,
  or at a shrine or guild (Dark Souls bonfires, guild registration).
  "You feel ready" until you rest is a nice idle-game hook.
- **Level-up costs:** sickness, hunger, a growth spurt that ruins your
  clothes, a breakthrough that can fail (qi deviation).
- **Batched offline levels** need a good summary line, not forty
  toasts: "While you were away: Woodcutting 31 to 34, and you found a
  better grip."

### Losing levels

- **Drain:** undead, curses, vampires and level-sucking attacks.
- **Spending levels as currency:** sacrifice levels for a wish, a
  revival or a crafted item (Minecraft enchanting does this).
- **Decay:** aging, disuse, injury, a broken core.
- **Voluntary reset:** in-world prestige (G35), or a monk giving power
  up for peace.
- **Debt:** a system that repossesses levels when you break its terms
  (L8).

### Where XP comes from, finely

- **Level difference:** weak enemies give nothing, so you must climb
  (most MMOs). Or the reverse: a world that rewards punching up.
- **Risk weighting:** more XP for more danger, or for winning while
  outmatched.
- **Learning by failing or being hurt:** Kenshi raises Toughness by
  getting beaten. Losing can be a route.
- **Witnessing:** XP from watching a master or a great event.
- **Party splits and kill steals:** shared credit, last-hit credit, or
  credit to whoever planned it (G40).
- **Rested XP:** time away banks a bonus (World of Warcraft). Natural
  fit for an idle game.
- **Catch-up:** behind-the-curve characters learn faster, so late
  starters and heirs aren't hopeless.
- **XP as an item:** potions, crystals, books, soul stones; it can be
  sold, stolen or inherited.

### Caps on the whole person, not one skill

- **Total skill cap:** Ultima Online lets skills sum to 700, so raising
  one means lowering another, with up, down and lock arrows. Clean
  "who are you" pressure without classes.
- **Soft caps:** returns shrink after a point (Dark Souls stats).
- **Mentor ceiling:** you can't pass your teacher without a new teacher,
  an insight or a betrayal.
- **Body ceiling:** a species, age or body sets a limit (L23).

### Matching levels to content

- **Scaling choices:** fixed zone levels (go back and crush them), world
  scales with you (Oblivion, often disliked), or level sync that pulls
  you down to the zone (Final Fantasy XIV).
- **Suppression:** a world, barrier or dungeon caps visitors' levels.
  Ties to world barriers.
- **Overleveling** trivializes content; some players love it. Underleveled
  clears should be possible through skill and prep where the world's gap
  rule allows.
- **Requirements:** item levels, zone keys, guild ranks. Gear score can
  act as a second level.

### Levels on everything

Not just people: items, monsters, zones, homes, towns, sects, guilds,
relationships, followers, companions, mounts, the system itself and
whole worlds can have levels. Each uses its own G, L and S codes. A
level 3 town next to a level 30 sect tells a story by itself.

### Levels in society

- **Registration:** level on your ID card, mandatory checks at gates,
  fines for hiding it.
- **Wages and prices by level:** a level 40 guard costs more; levels
  inflate an economy.
- **Discrimination and pride:** low-levels shut out, high-levels feared
  or drafted.
- **Level culture:** grinding as a youth rite, "level-for-age"
  expectations (the unread audit's near-miss), people who refuse to
  level on principle.
- **Children:** born at 0, born with levels, or levels inherited. Strong
  cultivators struggling to have children is a common trope.
- **Visible levels:** aura, eye color, hair turning white, a mark on
  the hand. The body can show the number when no screen does.
- **Upkeep:** higher levels eat more food, mana or worship.

### Skills that are hidden or emergent

- **Hidden skills found by doing:** do something new enough and a skill
  appears (The Wandering Inn, Kenshi).
- **Locked by the unknown:** the skill exists, but you don't know its
  name, so you can't train it on purpose.
- **Skill interactions found by play:** two skills combine in a way no
  menu lists until you've done it once.

### Timescales and eras

- **Seasons and ladders:** the world's rankings reset per era; legends
  of past seasons remain.
- **Generational drift:** what counted as OP a century ago is ordinary
  now (arms races in means and appraisal).
- **World level:** the whole world ranks up after an event, raising
  everyone's floor.

## Implementation considerations

- **Vanilla first.** `src/data/skills.js` already has about 70 skills with
  parents and milestones. Most families above can start as new skills
  with the same model; techniques reuse the Rat-step pattern in
  `src/techniques.js`.
- **Talents as capabilities.** `src/capabilities.js` already supplies
  conditional numeric grants with provenance; aptitudes fit there
  without a new system. Faculties that add verbs need a real handler.
- **Store only what changes play.** Derive accuracy, range and costs
  from level and adjectives instead of saving each axis per skill.
- **Save changes need migration.** New skill IDs add cleanly; renames
  and removals don't.

  Farming and Herbalism) through capabilities. Both use existing
  systems and can be played in a fresh save.

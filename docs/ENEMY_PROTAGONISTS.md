# Enemy protagonists: independent arcs and book comparisons

Date: 2026-10-01, America/New_York. Status: research and original future design proposals. No protagonist NPC, autonomous progression, narrative role or new theft gameplay is implemented. This expands [Rivals, identities and fate](RIVALS_IDENTITIES_AND_FATE.md), which owns the broader archetype, identity and appropriation catalogue. [PROJECT.md](../PROJECT.md) owns current direction and implementation status.

Requested follow-up: more considerations, books worth discussing, enemies who resemble protagonists and related possibilities. Inclusion of a book is a research comparison, not a declaration of favorite status or selection of a direct fan setting. The ordinary-adventure foundation and one-realm/one-prestige milestone remain unchanged.

## The central addition

An enemy protagonist has a life and progression story that the player can intersect. They have a goal, a way to grow, people who matter to them, information limits and possible endings. Hostility is a relationship at a particular moment, not the entire character definition.

That enables a different villain loop: investigate someone else's next opportunity → decide what to take, redirect or preserve → meet the consequences when their path changes. The player might steal a treasure, sponsor the rival's growth, exploit a system objective, impersonate a mentor or prevent a disaster that the rival was preparing to solve.

The strongest version lets the player create a protagonist as well as oppose one. Robbing a talented ordinary peer can give them a grievance and a comeback route. Saving someone can create a grateful competitor. Funding a weak disciple can produce a future ally whose ambitions eventually diverge from the player's.

### Three distinct meanings of “protagonist”

| Meaning | Gameplay requirement | What it does not automatically imply |
|---|---|---|
| A person with an independent arc | An objective, meaningful progression and choices beyond attacking the player | Special fortune, a system, invulnerability or mandatory revenge |
| A bearer of an exceptional advantage | A defined acquisition, operating, growth and retention contract for the advantage | Omniscience, moral goodness or unrestricted access to every cheat |
| A metaphysical story role | A world's explicit narrative rule, mandate or pattern that affects supported events | Universal plot armor across all worlds or forced losses for the player |

The first meaning can work in the ordinary YAIRPG world. The other two are optional world/origin mechanics. A genuinely talented rival can be memorable without being secretly selected by heaven.

### What each comparison still needs

**Fated Villain:** the synopsis confirms the harvesting premise, not every step. Before adapting a particular target, verify the chosen episode/chapter's vulnerability conditions, what is transferred, what counts as defeat and what the donor retains. Our proposed fortune reserve, fate anchors and investigator responses are not certified canon by that synopsis.

**Reverend Insanity:** the checked opening supports return knowledge, coordinated opposition and institutional concern about prodigies. Exact later luck-path, theft, disguise, immortal uniqueness and opponent behavior remain separate chapter questions. Bai Ning Bing is a concrete research lead for a competing prodigy, not evidence that every later relationship or action has been audited. The earlier [alternative-power dossier](RESEARCH_ALTERNATIVE_POWER.md) has additional early Gu acquisition/maintenance evidence.

**Omniscient Reader:** the publisher premise and author interview are enough for the foreknowledge comparison. They do not establish all regression histories, scenario exceptions or sponsorship mechanics. A full Junghyeok-as-independent-actor comparison needs a selected authenticated passage before assigning exact powers or reset behavior. Do not substitute adaptation details for novel rules without saying which version is used.

**Practical Guide:** the named passage is a specific narrative constraint, not proof that every hero survives a fixed number of encounters. Our idea of an encounter pattern with player-visible exit conditions is an adaptation proposal. A deeper Names/Roles audit should choose particular scenes and distinguish character interpretation from demonstrated outcomes. Reader comments under chapters are not original narrative evidence.

**Lord of Mysteries:** the two checked passages support acquired identity memories and one theft/counter interaction. They do not verify all of Amon's identity, fate or parasitism powers. A detailed adversary case should locate one exact operation and its counter. Platform Q&A and fanfiction results are not canonical evidence merely because they share the official platform's domain.

## Enemy-protagonist progression patterns

These eight patterns are our inventions. They extend the prior enemy catalogue by describing how someone can develop across encounters. Each can produce friendship, competition, hostility or separation.

| Arc pattern | First appearance | Their own progression route | What player intervention can change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Underdog comeback | Excluded peer with a particular aptitude | Earn recognition, find a teacher, master an unusual method | Injury, sponsorship or stolen credit changes what they seek and whom they trust |
| Regressor or foreknower | Arrives unusually prepared for a coming opening | Follow remembered leads, investigate divergences, repair failed plans | Changing a prerequisite makes one forecast unreliable without deleting all their knowledge |
| Cautious collector | Seeks a particular ingredient, manual or safe route | Gather supplies, research compatibility, avoid unnecessary conflict | Competing for a resource invites trade, evasion or targeted theft rather than automatic duels |
| Rival system bearer | Pursues objectives that can look strange to observers | Complete the external mechanism's declared tasks and unlocks | Deny, fulfil or redirect one task; the rival can choose between reward and personal loyalty |
| Concealed investigator | Has an ordinary public identity and a hidden inquiry | Gather evidence, qualify for access, uncover a binding or conspiracy | Give true information, forge a trail or make the player an investigated subject |
| Companion-centered protagonist | Depends on a creature, partner or mentor | Develop cooperation and complementary capabilities | A rescued companion may alter the conflict; loyalties are independent and cannot be transferred as ordinary loot |
| Institution builder | Wants to restore a school, settlement or lineage | Recruit, improve supply, recover legitimacy and secure a charter | Supporting the institution can create shared interests; stealing its mandate transfers burdens as well as access |
| Narrative champion | Embodies a world's promise, trial or heroic pattern | Meet milestones recognized by that world's rules | Solve the underlying problem, contest the mandate or change the pattern's applicable conditions |

A rival can change patterns after a setback. A chosen disciple who loses institutional support might become an independent collector. An underdog sponsored by the player might become an institution builder. Preserve the cause and the capabilities they actually acquired rather than replacing them with a freshly generated persona.

## More consequences worth considering

### You can be the inciting incident

The player's actions can create the wrong that motivates a rival. Record the actual event rather than generating arbitrary hatred: seized goods, false accusation, broken promise, abandoned rescue or disputed credit. The rival's interpretation can be mistaken, but it needs an evidence path. Repair can change their priorities; some losses cannot be completely undone by a dialogue apology.

The same mechanism supports positive arcs. Giving an overlooked artisan a commission can unlock their independent workshop; that workshop might later help, compete with or refuse the player. NPC growth does not have to be a punishment for kindness.

### A good person can be your enemy

An investigator might correctly discover the player's hidden theft. A guardian might need the inheritance to protect a settlement. A protagonist might oppose a villain player for a legitimate reason. These conflicts gain weight when their reasons are visible and consistent, rather than everyone becoming foolish or secretly corrupt to justify the player's victory.

Formal faction allegiance and personal sympathy can disagree. A righteous champion may dislike the clan that hired them. A demonic cultivator may keep a promise. Judgments belong to specific audiences, while an actual oath or world rule has its own mechanics.

### Taking a reward can change the campaign's problem

An inheritance could include a duty to maintain a seal. A public title could require supplying a town or teaching disciples. Stealing the power without replacing that function can create a later crisis. This is an authored consequence, not a universal punishment imposed on every theft.

The player might appropriate the benefit, assume the role properly, appoint someone else or deliberately abandon it. The rival might be relieved to lose the burden. Taking fate can therefore produce objectives, not merely another percentage bonus.

### Companions and patrons have their own choices

The rival's mentor, partner and friends are not a single transferable entourage. Each can know different facts, value different commitments and disagree about revenge. Recruiting one changes access and relationships; it does not automatically grant all their private knowledge or force their loyalty. Romantic relationships, if ever selected, need their own authored agency rather than becoming a special category of loot.

Taking a mentor's role is especially interesting: the player can genuinely teach, offer corrupt instruction, exploit the apprentice or release them from an old obligation. The apprentice's development and later discovery of the truth can be a recurring story.

### Rival stories can intersect each other

Two protagonists may pursue the same master, relic or succession. The player can broker cooperation, reveal an unfair claim, back one side or benefit from their conflict. A newly revealed third interest can shift the relationship graph without requiring more powerful enemies.

Avoid assuming all rivals form a coalition whenever the player wins. Shared interests, communication and mutual trust must support it. A coalition may be temporary, with disputed rewards and exit conditions.

### The player can outgrow a former protagonist

A rival's importance is not a permanent obligation to match the player's level. An old enemy can become an ordinary contact, settle down, finish their quest, lose influence or concede. A later encounter can show the distance the player has traveled. A finished arc should stop demanding attention unless something new actually happens.

Conversely, an untouched rival may complete their own supported milestone. They should not secretly gain infinite progression solely because the player spent time crafting. The applicable time, resources, opportunities and limits must be declared.

### Cross-world stories need compatibility

One world's champion can arrive in another as a refugee, envoy, challenger or seeker. The power, social recognition and fate contract can travel differently. A cultivation title need not authorize access to an industrial facility; a remembered solution might be useful even when its original energy source is unavailable.

Recognizing a protagonist-shaped story is player knowledge, not an automatic appraisal result for the character. A local NPC can be suspicious without knowing that the player has read a source novel or traveled through another world. Cross-world recognition needs a particular witness, power or recorded discovery.

## Three worked original scenarios

These are later candidates, not selected plots. They deliberately produce different kinds of conflict.

### The apprentice whose inheritance you intercepted

An ordinary student plans to claim a damaged research manual. The player can restore it first, negotiate shared study, take exclusive ownership or supply the student with another route. The manual and any appointment attached to it are separate claims.

If the player takes the manual, the student retains prior training, relationships and the knowledge that the book existed. A later scene can show them working under another instructor. Hostility follows what they know about the loss; a missing manual alone does not identify its thief. A sincere sponsorship can create a talented peer instead.

Playable distinction: the same resource contest changes both a capability and a later relationship. The rival's growth is one authored milestone with a visible cause, not an invisible daily level increase.

### Two people know tomorrow

A foreknowing rival expects a bridge failure and a hidden rescue reward. The player discovers the risk through ordinary investigation. Repairing the bridge changes the expected rescue situation. The rival must notice the changed conditions before adapting; they cannot instantly read the player's private preparation.

Options include cooperation, racing for the maintenance contract, misleading the rival or exposing exploitation of the danger. Successfully averting the disaster can earn a different outcome instead of requiring the player to let people suffer for the original reward. The rival can care about the victims, the reward or both according to their established motive.

Playable distinction: information and causal intervention matter more than combat power. Only the bounded bridge sequence needs forecast rules; no whole-world time loop is required.

### The rightful champion hunts the usurper

The player steals recognition for clearing a sealed ruin. The displaced champion has actual knowledge of the seal's maintenance and contacts who trust them. The player gains the office's services and its obligations, without acquiring that knowledge automatically.

The champion can gather proof, offer a bargain, attempt a public challenge or cooperate against a failing seal. The player can learn the duty, restore credit, share authority or maintain the cover. Local witnesses, institutional recognition and the physical seal are separate state.

Playable distinction: winning politically creates a new operating problem. The rival can be competent and justified, and the arc can end through restitution or a shared role instead of a mandatory death.

## Implementing independent arcs without an entire simulated society

The prior [engine proposal](ENGINE_EXPANSION.md) remains the architecture owner. Current source was rechecked: `Enemy` still defines combat templates and callbacks; `Combat_zone.get_next_enemies` creates encounters; `Person` supplies inventory/basic personal data; `load` still states that current enemies are not saved. `src/reputation_state.js` now supports saved regional reputation restoration, which is useful save work but does not establish individual NPC arcs. `src/rules.js` still provides caller-owned facts, outcome selection and receipts rather than scheduling or a resource transaction engine.

The smallest new model is a saved actor plus an authored arc with a few meaningful phases. Advance it at explicit scenario milestones or supported simulated deadlines. A rival's off-screen training can be one committed job with requirements and a result; it does not need to replay thousands of invisible attacks.

Five additions, all proposed:

| Addition | Minimal data or rule | Observable benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Arc phase and objective | Stable arc/actor ID, current phase, concrete goal, next eligible transition | The rival pursues something recognizable and can complete or abandon it |
| Acquisition and commitment | Required inputs, chosen route, pending job, due milestone/time and result receipt | NPC development has a cause and does not consume or create the same resource twice |
| Knowledge boundary | Observed fact, received report or scoped forecast, with source and revision | The rival adapts to information they could obtain; changed history can invalidate a prediction |
| Shared opportunity ownership | Named opening, claimant/participants, eligibility, resolution and renewal | The player and rival contest the same thing rather than receiving duplicate unique rewards |
| Encounter adaptation | Actor-to-combat/dialogue projection and explicit aftermath outcomes | The same person can return with changed preparation, surrender, negotiate or become an ally |

Use the same declared capability contracts where a player and NPC possess comparable powers. Authored NPC progression can be compressed, but exceptions should be intentional and legible. A system bearer does not need the player's entire interface; they need the mechanics actually exercised by the scene.

At first only the selected cast receives independent arcs. Ordinary enemies stay ordinary. Track off-screen claims and meaningful transitions, not prose logs for every moment. Procedural generation can later choose compatible backgrounds and motivations, while authored milestone rules supply coherent causes and endings.

By default do not use a live language model as an authority for irreversible NPC decisions, ownership or rewards. Finite authored choices and deterministic state rules are sufficient for the first prototype. An opt-in LLM-proposer mode behind a setting could propose such choices later, validated and saved once. This is an implementation judgment, not a claim that generated dialogue could never be useful.

## Failure cases specific to protagonist-like enemies

| Failure | Required correction |
|---|---|
| “Protagonist” means secretly impossible to defeat | Declare an applicable protection and its conditions, or permit ordinary failure |
| Every theft produces a stronger avenger | Choose a response from established motives and remaining means; retreat, bargain, alternate study and closure are valid |
| Rival knows a secret player action without evidence | Require observation, a report, a supported forecast or an explicit detection power |
| Regressor predicts an event after its causes changed | Mark that forecast uncertain/invalid; preserve only knowledge that still applies |
| Player and rival both receive a unique inheritance | Resolve the shared opportunity once with explicit ownership and participation |
| Recruiting a companion transfers all private knowledge | Transfer only the information they actually disclose or the power explicitly extracts |
| Stealing the title also copies the skills | Keep recognition, access, duties, memory and practiced capability separate |
| Defeating a clone ends the controller's entire arc | Apply the declared body/controller and survival rule |
| Ignoring a rival creates unchecked offline catastrophe | Respect supported time advancement, disclosed deadlines and stops for decisions; report committed aftermath |
| Rewind restores both donor reserve and retained stolen reserve | Reset or branch ownership coherently, or make duplication an intentional origin rule |
| A dead actor returns simply because the next scene needs them | Use only an established survival method; otherwise close or replace the arc explicitly |
| Every arc needs every combination of world and origin | Test meaningful shared interfaces and support a bounded set of combinations before expanding |

## Concurrent characters, rogue clones and alternate modes

Requested feasibility follow-up, 2026-10-01: could other instances or AIs play concurrently, pass through the same events, and support a rogue-clone or other-character mode? The answer is conditional: a bounded shared-world scenario is a plausible future extension. Full autonomous play of every system by an open-ended population would be a major architectural and content commitment. Neither is implemented or performance-tested here.

### Different kinds of concurrent play

| Mode or technique | What actually runs | What can interact | Relative scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent simulated journeys | Separate characters, saves, seeds and worlds using the same supported rules | Compare outcomes or later import a deliberately defined visitor; no shared treasure by default | A useful later simulation/testing mode, but not automatically a living shared world |
| Recorded rival / ghost | A previous journey's action trace or milestones | Race against its historical performance; define what happens when the current world diverges | Smaller decision-policy scope; a recording cannot spontaneously adapt |
| Bounded shared-world actors | A player and a small number of independently controlled people in one scenario | Contest opportunities, share resources, investigate, cooperate and create aftermath | Best candidate for proving meaningful concurrent decisions; actor/state isolation still needs implementation |
| Hybrid ongoing population | Important actors use detailed supported actions; distant people use declared jobs/milestones | Contacts and claims persist, with consistent handoff into a detailed scene | Plausible later extension if summarized progress preserves the same declared costs and outcome rules |
| Full autonomous population | Every actor uses the complete economy, quests, combat, origins and world travel | Extensive emergent competition and cooperation | Largest scope: actor extraction, scheduling, save size, balancing, debugging and readable consequences |

Independent browser copies do not solve shared-world concurrency: they have different state and cannot agree on ownership without a coordinator. Conversely, characters in one simulated world do not need separate operating-system processes or threads. One authoritative scheduler can resolve their overlapping jobs and commands in a declared order.

A pacing director is another independent axis. [RimWorld's developer description](https://rimworldgame.com/) says its AI storyteller controls the events offered to the colony. That is different from a character pursuing goals through those events. A future mode could have both, but a director should not silently rewrite a rival's costs, memory or ownership to manufacture drama.

### What the current engine implies

The source still exports one `character = new Hero()` from `src/character.js`. `src/skills.js` stores mutable XP/level state in a shared registry; `src/main.js` has shared `current_activity` and `current_enemies`, and its save/load path describes one player with unsaved combat enemies. Constructing another Hero does not isolate all those globals or give it a second independent game loop. Multiple browser controllers clicking the existing UI would not establish a supported shared-world mode.

For the first concurrent scenario, reuse supported event/rule definitions with explicit actor contexts. Separate each actor's inventory, capabilities/skill progress, knowledge, commitments, location and policy from the world's shared opportunities. Bridge the selected player's results into the ordinary game deliberately. Full autonomous access to legacy combat, crafting, travel and every quest requires extracting their player-specific assumptions in later increments. Do not claim whole-game parity from a small event simulation.

The same event definitions can serve the player and autonomous actors, including the same applicable prerequisites, costs, odds and consequences. They need not see the same information or choose the same option. Actor-specific abilities and intentional privileges can differ; an AI should not get undisclosed free resources merely because its training is off-screen.

### A sustainable first decision policy

At a meaningful decision boundary, expose the actor's eligible actions and known consequences, rank them against a small set of goals, commit one, and reconsider when the job ends or relevant information changes. Goals might include safety, mastery, loyalty, wealth and a particular obligation. Tie-breaking and randomness must be saved if they affect outcomes. This is a proposal, not an existing YAIRPG policy API.

Simple authored priorities are enough for a first actor. More general planning is a later option: Jeff Orkin's [F.E.A.R. developer talk overview](https://gdcvault.com/play/1013282/Three-States-and-a-Plan) describes action planning and replanning when circumstances change. It supports the feasibility of goal-based game AI, not a ready-made planner or performance guarantee for this RPG. A planner still needs legal actions, useful goals, bounded search and tools to explain its decisions.

A language model could eventually propose a high-level intention or optional dialogue, but ordinary game rules must validate commands and commit state. Frequent model calls would add latency, cost and replay dependencies; they are unnecessary for the first clone experiment. The actor's knowledge boundary, rather than a prompt alone, must determine which actions it can evaluate. This is an architectural judgment, not a benchmark or model-product recommendation.

### Rogue-clone example

Original proposal: the player creates a copy with the knowledge and selected practiced techniques they possessed at creation. The copy starts with a defined resource endowment and a separate mind. Equipment, money, soul-bound rights and unique fate anchors follow explicit creation rules; they are not silently duplicated with a whole-character object copy. The copy initially understands the player's habits but does not learn every subsequent secret action.

Its initial mandate is to research a route while the player gathers supplies. A dangerous expedition then asks it to sacrifice its own future for the player's benefit, while a local faction offers protection and independent instruction. Established self-preservation, loyalty, treatment and the offered terms can make it comply, negotiate, refuse, leave or actively oppose the player. “Rogue” is a change in control, goals or allegiance with a recorded cause, not proof that it is evil.

The player can bargain over resources, share recognition, restore trust, sever a link or confront it. The copy keeps its actual discoveries and individual relationships. It can exploit habits it remembers, but cannot perfectly counter a build it never observed. No betrayal is inevitable just because the scenario contains a clone.

Three alternative origins should be kept distinct: a conscious copy with personal aims, a temporary projection controlled by the player, and a constructed servant with a limited command policy. Their autonomy, rights, recovery and resource ownership are different contracts. A player-directed clone becoming autonomous changes the policy controlling one persistent actor; it need not create a new person or erase its history.

### Where depth becomes expensive

| Pressure | Bounded treatment | What would enlarge the commitment |
|---|---|---|
| Shared ownership | One authoritative world state; transfers and unique claims resolve once | Multiple separate save instances exchanging arbitrary mutable state |
| Planning cost | Reconsider on meaningful changes, cap candidate actions and avoid identical failed plans | Every actor searches every possible future on every combat tick |
| Time and offline behavior | Use supported simulated deadlines; stop at player decisions and summarize committed work | Silently advancing unported combat or allowing unseen actors to consume the player's entire campaign while closed |
| Content interaction | A small cast, shared event rules and a few observable consequences | Bespoke prose for every actor × origin × world × relationship × ending |
| Debugging and readability | Keep concise reason/commitment receipts and summarize significant changes | Outcomes that cannot be explained, replayed or connected to information and resources |

The largest likely maintenance cost is interacting consequences and clear presentation, not choosing between a few legal actions. That is a qualitative assessment; actual simulation costs need profiling. Actors can take repeatable jobs in batches only where batching produces the same declared resource, timing and outcome behavior as detailed execution. Summarization must not quietly grant a second unique reward or use a different combat success model.

Mode rules must say what advances world time during training, travel, rest and player choices. A competitive mode could allow others to claim disclosed openings while the player spends simulated time elsewhere. The ordinary campaign might use less pressure. Neither should make loading speed, tab throttling or browser closure an accidental competitive advantage. The player needs a readable opportunity/actor summary rather than an obligation to monitor every character continuously.

### Proposed test before selecting a whole mode

One isolated scenario with the player, one copy and one independent peer is enough to test the core idea. Provide repeatable preparation, one shared opportunity and one consequential event. Use one ordinary local setting; clone creation rules belong to this provisional test and do not select a permanent origin or reinstate cultivation content.

Five acceptance questions:

1. Do all three actors use the same supported event definitions, with appropriately different knowledge and choices?
2. Does one actor's decision change another's real options, rather than only generate flavor text?
3. Can cooperation, independent departure and conflict each arise from understandable circumstances?
4. Do ownership, pending jobs, selected policy and outcomes survive reload without resource duplication or changed resolved draws?
5. Can the player understand the meaningful changes through a concise journal, and does the bounded simulation run acceptably when profiled?

For this test, keep mode state separate from ordinary campaign progress and use generated saves on a fresh browser origin. A scenario checkpoint is not necessarily a new prestige layer. The recorded-trace, headless simulation or competitive population mode remains a later selection; none is promised by documenting the experiment.

## Future acceptance and completion status

A first independent-arc example passes when one persistent peer has a stated goal, the player can contest or support a named opportunity through two viable approaches, one later scene reflects the committed result, and save/reload cannot duplicate the claim, job or reward. A rival can lose their opportunity while retaining unrelated talent and relationships. Genuine closure or cooperation must be possible if supported by the scene.

Further gates apply only as features are selected: changed causes invalidate a foreknowledge prediction; a duty follows an appropriated office; a costly adaptation has an evidence source; supported deadlines process consistently through normal play and time jumps; resets preserve coherent actor/opportunity ownership. Runtime changes require a rebuild and fresh-origin browser checks with generated fixtures. Human review separately judges whether the person feels like someone with a story.

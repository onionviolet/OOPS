# R3: accessible diagrams and rendering cost (2026-11-10)

Research lane for UIC64 and UIC65 in [UI design core audit, round 40](../../audits/UI_DESIGN_CORE_AUDIT.md). Rules touched: UR37, UR38, UR93-UR99, MDL2, MDL8. Research proposals only, nothing here is an accepted decision (AD32). Facts from web sources are cited; claims I could not confirm are tagged **unverified**; numbers not measured by anyone are tagged **estimate**. Search was one pass of summaries, not a reading of every primary source, so cited pages are the next stop before a number becomes a rule.

## What the repo does today (read, not assumed)

- `figure.js` draws an inline `<svg role="img" aria-labelledby=title+desc focusable=false>` (line 240). The whole figure is one image to a screen reader; its facts live in the text form, the native `<title>` words and the sentence/table/matrix forms (`figure_forms.js`), all built from the same spec. That is the "readable alternative" the field asks for, and it is already shared by every figure.
- `figure_interact.js` has a pure graph model (`figureGraph`, `neighbourhood`, arrow-key moves, filter, compare, cards). So the structure model that accessibility libraries build already exists in our code.
- `visibilitychange` is already used by `main.js` (flush saves when hidden), `simulation_render.js` (skips refreshes while hidden, replays the latest per key on reveal) and `reports_dock.js`. No `<filter>` elements and no `will-change` appear in `src` or `styles` by my grep (the filter grep errored on a glob; re-run before relying on "none"). Seven keyframe or will-change hits exist in styles.

## A. Accessible interactive diagrams

### Findings

- **F1. ARIA Graphics Module** defines three roles: `graphics-document` (the whole graphic, default for a root svg), `graphics-object` (a structured part) and `graphics-symbol` (one object whose children are not exposed separately). Names come from `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby` or an svg `<title>`. Sources: [W3C Graphics ARIA](https://w3c.github.io/aria/graphics-aria/), [MDN graphics-document](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Reference/Roles/graphics-document_role), [MDN graphics-symbol](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Reference/Roles/graphics-symbol_role), [mappings](https://w3c.github.io/graphics-aam). Screen reader support for these roles in WebKit/VoiceOver: **unverified** (a WebKit bug on graphics roles exists, [181796](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=181796), status not checked). Default: treat them as optional hints on top of a text structure, never the only route.
- **F2. Data Navigator** (CMU) models any structure as a graph of nodes and edges, with keyboard, screen reader and other input modalities, and builds a real DOM layer you can focus. Source: [cmudig/data-navigator](https://github.com/cmudig/data-navigator), [IEEE VIS 2023 paper page](https://content.ieeevis.org/year/2023/paper_v-full-1580.html). Its model equals `figureGraph` plus `neighbourhood`; its idea to borrow is "edges are named moves" (next in ring, along an edge of kind K, up to a ring).
- **F3. Olli** turns a chart into a keyboard-navigable tree view that follows ARIA tree patterns, organized by level of detail (summary, axes, categories, points). Needs NVDA focus mode or JAWS forms mode. Source: [MIT Vis Group, Olli](https://vis.mit.edu/pubs/olli), [docs](https://umwelt-data.github.io/olli/using/). The idea to borrow is level-of-detail structure, which matches UR96 (layers, not length).
- **F4. Highcharts accessibility module** gives Tab between chart elements, arrow keys within, Space to activate; sonification is a separate module that needs the accessibility module for navigation. Sources: [feature overview](https://highcharts.com/docs/accessibility/accessibility-module-feature-overview), [audio chart navigation](https://www.highcharts.com/docs/sonification/navigation). Borrow: one Tab stop for the chart, arrows inside, a data-table alternative shipped beside the drawing.
- **F5. Chartability** is an audit framework: 50 tests, 14 critical, organized as POUR plus Compromising, Assistive, Flexible; its tests include a readable alternative to the chart, 3:1 contrast for geometry and data density. Sources: [site](https://chartability.fizz.studio/), [EU guide](https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/intermediate-accessibility-audit-with-chartability), [paper record](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14522). Use it as the checklist for done, not as a library.
- **F6. Diagram-specific research.** Arboretum compiles Mermaid/Graphviz specs into one node-edge form and renders tabular, screen-reader and tactile views; so far arrays and binary trees only. Benthic models diagrams as hypergraphs. Sources: [Arboretum, arXiv 2601.19168](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19168v1), [Benthic](https://vis.csail.mit.edu/pubs/benthic.pdf). The shape (one spec, several equal forms) is exactly `figure_forms.js`. Hypergraph is the useful word for a ritual step that joins many offerings at once; `edge {from,to}` cannot say that (gap, see A3).
- **F7. Games.** Evidence found is community and guideline material, not engineering detail: blind-accessible strategy and UI-heavy games lean on a screen-reader-friendly menu layer, full keyboard control with a shortcut per action, and narration of every card, relic and map position (the "Say the Spire" mod). Sources: [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/), [Slay the Spire review](https://inviocean.com/play/slay-the-spire-the-chance-to-hear-the-spire/), [Prosperous Universe](https://inviocean.com/play/experience-of-playing-unsighted-in-the-prosperous-universe/), [AFB overview](https://afb.org/aw/fall2023/Blindness-Accessibility-in-Video-Games-A-Deep-Dive). One cited developer note says a visual strategy game took about a week to make accessible and blind players were about 1% of players (secondary, **unverified**). I found nothing on how Civilization or similar games expose their maps; do not cite them.

### Patterns that fit figure plus sentence/table forms

- **A1. Keep the three-form contract (default).** Drawn figure, table and sentences carry identical facts (already the MDL2/`figure_forms.js` rule). Dial: which form opens first per player setting. Reason: it is the Chartability "readable alternative" test, already met.
- **A2. One composite widget with roving tabindex over the figure (default for interactive figures).** One Tab stop on the figure; arrow keys move among nodes by the model that `figure_interact.js` already has; Enter pins; Escape returns to the figure's own stop; the focused node's words go to a polite live region (the table inspector already uses `aria-live=polite`). Reason: Highcharts and Data Navigator both land here. Dial: arrows follow ring order by default, along edges when an edge kind is chosen. Reason for the dial: rings and webs need two kinds of "next". Do not announce on the 1-second tick; announce on focus and on settled events only (UR52, UR91).
- **A3. Name moves, not only nodes (default).** Expose "up a level, down a level, along edge kind K, next in ring" as a short help line and as buttons in the table inspector (they already jump to linked rows). Gap: multi-party joins (a rite step with several offerings) have no edge form; a `group` list on the node or a sentence ("step 3 joins X, Y and Z") covers it until a hypergraph field is wanted.
- **A4. Layered depth like Olli (default).** Layer 1 is the title and `summary`; layer 2 is the rings, tracks and legend; layer 3 is nodes with state words and links; layer 4 is `detail`/`why`. Default: expose layers 1 to 2 as headings in the text form, layers 3 to 4 as the table. Reason: UR96.
- **A5. Roles, lightly (dial).** Default keep `role="img"` on the whole svg with the sentence form adjacent, because it is the least risky for VoiceOver. Dial: when the figure is interactive, switch the host to `role="application"`-free semantics (a `<div role="group">` with real focusable children or `role="listbox"`/`"tree"` where it truly is one) rather than graphics roles. Reason: graphics roles have uncertain WebKit support (F1) and `role="img"` hides children by design. Test in VoiceOver before changing.
- **A6. Sonification and sound (LATER, dial).** A short tone per state (filled, strained, broken) on focus fits UR91's "sound words" and needs a mute (UR91). Source for the pattern: [Highcharts sonification docs](https://www.highcharts.com/docs/sonification/navigation). Reason it waits: no sound pipeline is accepted, and word narration already carries every fact.
- **A7. Reduced motion and effects off keep meaning (already law).** UR38 and MDL8 say effects are class-only and off under `data-effects off`; the accessibility rule adds that the live-region text is not tied to an effect.

## B. Rendering cost in WKWebView versus Chromium

The app uses WKWebView through Tauri. Headless Chromium proves structure, not feel (UR16, round 40 limits table).

### Findings

- **F8. SVG filters are the main Safari risk.** WebKit has long-open reports that SVG filters are slow, with one crashing iOS pages; another found a feTurbulence plus feDisplacementMap filter broken when the filter region was large; a Feb 2025 report found a feTurbulence grain filter made scrolling much slower than Chrome and removing it fixed it. Sources: [191197](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=191197), [200964](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=200964), [287982](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=287982), [GSAP forum, mobile feTurbulence](https://greensock.com/forums/topic/33075-gsap-and-feturbulence-mobile-performance/). Current status of those bugs: **unverified**. Firefox notes that turbulence cost rises with octaves (a Mozilla bug thread in the same search, not opened). iOS findings may not transfer to macOS desktop; **unverified** for our Mac.
- **F9. Animate transform and opacity only, with CSS or Web Animations.** web.dev says those two can be handled off the main thread by the compositor, whichever API drives them; other properties repaint or relayout. WebKit makes CSS and JS animations the same object model. Sources: [web.dev animations and performance](https://web.dev/articles/animations-and-performance), [WebKit, Web Animations](https://webkit.org/blog/8343/web-animations-in-webkit/), [Motion tier list](https://motion.dev/blog/web-animation-performance-tier-list). `will-change` is a hint, not a guarantee; each hinted layer holds memory, so hint on animation start and drop it on end (general guidance; WebKit-specific numbers **unverified**). Animating SVG child attributes (cx, d, stroke-dashoffset) generally stays on the main thread; **unverified for WebKit**, so measure.
- **F10. SVG versus canvas.** No single crossover. One benchmark poster saw losses above about 400 nodes for every technology, with SVG and canvas near equal; other sources put SVG trouble past about 1,000 elements, and say canvas wins for many simple primitives; JointJS ran 11,000 diagram elements smoothly unoptimized. Cost depends on animation frequency and update rate, not only count. Sources: [Horak 2018 poster](https://mt.inf.tu-dresden.de/cnt/uploads/Horak-2018-Graph-Performance-Poster.pdf), [yWorks](https://www.yworks.com/blog/svg-canvas-webgl), [JointJS](https://www.jointjs.com/blog/svg-versus-canvas), [Smus](https://smus.com/canvas-vs-svg-performance/). All are Chromium-era or unspecified browsers; WebKit-specific numbers: **unverified**.
- **F11. Hidden windows.** `document.hidden` and `visibilitychange` cover minimized windows and background tabs, and browsers stop `requestAnimationFrame` there ([MDN Page Visibility](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Page_Visibility_API)). Timers are throttled, not stopped; exact intervals vary and I did not confirm them in a spec. Do not rely on timer accuracy while hidden.
- **F12. Tauri on macOS.** Evidence is from issue threads and a PR, not official docs. WebKit throttles a hidden or occluded page's web process by window occlusion (an App Nap-style effect is a separate, secondary mechanism); a test fixture PR disabled occlusion detection through a private SPI, and a wry PR proposed a `disableBackgroundThrottling` option whose current exposure in Tauri 2 I could not confirm. Sources: [webdriverio PR 552](https://github.com/webdriverio/desktop-mobile/pull/552), [wry PR 1445](https://redirect.github.com/tauri-apps/wry/pull/1445), [tauri-plugin-macos-fps](https://docs.rs/crate/tauri-plugin-macos-fps/0.1.0). Consequence for an idle game: a tick loop in a hidden window can slow or stall in the app. Default: drive game time from timestamps (elapsed wall time, capped, replayed in a catch-up pass) and never from tick count. The repo's catch-up path already exists for offline return; confirm the hidden-window case uses it (open item).
- **F13. Tools.** Safari Web Inspector has a Timelines tab with Rendering Frames (16 ms and 33 ms reference lines) and an Energy Impact gauge based on average CPU use. Sources: [Rendering Frames](https://webkit.org/blog/3996/introducing-the-rendering-frames-timeline/), [Timelines tab](https://webkit.org/web-inspector/timelines-tab/), [power usage](https://webkit.org/blog/8970/how-web-content-can-affect-power-usage/). Attaching Safari to a Tauri WKWebView requires an inspectable webview (debug builds, or a flag in release): **unverified** for our Tauri version, check `src-tauri` config first.

### Budget suggestions (all estimates, adjust after section D)

Reason for all: many panels mounted, one-second ticks, desktop 1920x1080, one player. Dial: loosen per surface when measured.

| Item | Default budget | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nodes in one SVG figure (shapes plus text) | at most about 400 drawn children | the lowest threshold in F10; our nodes carry text and edges, so count all children, not just nodes |
| Figures redrawn per second | 0 at rest; changed figures only, on settled events | UR52, UR69; avoids re-parsing SVG every tick |
| Mounted but hidden figures | not drawn (mount on open) | MDL7 mount-on-open already; the cheapest node is the one not in the DOM |
| SVG filters on the live view | static, small region, 0 animated by default; at most 2 on screen, no feTurbulence or feDisplacementMap on a region larger than about 400x400 px | F8; filter cost scales with filtered area. Bake grain to a tiny tiled data-URI or reuse one pre-rendered pattern where the look allows |
| Filter parameters | turbulence `numOctaves` 1 to 2; blur `stdDeviation` at most about 4 | octaves multiply cost (Mozilla note, F8); numbers are estimates |
| Concurrent running CSS/WAAPI animations | at most about 10 on screen, transform/opacity only; infinite loops only on a focused element | F9; infinite ambient loops are what keep a page from idling |
| `will-change` | set on start, removed on end; none on static elements | memory per layer (general guidance) |
| Ambient canvas | one canvas, paused when hidden, sized to the area it needs | pixel cost scales with canvas size (F10, Smus) |
| Work while hidden | pause visual refreshes and canvases; keep simulation by timestamp catch-up | F11, F12 |

## C. How other idle games stay cheap

Source evidence is thin: my searches found general idle-game engineering notes and no source on the internals of Melvor Idle, Antimatter Dimensions or Universal Paperclips. I could not confirm what those three animate. Use only the patterns below and tag the rest **unverified**.

- **F14. Kittens Game and Universal Paperclips are deliberately plain:** text, numbers, buttons; a listicle calls Kittens Game numbers and milestones with no animations ([dinogame list](https://dinogame.gg/blog/best-browser-incremental-games/), secondary). Pattern: the reward is in text and numbers, so cost stays low and motion is scarce, which matches our "text first" line.
- **F15. Offline or return progress is computed from timestamps, then summarized.** A Kittens Game developer blog says offline progress takes the time since the last update and fast-forwards, treating it as "redshift" ([Incremental Musings, 2018](https://zinsho.github.io/incremental_musings/scripts/kittensgame/2018/02/19/kg-offline)). Developers elsewhere cap the catch-up tick count (one patch note: at most 1000) and separate rendering from logic ticks, since browsers throttle timers ([Industry Idle perf post, via mirror](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://ruoyusun.com/2022/01/28/game-pref.html), [itch.io thread](https://itch.io/post/14654594), secondary). Pattern for us: render once after catch-up, show a return summary (what changed, in words), and never replay N ticks of animation.
- **F16. Cache per-tick aggregates and batch UI updates per tick** (small-game patch notes, secondary). We already skip hidden refreshes (`simulation_render.js`).
- **F17. Antimatter Dimensions** is reported by a fan guide to run at a fixed 20 ticks per second internally (old, **unverified** for the current version). Do not copy it; our tick is one second.
- **Idle pattern list for us:** animate on events (a rite settles, a slot fills), keep ambient motion to one slow layer and none while hidden, reveal return changes as a recap list with stable anchors (UR52, UR91), and skip animation entirely during catch-up.

## D. Measurement plan (run later, in the Tauri app and headless Chromium)

Goal: a budget table that UR99 can cite (UIC64). Pass/fail numbers below are starting estimates; replace with measured ones.

1. **Setup.** One save with many panels mounted (the real heavy case), 1920x1080, same machine, power adapter on, no other heavy apps; Tauri app (release build) and headless Chromium. Four scenes: idle (no figure open), one figure open at 100, 250 and 400 children, a figure with one filter material, a figure with 10 settle animations.
2. **Frame cost.** In the app: Safari Web Inspector, Timelines, Rendering Frames and CPU (needs an inspectable webview, F13). In Chromium: Performance trace via Playwright or DevTools protocol. Metric: p95 frame time while the scene runs; also long tasks over 50 ms. Pass: idle p95 under 16 ms with no frames needed (page truly idle); scenes under 16 ms p95, none over 33 ms. Fail on either breaks the budget row.
3. **Tick cost.** `performance.measure` around the one-second tick plus the render pass, 300 samples. Pass: tick plus render median under 5 ms and p95 under 15 ms with all panels mounted; compare app against Chromium and record the ratio. Dial: raise the limit for the main hub only.
4. **Energy and idle.** Activity Monitor Energy Impact (or `powermetrics` on the app's process group, which needs sudo, so the human runs it) over 10 minutes in idle and in one figure scene. Pass: the figure scene adds under about 1 percentage point of one core compared with idle at rest (estimate). Reason: the game runs for hours.
5. **Filters and canvas.** Toggle each material (grain, blur, displacement) on a 400x400 and a 1200x800 region; record p95 frame time and scroll smoothness. Pass: under 16 ms p95 at the larger region or the material shrinks to the smaller region or becomes a baked tile.
6. **Hidden window.** Minimize and fully cover the app for 5 minutes with a timestamp-based tick log; restore and compare wall-clock catch-up against expected ticks. Pass: simulated time equals wall time within the cap and the first frame after restore draws once, not N times. Also run once with the window merely backgrounded behind another app (occlusion).
7. **Memory.** Safari memory or Activity Monitor for the web content process after 30 minutes with figures opened and closed 50 times. Pass: no growth above about 20 percent after settling (estimate; catches leaked SVG nodes and listeners).
8. **Record.** One line per scene: tool, build identity, p95, verdict, gap. Headless numbers do not stand in for the app (UR16); the app run is the authoritative gate.

Safety note: agents run Chromium headless and the app hidden (standing rule); a minimized-window test needs Weibao's say-so because it touches his screen.

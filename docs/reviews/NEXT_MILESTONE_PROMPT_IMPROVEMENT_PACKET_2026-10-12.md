## What game this is

The project grows from a single-player browser idle RPG into a broad text-based progression and life RPG. It should deepen native YAIR as well as support mechanically distinct original and source-derived worlds. The native opening, quests, professions, stats, equipment and center-menu play remain usable. New fiction is opt-in, with no compulsory restart or world jump for existing play.

The intended expanded entry is a suspenseful random starting point. Frontier is an optional campaign, not a required opening or prerequisite playthrough. The first milestone is one realm and one prestige layer; that sequences delivery rather than capping the game. Prestige, travel, ascension, recovery, resurrection, inheritance, renewal and reincarnation are distinct possibilities.

Long-term play may include ordinary work, homes, relationships, production, research, trade, exploration, combat, magic, cultivation, technology, unusual bodies, continuing lives, extreme powers and cross-world interactions. A life need not revolve around combat, death, rebirth or following a protagonist's plot.

Accepted direction that must survive:

- Worlds make their own rules. Do not impose one progression ladder, physiology, economy, fate, death law or idle/offline policy.
- Book worlds begin from actual source rules; canon, divergent, remix and swapped premises must be distinguished. This next step must reuse held research and cannot acquire new books.
- Bodies matter. Person, body, life and controller are not automatically the same thing. Knowledge, practiced execution and having required means are distinct.
- Author truth, actor belief, available evidence, observation and UI presentation differ. Hidden properties still have their actual effects. Knowledge comes through means held and used by that actor.
- Things cross worlds or lives through actual carriers. Inheritance comes from what survived and was actually transferred, not an automatic reset reward.
- Time, history and consequences matter. Natural-course events change when their actual causes change. NPCs need their own means, knowledge and information channels, but every distant person need not be simulated each tick.
- Technology needs more than knowing an idea: actual tools, materials, skill, capacity, people and adoption can matter.
- OP paths and one-off outliers are legitimate. Do not invent compensating flaws, universal counters, level-scaled enemies or endless replacement obstacles merely to preserve difficulty. A beaten obstacle stays beaten unless a real cause changes it.
- Small details matter as useful evidence, opportunities or texture. Not every detail needs a stat, quest or simulation system.
- Save failures, actual resource charges, cancellation terms and accessibility controls must remain trustworthy even when fiction contains misleading information.

These are design context, not authorization to implement every family.

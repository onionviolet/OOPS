## Round 1, 2026-10-18: angles the markets rounds left out

### ECC-A: overarching principles

- **ECC1: money is a means, not the point.** The life-sim direction puts living
  first. A hermit cultivator, a tribal hunter or a monk can live without
  commerce, and the economy should never be required to enjoy a world. Its depth
  is chosen per activity and per world (CX1–CX8, scope ladders).
- **ECC2: a reference basket per world.** Each world card can name what a meal,
  a night's lodging, a day's unskilled wage and a basic tool cost in local money.
  Designers price new goods against it, and default cross-world exchange rates
  come from comparing baskets (rough purchasing-power parity) before arbitrage,
  scarcity and carriers adjust them. That gives one consistent anchor instead
  of prices invented per item.
- **ECC3: prices tell you about the world.** Relative prices are lore: salt is
  dear inland, iron is cheap near the mines, books cost a month's wages before
  printing. A price list is a world fingerprint, and a player can read one.
- **ECC4: every coin has a source and a sink.** Coin enters through named
  faucets (wages, sales, loot, rewards, interest) and leaves through named sinks
  (purchases, fees, upkeep, gifts, theft, loss). This is an accounting rule, not
  a cap. It makes intended money engines (M5) legible and accidental ones visible.

### ECC-B: property and ownership (AD31)

- **ECC5: owning versus holding.** Legal ownership, actual custody and use are
  separate (EC5, SR custody). A rented room, a sect-assigned cave, a deed to a
  house you've never visited and a sword lent to a friend all differ.
- **ECC6: property costs and earns.** Upkeep, repairs, staff and taxes (where
  M26 institutions exist) on one side. Rent, produce, passive bonuses (AD31)
  and resale value on the other. A property can run at a loss and still be
  worth having for comfort or status.
- **ECC7: property persists in its world** (AD4). A house you left in one world
  can be standing, looted, squatted or sold for back taxes when you return, each
  for a dated reason.

### ECC-C: labor and the baseline income

- **ECC8: work is the floor.** Every world needs one honest, boring way to earn
  money: a day job, gathering for a buyer or a sect's chores. Its pay comes from
  the reference basket (ECC2). Everything else (crafting, trade, investment)
  competes against that floor.
- **ECC9: hiring.** Wages, room and board, and loyalty are a sink that turns money
  into time (M5). Hired help follows companion and NPC rules once NPC-heavy work
  resumes (AD33), and is coarse until then.

### ECC-D: crafting, research and knowledge

- **ECC10: show the margin.** A crafted item's sale screen can show what its
  inputs cost and what it sold for, as the player knows them. Profit is
  legible without a spreadsheet, and the numbers are known values, never truth
  (MR1).
- **ECC11: market research is a learnable field.** Price discovery, demand
  sense and appraisal grow through trading, mentors or books (M11). A merchant
  family origin starts with some of it.
- **ECC12: recipes and knowledge are tradeable goods** under MR's knowledge
  rules: sold, licensed, stolen or leaked, and they lose value as they spread.

### ECC-E: cheats that show the economy

- **ECC13: cheats that reveal:** price sight, demand sight, a ledger of the
  world's trades, buyer intent, a merchant's purse. Each one reveals what a
  mechanism in the world could plausibly know (AD38), and sealed truths stay
  sealed.
- **ECC14: cheats that break:** an infinite wallet, duplication, a System shop
  at fixed prices, a converter between currencies. These are intended play (AD22,
  EC4), and the in-world response follows M5. Distinguish an authored power from
  a save/reload accident (EC5).

### ECC-F: contracts and law

- **ECC15: a promise is only as good as its enforcer.** A handshake, a written
  contract, a guild-witnessed deal, a magic oath and a System quest each bind
  through a different agent. Where no enforcer exists, reputation is the only
  one (M26).
- **ECC16: fraud is a belief, not a truth change.** Short-weight scales, fake
  appraisals and counterfeit coin write wrong beliefs (AD7). Appraisal and
  experience are the defense.

### ECC-G: other world types

- **ECC17: game-like worlds** can have fixed vendor prices, bottomless vendor
  purses and instant selling. That is canon for them and fine.
- **ECC18: comic or gag worlds** can run on joke economies where plot
  importance sets prices or money appears for laughs. These are world style
  data (MR world styles), not a general rule.
- **ECC19: nonhuman economies:** dragon hoards valued by beauty rather than use,
  hive barter, spirit offerings, fae bargains (M15). A player who is
  nonhuman (nonhuman starts) may value things differently too.

### ECC-H: idle and prestige

- **ECC20: reinvestment is the idle core.** Money buys capacity (tools, staff,
  property, investments), and capacity makes more money. Each layer should
  open a new sink (M5) before the previous one stops mattering.
- **ECC21: what a reset touches.** Each prestige or continuation states what
  happens to coin, property, debts, investments and reputation (M12, M27).
  "Lost" goods stay in the world, not deleted (ECC7).

### ECC-I: micro details

- **ECC22: shop hours and presence.** Merchants sleep, close for festivals,
  travel and die. An idle game can abstract this, but a closed shop with a
  stated reason beats a shop that is always open in a world that pretends
  otherwise.
- **ECC23: haggling has tells and memory.** A merchant remembers being squeezed
  and being treated fairly (N7). Existing haggling margins can read that.
- **ECC24: weights and measures differ by place:** a local pound, the sect's
  catty, the System's unit. Conversions are part of the frictions in M15b.

### ECC-J: implementation implications

- **ECC25: one money authority.** Coin lives in one purse owner per currency
  (A15), quotes go through one formula (N2), and every transfer is a transaction
  that conserves coin (ECC4). No feature writes `character.money` directly.
- **ECC26: an economy debug ledger.** It lists faucets and sinks over a time
  window, purses per merchant, and the absorption curve per good (L12). It is
  read-only and the cheapest way to see whether a change broke a loop.

  write sites route through.
- **ECX2: traders currently sell at full base value with no fraction.** M3's
  "modest fixed fraction" for the universal buyer does not exist. Introducing
  one would lower current income, so L2 needs a deliberate rebalance or a
  higher specialist price, not just a cut.
- **ECX3: trader refresh deletes sold goods.** `trader_component.js` empties

  stock retention first.
- **ECX4: several "new" proposals already have shapes in code.** Generated
  travelers hold finite coin purses with escrowed fees (`generated_people.js`,
  `story_requests.js`). The village career has a 50-coin kit debt (`main.js`).

  `inheritance.money`, `debtsPaid`, obligations). L7, L20 and M27 extend these
  shapes rather than starting fresh.
- **ECX5: the saturation cap is chosen by `type_` key prefix in three places**
  (`market_saturation.js`, `trade.js` twice). Unify that before L1, or quotes
  drift from committed prices.
- **ECX6: one global exchange rate per currency pair is wrong for canon.**
  Paradise canon quotes one Soul Coin at two merchant prices (E2). Rates belong
  to the merchant or changer, with a spread. ECC2's basket comparison gives
  only a designer's starting point. ECR6 is amended.
- **ECX7: the world card needs "none stated" separate from "none."** Most world
  docs are silent on banks and taxes (E2). Silence is not absence, and a world's
  missing institutions stay open until a source or a designer decides (M26).
- **ECX8: some worlds forbid invented economy detail.** The Fated Villain notes
  rule out inventing an economy, so M15 and M26 content there is labelled
  original. Other worlds may carry similar source limits.
- **ECX9: AD27 still matches the M7 correction.** Its text says shared
  currencies come through means or cheats, and an issuer that spans worlds is a
  means. No accepted text needs changing. E1 had flagged this as a lag.
- **ECX10: stale pointers.** SSD58 in the scope ladders points at
  `src/exchange.js`, which is the exchange-clerk parcel simulation, not trade.
  N2's cited line numbers are a few lines off. Both are noted here, and their
  owners are left unedited.

### New angles from the lanes

- **ECC27: a visible purse and a stated refusal.** When a merchant can't buy,
  say why ("the grocer's till is empty until market day") and what the
  alternatives are. Hidden caps were the best-known complaint about Skyrim-style
  purses. (E2-A1)
- **ECC28: a fallible price ledger.** "Price seen where and when," with its
  observer and date, marked stale as it ages. (E2-A2, M11, M17)
- **ECC29: money by time horizon.** Each currency's card says whether it is spent
  now, during this life, or in the next life, so a reset never silently deletes
  an economy. (E2-A6, M12)
- **ECC30: accounts bind to identity.** A bank, guild ledger or System wallet
  belongs to an identity the character may lack or lose through transformation,
  a nonhuman body or a new life. (E2-A11)
- **ECC31: a "where could this go" view.** One panel shows sale price, crafting
  use, gift value, quest use and carrier appraisal for a held item, as far as
  the player knows. (E2-A8, M5.2)
- **ECC32: a debtor start.** An optional origin that opens with a debt
  structuring early goals, such as the TTKS T2 lifespan loan or a merchant's
  ledger. It keeps forgiving defaults. (E2-A5)
- **ECC33: honest "no money modelled" worlds.** The card can say that services
  run on favors, passes and mission pay, with no coin field forced. (E2-A14,
  ECR31)
- **ECC34: the player sets prices in their own shop,** or an assistant does by
  policy, with customer reaction shown. (E2-A12, M4)
- **ECC35: a raid or tax trigger reads known wealth,** never ledger wealth.
  RimWorld scales raids to true wealth, which M25 deliberately avoids. (E2-A9)
- **ECC36: register money as a domain.** Money, markets and traders are not
  registered with `domain_registry`. New economy work lands as registered
  domains with namespaced save slices (AR1, AR2, AR6). L14 can keep
  `character.money` as home coin and add a separate purse key, so existing
  saves need no migration. (E1)

### ECC-K: what a shop stocks

- **ECC38: four stock layers.** (1) Canon anchors: what the source shows a shop selling, at its stated price, present in its dated window. (2) Staples by shop type: what the trade lives on daily (bread at a bakery, rope and candles at a chandler); the count moves with a bad day or season, the row does not vanish. (3) Seeded variety: the existing keyed draw for rarer goods, quality spread and what the last delivery brought. (4) Ask and order: goods not on the shelf that can still be had (ECC42). Player deltas from receipts sit over all four (WA40).
- **ECC39: stock follows supply, not a permission list (AD38).** A port shop carries foreign goods because ships land there; an inland village shop carries them only after a caravan, and dearer (P8 TRADE_SUPPLY, M2). Staples are the dial a world sets per shop type; the reason is the supplier route.
- **ECC40: the buy side uses the same layers.** What a shop will buy and at what spread follows its trade: the smith buys ore and scrap, the apothecary buys herbs, nobody in the village buys a Beyonder ingredient openly. This matters once making systems sell output (MR27).

### ECC-L: shop dialogue

- **ECC41: the shopkeeper is a person first, the shop is one topic.** Trade opens from that person's talk (native textline or cast topic), so shop talk reuses the existing talk hosts and person tier (WA43), not a second dialogue tree. A System store or vending spirit sets presence to none: a terse menu voice, no memory.
- **ECC42: asking for what is not on the shelf.** "Ask for X" answers from real state: don't carry it; out until market day (the restock date from the keyed epoch); can order it (wait, deposit, obligation row in `obligations_ledger.js`); kept in the back for people they trust (standing); or "try the herbalist by the mill", which is route discovery. One talk surface turns layer 4 into play.
- **ECC43: lines read state, not a script.** A greeting picks from a small voiced pool by what is true: first visit or regular, what you bought last, whether you squeezed them (ECC23), hour and weather, stock news ("the rye was gone by noon"). Pick by keyed draw on (seed, person, day, visit count) so it replays and a preview spends nothing (MR4). No repeat within a few visits.
- **ECC44: haggling is talk with real levers.** Bulk, repeat custom, a flaw in the item, a price quoted elsewhere (market knowledge, M11), information or a favour as part payment, charm that suits this person, pressure that costs standing. Each lever works by its mechanism and the result is a contest on the person's state; the existing Haggling multiplier stays the base. They remember how it went.
- **ECC45: refusals speak in voice and give the reason.** Extend the `vendorRefusal` seam past sold-out: closed for the festival (ECC22), not to someone with your reputation, not to a child, not openly in daylight, sealed goods ("I don't know what you mean"). FBK3/FBK4 already require the reason; this gives it a speaker.
- **ECC46: selling is appraisal by conversation.** What the shopkeeper says about your item is bounded by what they can know (MEANS_AND_APPRAISAL): the smith judges the blade, not the curse on it. "Where did you get this?" for stolen, marked or canon-significant goods carries real consequences (law, rumor, a buyer who comes looking).
- **ECC47: the counter is a news and rumor channel.** Shopkeepers hear prices, shortages, who is new in town and who bought what; what they share depends on standing, whether you just bought something, and their discretion dial (privacy is a per-person trait). Knowledge goes through the existing knowledge owner, so it is dated and can be wrong. A shop line can be a discovery route for a sealed secret, referenced by ID.
- **ECC48: book shops speak canon.** Canon shopkeepers use the voice and idiom the readers extracted (WA41); a lead's canon purchase on its date is a small scene, not a silent transaction, and a diverged run lets the same person react to a different customer.
- **ECC49: idle-time speech is a recap, and standing orders are talk.** Offline there is no conversation; the return recap says what happened ("Dunstan set aside two loaves for you"). "Keep two loaves for me each morning" is a standing order made in talk, paid by the existing purse seam and tracked as an obligation.

### ECC-M: implementation implications

- **ECC50: no new save slice for talk.** Lines are stateless keyed draws. What must persist (visits, last haggle outcome, standing orders) goes in existing owners: relationship or reputation for memory, obligations for orders, receipts for purchases. Old saves are disposable, so no migration is owed.
- **ECC51: buying does not wait on dialogue by default.** Dial: a shop whose keeper must vet you (black market, guild counter) can put talk first, for that in-world reason. Desktop first: one speech line at the top of the trade panel and an "Ask about" row; the stock table stays one click away. Talk adds texture and options, not clicks before a known purchase.
- **ECC52: a "why this line" inspector.** A debug view lists the facts that chose the greeting, the haggle levers in play and their odds, read-only. It is the cheapest proof that lines follow state.

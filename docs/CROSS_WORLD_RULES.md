# Cross-world rules

Working defaults 2026-11-10 under AD56 (ideas that open possibilities are welcome), adjustable; the lines listed in the [limiting lines review](audits/LIMITING_LINES_REVIEW_2026-11-10.md) (LLR34–LLR36) stay proposals until Weibao answers. Drafted as proposals the same day (AD32). Requested by Weibao ("Another caht to work on the cross world rulebook?", [log](gm/USER_VISION_LOG_2026-11.md#cross-world-rulebook-2026-11-10)); ranked FRC18 in the [coverage audit](audits/FOUNDATIONAL_RULES_COVERAGE_2026-11-10.md). Rows marked **(restates)** repeat a rule that already binds through its own code and add no authority.

Covers what happens between and across worlds: the hub or home between worlds, what money, items, skills, titles, ties, knowledge and bodies are worth or keep when they cross, whose rules apply on each crossing edge, time across worlds, local prices and cross-world trade, travel authorities and routes, converged worlds, and what other people know of a traveler. Entry and starts (how a life begins in a world) stay with WER; this book is about the road, the market and the bookkeeping between. A world may set any line differently with an in-world reason and cost (AD5, AD38); each row gives the default, the dial and the reason.

What Weibao has said: many mechanically distinct worlds with meaningful interactions between them is his standing direction (PROJECT scope); worlds keep their own histories and crossing does not reset them (AD4, OD5); memories and the base cheat carry, the rest depends on world and means (AD11); local economies with means-based shared currencies, cross-world trade, arbitrage and useful knowledge or items (AD27, OD11); no global market and no universal conversion chosen; "Everything is possible, reasonably" (AD5); things work by how they work (AD38); hub worlds are a real premise family he already selected (Reincarnation Paradise, WER13).

Owners and neighbours (linked, not restated): WER1–WER17 in [world entry rules](WORLD_ENTRY_RULES.md) (starts, crossing methods XM1–XM10, hubs WER17); WKR17–WKR18 in [world kind rules](WORLD_KIND_RULES.md) (authority per edge, converged worlds); [multiverse structures](worlds/MULTIVERSE_STRUCTURES.md) (cosmologies, OD5, OD11 EC1–EC5, GW4, the five proposed records); TMR7, TMR9, TMR10, TMR17 in [time rules](TIME_RULES.md); ECR24–ECR28 in [domain rules](DOMAIN_RULES.md#economy-rules); ITM1, ITM4, ITM6 in [item rules](ITEM_RULES.md); TIE25 (held as LLR1) in [relationship rules](RELATIONSHIP_RULES.md); KNW in [knowledge rules](KNOWLEDGE_RULES.md); STN11 (LLR13) in [standing rules](STANDING_RULES.md); CHT5 and CHT14 in [cheat rules](CHEAT_RULES.md); Rulebook 4, 5 and 8 in [RULES_FROM_SCRATCH](RULES_FROM_SCRATCH.md#the-rulebook-accepted-2026-10-10); SR1–SR24 in [site rules](SITE_AND_EXPLORATION_RULES.md). ## Terms

| Term | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Crossing | One passage of a person, cargo or message from a world to another, by a carrier. Entry (WER) is the first crossing of a life; every later one is covered here |
| Edge | One directed crossing between two worlds (or a hub and a world), with its carrier, authority model and terms. The unit these rules attach to |
| Carrier | What makes a crossing possible and decides what comes through: a cheat, a hub, a ritual, a ship, a contract, a body's own power (AD11, WER9) |
| Hub | A world others are reached through (WER17): a mission hub, a crossroads, a cheat or system space, a between-lives realm, a private realm |
| Home | Where a traveler's life is based, and so where their stock, household and standing live. May be an ordinary world, a hub or a pocket realm |
| Carry | What a traveler keeps through a crossing, split into memory, base cheat, body, goods, standing and knowledge |
| Worth | What a thing is valued at where it lands, by that world's buyers and law, not its home price |
| Authority model | Which world's rules apply on an edge: `place`, `traveler`, `raw`, `equalized`, `dominant` (`AUTHORITY_MODELS`) |
| Instance | One concrete history of a world (`world_instances.js`). A return reaches an instance, not a name |
| Notice | What people in the destination know or suspect of an arrival (WER12) |

## The hub and the home

| Code | Rule | Default, dial, reason | Builds on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| XWR1 | **Any world can be a base; a hub is just a world that others are reached through.** A traveler's home is wherever their stock, household and standing live. | Default: the world of the current life; a hub, a pocket realm or a ship becomes home only when the traveler's stock and household are there. Dial: a world that offers a hub, optional, mandatory, hidden or found later (WER17). Reason: WER13 says no world is a required waiting room by accident, and hubs are a premise family Weibao already selected. | WER13, WER17, AD4 |
| XWR2 | **A hub states its answers once and every crossing through it reads them.** | Default: the WER17 answers (who runs it and why, arrival and leaving, what crosses and how, its time rate, failure and death stakes, whether visited worlds are copies, nested starts) are stored as the hub's record; each edge from the hub names the carrier from it. Dial: per hub. Reason: eight hub answers repeated per edge would disagree. | WER17, WKR6 |
| XWR3 | **The hub is the carrier where it says so, and may be the shop.** | Default: a mission hub carries chosen goods and standing through its own points, contracts or storage, and may sell and buy; it values what it buys by its own rules (a system buyer's own valuation, EC4). Dial: per hub; a hub with no market is valid. Reason: Rulebook 5 and AD11; a hub that carries nothing is only a menu. | AD11, CHT14 |
| XWR4 | **A hub's own stock and household are real and conserved.** | Default: stock stored at a hub is held, owned and placed like any container's (ITM4, ITM6); a hub that takes items in custody keeps them until the contract says otherwise; a hub's own people have their own records (TIE). Dial: a hub that deletes at the end of a life declares it, with the recap (XWR12). Reason: custody, not a counter; ECR24 and ECR25. | ITM4, ITM6, ECR24, ECR25 |

## What crosses

| Code | Rule | Default, dial, reason | Builds on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| XWR5 | **Every crossing names its carrier and what that carrier carries (restates AD11, WER9).** Memories and the base cheat carry; everything else follows the carrier's terms. | Default: the carry list is one record per edge covering memory, base cheat, body, goods, money, skills, titles, ties and knowledge, each marked carried, converted, kept at home or lost, in words the confirm step shows before commitment. Dial: per edge and cheat. Reason: AD11; WER3 asks the same nine questions for entry and the road back needs the same answers. | AD11, WER3, WER9 |
| XWR6 | **A title's record travels, its power follows its means.** Historical recognition, entitlement, active power and the means that make it work are different facts. | Default: the record that the traveler earned a title crosses as history (a person who knows can learn it, KNW); an entitlement or active power works abroad only where the destination recognises its issuer or the carrier brings the means. Dial: a title backed by a world law that is itself independent, or by the base cheat, works broadly; an equalising edge converts it; a power-bearing title can be a carried item. Reason: the multiverse title note separates these and selects no universal conversion; STN11 holds foreign credentials as unrecognised by default (LLR13). **Held for Weibao as LLR34.** | STN11, CHT5, multiverse titles |
| XWR7 | **Skills cross as learned functions and keep their real requirements.** | Default: a method the traveler learned stays learned; it works in the destination where the destination supplies its requirements (materials, an energy source, a body that supports it, a legal or social setting) and by the edge's authority model (XWR10). Where it cannot run, the traveler sees what is missing, and a substitute can be found or made. Dial: a faculty that is independent of its source works everywhere; a source-bound one (a god's grant) may have no claim abroad. Reason: multiverse structures' "retains its real requirements"; passage never copies mastery or treats a fragment as a learned method (`native_realm_traveler.js`). | AD38, ABR17 |
| XWR8 | **A body crosses with its history; the destination may not support it as it is.** | Default: a body keeps its injuries, aging, species and changes; a destination that cannot support it (air that does not feed qi, a gravity, a food) shows that before the crossing where the traveler could know, and the carrier or a local means can adapt the body or the traveler adapts by living there. Dial: a carrier that adapts bodies, a world whose people cannot cross, projection or an alternate body in place of the real one (`REALM_TRAVELER_MODES`). Reason: AD13 and AD38; a body that works "because the player wants it to" would make every edge the same. **Held for Weibao as LLR35.** | AD13, WKR17 |
| XWR9 | **Goods cross as conserved stock by a carrier.** | Default: carried goods leave the origin stock and arrive in the destination's by the carrier's terms; no duplication by crossing; a failed or interrupted crossing says where the goods are (held at the edge, lost, left behind). Dial: a carrier that copies (a deliberate power, ECR rewind exceptions), a hub that delivers duplicates under a contract. Reason: ECR conservation; save accidents are not authored duplication powers (EC5). | ITM6, ECR27, EC5 |
| XWR10 | **Each edge names whose rules apply (restates WKR17).** | Default: `place` for local mechanisms while what the body or mind carries keeps its home rules unless the edge says otherwise; `traveler`, `raw`, `equalized` and `dominant` are the other models. Where two laws meet, the edge names the order effects apply in; no winner is chosen from franchise prestige. Dial: per edge. Reason: a cultivator in a gun world keeps their qi but the gun world's air may not feed it. | WKR17, WDI14 |
| XWR11 | **Ties and knowledge cross as memory, not as links.** | Default: what the traveler remembers of a person crosses with them (memory is carried, AD11); a tie stays in its life and world (TIE25, held as LLR1); a copy of someone elsewhere is a different person with their own record. Dial: a world mechanism carries a tie with a cost (a soul bond, a pact, a summon); a counterpart who shares a source can be met by name through the edge. Reason: PIR22, AD11 and AD12. | TIE25, KNW1, PIR22 |

## Time across worlds

| Code | Rule | Default, dial, reason | Builds on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| XWR12 | **The world you leave is frozen; the recap says what you left (restates TMR7, TMR17).** | Default: a world not played does not advance; arriving back shows the minutes processed, the stop reason and only what the character could know (TMR10). Dial: a world that moves on in a summary tick or at a fixed ratio with an in-world reason, or a hub with its own time rate (XWR2). Reason: AD16 and Rulebook 7; reloading never grants stock. | TMR7, TMR10, TMR17 |
| XWR13 | **Time on the road is real and sits on the edge's own clock.** | Default: a crossing advances the root clock by its travel time, recorded on the edge (`travel_legs.js` legs have minutes and means); the traveler's body clock runs by what is played (TMR3, TMR15); a crossing with a time offset (a world that runs faster) says so before commitment. Dial: instant crossings by means; a time power at an edge works by how it works, with its cost (TMR11). Reason: TMR14, travel rarely decides play. | TMR3, TMR11, TMR14 |
| XWR14 | **Each world has its own calendar and the road reads both.** | Default: a crossing shows the date at the destination as well as at home; a schedule or deadline in either world is forewarned before a bank spend or a skip crosses it (TMR8, TMR13). Dial: a loop world's reset rule decides what a crossing into it keeps (multiverse cycles row). Reason: TMR2; a sect's lunar month and Hogwarts' terms are different years. | TMR2, TMR8, TMR13 |

## Value and the market

| Code | Rule | Default, dial, reason | Builds on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| XWR15 | **Prices are local; there is no global market or conversion (restates AD27).** | Default: each world's basket and buyers set price; a good that is cheap here and rare there is a real chance (EC1), intended arbitrage is a feature (ECR1). Dial: a shared currency through an accepting issuer or network (a hub's points, a guild's notes) with an exchange the issuer states (EC5). Reason: his OD11. | ECR1, AD27, EC1, EC5 |
| XWR16 | **Money crosses at the carrier's valuation, modestly by default (restates ECR27).** | Default: coin does not cross at face; a carrier (a hub exchange, a broker, a smuggler) swaps at its own rate and fee, and better carryover is earned; before a crossing the traveler sees what is likely to carry as far as they know it (ECR28). Dial: a shared currency; a cheat that converts. Reason: ECR27 and AD11. | ECR27, ECR28 |
| XWR17 | **Local price is a shape: a base per good plus a regional function, never an invented stock.** | Default: each world gives goods a base value and a periodic or regional price function (the Naev shape, NV-D4); price moves with the buyer's need and what the player does (EC3); a price move is not a stock move, and goods shipped between worlds are conserved stock (XWR9) while commodities with no stock behind them stay a quote (FRC46's warning). Dial: a supported market that holds prices steady; a system buyer's own valuation (EC4). Reason: Naev's own note that price movement alone does not show conserved stock; ECR conservation is still owed. | EC3, EC4, FRC46 |
| XWR18 | **Knowing, proving, teaching, making and selling are different capabilities (restates GW4, EC2).** | Default: carrying a method's memory does not carry tools, workers or a market; a product made abroad sells where there is a buyer, at that buyer's valuation. Dial: a cheat that removes a bottleneck through its real means; no tax, cap or forced normalization (AD5, AD38). Reason: his OD11; profitable loops stay rewards. | EC2, GW4, AD38 |
| XWR19 | **Brokerage, translation, appraisal and portable workshops are ordinary livelihoods across edges.** | Default: a trade, a service or a business that spans worlds is a livelihood (LVR) with its edge named; nothing is simulated for worlds the traveler is not in (XWR12). Dial: per world and cheat; a commerce cheat (EC4) is the shortcut. Reason: his OD11 "arbitrage and useful knowledge/items"; ideas, not a build wave. | LVR, EC4 |

## Travel and its authorities

| Code | Rule | Default, dial, reason | Builds on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| XWR20 | **A route is a list of legs with a means, minutes, a fare and hazards.** A route between worlds adds a crossing leg to the same shape. | Default: `travel_legs.js` quotes and settles a route (`crossing` is already a means); a world that wants gates, passes, tolls or a ticket states them as the leg's fare and gate. Dial: per route; a route that opens with means and use (OD5). Reason: SR1–SR24, one route shape; hazard rolls are keyed by world, route, leg and day so a replay is the same. | SR rules, WA37 |
| XWR21 | **A travel authority is a source of access, and rival ones are allowed.** A hub, a network, a guild, a god or a device grants crossings by its own terms. | Default: each authority names who it admits, what it carries, what it charges, what it refuses and what it may revoke; a traveler may hold several and use whichever fits; none is a power rank (multiverse clusters row). Dial: per authority; a passage built beyond a known network is a player project with real requirements. Reason: OD5 and the cluster row; access and compatibility grow by means. | WER17, CHT5 |
| XWR22 | **Return is reciprocal by default and depends on means (restates AD12).** | Default: the way back exists with the way in unless the edge says otherwise; a one-way edge is announced before the crossing wherever the traveler could know. Dial: a one-way crossing, a return from an unconscious body, a hostile hub. Reason: AD12 and the unconscious-body return; AD19 forgiving recovery. | AD12, AD19 |
| XWR23 | **A crossing can fail, be interrupted or be refused, and the aftermath is real.** | Default: failure leaves a state (goods held, a body placed, a debt) and a recap, never a silent reset; interruption is a stop point (TMR8). Dial: a harsh crossing declared in its terms. Reason: hazards and exits are authored; multiverse structures ask to review failed entry and interrupted return. | TMR8, AD19 |
| XWR24 | **Reaching a world is reaching an instance.** | Default: a return goes to the same instance (its people, claims and goods); a parallel or fresh version is a separate history with its own state, reached by means; counterparts after a fork are distinct people (XWR27). Dial: a replaying trial that resets with its declared domain. Reason: AD4, OD5 and `world_instances.js`. | AD4, OD5 |

## Converged worlds and other people

| Code | Rule | Default, dial, reason | Builds on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| XWR25 | **Converged worlds declare the merge (restates WKR18).** | Default: a merge event, real ownership and history conflicts, no automatic winner among laws unless a dominant law or an equalization is declared; refugees, contested land and rebuilt supply are the play. Dial: per merge. Reason: multiverse structures' converged row. | WKR18 |
| XWR26 | **Other people know what their means allow of a traveler (restates WER12).** | Default: a quiet crossing is not noticed by ordinary people; worlds whose canon reacts to outsiders (divination, fate, a system, a god's claim) notice through their own mechanisms; what is known of a traveler is a belief that can be wrong, kept in the knower's own store (KNW5, KNW6). Dial: a hub whose staff know every arrival; an edge that marks its users. Reason: drama comes from the world's laws; AD6. | WER12, KNW5, KNW6, AD6 |
| XWR27 | **A counterpart is a different person after the fork, and a traveler's other self is a different self.** | Default: a person in a branch or a parallel world shares a source with their counterpart and nothing else; a traveler who meets their own counterpart meets a stranger with a familiar face. Dial: a premise where two are one (shared body, shared mind, a bound soul, AD10). Reason: multiverse structures' branching row. **Held for Weibao as LLR36.** | AD10, multiverse branching row |
| XWR28 | **An imported thing keeps its provenance.** | Default: an item, a person's record or a method crossing keeps where it came from (the source world, the instance, the carrier), so a seller, an appraiser or a rival can read it by their means; the source is never leaked to the player beyond what they know (sealed secrets). Dial: a destination that cannot read the origin sees only its look. Reason: ITM1 (an attachment makes an individual) and KNW6. | ITM1, KNW6 |

## Building a crossing slice

1. Name the edge: the two worlds (or hub and world), the carrier, the authority model (XWR5, XWR10), and the instance it reaches (XWR24).
2. Fill the carry list, with each of the nine rows marked, and show it in words before the confirm (XWR5, WER3).
3. Put the legs on `travel_legs.js` and read the clock (XWR13, XWR20); add no clock counter (TMR1).
4. Conserve goods and money (XWR9, XWR16); local prices come from the destination's own shape (XWR17).
5. Say what the world left behind did, by freezing (XWR12), and what the recap shows (TMR10).
6. Tests: `tests/travel_legs.test.js`, `tests/world_instances.test.js`, a crossing fixture with an off-canon input (a different purse, carrier or timing, ST14); browser proof at the desktop viewport (AD43); save, reload and import when persistence changes.

## Open details

- Where the per-edge carry list is stored: proposal, a record on the edge (a `crossings` family already exists in `FAMILIES`); no field is added by this book.
- A hub-points currency is not modelled; Reincarnation Paradise and the portal ideaboard are the first readers (PRT1–PRT26).
- No slice-equivalence test exists for a crossing that is interrupted and resumed.
- Whether bodies, titles and counterparts (LLR34–LLR36) stay at these defaults is his call.
- `native_realm_traveler.js` retains one learned method by hard-coded id; the general shape for retained methods is XWR7.
